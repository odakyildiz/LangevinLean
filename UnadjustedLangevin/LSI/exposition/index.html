Unadjusted Langevin under LSI

Relative-entropy convergence under gradient smoothness, a logarithmic Sobolev inequality, and finite initial entropy.

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

The Gaussian interpolation has positive differentiable density, finite score moments, a weak equation, and a locally absolutely continuous entropy. The initial-time comparison and compatibility of constants follow from the stated assumptions. Every discrete divergence is finite.

01The Gibbs relative density and Gaussian Euler interpolation §

Write ν(dy)=J(y)dy\nu(dy)=J(y)dyν(dy)=J(y)dy with J=Z−1e−U>0J=Z^{-1}e^{-U}>0J=Z−1e−U>0. A positive continuously differentiable relative density rrr satisfying ∫r dν=1\int r\,d\nu=1∫rdν=1 defines probability rνr\nurν. Its logarithmic Sobolev inequality is

The entropy and Fisher information are initially extended nonnegative quantities. Their finiteness will follow for the Gaussian interpolation, whose positive continuously differentiable relative density belongs to this test class.

Take X∼μX\sim\muX∼μ independent of Z0∼N(0,Id)Z_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)Z0​∼N(0,Id​) and define

The drift during this interpolation is fixed at its initial value. Its discrepancy from the Langevin drift is the difference of ∇U(X)\nabla U(X)∇U(X) and ∇U(Yt)\nabla U(Y_t)∇U(Yt​). Estimating that difference gives a continuous entropy inequality whose two endpoint laws are exactly the two discrete laws needed for the recurrence.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

IsLogSobolev includes the probability normalization of its target and quantifies over the positive differentiable density class. The relative-density results subsequently identify the positive Gaussian mixture divided by Gibbs with that same interpolation law. This supplies the density to which the functional inequality is applied.
