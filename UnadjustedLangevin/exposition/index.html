Unadjusted Langevin under Strong Convexity

A quantitative sampling theorem with a constructed stationary reference.

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

The strongly convex convergence theorems construct their Brownian reference and prove Gibbs stationarity. The LSI extension derives its entropy bound from smoothness, LSI, and finite initial KL.

01From convexity and smoothness to sharp contraction §

Write g=∇Ug=\nabla Ug=∇U. Strong convexity and gradient Lipschitzness give the complementary quadratic models

U(x)+⟨g(x),y−x⟩+m2∥y−x∥2≤U(y)≤U(x)+⟨g(x),y−x⟩+L2∥y−x∥2.U(x)+\langle g(x),y-x\rangle+\frac m2\|y-x\|^2 \le U(y)\le U(x)+\langle g(x),y-x\rangle+\frac L2\|y-x\|^2.U(x)+⟨g(x),y−x⟩+2m​∥y−x∥2≤U(y)≤U(x)+⟨g(x),y−x⟩+2L​∥y−x∥2.

The lower inequality follows by subtracting m∥x∥2/2m\|x\|^2/2m∥x∥2/2 and using the supporting-plane inequality for a differentiable convex function. For the upper inequality, integrate the gradient along the segment from xxx to yyy; the remainder is bounded by L∫01t dt ∥y−x∥2L\int_0^1t\,dt\,\|y-x\|^2L∫01​tdt∥y−x∥2. Thus no Hessian assumption is required.

Apply cocoercivity to the shifted function U−m∥⋅∥2/2U-m\|\cdot\|^2/2U−m∥⋅∥2/2, whose quadratic upper-model constant is L−mL-mL−m. With d=x−yd=x-yd=x−y and v=g(x)−g(y)v=g(x)-g(y)v=g(x)−g(y), expanding its conclusion yields

mL∥d∥2+∥v∥2≤(m+L)⟨v,d⟩.mL\|d\|^2+\|v\|^2\le(m+L)\langle v,d\rangle.mL∥d∥2+∥v∥2≤(m+L)⟨v,d⟩.

∥v−m+L2d∥≤L−m2∥d∥.\left\|v-\frac{m+L}{2}d\right\|\le\frac{L-m}{2}\|d\|.​v−2m+L​d​≤2L−m​∥d∥.

02Normalize the target and control its score §

Strong convexity gives, at any fixed x0x_0x0​, a quadratic lower bound for U(x)U(x)U(x). Completing its square shows that e−U(x)e^{-U(x)}e−U(x), even when multiplied by any fixed quadratic polynomial in xxx, is dominated by an integrable translated Gaussian. Therefore the partition function is finite, strictly positive, and the normalized Gibbs law has a finite second moment.

A second moment needed below concerns the gradient. The smooth upper model implies, for any vvv,

U(x−v)≤U(x)−⟨g(x),v⟩+L2∥v∥2.U(x-v)\le U(x)-\langle g(x),v\rangle+\frac L2\|v\|^2.U(x−v)≤U(x)−⟨g(x),v⟩+2L​∥v∥2.

Exponentiate the rearranged inequality and integrate. Translation of Lebesgue measure preserves the partition function, giving

∫e⟨g(x),v⟩ π(dx)≤eL∥v∥2/2.\int e^{\langle g(x),v\rangle}\,\pi(dx)\le e^{L\|v\|^2/2}.∫e⟨g(x),v⟩π(dx)≤eL∥v∥2/2.

For each unit coordinate vector, use both signs of a scalar multiple of that vector. The resulting sub-Gaussian bound yields a centered directional score with second moment at most LLL. Summing over an orthonormal basis gives

∫∥g(x)∥2 π(dx)≤Ld.\int\|g(x)\|^2\,\pi(dx)\le Ld.∫∥g(x)∥2π(dx)≤Ld.

This derivation uses the original first-derivative assumptions. It also proves the gradient integrability that will justify drift integrals, rather than treating that integrability as an additional assumption.

03Construct a stationary comparison diffusion §

Take the constructed centered Gaussian process BtB_tBt​, with independent increments, covariance E[BsBtT]=min⁡(s,t)Id\mathbb E[B_sB_t^{\mathsf T}]=\min(s,t)I_dE[Bs​BtT​]=min(s,t)Id​, and continuous dependence on time in mean square. Choose it independently of Y0∼πY_0\sim\piY0​∼π. In the Hilbert space of square-integrable random vectors, solve

Yt=Y0−∫0tg(Ys) ds+2Bt.Y_t=Y_0-\int_0^t g(Y_s)\,ds+\sqrt2 B_t.Yt​=Y0​−∫0t​g(Ys​)ds+2​Bt​.

The drift induces an LLL-Lipschitz map on that Hilbert space. Picard iteration converges on finite time intervals, and the integral inequality for the difference of two candidate solutions gives uniqueness by Gronwall. The resulting process is continuous in mean square. Brownian increments on disjoint intervals are independent and have covariance equal to the interval length.

Stationarity needs a separate argument. Start a Metropolis-adjusted Euler chain in π\piπ. Detailed balance makes every adjusted marginal exactly π\piπ. At a mesh size δ\deltaδ, the expected rejection-weighted displacement under Gibbs is at most Cδ3/2C\delta^{3/2}Cδ3/2, where CCC is finite and independent of sufficiently small δ\deltaδ. Common-noise proposals are nonexpansive in the small-step regime, so iterating the coupled transition for nnn steps gives a joint law of the unadjusted and adjusted endpoints with mean separation at most nCδ3/2nC\delta^{3/2}nCδ3/2.

Fix a horizon T>0T>0T>0, take δ=T/n\delta=T/nδ=T/n, and realize the unadjusted endpoint using the Brownian mesh increments. The bound becomes

E∥XnEuler−Xnadjusted∥≤TCδ⟶0.\mathbb E\|X_n^{\mathrm{Euler}}-X_n^{\mathrm{adjusted}}\| \le TC\sqrt\delta\longrightarrow0.E∥XnEuler​−Xnadjusted​∥≤TCδ​⟶0.

The matching-input Euler theorem also gives convergence of the unadjusted endpoint to YTY_TYT​ in mean square. For a frequency vvv, the bounded characteristic test x↦ei⟨v,x⟩x\mapsto e^{i\langle v,x\rangle}x↦ei⟨v,x⟩ has Lipschitz constant ∥v∥\|v\|∥v∥. Its expectations therefore converge both under the vanishing mean-separation coupling and under mean-square Euler convergence. Since the adjusted endpoint has law π\piπ, the characteristic function of YTY_TYT​ equals that of π\piπ. Uniqueness of characteristic functions proves Law⁡(YT)=π\operatorname{Law}(Y_T)=\piLaw(YT​)=π. At T=0T=0T=0, the conclusion is the initialization assumption.

Hence ∥g(Yt)∥L2≤Ld\|g(Y_t)\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Ld}∥g(Yt​)∥L2​≤Ld​ uniformly in time. The construction supplies both the stationary law and the independent future Brownian increments needed for the one-step comparison.
