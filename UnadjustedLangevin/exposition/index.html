Unadjusted Langevin under Strong Convexity

A Wasserstein convergence bound for a strongly convex potential.

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.651.651.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

The proof establishes Gibbs stationarity and compares ULA with a stationary Langevin process. It assumes differentiability, strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient. The separate LSI report proves an entropy estimate from smoothness, LSI and finite initial relative entropy.

02Gibbs normalization and score moments §

Strong convexity at a fixed x0x_0x0​ bounds U(x)U(x)U(x) below by a positive quadratic plus an affine function. Completing the square bounds e−U(x)e^{-U(x)}e−U(x), and its product with a quadratic polynomial in xxx, by integrable translated Gaussians. Strict positivity then gives a positive finite partition and a finite Gibbs second moment (see ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13 and ULA-SC, Section 3.26.14).

The gradient second moment follows from the smooth upper model. For every vvv,

U(x−v)≤U(x)−⟨g(x),v⟩+L2∥v∥2.U(x-v)\le U(x)-\langle g(x),v\rangle+\frac L2\|v\|^2.U(x−v)≤U(x)−⟨g(x),v⟩+2L​∥v∥2.

∫e⟨g(x),v⟩ π(dx)≤eL∥v∥2/2.\int e^{\langle g(x),v\rangle}\,\pi(dx)\le e^{L\|v\|^2/2}.∫e⟨g(x),v⟩π(dx)≤eL∥v∥2/2.

∫∥g(x)∥2 π(dx)≤Ld.\int\|g(x)\|^2\,\pi(dx)\le Ld.∫∥g(x)∥2π(dx)≤Ld.
