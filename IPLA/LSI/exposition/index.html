Interacting Particle Langevin under LSI

Joint entropy, per-particle scaling, and the parameter marginal.

Unpublished proof

The IPLA LSI proof presented here is unpublished. It derives the joint entropy bound, per-particle normalization, and parameter-marginal consequence by applying the shared ULA entropy theorem to the actual lifted IPLA target. The papers below are background references for that construction.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices
  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2

The lifted-target entropy bound follows from the full actual Gaussian ULA proof and finite initial joint KL. The LSI hypothesis concerns the actual lifted target; all interpolation and moment estimates are derived.

01The geometry fixes the dimension, gradient constant, and step §

Each physical pair (θ,xi)(\theta,x^i)(θ,xi) is extracted from the scaled state by (θ,Nzi)(\theta,\sqrt N z_i)(θ,N​zi​). The two gradient-block formulas IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.5 show that differentiating the lifted sum gives the averaged parameter drift and ordinary latent drifts at step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N. The corresponding Euclidean state has

D=dθ+Ndx,LN=NL,hN=γ/N.D=d_\theta+Nd_x,\qquad L_N=NL,\qquad h_N=\gamma/N.D=dθ​+Ndx​,LN​=NL,hN​=γ/N.

The dimension identity is the coordinate-space dimension formula IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1. The parameter block of the lifted gradient is a sum over particles; each latent block contributes a factor N\sqrt NN​. Squaring and summing the blocks, then applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the common-parameter sum, proves the gradient Lipschitz constant NLNLNL IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.4. The coordinate Gaussian laws and independence identify the actual noise-driven IPLA law with this ULA transition IPLA-SC, Section 3.16.3. No equality between dθd_\thetadθ​ and dxd_xdx​ is needed.

The scaling can also be verified directly in physical coordinates. The parameter component of a step is the previously established physical parameter update IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.7:

θk+1=θk−γN∑i∇θU(θk,xki)+2γNZkθ.\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\frac\gamma N\sum_i\nabla_\theta U(\theta_k,x_k^i) +\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{N}}Z_k^\theta.θk+1​=θk​−Nγ​i∑​∇θ​U(θk​,xki​)+N2γ​​Zkθ​.

For the latent block, multiply the scaled update by N\sqrt NN​; the physical latent update IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.7 is

xk+1i=xki−γ∇xU(θk,xki)+2γZki.x_{k+1}^i=x_k^i-\gamma\nabla_xU(\theta_k,x_k^i)+\sqrt{2\gamma}Z_k^i.xk+1i​=xki​−γ∇x​U(θk​,xki​)+2γ​Zki​.

Thus the lifted Gaussian transition is exactly the intended particle algorithm, with the same joint innovation law. The dimension DDD counts all coordinates of that joint state, and the full joint entropy is the natural input to the shared ULA entropy theorem ULA-LSI, Section 3.29.7.

02Apply the ULA one-step estimate with the lifted target §

Apply the ULA one-step entropy theorem ULA-LSI, Section 3.29.6 to (VN,ΠN)(V_N,\Pi_N)(VN​,ΠN​), with constant βN\beta_NβN​, dimension DDD, smoothness NLNLNL, and step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N. Its step restriction becomes

γN≤βN4(NL)2⟺γ≤βN4NL2.\frac\gamma N\le\frac{\beta_N}{4(NL)^2} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \gamma\le\frac{\beta_N}{4NL^2}.Nγ​≤4(NL)2βN​​⟺γ≤4NL2βN​​.

The one-step forcing simplifies without losing a particle factor:

6hN2DLN2=6(γ/N)2D(NL)2=6γ2DL2.6h_N^2D L_N^2=6(\gamma/N)^2D(NL)^2=6\gamma^2DL^2.6hN2​DLN2​=6(γ/N)2D(NL)2=6γ2DL2.

Consequently the unnormalized joint entropy obeys

Hk+1≤e−βNγ/NHk+6γ2DL2.H_{k+1}\le e^{-\beta_N\gamma/N}H_k+6\gamma^2DL^2.Hk+1​≤e−βN​γ/NHk​+6γ2DL2.

The lifted LSI assumption includes probability normalization of the Gibbs target ULA-LSI, Section 3.1.3. The shared finiteness theorem ULA-LSI, Section 3.29.2 supplies finite KL before the one-step theorem is applied and its rescaled constants are simplified. Every analytic input is supplied by the actual Gaussian ULA construction.

The product βNhN\beta_Nh_NβN​hN​ equals βNγ/N\beta_N\gamma/NβN​γ/N, which fixes the rate in the one-step exponential. The numerical step condition also yields βNγ/N≤1/4\beta_N\gamma/N\le1/4βN​γ/N≤1/4 through the lifted compatibility inequality ULA-LSI, Section 3.6.9. Hence the geometric-denominator estimate ULA-LSI, Section 3.20.2 used in the ULA proof is available with the rescaled constants, without losing a particle factor.

The recurrence is for the unnormalized joint divergence. The additive term has no remaining factor N−1N^{-1}N−1 because the squared step contributes N−2N^{-2}N−2 and the squared lifted smoothness contributes N2N^2N2. Normalization by NNN is postponed until the complete joint inequality has been derived.

The particle substitution and scalar algebra use the shared ULA proof from smoothness, LSI, and finite initial KL.

03Normalize by the particle number only after deriving the joint bound §

The shared full ULA entropy theorem ULA-LSI, Section 3.29.7 gives the unnormalized floor after the lifted substitution:

8hNDLN2βN=8γNDL2βN.\frac{8h_NDL_N^2}{\beta_N} =\frac{8\gamma NDL^2}{\beta_N}.βN​8hN​DLN2​​=βN​8γNDL2​.

Divide the entire inequality by positive NNN, including the initial entropy and the floor. This proves

HkN≤e−βNγk/NH0N+8γDL2βN.\frac{H_k}N\le e^{-\beta_N\gamma k/N}\frac{H_0}N +\frac{8\gamma DL^2}{\beta_N}.NHk​​≤e−βN​γk/NNH0​​+βN​8γDL2​.

Now, and only under the separate lifted-target assumption βN=Nα\beta_N=N\alphaβN​=Nα, use D/N=dx+dθ/ND/N=d_x+d_\theta/ND/N=dx​+dθ​/N. The rate is e−αγke^{-\alpha\gamma k}e−αγk, and the per-particle floor is (8γL2/α)(dx+dθ/N)(8\gamma L^2/\alpha)(d_x+d_\theta/N)(8γL2/α)(dx​+dθ​/N). Shared parameter coordinates prevent a direct tensorization argument from a pair LSI; the target for which LSI is assumed remains ΠN\Pi_NΠN​.

The normalization is a scalar division of an already established inequality, not a change of reference measure. Under βN=Nα\beta_N=N\alphaβN​=Nα, substitute D=dθ+NdxD=d_\theta+Nd_xD=dθ​+Ndx​ only after the division:

8γDL2Nα=8γL2α(dθN+dx).\frac{8\gamma DL^2}{N\alpha} =\frac{8\gamma L^2}{\alpha}\left(\frac{d_\theta}{N}+d_x\right).Nα8γDL2​=α8γL2​(Ndθ​​+dx​).

This exposes two distinct dimension contributions. The shared parameter dimension is amortized over particles, while the latent dimension contributes once per particle and survives in the normalized floor. The assumption βN=Nα\beta_N=N\alphaβN​=Nα concerns the interacting lifted target; shared coordinates mean it cannot be inferred by independent tensorization of unrelated pair laws.

The general lifted-constant bound, division by N, and linear-LSI specialization are checked with their assumptions visible.

04Project to the parameter marginal using data processing §

Let PθP_\thetaPθ​ be the continuous linear parameter projection IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.6, μkθ=Pθ#μk\mu_k^\theta=P_{\theta\#}\mu_kμkθ​=Pθ#​μk​, and ΠNθ=Pθ#ΠN\Pi_N^\theta=P_{\theta\#}\Pi_NΠNθ​=Pθ#​ΠN​. Relative entropy decreases under a measurable map:

HΠNθ(μkθ)≤HΠN(μk).H_{\Pi_N^\theta}(\mu_k^\theta)\le H_{\Pi_N}(\mu_k).HΠNθ​​(μkθ​)≤HΠN​​(μk​).

This controls the unnormalized parameter KL. In particular, under βN=Nα\beta_N=N\alphaβN​=Nα,

HΠNθ(μkθ)≤e−αγkHΠN(μ0)+8γNL2α(dx+dθN).H_{\Pi_N^\theta}(\mu_k^\theta) \le e^{-\alpha\gamma k}H_{\Pi_N}(\mu_0) +\frac{8\gamma NL^2}{\alpha}\left(d_x+\frac{d_\theta}N\right).HΠNθ​​(μkθ​)≤e−αγkHΠN​​(μ0​)+α8γNL2​(dx​+Ndθ​​).

The initial entropy remains the full joint entropy. Data processing does not allow it to be replaced by the smaller initial parameter entropy on the right. Likewise, a normalized joint bound must be multiplied by NNN before it bounds the unscaled parameter KL. The proof establishes finite joint KL, transfers finiteness to the marginal, and then compares their ordinary finite values.

For a density-based view of data processing, let R=dμk/dΠNR=d\mu_k/d\Pi_NR=dμk​/dΠN​. The density of the parameter marginal is the conditional mean EΠN[R∣θ]\mathbb E_{\Pi_N}[R\mid\theta]EΠN​​[R∣θ]. Convexity of ulog⁡uu\log uulogu and conditional Jensen therefore give

∫E[R∣θ]log⁡E[R∣θ]dΠN≤∫Rlog⁡R dΠN.\int\mathbb E[R\mid\theta]\log\mathbb E[R\mid\theta]d\Pi_N \le\int R\log R\,d\Pi_N.∫E[R∣θ]logE[R∣θ]dΠN​≤∫RlogRdΠN​.

This proves the marginal inequality with the original, unnormalized joint entropy on the right. There is no factor N−1N^{-1}N−1 in data processing itself. A bound for entropy per particle must consequently be multiplied by NNN before being used as a bound on the ordinary parameter marginal KL.

The parameter data-processing argument and all normalization factors are checked. This conclusion concerns a probability marginal, not distance to a likelihood maximizer.
