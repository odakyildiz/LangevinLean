Interacting Particle Langevin under Strong Convexity

A root mean square parameter estimate for the particle recursion.1

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

The bound follows from strong convexity and gradient Lipschitzness of the joint potential. Parameter and latent dimensions need not agree. Normalization, moments and concentration of the Gibbs measure are proved under these assumptions. The separate entropy result assumes LSI for the joint particle target and finite initial joint relative entropy.

02The particle recursion as a Gaussian Euler update §

The chain rule in the parameter and latent blocks gives

∇θVN(z)=∑i∇θUy(θ,xi),∇ziVN(z)=N∇xUy(θ,xi).\nabla_\theta V_N(z)=\sum_i\nabla_\theta U_y(\theta,x^i),\qquad \nabla_{z_i}V_N(z)=\sqrt N\nabla_xU_y(\theta,x^i).∇θ​VN​(z)=i∑​∇θ​Uy​(θ,xi),∇zi​​VN​(z)=N​∇x​Uy​(θ,xi).

Use Gaussian Euler for VNV_NVN​ at step s=γ/Ns=\gamma/Ns=γ/N. The parameter drift is the average pair gradient and its Gaussian coefficient is 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​. Multiplying a scaled latent update by N\sqrt NN​ gives drift coefficient γ\gammaγ and noise coefficient 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. These are the physical IPLA updates.

The independent next innovation is independent of the current state, so its conditional update is the Gaussian Euler transition. Iterating this transition gives the particle laws. The restriction s≤2/(Nm+NL)s\le2/(Nm+NL)s≤2/(Nm+NL) is equivalent to γ≤2/(m+L)\gamma\le2/(m+L)γ≤2/(m+L) under positive particle count.

03Latent integration and marginal strong convexity §

The normalization and second-moment theorems ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13 and ULA-SC, Section 3.26.14 apply to the strongly convex summed potential. Change the scaled latent blocks back to physical vectors. Its volume factor is independent of θ\thetaθ and cancels on normalization. Tonelli gives

∫exp⁡ ⁣(−∑iUy(θ,xi))∏idxi=∏i∫e−Uy(θ,x)dx=ℓy(θ)N.\int\exp\!\left(-\sum_iU_y(\theta,x^i)\right)\prod_i dx^i =\prod_i\int e^{-U_y(\theta,x)}dx=\ell_y(\theta)^N.∫exp(−i∑​Uy​(θ,xi))i∏​dxi=i∏​∫e−Uy​(θ,x)dx=ℓy​(θ)N.

The nonnegative product integral factors before finiteness is used. Gaussian domination and positivity then make F(θ)=−log⁡ℓy(θ)F(\theta)=-\log\ell_y(\theta)F(θ)=−logℓy​(θ) an ordinary real marginal potential.

To prove curvature of FFF, subtract the parameter quadratic corresponding to mmm from UyU_yUy​. The remaining exponential is jointly log-concave. The finite-dimensional Prékopa–Leindler theorem preserves log-concavity under latent integration, and restoration of the quadratic gives the claimed strong convexity. Continuity and coercivity give a minimizer, whose uniqueness follows from strict convexity. It is the marginal-likelihood maximizer θ⋆\theta_\starθ⋆​.
