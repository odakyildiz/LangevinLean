Main theorems of the paper

Theorem 1 — Invariant measure

Paper assumptions. A1 and A2. Initialise s0=s⋆s_0=s_\stars0​=s⋆​ deterministically and Z0∼ν⋆Z_0\sim\nu_\starZ0​∼ν⋆​, independently of the Brownian driver.

A2 interpretation. Frozen invariance means an actual stationary weak frozen SDE on some Brownian probability space, with continuous adapted paths and its original local integral equation. A generator identity alone does not supply A2.

Added analytic assumptions: none. The frozen process may be weak and on a different probability space. No global Lipschitz bound or additional drift-growth condition is imposed.

Statement. For every t≥0t\ge0t≥0,

L(st,Zt)=μ⋆:=δs⋆⊗ν⋆.\mathcal L(s_t,Z_t)=\mu_\star:=\delta_{s_\star}\otimes\nu_\star.L(st​,Zt​)=μ⋆​:=δs⋆​​⊗ν⋆​.

For time-dependent Ψt\Psi_tΨt​, this asserts a stationary solution. The target is the A2 critical point.

Theorem 2 — Contraction under joint twisted monotonicity

Paper assumptions. A1 and A3, deterministic initial optimiser states, and initial sampler laws in P2\mathcal P_2P2​. For the stationary-target and uniqueness conclusions, also A2.

Added condition — constant diffusion branch. For every optimiser pair and every joint probability law with finite quadratic moment, the signed sampler work ⟨Δz,DzΔb⟩\langle\Delta z,D_z\Delta b\rangle⟨Δz,Dz​Δb⟩ is absolutely integrable (FiniteCouplingWork). This is the ordinary finite signed-integral reading of universal A3, imposed separately from the conditional inequality. It excludes some superlinear dissipative models admitted by an extended-negative-work reading.
Explicit measurability — constant diffusion branch. The projected observation and drift are jointly Borel, the optimiser response is jointly Borel in time, state and gradient, and each frozen drift is Borel.
Explicit regularity — linear-growth branch. Each frozen drift and diffusion column is Borel. Along the canonical strong solutions, drift and diffusion are jointly strongly measurable, and the drift is progressively strongly measurable. The parameter projection is continuous, as Euclidean coordinate projections are. Moments, stochastic-integral matching and the energy inequality are proved from these primitives and A1(ii).
Solution and target interpretation. Solutions satisfy their original local SDE and optimiser integral equation. Observation integrals are defined for the comparison laws. A2 has the weak-SDE meaning in Theorem 1.

Statement. Let dtd_tdt​ be the distance between the two optimiser–law pairs at time ttt. For every t≥0t\ge0t≥0,

dt≤κ e−ρtd0.d_t\le\sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\rho t}d_0.dt​≤κ​e−ρtd0​.

With A2, this gives

W2(μt,μ⋆)≤κ e−ρtW2(μ0,μ⋆).\begin{gathered}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_\star)\\ \le\sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\rho t}W_2(\mu_0,\mu_\star). \end{gathered}W2​(μt​,μ⋆​)≤κ​e−ρtW2​(μ0​,μ⋆​).​

The stationary law is unique among finite-second-moment laws of the form δs⊗ν\delta_s\otimes\nuδs​⊗ν satisfying these solution hypotheses.

Theorem 3 — Finite-particle interaction error

Paper assumptions. A1, A3, A4 and A5; N≥1N\ge1N≥1; a deterministic initial optimiser; i.i.d. sampler initial states with a common P2\mathcal P_2P2​ law, independent of independent Brownian drivers; and an actual interacting-system solution. Start the interacting and mean-field copy systems synchronously and drive each paired sampler with the same Brownian motion.

Given solution. The interacting solution obeys the original native empirical SDE on the independent whole-seed product, with the usual adaptedness and local-integrability domains. Theorem 4 — Parameter error bound of particle system

Paper assumptions. A1–A5, the coupled initialisation and given interacting solution of Theorem 3, and the stationary target of Theorem 1. The parameter projection is the Euclidean coordinate projection, hence one-Lipschitz. Put d0=d((s0,ν0),(s⋆,ν⋆))d_0=d((s_0,\nu_0),(s_\star,\nu_\star))d0​=d((s0​,ν0​),(s⋆​,ν⋆​)).
