Exact declarations · explicit assumptions

Raw constant-noise target error from source response and variance

Actual raw same-driver reference and stationary-target equations use source response Lipschitz and uniform fluctuations to derive relaxation; the raw particle triangle gives actual target parameter error.

Assumptions and formal scope. The original raw mean-field and particle data, actual stationary-target law, source A3/A4/A5 and original initial coupling are explicit. This result uses the realized initial-coupling RMS; optimal initial-law infimum requires its separate law construction. No abstract weak realizer or final work/energy premise is supplied.

Mathematical role and assumptions

Actual parameter RMS has exact relaxation plus finite-particle error. The initial relaxation cost is the genuine realized reference/target coupling; choosing an optimal initial coupling gives the source transport prefactor. No abstract mean-field weak solution, supplied energy or drift-growth premise is needed beyond the original raw equations and actual reference laws.

Read this statement with the module assumption note above. The complete proof appears alongside it; the full source preserves every shared variable and typeclass.
