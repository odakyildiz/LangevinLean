Transferring frozen stationarity between Brownian sources

A frozen sampler invariant law can be realised on a different Brownian probability space from the coupled optimiser–sampler solution. The transfer uses actual strong existence on initial-law times Wiener-path space and pathwise uniqueness. An invariant output law is derived after transport; it is not assumed for the solution being studied.

Assumptions and formal scope. A1 supplies one actual canonical full-system solution and same-driver uniqueness. A2 is interpreted as an actual stationary weak frozen SDE realization on some probability space. No common-source frozen strong solver, assumed input/output law, global drift moment or noise matching is supplied.

01The stationary complete-state product law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Fix T≥0T\ge0T≥0, a probability law ν⋆\nu_\starν⋆​, and optimiser state s⋆s_\stars⋆​. Suppose a frozen sampler on some filtered Brownian source has law ν⋆\nu_\starν⋆​ at every 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T0≤t≤T. Its observation is integrable with mean zero, and Ψ(t,s⋆,0)=0\Psi(t,s_\star,0)=0Ψ(t,s⋆​,0)=0 throughout the interval. Assume a coupled strong solution exists on independent initial-law times Wiener-path space, with initial state (s⋆,Z0)(s_\star,Z_0)(s⋆​,Z0​), Z0∼ν⋆Z_0\sim\nu_\starZ0​∼ν⋆​. Assume pathwise uniqueness on the frozen source and on the original solution's source after continuous Brownian modification and null augmentation. If an original solution starts with optimiser s⋆s_\stars⋆​ and sampler law ν⋆\nu_\starν⋆​, then its full joint law at each t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T] is δs⋆⊗ν⋆\delta_{s_\star}\otimes\nu_\starδs⋆​​⊗ν⋆​.

Proof. The frozen sampler together with the constant optimiser satisfies the coupled equation, because its mean observation and optimiser velocity vanish. The map sending a sample to its initial state and continuous Brownian path preserves the initial-law times Wiener probability measure. Pull back the canonical path-space solution through this map. The pulled-back solution and the frozen one have the same driver and initial state; pathwise uniqueness gives their equality. Therefore the canonical solution has constant optimiser and sampler law ν⋆\nu_\starν⋆​. Repeat this argument for the original solution using its initial/path map and uniqueness. Its optimiser is s⋆s_\stars⋆​, its sampler law is ν⋆\nu_\starν⋆​, and hence the joint pushforward is δs⋆⊗ν⋆\delta_{s_\star}\otimes\nu_\starδs⋆​​⊗ν⋆​. The last identity follows by pushing the sampler law through z↦(s⋆,z)z\mapsto(s_\star,z)z↦(s⋆​,z). □\square□

How the Lean proof follows this argument

stationary_product_law_of_weak_frozen first obtains optimiser and law identities from stationary_of_weak_frozen. That result constructs F.constantData, transports it and the original solution to the same path-space solution using law_eq_pathspace_solution, and combines the resulting equalities. The final proof rewrites the optimiser, uses Measure.dirac_prod, then Measure.map_map. The quantifier ht is the closed finite horizon, including zero and the terminal time.
