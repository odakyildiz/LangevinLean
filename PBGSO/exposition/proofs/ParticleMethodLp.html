Force data of the particle methods and the Lipschitz bounds of the cloud forces

This section fixes the force data shared by the two numerical methods (assumption N1) and proves the Lipschitz estimates for the forces acting on a whole particle cloud. Let Θ\ThetaΘ, X\mathcal XX and C\mathcal CC be real normed spaces: Θ\ThetaΘ contains the optimiser parameter, X\mathcal XX the position of a single particle, and C\mathcal CC a coordinate vector describing a cloud of N≥1N\ge1N≥1 particles. A continuous linear bijection E:C→XN\mathcal E:\mathcal C\to\mathcal X^NE:C→XN with continuous inverse reads off the particles; XN\mathcal X^NXN carries the norm ∥x∥∞=max⁡1≤i≤N∥xi∥\|x\|_\infty=\max_{1\le i\le N}\|x_i\|∥x∥∞​=max1≤i≤N​∥xi​∥, and Θ×C\Theta\times\mathcal CΘ×C carries the norm ∥(θ,c)∥=max⁡{∥θ∥,∥c∥}\|(\theta,c)\|=\max\{\|\theta\|,\|c\|\}∥(θ,c)∥=max{∥θ∥,∥c∥}. The observation F:Θ×X→ΘF:\Theta\times\mathcal X\to\ThetaF:Θ×X→Θ and the sampler force Fx:Θ×X→XF_x:\Theta\times\mathcal X\to\mathcal XFx​:Θ×X→X are two unrelated maps with joint Lipschitz constants ℓF,ℓx≥0\ell_F,\ell_x\ge0ℓF​,ℓx​≥0. From them we build the empirical mean force Fˉ(θ,c)=N−1∑iF(θ,(Ec)i)\bar F(\theta,c)=N^{-1}\sum_iF(\theta,(\mathcal Ec)_i)Fˉ(θ,c)=N−1∑i​F(θ,(Ec)i​), which drives the parameter, and the stacked sampler force Fx(θ,c)=E−1((Fx(θ,(Ec)i))i=1N)\mathcal F_x(\theta,c)=\mathcal E^{-1}\bigl((F_x(\theta,(\mathcal Ec)_i))_{i=1}^N\bigr)Fx​(θ,c)=E−1((Fx​(θ,(Ec)i​))i=1N​), which drives the cloud.

The main result is that Fˉ\bar FFˉ is globally Lipschitz with constant LF=ℓF(1+∥E∥)L_F=\ell_F(1+\|\mathcal E\|)LF​=ℓF​(1+∥E∥), and Fx\mathcal F_xFx​ with constant Lx=∥E−1∥ ℓx(1+∥E∥)L_x=\|\mathcal E^{-1}\|\,\ell_x(1+\|\mathcal E\|)Lx​=∥E−1∥ℓx​(1+∥E∥). Both forces are then evaluated at square-integrable random arguments (θ,X)(\theta,X)(θ,X) defined on one probability space, so that the parameter and all particles may be dependent. The lifted forces act sample by sample and satisfy the same Lipschitz bounds in L2L^2L2. We also show that one step of either method, computed on square-integrable random variables, coincides almost surely with the update applied to the sampled values. For the standard Euclidean encoding of RNdx\mathbb R^{Nd_x}RNdx​ with dx≥1d_x\ge1dx​≥1 one has ∥E∥=1\|\mathcal E\|=1∥E∥=1 and ∥E−1∥=N\|\mathcal E^{-1}\|=\sqrt N∥E−1∥=N​, hence LF=2ℓFL_F=2\ell_FLF​=2ℓF​ and Lx=2N ℓxL_x=2\sqrt N\,\ell_xLx​=2N​ℓx​; these are the force constants in Theorems New A and New B.

Assumptions and formal scope. Assume positive particle count, a continuous linear cloud encoding with inverse, separate primitive joint Lipschitz force bounds, actual compatible integral reference segments, finite-horizon force/velocity bounds and the displayed square-root modulus. Brownian independence is explicit for M-MFPO; HOL uses the constructed canonical Gaussian source. The paper's independent isotropic particle noises use the explicit flattened Euclidean encoding; an arbitrary encoding transforms raw noise. No force-lift identification premise remains, but original-target bias and uniform-in-time mixing are separate.
