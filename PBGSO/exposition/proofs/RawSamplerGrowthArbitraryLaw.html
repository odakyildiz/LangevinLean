Law contraction on arbitrary Brownian probability spaces

For probability laws μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν with finite second moment, let W2(μ,ν)W_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(μ,ν) be the infimum of root mean squared displacement over their couplings. The optimiser–law distance is d((s,μ),(s′,ν))=(∥s−s′∥2+W2(μ,ν)2)1/2d((s,\mu),(s',\nu))=(\|s-s'\|^2+W_2(\mu,\nu)^2)^{1/2}d((s,μ),(s′,ν))=(∥s−s′∥2+W2​(μ,ν)2)1/2. The model may be realised on two different filtered Brownian probability spaces.

Assumptions and formal scope. The canonical solutions satisfy primitive original raw growth and regularity, with source same-driver uniqueness on the proved valid drivers. No original output matching, noise identity, moment bound or coupling inequality is supplied. The bare constant-diffusion alternative remains separate.

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let two actual original strong solutions on [0,T][0,T][0,T], T≥0T\ge0T≥0, have initial optimiser states s0,s0′s_0,s'_0s0​,s0′​ and probability sampler laws μ,ν∈P2\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_2μ,ν∈P2​. Assume actual strong solutions also exist on each independent initial-law times canonical Brownian source and on each initial-law times Wiener-path source, with these initial conditions. Assume same-driver pathwise uniqueness on both original sources and both canonical sources after null augmentation and continuous modification. Suppose the model satisfies joint dissipativity with rate ρ\rhoρ in a quadratic geometry of condition number κ\kappaκ.

In addition, assume the canonical solutions have jointly measurable drift and diffusion realizations, progressive drift, Borel diffusion sections, a bounded parameter path on the finite horizon, and a nonnegative linear-growth bound ∥b(P(s),z)∥+∥(σi(P(s),z))i∥≤C(1+∥P(s)∥+∥z∥)\|b(P(s),z)\|+\|(\sigma_i(P(s),z))_i\|\le C(1+\|P(s)\|+\|z\|)∥b(P(s),z)∥+∥(σi​(P(s),z))i​∥≤C(1+∥P(s)∥+∥z∥). These are primitive coefficient regularity assumptions; their moment and energy consequences are proved.

Then the laws of the two original solutions satisfy

d((sT,μT),(sT′,νT))≤κ e−ρTd((s0,μ),(s0′,ν)).d((s_T,\mu_T),(s'_T,\nu_T))\le\sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\rho T}d((s_0,\mu),(s'_0,\nu)).d((sT​,μT​),(sT′​,νT​))≤κ​e−ρTd((s0​,μ),(s0′​,ν)).

Proof. The canonical initial sampler is the first projection, whose law is the prescribed initial law because the independent Brownian factor has total mass one. For each original solution, the initial/path probability map transports its canonical path-space solution back to the original source. Pathwise uniqueness gives equality of the original and canonical optimiser trajectories and sampler marginal laws.

Apply the established canonical coupling contraction. Its construction starts with any initial coupling, uses common Brownian coordinates, derives the quadratic energy inequality and applies exponential comparison. Comparing the weighted and Euclidean energies contributes u/ℓ=κu/\ell=\kappau/ℓ=κ; taking square roots contributes κ\sqrt\kappaκ​. Infimising over initial couplings produces W2W_2W2​, and therefore the displayed distance. Finally substitute the original/canonical law and optimiser identities. This last substitution introduces no assumption that the original sources were synchronously coupled. □\square□

How the Lean proof follows this argument

law_distance_decay first proves hA0 and hB0 by pushforward of the first product projection. law_unique_two_sources gives hUA and hVB, matching each original solution to its canonical one. It then calls RawSamplerGrowthLaw.law_distance_decay with A.regularity and B.regularity. The final rewrites replace canonical initial states and terminal laws by the original data. MemLp at exponent two is square integrability, and Q.map denotes the distribution of a random variable under the probability measure Q.
