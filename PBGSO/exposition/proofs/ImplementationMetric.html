Contracting metrics for the two particle methods from the joint monotonicity assumption

This section constructs, for each of the two numerical methods, a Hilbert metric on the random particle states in which the drift of the continuous particle system is dissipative with the rate ρ\rhoρ of assumption A3. The random state of M-MFPO is y=((m,V),(θ,X))y=((m,V),(\theta,X))y=((m,V),(θ,X)): the parameter momentum mmm and parameter θ\thetaθ are square-integrable random vectors in Θ\ThetaΘ, and the particle velocities VVV and positions XXX are square-integrable random cloud vectors in C\mathcal CC. The random state of HOL-MYPGD is y=(θ,X,U,V)y=(\theta,X,U,V)y=(θ,X,U,V) with three random cloud vectors. In both cases the state space is normed by the maximum of the four L2L^2L2 norms, written ∥y∥\|y\|∥y∥.

Assumption A3 is a statement about laws: for any two optimiser states and any coupling of two sampler laws, the weighted work of the drifts is at most −ρ-\rho−ρ times the weighted squared distance. Applied to the empirical laws of two clouds, it becomes a dissipativity inequality for the empirical drift on the finite-dimensional state space S×ZNS\times Z^NS×ZN (optimiser phase and NNN particle phases). Factoring the weight, integrating over the sample space and transporting by the linear regrouping of coordinates gives a bounded linear map TTT from the random states into L2(Ω;Rn)L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^n)L2(Ω;Rn) with

m∥y∥≤∥Ty∥≤U∥y∥,⟨T(y−y′),T(b(y)−b(y′))⟩≤−ρ∥T(y−y′)∥2,m\|y\|\le\|Ty\|\le U\|y\|,\qquad\bigl\langle T(y-y'),T(b(y)-b(y'))\bigr\rangle\le-\rho\|T(y-y')\|^2,m∥y∥≤∥Ty∥≤U∥y∥,⟨T(y−y′),T(b(y)−b(y′))⟩≤−ρ∥T(y−y′)∥2,

where bbb is the kinetic or higher-order drift and m,U>0m,U>0m,U>0 depend on the A3 eigenvalue bounds, on NNN and on the norms of the regrouping map. This metric is used for the exact-flow contraction and the uniform moment bounds of Theorems New A and New B. The section also proves the samplewise identity between the drift computed on the random variables and the empirical drift of the paper's model, which is what makes A3 applicable.

Assumptions and formal scope. Finite-dimensional optimiser and sampler coordinates, primitive ParticleForceData and source A3 are explicit. The metric factor, coercivity, random-state dissipativity and all coordinate identities are conclusions.
