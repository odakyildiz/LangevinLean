Smooth dependence and the continuous Jacobian

Consider an actual globally defined flow Φt\Phi_tΦt​ of a finite-dimensional autonomous C1C^1C1 vector field. Joint differentiability in time and initial state is derived from these hypotheses. For the frictionless phase field (p,q)↦(−F(q),p)(p,q)\mapsto(-F(q),p)(p,q)↦(−F(q),p), the divergence is zero even when FFF is not a gradient.

Assumptions and formal scope. Assume a finite-dimensional real complete normed state, a C1C^1C1 primitive field, the actual all-time ODE and identity initial map. Joint flow regularity, variational equations, a global Lipschitz bound and determinant preservation are conclusions, not premises. The phase force is arbitrary and may be nonconservative.

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Suppose F:Rd→RdF:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^dF:Rd→Rd is C1C^1C1, and for every initial phase point xxx there is a global trajectory Φ(t,x)\Phi(t,x)Φ(t,x) satisfying ∂tΦ(t,x)=(−F(qt),pt)\partial_t\Phi(t,x)=(-F(q_t),p_t)∂t​Φ(t,x)=(−F(qt​),pt​) and Φ(0,x)=x\Phi(0,x)=xΦ(0,x)=x. Then Φ\PhiΦ is jointly C1C^1C1, and det⁡DxΦ(t,x)=1\det D_x\Phi(t,x)=1detDx​Φ(t,x)=1 for every real ttt and every xxx, including zero dimension.

Proof. A C1C^1C1 field is Lipschitz on a compact ball because its continuous derivative is bounded there. On any compact time interval, both continuous trajectories lie in one such ball, so the usual difference estimate and Grönwall give uniqueness. To obtain differentiability in the initial value near a fixed trajectory, choose a smooth compact cutoff equal to one on an open neighbourhood of its compact orbit. The cut-off field is globally Lipschitz and C1C^1C1, and global Picard iteration constructs its flow with C1C^1C1 dependence. Its continuous dependence keeps nearby trajectories inside that open neighbourhood for the whole compact time interval. Uniqueness then identifies the cut-off and original trajectories locally. Thus their solution maps agree in a neighbourhood of each (t,x)(t,x)(t,x), proving joint C1C^1C1 regularity.

Let J(t)=DxΦ(t,x)J(t)=D_x\Phi(t,x)J(t)=Dx​Φ(t,x). Differentiating the ODE in the initial condition gives J′=Df(Φt(x))JJ'=Df(\Phi_t(x))JJ′=Df(Φt​(x))J, J(0)=IJ(0)=IJ(0)=I. The derivative of the phase field has blocks (0−DF(q)I0)\begin{pmatrix}0&-DF(q)\\I&0\end{pmatrix}(0I​−DF(q)0​), whose trace is zero. The determinant differentiation formula for this variational equation gives (det⁡J)′=tr⁡(Df)det⁡J=0(\det J)'=\operatorname{tr}(Df)\det J=0(detJ)′=tr(Df)detJ=0. Hence det⁡J(t)=det⁡I=1\det J(t)=\det I=1detJ(t)=detI=1, for positive or negative time. □\square□

How the Lean proof follows this argument

flow_contDiff discharges the cutoff-solution existence interface using exists_global_ode_flow. The preceding flow_contDiff_of_cutoff_solutions constructs a compact orbit, applies exists_global_lipschitz_C1_cutoff, proves the orbit equality by uniqueness, obtains a neighbourhood of nearby initial values through the compact-open topology, and transfers differentiability by eventual equality. phase_flow_fderiv_det_one_source supplies this derived regularity to phase_flow_fderiv_det_one_C1. Thus neither a Jacobian identity nor differentiability of a chosen solution map is an input.
