Exact declarations · explicit assumptions

Optimal Wasserstein law contraction and stationary uniqueness

Genuine finite-second-moment couplings bound their marginal transport distance. Arbitrarily approximating couplings pass the derived generator energy bound to the exact optimiser–law product distance, with κe−ρt\sqrt\kappa e^{-\rho t}κ​e−ρt. Characteristic functions prove zero-distance separation and stationary law equality.

Assumptions and formal scope. The weak-law theorem requires a synchronous coupled weak solution with the same specified marginal trajectories for every initial coupling, and the explicit quadratic test domain. It does not assume an optimal coupling or a Wasserstein conclusion. The additive existence corollary constructs both actual integral solutions and their law bound for the selected L2L^2L2 initial coupling; their coupling-independent output-law identification remains separate.
