Finite second moments need not give finite signed work

The scalar drift b(x)=−x−x3b(x)=-x-x^3b(x)=−x−x3 is strongly dissipative, yet its signed work against the point zero contains a fourth power. This distinguishes a conditional work inequality from a universal finite-integrability assumption on P2\mathcal P_2P2​.

Assumptions and formal scope. No stochastic contraction counterexample is claimed. The result demonstrates that universal finite signed work is substantive; an extended-negative-work interpretation includes more superlinear systems.

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. There is a probability measure μ\muμ on R\mathbb RR with ∫x2dμ<∞\int x^2d\mu<\infty∫x2dμ<∞ but ∫x4dμ=∞\int x^4d\mu=\infty∫x4dμ=∞. For b(x)=−x−x3b(x)=-x-x^3b(x)=−x−x3, one has (x−y)(b(x)−b(y))≤−(x−y)2(x-y)(b(x)-b(y))\le-(x-y)^2(x−y)(b(x)−b(y))≤−(x−y)2 for every pair of reals, but x(b(x)−b(0))x(b(x)-b(0))x(b(x)−b(0)) is not integrable for such a law.

Proof. If every probability law integrable against 1+x21+x^21+x2 also integrated x4x^4x4, the universal-integrability growth theorem would imply a finite C≥0C\ge0C≥0 with x4≤C(1+x2)x^4\le C(1+x^2)x4≤C(1+x2) for every real xxx. That theorem is proved by a countable atomic measure concentrating successively on points where the ratio is unbounded. At x=C+1x=\sqrt{C+1}x=C+1​, the alleged inequality becomes (C+1)2≤C(C+2)(C+1)^2\le C(C+2)(C+1)2≤C(C+2), or 1≤01\le01≤0. Hence such a law exists. Integrability of 1+x21+x^21+x2 implies integrability of x2x^2x2.

Since x3−y3=(x−y)(x2+xy+y2)x^3-y^3=(x-y)(x^2+xy+y^2)x3−y3=(x−y)(x2+xy+y2) and x2+xy+y2≥0x^2+xy+y^2\ge0x2+xy+y2≥0,

(x−y)(b(x)−b(y))=−(x−y)2(1+x2+xy+y2)≤−(x−y)2.(x-y)(b(x)-b(y))=-(x-y)^2\bigl(1+x^2+xy+y^2\bigr)\le-(x-y)^2.(x−y)(b(x)−b(y))=−(x−y)2(1+x2+xy+y2)≤−(x−y)2.

But x(b(x)−b(0))=−x2−x4x(b(x)-b(0))=-x^2-x^4x(b(x)−b(0))=−x2−x4. If this were integrable, subtracting the integrable quadratic term from its negative would make x4x^4x4 integrable, a contradiction. The example concerns the finite-work domain; it does not disprove a contraction estimate on laws for which the required work argument is justified. □\square□

How the Lean proof follows this argument

exists_quadratic_not_quartic argues by contradiction using UniversalMomentGrowth.growth_of_probability_integrability and tests the resulting bound at the square root of C+1. drift_strongly_dissipative expands the polynomial and uses the nonnegative factor x^2+x*y+y^2. exists_p2_nonintegrable_work obtains MemLp from integrability of the squared norm. Its final contradiction rewrites the quartic function as the negative work minus the quadratic term and invokes closure of integrable functions under subtraction.
