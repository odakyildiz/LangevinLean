Constructed mean-field solutions with joint metric contraction

Picard constructs both actual general mean-field integral solutions from primitive Lipschitz observation, response and sampler maps. A joint metric monotonicity premise for their constructed field yields the exponential condition-number estimate.

Assumptions and formal scope. Assume a probability space and complete real Banach coordinates, LipschitzDynamics, time-independent primitive maps, a common continuous L2L^2L2 sampler input starting at zero, a continuous linear metric map into a Hilbert space, positive lower and nonnegative upper norm-comparison constants, and the stated joint drift inequality. Positive ρ\rhoρ gives decay. No energy derivative or pre-existing solution is assumed; multiplicative noise and the optimal initial-law infimum are outside this theorem; LawContraction supplies the actual law projection and the weak-generator optimal transport bridge.

Mathematical role and assumptions

Existence and contraction of actual general mean-field integral solutions. The optimiser receives no additive input. CCC specifies the full twisted metric and its lower and upper comparison constants are explicit.
