Finite-horizon increments from actual integral equations

Continuity bounds the drift along a continuous solution on a compact time interval. Subtracting the actual integral equations gives a drift-times-step plus input-increment bound, then a square-root modulus for steps at most one. Brownian input differences are identified with the shifted coordinate increment.

Assumptions and formal scope. Supply the actual integral equation, drift continuity, a nonnegative finite-horizon drift radius, an input square-root increment bound and a segment inside the horizon. The drift radius is proved to exist from continuity but is horizon dependent. This does not prove a uniform-in-time moment bound or a variable-diffusion Itô estimate.
