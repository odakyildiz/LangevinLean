The normalized variational problem

This section proves the variational characterization of the evidence. Let M≥1M\ge1M≥1 be the number of data, let A\mathcal AA be an arbitrary set of parameters and let ZZZ be a measurable space of latent variables. A posterior family consists of a real function ℓ:A→R\ell:\mathcal A\to\mathbb Rℓ:A→R, the log evidence, and, for each parameter a∈Aa\in\mathcal Aa∈A and each datum m∈{1,…,M}m\in\{1,\dots,M\}m∈{1,…,M}, a probability measure πam\pi_a^mπam​ on ZZZ, the posterior of datum mmm. For a collection q=(q1,…,qM)q=(q^1,\dots,q^M)q=(q1,…,qM) of probability measures on ZZZ, the free energy is

F(a,q)=−ℓ(a)+1M∑m=1MKL(qm∥πam),F(a,q)=-\ell(a)+\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M\mathrm{KL}(q^m\|\pi_a^m),F(a,q)=−ℓ(a)+M1​m=1∑M​KL(qm∥πam​),

where KL\mathrm{KL}KL denotes relative entropy. We prove that F(a,q)≥−ℓ(a)F(a,q)\ge-\ell(a)F(a,q)≥−ℓ(a), with equality exactly when qm=πamq^m=\pi_a^mqm=πam​ for every mmm, and deduce the corrected form of the paper's Proposition 1: a pair (a⋆,q⋆)(a_\star,q_\star)(a⋆​,q⋆​) minimizes FFF among all pairs with finite relative entropies if and only if a⋆a_\stara⋆​ maximizes ℓ\ellℓ and q⋆m=πa⋆mq_\star^m=\pi_{a_\star}^mq⋆m​=πa⋆​m​ for every mmm. No existence of a maximizer is asserted; existence and uniqueness are proved later from strong convexity of the marginal potential.

In the application A\mathcal AA is the space of parameters a=(θ,ϕ)a=(\theta,\phi)a=(θ,ϕ), πam\pi_a^mπam​ is the normalized tilted posterior Zm(a)−1e−Um(a,z) dzZ_m(a)^{-1}e^{-U_m(a,z)}\,dzZm​(a)−1e−Um​(a,z)dz of datum mmm, and ℓ(a)=M−1∑mlog⁡Zm(a)\ell(a)=M^{-1}\sum_m\log Z_m(a)ℓ(a)=M−1∑m​logZm​(a) is the average tilted log evidence. The results hold for every parameter set and every measurable latent space, in particular for tilted models. The last step shows that a zero tilting penalty at a maximizer of the tilted evidence does not identify a maximizer of the original evidence, and gives a sufficient condition that does.

Mathematical statement and proof

All inequalities are therefore equalities, and ℓ(a)=ℓ(b)\ell(a)=\ell(b)ℓ(a)=ℓ(b). □\square□

Claim 2 (counterexample). On the two-point set A={0,1}\mathcal A=\{0,1\}A={0,1} let ℓ(0)=0\ell(0)=0ℓ(0)=0, ℓ(1)=1\ell(1)=1ℓ(1)=1 and let the gap g=ℓ−ℓ~g=\ell-\tilde\ellg=ℓ−ℓ~ be g(0)=0g(0)=0g(0)=0, g(1)=2g(1)=2g(1)=2. Proof. The tilted values are ℓ~(0)=0\tilde\ell(0)=0ℓ~(0)=0 and ℓ~(1)=1−2=−1\tilde\ell(1)=1-2=-1ℓ~(1)=1−2=−1, so 000 maximizes ℓ~\tilde\ellℓ~. The remaining assertions are the numerical facts 0≤00\le00≤0, 0≤20\le20≤2 and 0<10<10<1. □\square□

Thus a zero penalty at the tilted maximizer itself does not imply that it maximizes the original evidence. Claim 1 shows what does: a maximizer of the original evidence at which the tilting gap vanishes.

How the Lean proof follows this argument
  1. zero_gap_comparator: hupper is ℓ~≤ℓ\tilde\ell\le\ellℓ~≤ℓ, htrue and hzero are maximality of bbb and the zero gap, and hmax is maximality of aaa for the tilted evidence. The proof rewrites hmax b with hzero to obtain ℓ(b)≤ℓ~(a)\ell(b)\le\tilde\ell(a)ℓ(b)≤ℓ~(a), then concludes by le_antisymm (htrue a) (h.trans (hupper a)), which is the displayed chain.
  2. zero_gap_at_tilted_max_not_sufficient uses the type Bool as the two-point set, with false in the role of 000 and true in the role of 111; the functions are defined by if b then … else …. After dsimp, the four conjuncts are proved by norm_num, with a case split cases b for the two universally quantified ones.
