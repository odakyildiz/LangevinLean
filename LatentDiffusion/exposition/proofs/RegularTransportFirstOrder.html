The extended log-Sobolev and Talagrand inequalities

This section proves the two functional inequalities that drive the convergence of the classical flow: the extended log-Sobolev inequality (the paper's Theorem stating that strong log-concavity implies extended LSI) and a Talagrand-type quadratic growth inequality, both for the normalized free energy of MMM data. The setting is that of the previous section, now for each datum mmm: a probability law QmQ_mQm​ on ZZZ with positive continuously differentiable density ρm\rho_{m}ρm​, regular displacement data TmT_mTm​ from QmQ_mQm​, and a jointly λ\lambdaλ-strongly convex potential UmU_mUm​ on P×ZP\times ZP×Z. With p0p_0p0​ the current parameter, p1p_1p1​ the target parameter and Fm\mathcal F_mFm​ the free energy of datum mmm along the displacement, put F=M−1∑mFm\mathcal F=M^{-1}\sum_m\mathcal F_mF=M−1∑m​Fm​, and define

The main result is 2λ(F(0)−F(1))≤I2\lambda\bigl(\mathcal F(0)-\mathcal F(1)\bigr)\le I2λ(F(0)−F(1))≤I. In the convergence theorem F(0)\mathcal F(0)F(0) is the free energy of the current state and F(1)\mathcal F(1)F(1) that of the posterior optimizer, so this is 2λ(F−F⋆)≤I2\lambda(F-F_\star)\le I2λ(F−F⋆​)≤I.
