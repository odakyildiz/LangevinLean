Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


These transport lemmas connect measurable random maps to their iterated independent-noise laws. They preserve every marginal and take the infimum over initial couplings. Their inputs are a pathwise contraction and the measured L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} discrepancy between two explicit maps; these analytic inputs are proved separately for OBABO and its Hamiltonian comparator.



A common independent innovation extends any input coupling into an output coupling. Minkowski’s inequality separates contraction from local kernel discrepancy, producing an affine transport recurrence against an invariant comparator.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.15]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.33]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Extend every coupling by a common independent innovation}{Extend every coupling by a common independent innovation}}


Given any input coupling \(c\) of \(\mu,\nu\), append one independent innovation with law Q. Let the numerical output be \(F(x,s)\) and the comparator output be \(G(y,s)\). Their difference decomposes exactly as

\[F(x,s)-G(y,s)=[F(x,s)-F(y,s)]-[G(y,s)-F(y,s)].\]

Minkowski and the pathwise contraction bound the first term by q times the original coupling cost. The law of \((y,s)\) is proved to be exactly \(\nu\otimes Q\); the pushforward formula for L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} therefore identifies the second term with the local discrepancy under that fixed product law. Both output marginals are the random-map laws. The resulting bound holds for every c, so the established affine-infimum theorem gives \(W_2(F_\#(\mu\otimes Q),G_\#(\nu\otimes Q))\le qW_2(\mu,\nu)+b\). No optimal-coupling existence is assumed.



Take any coupling of the two input measures and adjoin one independent innovation. Updating both coupled states produces the desired output marginals. Minkowski’s inequality separates the contractive shared-map discrepancy from the local discrepancy between the two maps.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.16]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.2]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.18.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.22.10]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.22.15]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.22.2]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Iterate the Markov laws against an invariant comparator}{Iterate the Markov laws against an invariant comparator}}


Suppose the explicit comparator map preserves \(\pi\) under the independent source Q and its local L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} error is at most b. Applying the preceding transport theorem at every numerical iteration gives \(e_{n+1}\le qe_n+b\). The proof first establishes that each iterated law is a probability and that every transport distance is finite, using the extended-real recurrence and finite initial distance. The resulting finite transport distances can then be regarded as ordinary real numbers. The proved geometric recurrence yields

\[W_2(\mu_n,\pi)\le q^nW_2(\mu_0,\pi)+\frac{b}{1-q},\qquad0\le q<1.\]

This general lemma keeps the invariance and integrated local-error hypotheses explicit. The Hamiltonian and local-state modules supply them for the OBABO comparison; the convergence theorem itself is not used as an input.



If the comparator transition preserves the target, each step compares the numerical law directly with the same target. Iterating the one-step affine Wasserstein inequality yields a geometric transient and a sum of local errors. This is the corresponding measure estimate of the pathwise recurrence.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.15.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.4]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.33.11]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.33.6]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Expose finite output transport before taking real values}{Expose finite output transport before taking real values}}


The extended affine one-step bound also proves finite output distance at every Markov iteration. Start with finite initial transport. At each step the finite nonnegative coefficient times the preceding finite distance, plus the finite local-error bound, is finite. The distance lies below that finite quantity. This induction is stated separately so coordinate projections and mean-square consequences can use a real Wasserstein estimate without silently converting infinity to zero.



A finite-cost coupling and a finite local root-mean-square defect give a finite output cost. Establishing this before converting the extended transport value to a real number makes the affine recurrence legitimate. The final infimum is taken over couplings.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.33.11]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.33.6]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.
