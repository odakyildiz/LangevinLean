These transport lemmas connect actual measurable random maps to their iterated independent-noise laws. They preserve every marginal and take the actual infimum over initial couplings. Their inputs are a pathwise contraction and the measured L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} discrepancy between two explicit maps; these analytic inputs are proved separately for OBABO and its Hamiltonian comparator.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Extend every coupling by a common independent innovation}{Extend every coupling by a common independent innovation}}


Given any input coupling \(c\) of \(\mu,\nu\), append one independent innovation with law Q. Let the numerical output be \(F(x,s)\) and the comparator output be \(G(y,s)\). Their difference decomposes exactly as

\[F(x,s)-G(y,s)=[F(x,s)-F(y,s)]-[G(y,s)-F(y,s)].\]

Minkowski and the pathwise contraction bound the first term by q times the original coupling cost. The law of \((y,s)\) is proved to be exactly \(\nu\otimes Q\); the pushforward formula for L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} therefore identifies the second term with the actual local discrepancy under that fixed product law. Both output marginals are the actual random-map laws. The resulting bound holds for every c, so the established affine-infimum theorem gives \(W_2(F_\#(\mu\otimes Q),G_\#(\nu\otimes Q))\le qW_2(\mu,\nu)+b\). No optimal-coupling existence is assumed.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Iterate the actual Markov laws against an invariant comparator}{Iterate the actual Markov laws against an invariant comparator}}


Suppose the explicit comparator map preserves \(\pi\) under the independent source Q and its actual local L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} error is at most b. Applying the preceding transport theorem at every numerical iteration gives \(e_{n+1}\le qe_n+b\). The proof first establishes that each iterated law is a probability and that every transport distance is finite, using the extended-real recurrence and finite initial distance. Only then does it convert to real distances. The proved geometric recurrence yields

\[W_2(\mu_n,\pi)\le q^nW_2(\mu_0,\pi)+\frac{b}{1-q},\qquad0\le q<1.\]

This general lemma keeps the invariant-map and integrated-local-error obligations explicit. The Hamiltonian and local-state modules supply them for the actual OBABO comparison; the convergence theorem itself is not used as an input.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Expose finite output transport before taking real values}{Expose finite output transport before taking real values}}


The extended affine one-step bound also proves finite output distance at every actual Markov iteration. Start with finite initial transport. At each step the finite nonnegative coefficient times the preceding finite distance, plus the finite local budget, is finite. The actual distance lies below that finite quantity. This induction is exposed separately so coordinate projections and mean-square consequences can use a real Wasserstein estimate without silently converting infinity to zero.
