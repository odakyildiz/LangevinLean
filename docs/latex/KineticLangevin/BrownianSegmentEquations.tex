\author{LangevinLean Project}

Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


This module works with one scalar Brownian motion constructed on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) from an isonormal Gaussian map. Let \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) be the real Hilbert space of (classes of) functions on \(\mathbb R\) that are square integrable for Lebesgue measure, and let \(I:L^2(\mathbb R)\to L^2(\mathbb P)\) be a linear isometry whose values are centred Gaussian random variables; such a map is built from a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) and an independent family of standard Gaussian variables. The Brownian motion is \(B_t=I(\mathbf 1_{(0,t]})\), \(t\ge0\), so that \(\mathbb E[B_sB_t]=\langle\mathbf 1_{(0,s]},\mathbf 1_{(0,t]}\rangle=\min(s,t)\). For a continuous \(k:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) and \(t\ge0\) the time kernel \(\kappa^k_t\in L^2(\mathbb R)\) is the class of \(r\mapsto\mathbf 1_{(0,t]}(r)\,k(t-r)\), and \(I(\kappa^k_t)\) is the Wiener integral \(\int_0^tk(t-r)\,dB_r\).




For \(a\ge0\) let \(\tau_a\) be the translation \((\tau_ag)(r)=g(r-a)\) of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\); it is a linear isometry because Lebesgue measure is translation invariant. The segment response


\[J^a_t(k)=I(\tau_a\kappa^k_t)=\int_a^{a+t}k(a+t-r)\,dB_r,\qquad a,t\ge0,\]


is the Wiener integral of \(k\) against the Brownian fluctuations on the interval \((a,a+t]\). With \(\psi_0(u)=e^{-\gamma u}\) and \(\psi_1(u)=\int_0^u\psi_0(s)\,ds\) put \(V^a_t=J^a_t(\psi_0)\) and \(P^a_t=J^a_t(\psi_1)\) (velocity and position responses), and \(W^a_t=B_{a+t}-B_a\) (Brownian increment). The module proves, as identities in \(L^2(\mathbb P)\), that \(J^a_t(1)=W^a_t\), that \(t\mapsto J^a_t(k)\) is continuous, that \(P^a_t=\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds\), that \(V^a_t+\gamma P^a_t=W^a_t\), and the integration-by-parts formulas \(P^a_t=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}W^a_s\,ds\) and \(V^a_t=W^a_t-\gamma\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}W^a_s\,ds\). The case \(a=0\) was treated in the module on Brownian convolutions; the translated versions show that the Gaussian innovations used on the numerical interval \((a,a+t]\) are built from the same Brownian path as the continuous kinetic diffusion on that interval, and they are used to construct exact kinetic segments driven by the same increments.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The Brownian increment on a translated interval}{The Brownian increment on a translated interval}}


\textbf{Definition.} For \(a,t\ge0\) the Brownian increment on \((a,a+t]\) is \(W^a_t=B_{a+t}-B_a\in L^2(\mathbb P)\).




\textbf{Claim.} For all \(a,t\ge0\), the segment kernel of the constant function \(1\) satisfies


\[\tau_a\kappa^1_t=\mathbf 1_{(0,a+t]}-\mathbf 1_{(0,a]}\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb R),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad J^a_t(1)=W^a_t .\]


\textbf{Proof.} By definition of the translation, \((\tau_a\kappa^1_t)(r)=\kappa^1_t(r-a)=\mathbf 1_{(0,t]}(r-a)=\mathbf 1_{(a,a+t]}(r)\) for almost every \(r\) (the elements of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) are classes, so the identity of representatives is an almost-everywhere identity). Next we show \(\mathbf 1_{(0,a+t]}(r)-\mathbf 1_{(0,a]}(r)=\mathbf 1_{(a,a+t]}(r)\) for every real \(r\), distinguishing three cases.



\begin{itemize}


\item If \(r\le0\) or \(r>a+t\), all three indicators vanish.

\item If \(0<r\le a\), then also \(r\le a+t\) because \(t\ge0\); the left-hand side is \(1-1=0\), and \(r\notin(a,a+t]\).

\item If \(a<r\le a+t\), then \(r>0\) because \(a\ge0\); the left-hand side is \(1-0=1\), and \(r\in(a,a+t]\).

\end{itemize}



Two elements of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) with almost everywhere equal representatives are equal, which proves the first identity. Applying the linear map \(I\) gives


\[J^a_t(1)=I(\tau_a\kappa^1_t)=I(\mathbf 1_{(0,a+t]})-I(\mathbf 1_{(0,a]})=B_{a+t}-B_a=W^a_t .\qquad\square\]


The nonnegativity of \(a\) and \(t\) is used in the case distinction; it ensures \((0,a]\subseteq(0,a+t]\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{Continuity and the time primitive of the velocity response}{Continuity and the time primitive of the velocity response}}


\textbf{Claim.} (a) For every continuous \(k\) and every \(a\ge0\), the map \(t\mapsto J^a_t(k)\) from \([0,\infty)\) to \(L^2(\mathbb P)\) is continuous. (b) If \(\gamma\ge0\), then for all \(a,t\ge0\)


\[P^a_t=\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds ,\]


where the right-hand side is the Bochner integral in the Hilbert space \(L^2(\mathbb P)\) of the continuous map \(s\mapsto V^a_s\).




Recall that for a continuous map \(F:[0,t]\to\mathcal X\) into a Banach space \(\mathcal X\), the Bochner integral \(\int_0^tF(s)\,ds\) exists and equals the limit of Riemann sums, and that every bounded linear map \(\Lambda:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) between Banach spaces satisfies \(\Lambda\bigl(\int_0^tF(s)\,ds\bigr)=\int_0^t\Lambda(F(s))\,ds\).




\textbf{Proof.} (a) In the module on Brownian convolutions it was proved that \(t\mapsto\kappa^k_t\) is continuous from \([0,\infty)\) into \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) (by dominated convergence: on a bounded time range the kernels are bounded by \(\sup|k|\) and supported in a bounded interval). The maps \(\tau_a\) and \(I\) are linear isometries, hence continuous, and \(J^a_t(k)=I(\tau_a(\kappa^k_t))\) is a composition of continuous maps.




(b) Put \(\Lambda=I\circ\tau_a\), a bounded linear map from \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) to \(L^2(\mathbb P)\). For \(\gamma\ge0\) the deterministic primitive identity


\[\kappa^{\psi_1}_t=\int_0^t\kappa^{\psi_0}_s\,ds\qquad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb R)\qquad(1)\]


was proved earlier: both sides have inner product \(\int_0^tg(r)\psi_1(t-r)\,dr\) with every \(g\in L^2(\mathbb R)\), by Fubini's theorem applied to \((s,r)\mapsto\mathbf 1_{\{r\le s\}}e^{-\gamma(s-r)}g(r)\) on \((0,t]^2\) and the computation \(\int_r^te^{-\gamma(s-r)}\,ds=\psi_1(t-r)\). The function is integrable on \((0,t]^2\) because \(g\) is integrable on the bounded interval \((0,t]\) (Cauchy–Schwarz) and \(0\le e^{-\gamma(s-r)}\le1\) when \(\gamma\ge0\) and \(r\le s\); this is where \(\gamma\ge0\) is used. The map \(s\mapsto\kappa^{\psi_0}_s\) is continuous by (a), hence integrable on \([0,t]\). Applying \(\Lambda\) to (1) and moving \(\Lambda\) inside the integral,


\[P^a_t=\Lambda(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)=\Lambda\Bigl(\int_0^t\kappa^{\psi_0}_s\,ds\Bigr)=\int_0^t\Lambda(\kappa^{\psi_0}_s)\,ds=\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds .\qquad\square\]


The identity holds in \(L^2(\mathbb P)\); no pointwise evaluation of the random variables is involved.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The damped Brownian integral equation}{The damped Brownian integral equation}}


\textbf{Claim.} (a) For every real \(\gamma\) and all \(a,t\ge0\),


\[V^a_t+\gamma P^a_t=W^a_t .\]


(b) If \(\gamma\ge0\), then for all \(a,t\ge0\)


\[V^a_t=W^a_t-\gamma\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds .\]


\textbf{Proof.} (a) The response functions satisfy \(\gamma\psi_1(u)=1-\psi_0(u)\) for every real \(u\): both sides vanish at \(u=0\) and have derivative \(\gamma e^{-\gamma u}\), so they agree by the fundamental theorem of calculus. Multiplying by the indicator, \(\kappa^{\psi_0}_t+\gamma\kappa^{\psi_1}_t=\mathbf 1_{(0,t]}=\kappa^1_t\) in \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). The case \(a=0\) of the claim, \(I(\kappa^{\psi_0}_t)+\gamma I(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)=B_t\), is the image of this identity under \(I\). Applying instead the linear map \(\Lambda=I\circ\tau_a\) and the previous step's identity \(\Lambda(\kappa^1_t)=J^a_t(1)=W^a_t\),


\[V^a_t+\gamma P^a_t=\Lambda(\kappa^{\psi_0}_t)+\gamma\Lambda(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)=\Lambda\bigl(\kappa^{\psi_0}_t+\gamma\kappa^{\psi_1}_t\bigr)=\Lambda(\kappa^1_t)=W^a_t .\]


(b) For \(\gamma\ge0\) the previous step gives \(P^a_t=\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds\). Substituting this into (a) and moving \(\gamma\int_0^tV^a_s\,ds\) to the right-hand side gives the claim. \(\square\)




Identity (b) says that \(s\mapsto V^a_s\) solves the linear equation \(dV=-\gamma V\,ds+dW^a\) in integral form, with the Brownian increment of the interval \((a,a+t]\) as forcing; this is the velocity equation of the kinetic diffusion without the force term.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The ordinary Brownian convolution formula}{The ordinary Brownian convolution formula}}


\textbf{Claim.} If \(\gamma\ge0\), then for all \(a,t\ge0\)


\[P^a_t=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,W^a_s\,ds\qquad\text{and}\qquad V^a_t=W^a_t-\gamma\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,W^a_s\,ds,\]


with Bochner integrals in \(L^2(\mathbb P)\) of continuous integrands.




\textbf{Proof.} The case \(a=0\) of the first formula, \(P_t=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}B_s\,ds\) with \(P_t=I(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)\), was proved in the module on Brownian convolutions: the map \(s\mapsto e^{\gamma s}P_s\) has derivative \(e^{\gamma s}(V_s+\gamma P_s)=e^{\gamma s}B_s\) for \(s>0\), by the product rule, \(P_s=\int_0^sV_r\,dr\) and part (a) of the previous step with \(a=0\); integrating from \(0\) to \(t\) and using \(P_0=0\) gives \(e^{\gamma t}P_t=\int_0^te^{\gamma s}B_s\,ds\).




We transfer this formula to the interval \((a,a+t]\). The map \(s\mapsto\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}=\kappa^1_s\) is continuous into \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) by part (a) of the second step, hence so is \(s\mapsto e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}\), and its integral over \([0,t]\) exists. Since \(I\) commutes with this integral and \(I(\mathbf 1_{(0,s]})=B_s\),


\[I(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}B_s\,ds=I\Bigl(\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}\,ds\Bigr).\]


The map \(I\) is injective, being an isometry, so


\[\kappa^{\psi_1}_t=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}\,ds\qquad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb R).\qquad(1)\]


Apply \(\Lambda=I\circ\tau_a\) to (1), move it inside the integral, use linearity to pull out the scalar \(e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\), and use \(\Lambda(\mathbf 1_{(0,s]})=\Lambda(\kappa^1_s)=W^a_s\) from the first step:


\[P^a_t=\Lambda(\kappa^{\psi_1}_t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\Lambda(\mathbf 1_{(0,s]})\,ds=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}W^a_s\,ds .\]


For the velocity, substitute this expression for \(P^a_t\) into the identity \(V^a_t+\gamma P^a_t=W^a_t\) of the previous step and solve for \(V^a_t\). \(\square\)




The right-hand sides involve only the continuous path \(s\mapsto W^a_s\) and ordinary integrals, so they can be inserted into the pathwise kinetic integral equation with continuous forcing.
