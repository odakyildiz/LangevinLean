Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


The Gibbs stationarity proof needs the joint law of the initial phase and all future filtered Brownian increments. This section derives that law on each arbitrary initial-position coupling extension. It combines the explicit product-source projection, the exact integration-by-parts response and the established independent thermal Gaussian stream.



The reference initial phase and the entire Brownian source have the required independent product law on the coupling extension. Exact filtered-increment identities supply thermal stream used by stationarity transfer.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Project the coupling source onto an independent phase/\allowbreak{}Brownian product}{Project the coupling source onto an independent phase/Brownian product}}


The projection sends the full sample to \(((V,X_\mathrm{ref}),\omega_\mathrm{Brownian})\). Its law is \((\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\pi)\otimes P_\mathrm{Brownian}\). First, the argument projects the initial coupling onto its second marginal, retaining the independent Gaussian/\allowbreak{}Brownian source. It then applies measure-preserving product associativity and swaps the position and velocity factors. Thus independence of the complete reference initial phase and the complete Brownian source is proved for the product construction.



Project the coupling source onto the reference initial phase and the Brownian coordinate. Product associativity and the reference marginal identify its law as the product of the Gibbs phase and the entire Brownian source. This proves the required independence jointly, rather than interval by interval.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.8.16]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.8.17]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The full-input OU increment}{The full-input OU increment}}


The deterministic integration-by-parts input used in the kinetic splitting proof is \(\Delta W-\gamma\int_0^he^{-\gamma(h-s)}\Delta W_sds\). The local-input increment identity replaces each difference of the full constructed input by its constructed segment input. Applying the vector exponential-response formula through the continuous linear source lift proves that this OU increment is exactly the filtered velocity response. Its representative is consequently the scaled constructed vector response on the original Brownian source, pulled onto the coupling extension.



The filtered increment expressed through the full Brownian input equals the local constructed velocity response. This identity follows from the increment formula and bounded linearity of source pullback. Consequently the global-flow stationarity argument uses the same innovations as the local construction.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The full independent thermal Gaussian joint law}{The full independent thermal Gaussian joint law}}


The vector OU stream already has the exact iid Gaussian law with scale \(\sqrt{1-\psi_0(h)^2}\). The product-source joint-law theorem combines this stream with the initial phase target. Composition with the proved phase/\allowbreak{}Brownian projection law transfers that product law to the coupling extension. Finally, the representative identities identify its initial phase with the constructed reference initial \(L^{2}\) phase and its stream with the full-input OU increments. The resulting joint law is precisely the input-law hypothesis of the proved kinetic Gibbs stationarity transfer theorem.



The vector response stream has the independent thermal Gaussian law. Combining it with the phase/\allowbreak{}source projection gives the joint product law of reference initialization and all thermostat innovations. This supplies the precise source hypothesis of the splitting-limit stationarity theorem.
