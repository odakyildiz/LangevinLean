Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


The two-kernel covariance is strictly positive definite on every positive-duration step. This supplies the Cholesky input required by the first particle algorithm, rather than merely positive semidefiniteness of an abstract Gaussian covariance.



Linear independence of the exponential and its first primitive makes their positive-time Gram covariance strictly positive definite. This supplies the exact nondegeneracy needed for an invertible Gaussian covariance factor.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Use strict positivity of the integrated exponential}{Use strict positivity of the integrated exponential}}


The first integrated response is \(\psi_1(h)=\int_0^he^{-\gamma t}\,dt\). For \(h>0\), the continuous integrand is positive everywhere, hence this integral is strictly positive for every real \(\gamma\). The argument invokes strict positivity of a continuous interval integral, using its positive value at zero. This avoids dividing by friction and includes \(\gamma=0\).



The exponential response is positive at every point, so its integral over a positive-length interval is strictly positive. This argument does not require a positive friction sign. It provides the nonvanishing primitive needed to distinguish the two response functions.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Show every nonzero coefficient vector produces a nonzero response on the interval}{Show every nonzero coefficient vector produces a nonzero response on the interval}}


For \(z=(z_0,z_1)\ne0\), consider \(z_0\psi_0(t)+z_1\psi_1(t)\). If \(z_0\ne0\), its value at zero is \(z_0\), because \(\psi_0(0)=1\), \(\psi_1(0)=0\). If \(z_0=0\), then \(z_1\ne0\), and its value at \(h\) is nonzero because \(\psi_1(h)>0\). The argument handles the two finite coordinates explicitly and proves a strictly positive squared response at a point of \([0,h]\).



A nonzero coefficient combination cannot vanish on the whole interval. Its value at zero detects the exponential coefficient; if that coefficient vanishes, positivity of the first primitive detects the other coefficient. Continuity then makes its square positive on a set of positive measure.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Integrate the square to prove strict positive definiteness}{Integrate the square to prove strict positive definiteness}}


The already proved Gram identity is \[z^\top C_hz=\int_0^h\bigl(z_0\psi_0(t)+z_1\psi_1(t)\bigr)^2\,dt.\] The integrand is continuous and nonnegative, and the previous result supplies a point where it is positive. Its integral is therefore strictly positive for every nonzero \(z\). The argument rewrites the matrix quadratic form as the exact scalar integral, proves its continuity from the finite kernel sum, and combines strict integral positivity with the proved covariance symmetry. Thus \(C_h\) is positive definite, which justifies an invertible triangular Cholesky factor.



The covariance quadratic form is the integral of that continuous square. It is therefore positive for every nonzero coefficient vector. Strict positive definiteness gives a positive leading variance and positive Schur complement, exactly the conditions used in the explicit Gaussian factor.
