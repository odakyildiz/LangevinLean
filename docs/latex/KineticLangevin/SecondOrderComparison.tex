\section{\texorpdfstring{Project the cutoff displacement with the exact coefficient}{Project the cutoff displacement with the exact coefficient}}


Let \(D\) be the \(L^2\) position discrepancy and \(E\) its transformed phase norm. The position projection gives \(D\le(\sqrt2/\gamma)E\), with \(\gamma>0\). The complete free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck correction has the bound

\[\frac{\sqrt2t^2}{2}\left[M_2\sqrt\alpha\,D+2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}\right].\]

Since the coefficient of \(D\) is nonnegative, substitution of the position bound preserves the inequality. The two factors \(\sqrt2\) multiply to two and cancel the denominator two, giving

\[\frac{\sqrt2t^2}{2}\left[M_2\sqrt\alpha\,D+2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}\right]\le\frac{M_2\sqrt\alpha\,t^2}{\gamma}E+\sqrt2t^2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}.\]

The position projection coefficient is needed here because the cutoff bounds the position discrepancy, whereas contraction controls the transformed phase norm. This scalar calculation preserves both the friction factor and the tail coefficient.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The frozen-linear estimate from the potential}{The frozen-linear estimate from the potential}}


The gradient acting on square-integrable random vectors has the same Lipschitz constant and centered strong-smooth inequality as the pointwise gradient. The random Hessian, acting by pointwise application, has operator norm at most \(M\). These are the hypotheses of the frozen-linear contraction estimate in the Hilbert space of square-integrable vectors.



Under \(\gamma^2\ge m+M\) and \(5\gamma Mt\le m\), with the stated positivity and nonnegative-time assumptions, that estimate gives contraction factor

\[1-\frac{mt}{2\gamma}.\]

The sector inequality follows from the original potential's strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient. The derivative defining its Hessian supplies the operator bound. Thus the factor is derived from the potential hypotheses for the same random variables used in the comparison.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The repaired source comparison inequality}{The repaired source comparison inequality}}

\texttt{klmc2\allowbreak{}Source\allowbreak{}Step\_\allowbreak{}comparison\_\allowbreak{}bound} gives the formal counterpart of this step. The main intermediate facts are \texttt{D}, \texttt{C}, \texttt{h\allowbreak{}C}, \texttt{hbound}, \texttt{h\allowbreak{}Corr}, \texttt{h\allowbreak{}Pos} in that order. Named proof applications include \texttt{hh.\allowbreak{}trans\_\allowbreak{}eq}; the adjacent proof shows their hypotheses and order of use.
