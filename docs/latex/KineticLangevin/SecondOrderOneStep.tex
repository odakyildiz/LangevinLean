These results connect the defined four-kernel KLMC2 update to the true stationary kinetic segment and combine the local and repaired comparison estimates. Every moment and error constant has already been derived from path equations and Gaussian/\allowbreak{}Gibbs marginals.



The exact local Taylor defect and repaired stochastic comparison are combined through an auxiliary exact path. Their compatible step restrictions give the second-order coupling recurrence.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Recover the local friction restriction from the source step size}{Recover the local friction restriction from the source step size}}


The restriction 5 gamma M h at most m, together with m at most M and M positive, implies 5 gamma h at most one. Cancelling the positive M gives gamma h at most one fifth. This is precisely the small-time restriction used to convert the cubic and quartic local component estimates into the transformed quarter-bound.



The source step-size restriction implies each smaller scalar restriction needed by the local and synchronous-comparison estimates. Multiplying only by positive curvature and friction quantities preserves these implications. This step verifies compatibility of the two analytic bounds.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Bound the local error of the defined step}{Bound the local error of the defined step}}


Use the corrected-step identity for all four correlated Brownian kernels. The initial velocity and fresh filtered noise define the complete free-OU path. The stationary reference has the exact mild and kinematic equations, with Gaussian-times-Gibbs time marginals. The proved local theorem therefore bounds the transformed norm of the defined step minus the exact endpoint by t\textasciicircum{}3 times [M2 sqrt(p\textasciicircum{}2+2p)+M sqrt(Mp)] divided by four. The exact fourth-moment dimension remains valid in every dimension.



The defined corrected endpoint equals the analytic corrected free-OU endpoint by the exact four-response identity. The cubic local Taylor estimate therefore applies to the numerical update. Its coefficient uses the derived stationary Gaussian and Gibbs moments.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Combine local defect and repaired synchronous comparison}{Combine local defect and repaired synchronous comparison}}


Insert the numerical update starting at the stationary reference into the difference between the numerical update and exact reference endpoint. The transformed triangle inequality splits this into comparison of two numerical steps and the reference local defect. Proposition 3 controls the former, including both fresh stochastic Hessian terms. The preceding local theorem controls the latter. The result is the source affine one-step inequality with factor 1-mt/\allowbreak{}(2 gamma)+M2 sqrt(alpha)t\textasciicircum{}2/\allowbreak{}gamma, Gaussian tail sqrt(2)t\textasciicircum{}2(M-m)exp(-(alpha-p)/\allowbreak{}8), and cubic local term. Its remaining path and law premises are supplied by the constructed canonical segment in the final sampler theorem.



Insert an auxiliary exact endpoint between the numerical and reference endpoints. The local defect controls one gap and the repaired synchronous comparison controls the other. Their sum gives the affine one-step recurrence for the canonical KLMC2 coupling error.
