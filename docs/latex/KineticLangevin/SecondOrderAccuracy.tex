\section{\texorpdfstring{Apply the sampler theorem to a quadratic error allocation}{Apply the sampler theorem to a quadratic error allocation}}


The exact Gaussian-chain theorem gives \(W_2(\nu_n,\pi)\le\sqrt2q^nD+b(h)\), where \(q=1-mh/(4\gamma)\). The proved source restrictions make \(0\le q<1\). For \(h\le1\), the complete persistent error term satisfies \(b(h)\le B_ph^2\). Allocate \(B_ph^2\le0.94\varepsilon\) and choose the natural ceiling of \(\max\{0,\log(24D/\varepsilon)/(mh/(4\gamma))\}\). The shared scalar accuracy lemma makes the total at most \(\varepsilon\). A zero initial distance needs no transient iterations; finite transport is retained explicitly.



The exact sampler bound is decomposed into its decaying transient and complete persistent error term, including the Gaussian tail. Bounding that persistent error by a quadratic upper bound permits an explicit square-root step choice. The logarithmic ceiling count then controls the remaining initial discrepancy.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Choose the positive step and integer count directly}{Choose the positive step and integer count directly}}


Use the minimum of \(1\), \(m/(5\gamma M)\), \(m/(4\sqrt{5p}M_2)\), and \(\sqrt{0.94\varepsilon/B_p}\). The step-specification theorem supplies positivity, both source restrictions and the quadratic bias allocation. Construct the nonnegative real step and the rounded iteration count, then apply the preceding sampler accuracy theorem. For fixed curvature constants, the explicit coefficient is of order \(p\), so the formula displays the \(\sqrt{p/\varepsilon}\) step count up to its visible logarithm. Positive dimension is required for this particular step prescription; the general sampling bound retains all finite dimensions.



The prescribed minimum enforces all source restrictions as well as the accuracy allocation.
