This section identifies the complete law of the exponential-integrator recursion. It proves the independent phase/\allowbreak{}source product by coordinate reassociation, carries the whole innovation-stream law to the initialization product, and identifies the resulting position law with the iterated Markov transition.



The specified probability source’s entire innovation stream is independent of the initial phase. Pushing that joint law through the original recursion identifies the analytic output with the Gaussian Markov iterate.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The initial phase and source product law}{The initial phase and source product law}}


The sampler begins on \(\nu\otimes(\kappa\otimes Q)\): the first coordinate is initial position, the second is initial velocity, and the third is the full future random source. The coordinate map \((x,(v,\omega))\mapsto((v,x),\omega)\) has law \((\kappa\otimes\nu)\otimes Q\). One proves this by the measure-preserving product associator followed by the measure-preserving swap of position and velocity. If the source function \(W\) has the entire iid innovation-stream law \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\), composing that map gives \[\operatorname{Law}((V_0,X_0),(\xi_k)_{k\ge0})=(\kappa\otimes\nu)\otimes\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}.\] This proves joint independence of initialization and the whole future stream from the product source; there is no separate independence hypothesis at each iteration.



The independent standard Gaussian velocity and initial position law form the initial phase product. The constructed innovation stream is independent of this whole phase. Reassociating the product factors proves the joint input law needed for the explicit Markov recursion.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The recursion without changing its innovations}{The recursion without changing its innovations}}


The shared KLMC recursion is exactly the ordinary recursion \(z_{k+1}=\mathcal K_h(z_k,\xi_k)\). One proves equality with the generic noise-driven chain by induction: both initial values are the supplied phase state, and the successor equations apply the same update to the same preceding state and the same correlated phase innovation. This identity allows the generic independent-noise law theorem to describe the defined exponential chain.



The original pointwise KLMC update is applied with the same constructed innovation at each index. Its endpoint agrees with the fixed-source numerical map by induction. Consequently the output-law identification does not rely on replacing the source by a different but similar noise sequence.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The full Markov position-law identity}{The full Markov position-law identity}}


The pointwise sampler uses \((V_0,X_0)=(v,x)\) and innovations \(W(\omega)_k\) on the explicit initialization/\allowbreak{}source product. The gradient Lipschitz assumption proves joint measurability of the defined update. The joint initial/\allowbreak{}stream law then identifies the phase pushforward of the noise-driven recursion with the \(n\)-fold Markov transition. The argument applies the position projection to that equality, combines the two successive measurable pushforwards, unfolds the product-sampler map, and rewrites the proved recursion identity. The conclusion identifies the numerical position law with the position marginal of the Markov iterate initialized at \(\kappa\otimes\nu\). The only source-law premise concerns the input stream; specified Gaussian modules prove it from their constructed Brownian source. No output-law agreement is assumed.



Push the verified joint input law through the measurable recursion and then project position. This gives equality with the iterated independent-Gaussian KLMC transition law. The coupling-independent output appearing in the analytic theorem is therefore the Markov sampler output.
