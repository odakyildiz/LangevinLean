\section{\texorpdfstring{Keep each deterministic and stochastic response on the same source}{Keep each deterministic and stochastic response on the same source}}


Let \(L\) be the continuous linear pullback from the specified Brownian \(L^{2}\) space to the coupled probability space, including the factor \(\sqrt{2\gamma}\). Define \[R_k(t)=\psi_k(\gamma,t)V_0+L N_{\psi_k}(a,t),\qquad C_k(t)=\varphi_{k+1}(\gamma,t)V_0+L N_{\varphi_{k+1}}(a,t).\] Here every noise response uses exactly the same Brownian interval beginning at \(a\). In particular, \(R_0\) is the free OU velocity and \(C_1,C_2\) are the full \(\varphi _{2}\) and \(\varphi _{3}\) correction responses. The proof defines these \(L^{2}\) elements and proves continuity of each \(R_k\) from the response coefficient and constructed convolution continuity.



The deterministic initial-velocity response and the stochastic Wiener responses are kept in one free-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path. This is necessary because the Hessian acts on their sum. Splitting them into unrelated noise variables would destroy the exact corrected-step identity.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The exact primitive and damped-convolution identities}{The exact primitive and damped-convolution identities}}


The deterministic response coefficients and stochastic responses satisfy the same time-integral identities. Consequently \[R_{k+1}(t)=\int_0^tR_k(s)\,ds,\qquad C_k(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}R_k(s)\,ds.\] One proves integrability of every continuous summand, splits each Bochner integral, moves the fixed initial velocity outside its scalar integral, and commutes the source pullback with the integral. It then substitutes the proved Wiener \(\psi\) primitive or \(\varphi\) convolution identity. This establishes the complete correction identity, including the fresh stochastic terms absent from the displayed subtraction in the source paper.



Taking a time primitive or a damped convolution transforms each response kernel into its precise higher-order kernel. The identities are proved before applying the random Hessian. Thus every deterministic and stochastic coefficient is matched with the correct response.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Pass a random Hessian operator through the integrals}{Pass a random Hessian operator through the integrals}}


A uniformly bounded measurable random Hessian difference acts as a proved continuous linear map \(A\) on the \(L^{2}\) space. Therefore it commutes with every time primitive and with the damped convolution. One proves this for an arbitrary continuous linear \(A\); the random-Hessian construction is an application satisfying these hypotheses. The resulting full correction identities are \[AC_1(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\int_0^sAR_0(r)\,dr\,ds,\] and \[AC_2(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\int_0^s\int_0^rAR_0(u)\,du\,dr\,ds.\] The proof checks continuity of every relevant \(L^{2}\) integrand and applies the continuous linear integral-commutation theorem, then rewrites the proved primitive identities on the nonnegative integration intervals. Thus Gaussian truncation can be applied to \(AR_0(r)\) at each time, with no independence between the random Hessian and the velocity required.



The frozen Hessian is constant with respect to local time, although random in the sample point. Its induced bounded linear operator on second-mean variables can therefore pass through the time integrals. No assumption of independence from the integrand is needed.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The \(\varphi _{2}+\gamma \varphi _{3}\) cancellation for the noises}{The φ ₂+γ φ ₃ cancellation for the noises}}


The scalar response identity \(\varphi_2+\gamma\varphi_3=\psi_2\) also holds for the jointly sampled stochastic responses. One proves the corresponding equality of deterministic time-kernel \(L^{2}\) elements by their almost-everywhere indicator representatives. It applies the same time translation, isonormal map, and spatial lift, all continuous and linear. This proves \[N_{\varphi_2}(a,t)+\gamma N_{\varphi_3}(a,t)=N_{\psi_2}(a,t).\] Combining this with the identical scalar coefficient relation gives the exact combined correction identity \[C_1(t)+\gamma C_2(t)=R_2(t).\] This cancellation includes both fresh stochastic corrections. This permits the transformed phase estimate to use a second time primitive of the stationary free OU velocity and recover the correct quadratic time coefficient.



The coefficient relation combines terms from the velocity and position corrections before their norm is estimated. The same relation holds for the random responses because they are linear images of the deterministic kernels. This retains the cancellation omitted by a termwise deterministic-only comparison.
