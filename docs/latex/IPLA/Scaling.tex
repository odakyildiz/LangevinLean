Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Substitution of the lifted curvature, gradient constant, dimension, and step into the Euclidean Langevin estimate gives the particle estimate. The dimension factor reduces to \(D/N=d_x+d_\theta/N\) because the scaled step is the physical step divided by the particle count. All substitutions below are equalities, so the numerical coefficient is preserved.



\section{\texorpdfstring{Step restriction and square-\allowbreak{}root bias}{Step restriction and square-root bias}}


Assume \(N>0\). Division by \(N\) preserves inequalities, and \(Nm+NL=N(m+L)\). Hence \(\gamma/N\le2/(Nm+NL)\) is equivalent to \(\gamma\le2/(m+L)\). The regularity ratio \(NL/(Nm)\) equals \(L/m\), while the dimension–step product satisfies

\[\frac\gamma N(d_\theta+Nd_x)=\gamma\left(d_x+\frac{d_\theta}{N}\right).\]

Nonnegativity of the dimensions and step permits the square-root product rule, giving

\[\frac{33}{20}\frac{NL}{Nm}\sqrt{\frac\gamma N D}=\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma.\]

In terms of the lifted quantities \(m_N=Nm\), \(L_N=NL\), \(h=\gamma/N\), and \(D=d_\theta+Nd_x\), positivity of \(N\) gives \(L_N/m_N=L/m\) and

\[hD=\gamma(d_x+d_\theta/N).\]

The square-root identity is therefore \(\sqrt{hD}=\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma\). Every cancellation of \(N\) uses \(N>0\). The latent contribution is proportional to \(d_x\), whereas the parameter contribution is divided by \(N\). Thus particle count reduces the parameter contribution to discretization error; at a fixed physical step, the latent contribution persists. The admissible physical step does not depend on particle count.



\section{\texorpdfstring{The transport estimate after scaling}{The transport estimate after scaling}}


Assume a family of comparison processes is available for every finite-cost initial coupling and that the initial \(W_2\) distance is finite. The Langevin transport theorem gives

\[W_2(\nu_k,\pi)\le(1-m_Nh)^kW_2(\nu_0,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac{L_N}{m_N}\sqrt{hD}.\]

The assumptions \(m\le L\), \(m>0\), \(\gamma>0\), and the physical step restriction imply the lifted hypotheses by multiplication and division by positive \(N\). The identity \(Nm(\gamma/N)=m\gamma\) simplifies the contraction, and the preceding square-root identity simplifies the dimension term. In particular \((1-m_Nh)^k=(1-m\gamma)^k\). The initial transport distance is already measured in the scaled state norm and acquires no additional particle factor. This proves the finite-time particle estimate under the stated comparison-process premise; the Gaussian construction establishes that premise for the algorithm laws.
