Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


The interacting particle algorithm with step \(\gamma\) is the unadjusted Langevin algorithm for the lifted potential \(V_N\) on \(\mathcal Z\) with step \(h=\gamma/N\), and \(V_N\) has convexity constant \(m_N=Nm\), gradient Lipschitz constant \(L_N=NL\) and dimension \(D=d_\theta+Nd_x\). The Wasserstein estimate for the Langevin algorithm is stated in terms of \(m_N\), \(L_N\), \(h\) and \(D\); its step condition is \(h\le2/(m_N+L_N)\), its contraction factor is \(1-m_Nh\) and its discretization term is \(\frac{33}{20}\frac{L_N}{m_N}\sqrt{hD}\). This section rewrites these three quantities in the particle parameters: for \(N\ge1\),


\[h\le\frac2{m_N+L_N}\iff\gamma\le\frac2{m+L},\qquad m_Nh=m\gamma,\qquad\frac{33}{20}\frac{L_N}{m_N}\sqrt{hD}=\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+\frac{d_\theta}N}\,\sqrt\gamma .\]


All three are identities, so no constant is lost. They are used in the proof of the main theorem. The last result of the section states the resulting transport estimate under an explicit hypothesis that a family of stationary reference processes is available; in the main theorem that hypothesis is not needed, because the reference processes are constructed from the Gaussian innovations.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The step condition and the discretization term in particle variables}{The step condition and the discretization term in particle variables}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \(N\ge1\) and \(m,L,\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and use the convention \(a/0=0\).




(a) \(\displaystyle\frac\gamma N\le\frac2{Nm+NL}\iff\gamma\le\frac2{m+L}\).




(b) If moreover \(d_\theta,d_x\in\mathbb N\) and \(\gamma\ge0\), then


\[\frac{33}{20}\cdot\frac{NL}{Nm}\cdot\sqrt{\frac\gamma N\,(d_\theta+Nd_x)}=\frac{33}{20}\cdot\frac Lm\cdot\sqrt{d_x+\frac{d_\theta}N}\cdot\sqrt\gamma .\]


\textbf{Proof.} (a) By distributivity \(Nm+NL=N(m+L)\), so \(\frac2{Nm+NL}=\frac2{(m+L)N}=\frac1N\cdot\frac2{m+L}\); this holds also when \(m+L=0\), both sides then being \(0\). The left inequality therefore reads \(\frac\gamma N\le\frac1N\cdot\frac2{m+L}\). Since \(N>0\), multiplying or dividing an inequality by \(N\) preserves it, so this is equivalent to \(\gamma\le\frac2{m+L}\).




(b) Since \(N\ne0\), the common factor cancels: \(\frac{NL}{Nm}=\frac Lm\) (both sides are \(0\) if \(m=0\)). Next,


\[\frac\gamma N\,(d_\theta+Nd_x)=\gamma\Bigl(\frac{d_\theta}N+d_x\Bigr)=\Bigl(d_x+\frac{d_\theta}N\Bigr)\gamma .\]


The factor \(a=d_x+d_\theta/N\) is nonnegative, and for \(a\ge0\) and \(b\ge0\) one has \(\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b\). Hence \(\sqrt{\frac\gamma N(d_\theta+Nd_x)}=\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\,\sqrt\gamma\), and multiplying by \(\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\) gives (b). \(\square\)




In (b) the hypothesis \(\gamma\ge0\) is not needed if the square root of a negative number is read as \(0\): the rule \(\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b\) only requires \(a\ge0\) under that convention. In the lifted notation, (a) says that the step condition for \(V_N\) at step \(h=\gamma/N\) is the step condition \(\gamma\le2/(m+L)\) of the particle algorithm, independently of \(N\); and (b) says \(\frac{L_N}{m_N}\sqrt{hD}=\frac Lm\sqrt{(d_x+d_\theta/N)\gamma}\). In the discretization term the latent dimension appears with coefficient one and the parameter dimension is divided by \(N\): at a fixed step, increasing the number of particles reduces only the parameter contribution.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The transport estimate under an explicit reference family}{The transport estimate under an explicit reference family}}


Let \(H\) be a real Hilbert space with its Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra (second countable), \((\Omega,\mathbb P)\) a probability space, and \(L^2=L^2(\mathbb P;H)\). For a potential \(W:H\to\mathbb R\) with \(K\)-Lipschitz gradient, a step \(h\), a dimension parameter \(d\in\mathbb N\), measures \(\nu_0,\pi\) and a sequence of measures \((\nu_n)_{n\ge0}\) on \(H\), a \emph{reference family} is the following data: for every coupling \(c\) of \(\nu_0\) and \(\pi\) with finite quadratic cost \(\operatorname{cost}(c)=(\int\|x-y\|^2dc)^{1/2}\), there are



\begin{itemize}


\item an initial state \(X_0^c\in L^2\) with law \(\nu_0\) and innovations \(\xi_0^c,\xi_1^c,\dots\in L^2\);

\item for every \(n\), continuous maps \(t\mapsto Y_n^c(t)\in L^2\) and \(t\mapsto B_n^c(t)\in L^2\) on \([0,h]\) with \(Y_n^c(h)=Y_{n+1}^c(0)\), \(B_n^c(h)=\sqrt{2h}\,\xi_n^c\), \(Y_n^c(0)\) of law \(\pi\), the integral equation \(Y_n^c(t)-Y_n^c(0)=-\int_0^t\nabla W(Y_n^c(s))\,ds+B_n^c(t)\) for \(t\in[0,h]\), and the moment bounds \(\|\nabla W(Y_n^c(t))\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Kd}\) and \(\|B_n^c(t)\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{2d}\sqrt t\) for \(t\in[0,h]\);

\item the initial bound \(\|X_0^c-Y_0^c(0)\|_{L^2}\le\operatorname{cost}(c)\), and the identity that the law of the Langevin chain \(X_{n+1}^c=X_n^c-h\nabla W(X_n^c)+\sqrt{2h}\,\xi_n^c\) is \(\nu_n\) for every \(n\).

\end{itemize}



Thus \(Y_n^c\) is a stationary Langevin diffusion on the \(n\)-th time interval, driven by the same Gaussian increments as the algorithm and started close to its initial state.




\textbf{Claim.} Let \(N\ge1\), \(d_\theta,d_x\in\mathbb N\), \(m,\gamma\in\mathbb R\) and \(L\ge0\) with \(0<m\le L\), \(\gamma>0\) and \(\gamma\le2/(m+L)\). Let \(W:H\to\mathbb R\) be differentiable and \(Nm\)-strongly convex with \(NL\)-Lipschitz gradient. Let \(\nu_0,\pi,(\nu_n)\) be measures on \(H\) with \(W_2(\nu_0,\pi)<\infty\), and assume that a reference family exists for \(W\), the step \(\gamma/N\), the constant \(NL\) and the dimension parameter \(d=d_\theta+Nd_x\). Then for every \(k\in\mathbb N\),


\[W_2(\nu_k,\pi)\le(1-m\gamma)^k\,W_2(\nu_0,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\,\frac Lm\,\sqrt{d_x+\frac{d_\theta}N}\,\sqrt\gamma .\]


\textbf{Proof.} We apply the Langevin transport estimate with a reference family \cite[Section~3.47.4]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}: if \(W\) is differentiable and \(m'\)-strongly convex with \(L'\)-Lipschitz gradient, \(0<m'\le L'\), \(0<h\le2/(m'+L')\), \(W_2(\nu_0,\pi)<\infty\) and a reference family exists for \(W,h,L',d\), then \(W_2(\nu_n,\pi)\) is finite and


\[W_2(\nu_n,\pi)\le(1-m'h)^nW_2(\nu_0,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac{L'}{m'}\sqrt{hd}\qquad\text{for all }n.\qquad(1)\]


Take \(m'=Nm\), \(L'=NL\), \(h=\gamma/N\) and \(d=d_\theta+Nd_x\), and verify the hypotheses: \(m'>0\) because \(N>0\) and \(m>0\); \(m'\le L'\) by multiplying \(m\le L\) by \(N\ge0\); \(h>0\) because \(\gamma>0\) and \(N>0\); and \(h\le2/(m'+L')\) by part (a) of the previous step applied to \(\gamma\le2/(m+L)\). In (1) with \(n=k\), the contraction factor is \(m'h=Nm\cdot\gamma/N=m\gamma\), and the last term equals \(\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma\) by part (b) of the previous step (with \(\gamma\ge0\)). \(\square\)




The initial distance \(W_2(\nu_0,\pi)\) is measured in the norm of \(H\); in the application \(H=\mathcal Z\) with the scaled norm, and it acquires no particle factor. This result keeps the reference family as a hypothesis. The main theorem does not use it: there, the corresponding estimate is obtained from the Gaussian innovations alone, by a version of (1) in which the reference processes are constructed, and only the two identities of the previous step are used.

\texttt{scaled\_\allowbreak{}dalalyan} works on a general Hilbert space \texttt{H} (instances \texttt{Inner\allowbreak{}Product\allowbreak{}Space \ensuremath{\mathbb R} H}, \texttt{Complete\allowbreak{}Space H}, \texttt{Borel\allowbreak{}Space H}, \texttt{Second\allowbreak{}Countable\allowbreak{}Topology H}) with a potential \texttt{U : H \ensuremath{\to} \ensuremath{\mathbb R}} that already carries the lifted constants: \texttt{hsc : Strong\allowbreak{}Convex\allowbreak{}On Set.\allowbreak{}univ ((N : \ensuremath{\mathbb R}) * m) U} and \texttt{h\allowbreak{}Lip : Lipschitz\allowbreak{}With ((N : \ensuremath{\mathbb R}\ensuremath{\ge}0) * L) (gradient U)}. The remaining hypotheses are \texttt{hm : 0 < m}, \texttt{hm\allowbreak{}L : m \ensuremath{\le} (L : \ensuremath{\mathbb R})}, \texttt{h\ensuremath{\gamma} : 0 < \ensuremath{\gamma}}, \texttt{hsmall : \ensuremath{\gamma} \ensuremath{\le} 2 /\allowbreak{} (m + (L : \ensuremath{\mathbb R}))}, \texttt{hfinite : wasserstein2 \ensuremath{\nu}\ensuremath{{}_{0}} \ensuremath{\pi} ≠ \ensuremath{\top}} and \texttt{family : Reference\allowbreak{}Family P (d\ensuremath{\theta} + N * dx) U (\ensuremath{\gamma} /\allowbreak{} N) ((N : \ensuremath{\mathbb R}\ensuremath{\ge}0) * L) h\allowbreak{}Lip \ensuremath{\nu}\ensuremath{{}_{0}} \ensuremath{\pi} \ensuremath{\nu}}. The structure \texttt{Reference\allowbreak{}Family} of the Langevin library has exactly the fields listed above (initial state, innovations, an \texttt{L2\allowbreak{}Reference} with segments, increments, joining, stationarity, integral equation and the two moment bounds, the initial cost bound and the output laws). The conclusion is stated with \texttt{wasserstein2\allowbreak{}Real}, the real number obtained from \texttt{wasserstein2} by \texttt{ENNReal.\allowbreak{}to\allowbreak{}Real}; the general theorem proves that the distances involved are finite, so no information is lost.

\item \texttt{h\allowbreak{}NL} is \(Nm\le NL\), from \texttt{mul\_\allowbreak{}le\_\allowbreak{}mul\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}nonneg\_\allowbreak{}left hm\allowbreak{}L hn.\allowbreak{}le}; \texttt{hs} is the lifted step condition, from \texttt{(scaled\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}iff N h\allowbreak{}N m (L : \ensuremath{\mathbb R}) \ensuremath{\gamma}).\allowbreak{}mpr hsmall}.

\end{enumerate}
