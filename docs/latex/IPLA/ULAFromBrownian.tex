Assume the Brownian reference satisfies the Langevin integral equation, stationarity, and the drift and noise moment estimates. Subtraction from the Euler update gives an affine recurrence for their mean-square discrepancy. Summation gives the finite-time coupling estimate. Equality of numerical marginals across initial couplings then permits the initial transport infimum.



\section{\texorpdfstring{The transport estimate from the reference integral equation}{The transport estimate from the reference integral equation}}


Under the common-noise integral equation, stationary Gibbs law, and moment bounds, the discrepancy satisfies \(e_{k+1}\le(1-mh)e_k+(33/20)Lh\sqrt{hd}\). Summation gives

\[W_2(\mu_k,\pi)\le(1-mh)^ke_0+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{hd}.\]

The common Gaussian increment cancels on subtraction. The remaining terms are the contractive gradient-step difference and the integral of the reference force minus its left-endpoint value. The local estimate bounds the latter uniformly on each interval. This intermediate result retains its reference-process assumptions. The Gaussian construction proves them and permits the initial coupling infimum. For IPLA the substitutions are \(V=V_N\), \(h=\gamma/N\), \(m_N=Nm\), and \(L_N=NL\). No invariant distribution of the discrete Euler chain is assumed.
