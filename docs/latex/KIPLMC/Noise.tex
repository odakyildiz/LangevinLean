Velocity and position innovations within a coordinate share a Brownian source and are correlated. Distinct spatial coordinates are independent. This module constructs that joint probability measure using the kinetic library's two-dimensional Gram Gaussian, directly on the parameter-and-particle coordinate set. It works also when one of the dimensions is zero.


\section{Construct independent spatial Gaussian blocks and assemble phase vectors}


The coordinate set is \(I=\{1,\ldots,d_\theta\}\sqcup(\{1,\ldots,N\}\times\{1,\ldots,d_x\})\). For each \(i\in I\), take the shared kinetic Gaussian \((\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_i\prime)\sim\mathcal N(0,C_{\gamma,h})\), independently across \(i\). Their finite product measure is \texttt{exponentialNoiseBlocks}. The assembly map collects the first entries into the velocity vector and the second entries into the position vector. Lean defines these two Euclidean vectors with \texttt{WithLp.toLp} and proves the map measurable coordinate by coordinate. Pushing the product measure forward gives \texttt{exponentialNoise}. Probability follows from the Gaussian probability instances and preservation of total mass under the measurable map; no normalization assumption is supplied.

\section{Prove coordinate independence and retain the within-pair covariance}


The product-space evaluation maps are independent, so the Gaussian blocks belonging to distinct coordinates are independent. Evaluating the assembled velocity and position at a fixed spatial coordinate recovers its original two-dimensional Gaussian block exactly. Lean composes the two pushforwards, proves equality of the composed function with the block evaluation map by checking the two possible indices, and uses the product-measure evaluation law. The covariance of the pair is therefore exactly the kinetic Gram matrix, already proved positive semidefinite in the shared library. Multiplication by \(\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\) is performed by the particle step, not by this base noise law.
