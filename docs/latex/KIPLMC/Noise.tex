\title{\texorpdfstring{Correlated Gaussian velocity and position innovations in particle coordinates}{Correlated Gaussian velocity and position innovations in particle coordinates}}
\author{LangevinLean Project}
\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


One KIPLMC1 step adds to the velocity and to the position two Wiener integrals of the same Brownian increment, \(\int_0^h\psi_0(h-s)\,dW_s\) and \(\int_0^h\psi_1(h-s)\,dW_s\), coordinate by coordinate. In each coordinate this pair is a centered Gaussian vector in \(\mathbb R^2\) with the Gram covariance


\[C(\gamma,h)_{kl}=\int_0^h\psi_k(t)\psi_l(t)\,dt\qquad(k,l\in\{0,1\}),\]


and different coordinates are independent. This section constructs the corresponding probability measure \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)\) on \(\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_N\): take independent copies of the two-dimensional Gaussian law \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}=\mathcal N(0,C(\gamma,h))\), one for each coordinate of the scaled particle space, and collect the first entries into a velocity vector and the second entries into a position vector. We prove that the construction gives a probability measure, that the blocks of distinct coordinates are independent, and that the velocity–position pair of each coordinate has law exactly \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\). The factor \(\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\) is applied by the update, not by this measure. The construction works for arbitrary dimensions, including \(d_\theta=0\) or \(d_x=0\).



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.26]{LangevinLean2026Kinetic}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Independent Gaussian blocks indexed by the coordinates}{Independent Gaussian blocks indexed by the coordinates}}


\textbf{Definitions.} The coordinate set of \(\mathcal Z_N\) is \(I_N=\{1,\dots,d_\theta\}\sqcup(\{1,\dots,N\}\times\{1,\dots,d_x\})\). Let \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\) be the centered Gaussian measure on \(\mathbb R^2\) with covariance matrix \(C(\gamma,h)\) above, constructed in the kinetic report (\cite[Section~3.26.3]{LangevinLean2026Kinetic}). For \(h\ge0\) the matrix \(C(\gamma,h)\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite, since for \(u\in\mathbb R^2\)


\[u^{\mathsf T}C(\gamma,h)u=\int_0^h\bigl(u_0\psi_0(t)+u_1\psi_1(t)\bigr)^2\,dt\ge0,\]


so \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\) is the law of a centered Gaussian vector whose covariances are the entries of \(C(\gamma,h)\). The block measure is the finite product


\[\beta_{\gamma,h}=\bigotimes_{a\in I_N}\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\qquad\text{on }(\mathbb R^2)^{I_N},\]


the joint law of independent random vectors \(\omega_a\in\mathbb R^2\), \(a\in I_N\), each with law \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\).




\textbf{Claim.} \(\beta_{\gamma,h}\) is a probability measure.




\textbf{Proof.} A product of finitely many probability measures is a probability measure: the product measure of the whole space is the product of the total masses, \(\prod_{a\in I_N}1=1\). \(\square\)




The entries of \(C(\gamma,h)\) are the covariances of the two Wiener integrals displayed in the summary, by the Itô isometry and the substitution \(t=h-s\); in particular the off-diagonal entry is positive for \(h>0\), so velocity and position innovations of the same coordinate are correlated.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Assembling the velocity and position innovations}{Assembling the velocity and position innovations}}


\textbf{Definitions.} The assembly map \(A:(\mathbb R^2)^{I_N}\to\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_N\) collects first and second entries:


\[A(\omega)=\bigl((\omega_{a,0})_{a\in I_N},\;(\omega_{a,1})_{a\in I_N}\bigr).\]


The KIPLMC1 innovation law is \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)=A_\#\beta_{\gamma,h}\); a sample \((\xi,\xi')\) has velocity part \(\xi_a=\omega_{a,0}\) and position part \(\xi'_a=\omega_{a,1}\).




\textbf{Claim.} \(A\) is measurable, and \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)\) is a probability measure.




\textbf{Proof.} Every coordinate of \(A(\omega)\) is a coordinate of \(\omega\), so \(A\) is linear and continuous, hence Borel measurable. Therefore \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)(\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_N)=\beta_{\gamma,h}(A^{-1}(\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_N))=\beta_{\gamma,h}((\mathbb R^2)^{I_N})=1\) by the previous step. \(\square\)




The assembly only rearranges coordinates, so all joint moments of \(\eta_1\) are those of the blocks: \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\) are centered Gaussian vectors in \(\mathcal Z_N\) with covariances \(C_{00}I\) and \(C_{11}I\) and cross-covariance \(C_{01}I\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{Independence across coordinates and the law of each pair}{Independence across coordinates and the law of each pair}}


\textbf{Claim.} (a) Under \(\beta_{\gamma,h}\) the random vectors \(\omega\mapsto\omega_a\), \(a\in I_N\), are mutually independent. (b) For every coordinate \(a\in I_N\), the image of \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)\) under \((\xi,\xi')\mapsto(\xi_a,\xi'_a)\in\mathbb R^2\) is \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\).




\textbf{Proof.} (a) For a product of probability measures \(\bigotimes_a\mu_a\) and measurable sets \(S_a\subseteq\mathbb R^2\), the event \(\{\omega_a\in S_a\text{ for all }a\in J\}\) for a finite set \(J\) of indices is the box \(\prod_{a\in J}S_a\times\prod_{a\notin J}\mathbb R^2\), whose measure is \(\prod_{a\in J}\mu_a(S_a)\cdot1\). This is mutual independence of the coordinate maps.




(b) Let \(\pi_a(\xi,\xi')=(\xi_a,\xi'_a)\). The image of \(\eta_1=A_\#\beta_{\gamma,h}\) under \(\pi_a\) is \((\pi_a\circ A)_\#\beta_{\gamma,h}\), since both maps are measurable. By the definition of \(A\), \(\pi_a(A(\omega))=(\omega_{a,0},\omega_{a,1})=\omega_a\), checked on the two entries. Finally, the image of a product of probability measures under the evaluation \(\omega\mapsto\omega_a\) is the factor \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}\): for measurable \(S\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) the preimage is \(S\times\prod_{b\ne a}\mathbb R^2\), of measure \(\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}(S)\). \(\square\)




Thus \(\eta_1(\gamma,h)\) is the law of independent coordinate pairs, each with covariance \(C(\gamma,h)\), which is the noise of Algorithm 1 in scaled coordinates: the parameter coordinates give \((\varepsilon^0,\varepsilon^{0\prime})\) and the latent coordinates of particle \(i\) give \((\varepsilon^i,\varepsilon^{i\prime})\). Multiplication by \(\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\) in the update, and by \(\sqrt N\) when the latent coordinates are unscaled, produces the amplitudes \(\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\) and \(\sqrt{2\gamma}\) of the paper.
