The Metropolis-adjusted Langevin proposal provides an exactly invariant approximation to the diffusion. This module proves that its mean rejected displacement under Gibbs initialization is at most \(Ch\sqrt h\), with a finite constant derived from the potential assumptions.


\section{Expand the logarithmic acceptance ratio}


For \(y=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\xi\), define

\[R(x,y)=U(x)-U(y)-\frac{\|x-y+h\nabla U(y)\|^2-\|y-x+h\nabla U(x)\|^2}{4h}.\]

Expanding squared norms for \(h\ne0\) gives

\[R=U(x)-U(y)+\tfrac12\langle\nabla U(x)+\nabla U(y),y-x\rangle-\tfrac h4(\|\nabla U(y)\|^2-\|\nabla U(x)\|^2).\]

The smooth upper models in both directions bound the trapezoid error by \(L\|y-x\|^2\). Factoring a difference of squares and using gradient Lipschitzness bounds the score-norm difference by \(L\|y-x\|(\|\nabla U(x)\|+\|\nabla U(y)\|)\). The triangle inequality combines these into the displayed bound for \(|R|\).

\section{From rejection probability to displacement}


The elementary inequality \(e^r\ge1+r\) implies \(0\le1-\min(1,e^r)\le|r|\). Hence the rejected displacement is bounded by \(\|y-x\||R|\). Define \(A(x,\xi)=\|\nabla U(x)\|+\sqrt2\|\xi\|\) and \(D_h=\|y-x\|(1-\min(1,e^R))\). Both are nonnegative. When \(0<h\le1\), \(h\le\sqrt h\), so \(\|y-x\|\le\sqrt h A\). Also \(\|\nabla U(y)\|\le\|\nabla U(x)\|+L\|y-x\|\). Substituting these estimates gives

\[D_h(x,\xi)\le h\sqrt h\,L(2+L)A(x,\xi)^3.\]

\section{Derive the integrable cubic envelope}


Absorb \(\|x\|^4\) into half the Gaussian decay to integrate \(\|x\|^4e^{-a\|x\|^2}\). Strong convexity supplies the Gaussian envelope for \(e^{-U}\), giving a finite fourth Gibbs moment and then \(x\in L^3(\pi)\). The Lipschitz gradient has at most linear growth, while a standard Gaussian has all finite moments. On \(\pi\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)\), Minkowski therefore gives \(A\in L^3\), so \(A^3\) is integrable.

\section{Integrate the pointwise estimate}


Define \(C=L(2+L)\int A^3d(\pi\otimes\mathcal N(0,I))\). This is a nonnegative finite real number. The preceding envelope establishes integrability of \(D_h\), so integration preserves the pointwise inequality and gives \(\mathbb ED_h\le Ch\sqrt h\) uniformly over \(0<h\le1\). The existential endpoint packages this explicit constant and its uniform estimate.
