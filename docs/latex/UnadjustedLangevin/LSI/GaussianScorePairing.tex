Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


At positive time the Gaussian component score is \(S_{t,x}(y)=-(y-x+t\nabla U(x))/(2t)\). Differentiation of the mixture and positivity of \(p_t\) give \(p_t\nabla\log p_t=\int K_tS_{t,x}d\mu\). For a continuous field \(V\in L^2(\rho_t)\), this identity may be paired with \(V\) and integrated.



Under the initial/\allowbreak{}output pair law, the component score has energy \(d/(2t)\), and \(V(y)\) has the same energy as under \(\rho_t\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore proves absolute integrability before Fubini is used. The resulting identity equates the kernel-weighted component-score pairing with the mixture-score pairing. Changing variables to \(Y_t=X-t\nabla U(X)+\sqrt{2t}Z\) expresses the same pairing on the fixed independent initial/\allowbreak{}noise space used for entropy production.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The score of one Gaussian component}{The score of one Gaussian component}}


For a fixed initial state \(x\), the Gaussian proposal has center \(c_t(x)=x-t\nabla U(x)\). Its score in the proposed state is \(S_{t,x}(y)=\nabla_y\log K_t(x,y)=-(y-c_t(x))/(2t)\). This definition is an explicit affine vector field. It is different from the mixture score, which averages this field over the posterior initial-state distribution.



Differentiate the logarithm of one Gaussian component in the observation variable. The normalizer is constant in that variable, leaving the negative centered displacement divided by \(2t\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{Square-integrability of the component score}{Square-integrability of the component score}}


Represent the initial/\allowbreak{}output pair as \((X,Y_t)\), with \(Y_t=c_t(X)+\sqrt{2t}Z\) and independent standard Gaussian \(Z\). Substituting into the component score cancels the center: \(S_{t,X}(Y_t)=-(\sqrt{2t}/(2t))Z\). The Gaussian identity random variable is square integrable; scalar multiplication and pullback to the product preserve this property. The map-measure theorem transfers it to the pair law. No initial position or gradient moment is needed for this component score.



Under the independent-noise representation the centered component displacement is \(\sqrt{2t}Z\). Its score energy is therefore \(d/(2t)\), finite for every positive time.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Pulling a finite-energy state field to the pair space}{Pulling a finite-energy state field to the pair space}}


If \(V\in L^2(\rho_t)\), then \(V(Y_t)\in L^2(\mu\otimes\mathcal N(0,I))\), because \(\rho_t\) is the pushforward by the proposal. Map the independent pair to \((X,Y_t)\); a second application of the map-measure equivalence gives \((x,y)\mapsto V(y)\in L^2\) under the pair law. Continuity of \(V\) supplies the measurability required in both transfers.



A state-space field with finite square energy under \(\rho_t\) has the same square energy when composed with the proposed coordinate on the joint pair. This is pushforward change of variables.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Integrability of the score-test pairing}{Integrability of the score-test pairing}}


The component score and the state field \(V(y)\) are both square integrable under the pair law. Holder's inequality makes the product of their norms integrable, and \(|\langle S,V\rangle|\le\|S\|\|V\|\) proves integrability of their inner product. The exact pair-density identity then rewrites this law as \(K_t(x,y)\mu(dx)dy\). The weighted-density equivalence gives joint integrability of \(K_t\langle S_{t,x},V(y)\rangle\) under \(\mu\otimes dy\), which is the hypothesis needed for Fubini in the averaging calculation.



Cauchy–Schwarz on the pair probability bounds the absolute pairing integral by the product of component-score and state-field \(L^2\) norms. Hence the signed pairing is integrable before its order is exchanged.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The weighted mixture-score identity}{The weighted mixture-score identity}}


The already constructed spatial derivative of the Gaussian mixture identifies its score as the posterior mean of the component score. The posterior density in \(x\) is \(K_t(x,y)/p_t(y)\), where \(p_t(y)=\int K_t(x,y)d\mu(x)>0\). Substitute this explicit weighted-density formula into the posterior integral and multiply by \(p_t(y)\). The common denominator cancels pointwise, giving \(p_t(y)\nabla\log p_t(y)=\int K_t(x,y)S_{t,x}(y)d\mu(x)\). The precision and step-size forms have equal Gaussian normalizers, so this identity follows from the previously proved mixture derivative formula.



Integrating the component gradient over the starting law gives \(\nabla p_t\). Dividing by the positive mixture identifies its averaged score with \(\nabla\log p_t\), the posterior-score identity in weighted form.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Integrability of the kernel-weighted score pairing}{Integrability of the kernel-weighted score pairing}}


The component score is \(L^2\) under the pair probability law, hence is also \(L^1\). Identify that law with its explicit density \(K_t\) under \(\mu\otimes dy\). The vector-valued weighted-density integrability theorem gives \((x,y)\mapsto K_t(x,y)S_{t,x}(y)\in L^1(\mu\otimes dy)\). In particular, Fubini supplies integrability of the vector section in \(x\) for almost every output \(y\); this is needed before moving an inner product through its integral.



The Gaussian score has finite square norm and the tested state field has finite square norm. Their product is therefore integrable, supplying the precise Fubini hypothesis for the following averaging identity.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Averaging the Gaussian component score}{Averaging the Gaussian component score}}


For almost every \(y\), vector-section integrability permits the linear functional \(w\mapsto\langle w,V(y)\rangle\) to commute with integration. The pointwise weighted-score identity then gives \(\int K_t(x,y)\langle S_{t,x}(y),V(y)\rangle d\mu(x)=p_t(y)\langle\nabla\log p_t(y),V(y)\rangle\).



The scalar pairing is jointly integrable by the preceding Holder argument, so Fubini exchanges the initial-state and output integrals. Integrate the displayed identity in \(y\). Finally \(p_t(y)dy=\rho_t(dy)\), with its explicit measurable finite density, changes the weighted Lebesgue integral into the integral under the interpolation law. Thus \(\int K_t\langle S_{t,x},V\rangle d\mu\,dy=\int\langle\nabla\log p_t,V\rangle d\rho_t\).



Average the component score at a fixed observation, then integrate its pairing with the state test. The mixture-gradient identity converts the result into the pairing with the Lebesgue score under \(\rho_t\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{The score identity on the independent-noise space}{The score identity on the independent-noise space}}


Apply the measurable pair map \(T_t(x,z)=(x,Y_t(x,z))\) to the continuous pairing \(\langle S_{t,x}(y),V(y)\rangle\). The map-integral theorem expresses its pair-law integral as an integral over the fixed independent initial/\allowbreak{}noise space. The exact pair-density theorem expresses the same integral as the Gaussian-weighted Lebesgue pairing just evaluated.



Substitute \(Y_t=c_t(x)+\sqrt{2t}z\) in the component score. The residual becomes \(\sqrt{2t}z\), so the result is \(\mathbb E\langle-(\sqrt{2t}/(2t))Z,V(Y_t)\rangle=\mathbb E_{\rho_t}\langle\nabla\log p_t,V\rangle\). With \(V=\nabla\log r_t\), this identity turns the Gaussian-noise part of the raw entropy flux into the Lebesgue-score pairing needed for the full Fisher term.



Use the joint-law identity to rewrite the same integral on the original independent state-noise space. This lets the entropy flux be combined with the exact displacement and gradient-error estimates already proved there.
