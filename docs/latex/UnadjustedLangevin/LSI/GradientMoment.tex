\title{\texorpdfstring{A gradient moment bound from a coupling}{A gradient moment bound from a coupling}}
\author{LangevinLean Project}

Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Let \((X,Y)\) couple \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), with finite squared distance, and suppose the gradient is \(L\)-Lipschitz. The inequality \(\|\nabla U(X)\|\le L\|X-Y\|+\|\nabla U(Y)\|\) gives a second-moment bound under \(\mu\) when that moment is finite under \(\nu\). For a normalized Gibbs target, the latter is at most \(nL\). An explicitly assumed entropy bound on the coupling cost then gives the stated entropy-dependent gradient estimate. The final ULA entropy theorem instead obtains its gradient moment directly from exponential integrability of the Gibbs score.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.27]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Integrability and the coupling estimate}{Integrability and the coupling estimate}}


Let \(c\) have marginals \(\mu,\nu\), and assume \(\int\|x-y\|^2dc<\infty\) and \(\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu<\infty\). Lipschitzness and \((a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2\) give the pointwise inequality

\[\|\nabla U(x)\|^2\le2L^2\|x-y\|^2+2\|\nabla U(y)\|^2.\]

The right-hand side is integrable under \(c\). Measurability and domination imply integrability of the first-coordinate gradient square, hence of \(\|\nabla U\|^2\) under \(\mu\). Integration and the marginal identities give

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2dc(x,y)+2\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu.\]

Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The estimate for a Gibbs target}{The estimate for a Gibbs target}}


Let \(\nu=Z^{-1}e^{-U}dx\) be a Gibbs probability measure, with differentiable \(U\) and \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient. Probability normalization supplies a finite positive partition function. The Gibbs score estimate proves square-integrability and \(\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu\le nL\), without strong convexity. Substituting these facts in the coupling estimate gives

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2dc+2nL.\]

The same argument proves gradient square-integrability under \(\mu\); it is not assumed before this conclusion.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.26.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.27.6]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.27.7]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Substitution of an entropy bound on the coupling cost}{Substitution of an entropy bound on the coupling cost}}


Suppose the given finite-cost coupling also satisfies

\[\int\|x-y\|^2dc(x,y)\le\frac2\alpha\operatorname{KL}(\mu\mid\nu),\qquad \alpha>0,\quad \operatorname{KL}(\mu\mid\nu)<\infty.\]

Multiply this inequality by \(2L^2\ge0\) and substitute it in the preceding gradient estimate. Since \(2L^2(2/\alpha)=4L^2/\alpha\),

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le\frac{4L^2}{\alpha}\operatorname{KL}(\mu\mid\nu)+2nL.\]

The cost-to-entropy estimate is an explicit hypothesis for the supplied coupling. This result does not prove a transport inequality from LSI.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.1]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.26.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.
