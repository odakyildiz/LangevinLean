For any finite-cost coupling of \(X\sim\mu\) and \(Y\sim\nu\), an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient gives \(\mathbb E_\mu\|\nabla U\|^2\le2L^2\mathbb E\|X-Y\|^2+2\mathbb E_\nu\|\nabla U\|^2\). This module proves that estimate together with gradient square integrability. For a normalized Gibbs target the second term is bounded by \(2nL\). An explicit entropy-cost coupling then supplies the exact gradient-moment bound used by the ULA proof. The final Gaussian ULA theorem instead derives its moment directly from entropy in EntropyMoment.lean; no transport implication is needed there.


\section{Derive both integrability and the coupling moment estimate}


Let \(c\) be a joint measure with marginals \(\mu\) and \(\nu\). Assume \(\int\|x-y\|^2dc<\infty\), that the gradient is \(L\)-Lipschitz, and that \(\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu<\infty\). The theorem proves square integrability under \(\mu\) and

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2\,dc(x,y)+2\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu.\]

At a pair \((x,y)\), write \(\nabla U(x)=(\nabla U(x)-\nabla U(y))+\nabla U(y)\). The triangle inequality and Lipschitzness give \(\|\nabla U(x)\|\le L\|x-y\|+\|\nabla U(y)\|\). Squaring and applying \((a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2\) proves the pointwise bound \texttt{hpoint}. Lean obtains the last elementary inequality from the nonnegative square \((a-b)^2\).



Before integrating the inequality, the proof establishes integrability: \texttt{hsecond} pulls the target squared-gradient integrand to the second marginal, while \texttt{hmajor} combines it with the integrable coupling cost. This dominating function and continuity of the gradient prove \texttt{hfirst}, integrability of the first-coordinate squared gradient. The map-integrability equivalence and the first-marginal identity then prove \texttt{h\ensuremath{\mu}}. Thus a missing moment is derived, rather than represented by a possibly undefined real integral.



Finally \texttt{integral\_mono} integrates \texttt{hpoint}. The identities \texttt{hfirst\_marginal} and \texttt{hsecond\_marginal}, proved by the pushforward integral formula, replace the joint-coordinate integrals by their marginal integrals and give exactly the displayed estimate.

\section{Insert the derived target score bound}


For the normalized Gibbs probability \(\nu=Z^{-1}e^{-U}dx\), differentiability of \(U\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient imply the target estimate \(\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu\le nL\). Consequently every finite-cost coupling gives

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2dc+2nL.\]

The proof first derives \(Z<\infty\) from probability normalization, using \texttt{gibbsPartition\_ne\_top\_of\_probability}. The shared theorem \texttt{ULA.memLp\_gradient\_gibbsMeasure} then supplies target square integrability; its \(L^2\) conclusion is converted into the integrability input for the preceding coupling theorem. The sharp target moment itself comes from \texttt{ULA.integral\_norm\_sq\_gradient\_gibbs\_le}.



Those shared Gibbs results use translation invariance and a sub-Gaussian score argument under first-order smoothness, so strong convexity is unnecessary here. Substituting their target bound into the coupling estimate proves the stated result and retains the newly derived square integrability under \(\mu\).

\section{Obtain the exact entropy-dependent coefficient from transport control}


Suppose the preceding finite-cost coupling additionally satisfies

\[\int\|x-y\|^2dc(x,y)\le\frac2\alpha D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu),\qquad \alpha>0,\quad D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu)<\infty.\]

Substitution into the coupling bound gives

\[\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le\frac{4L^2}{\alpha}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu)+2nL.\]

The Lean hypothesis \texttt{htransport} is exactly the displayed cost estimate for the supplied coupling. It is multiplied by the nonnegative factor \(2L^2\); \texttt{heq} verifies the coefficient identity \(2L^2(2/\alpha)=4L^2/\alpha\). The result also returns gradient square integrability from the previous theorem. Separate hypotheses record positive \(\alpha\) and finite extended KL before its real value is used.



This theorem completes the coupling-to-moment calculation and connects it to the already proved target score bound. It does not assert that LSI constructs this coupling or prove the LSI-to-Talagrand implication. The existing ULA endpoint still displays its gradient-moment input in this reusable coupling-based interface. The complete endpoint uses the direct score-exponential estimate in EntropyMoment.lean.
