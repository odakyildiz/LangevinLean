Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Let \(Y,Z\) solve the integral equation with the same continuous additive input, initial states \(x,y\), and an \(L\)-Lipschitz drift. Subtraction cancels the input and yields \(u(t)\le\|x-y\|+L\int_0^tu(s)ds\) for the continuous function \(u(t)=\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|\). Grönwall gives \(u(t)\le e^{Lt}\|x-y\|\). Equal initial states therefore give equal solutions on the interval.



The same argument in \(L^2(P;K)\) gives square-mean stability and uniqueness. Equality of classes is almost-everywhere equality of representatives at each fixed time; the conclusion alone does not supply a single null set valid for all real times.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Stability for a common additive input}{Stability for a common additive input}}


Subtract the two integral equations so that their common additive input cancels. The integral norm inequality and Lipschitz drift give \(u(t)\le\|x-y\|+L\int_0^t u(s)ds\), where \(u(t)=\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|\) is continuous. Grönwall's inequality yields \(u(t)\le\|x-y\|e^{Lt}\). All time integrals are justified by continuity on the compact horizon.



After cancellation of the common input, the norm difference is bounded by initial separation plus \(L\) times its time integral. The exponential factor comes from this scalar Grönwall comparison, without differentiating the input.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Uniqueness with equal initialization}{Uniqueness with equal initialization}}


Set the two initial states equal in the stability estimate. The right side becomes \(0e^{Lt}=0\), forcing \(\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|=0\) for each \(t\in[0,h]\). Norm-zero equivalence gives equality of the vectors, so the two functions agree on the interval.



Equal initialization sets the multiplicative initial distance to zero. Nonnegativity of the norm then forces equality of the solutions at every time on the interval, giving uniqueness among the stated continuous integral solutions.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Stability of square-integrable solutions}{Stability of square-integrable solutions}}


Specialize Banach-space stability to the gradient-composition map on \(L^2(P;K)\). Its Lipschitz theorem supplies the required constant, so \(\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|_2\le e^{Lt}\|Y(0)-Z(0)\|_2\). This compares solutions with a common \(L^2\)-noise input and possibly different initial random variables.



All operations now take place in \(L^2\), so the resulting distance is a root mean square difference. A different coupling of the initial random vectors changes that initial norm but not the stability constant.
