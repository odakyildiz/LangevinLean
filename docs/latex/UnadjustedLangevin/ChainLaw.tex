Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Let \(F\) be a measurable update and define \(X_0=x\), \(X_{n+1}=F(X_n,w_n)\). The output at a fixed finite index is a measurable function of initialization and the finite innovation prefix. If initialization and the whole innovation sequence are independent with laws \(\mu,Q\), the output law is the pushforward of \(\mu\otimes Q\) by this recursion map.



For independent identically distributed innovations with common law \(\eta\), their sequence law is \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\). Consequently different probability-space constructions with these same input laws have identical output laws. Specializing the update to Gaussian Euler identifies both the pointwise ULA recursion and its square-integrable representative with these distributions.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The recursion driven by a noise sequence}{The recursion driven by a noise sequence}}


Define \(X_0=x\), \(X_{n+1}=F(X_n,w_n)\) for any update and noise path. No distribution assumptions are part of the recursion; they enter only when a law is assigned to its inputs.



For a fixed input \((x,w)\), the finite-time state uses only the first finitely many coordinates of the innovation path. No probability measure is needed to evaluate it. This separates the deterministic algorithm from its probabilistic realization and permits the same recursion to be applied on product probability spaces and on any probability space with the stated input laws.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The noise-driven chain at zero}{The noise-driven chain at zero}}


The zero clause of the recursion evaluates to the supplied initial state. This defining identity is the base case for all law and measurability inductions.



Before any update, the output is exactly \(x\), independently of the innovation path. Consequently its law under an independent input product must be the initial marginal. The same identity also starts the induction proving that two deterministic recursions with the same update and inputs agree at every finite step.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The successor recursion}{The successor recursion}}


The successor clause unfolds to \(X_{n+1}=F(X_n,w_n)\). Substitution of the Euler update identifies this recursion with ULA by induction.



The successor equation identifies which innovation is used next: state \(n\) uses only indices below \(n\), and its successor consumes \(w_n\). The state therefore depends on the preceding innovation prefix. A fresh-noise theorem later separates the prefix \((w_0,\ldots,w_{n-1})\) from this next coordinate.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Measurability of the noise-driven chain}{Measurability of the noise-driven chain}}


Induct on \(n\). The base map is the initial-state projection. At the successor, pair the measurable current state with the measurable coordinate evaluation \(w\mapsto w_n\), then compose with the measurable uncurried update. Thus each iterate is a measurable function of initialization and the full noise path.



This induction requires only measurability of the update in both of its inputs. In particular, it also applies when the update is discontinuous. Measurability of each iterate ensures that its distribution is defined and permits the subsequent integration over the initial state and noise sequence.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The marginal law of the recursion}{The marginal law of the recursion}}


Push \(\mu\otimes Q\) through the measurable recursion output at index \(n\). The product gives independent initialization and noise path. This output law depends only on the initial law, noise-path law, and update, not on a particular sample-space construction.



If \(F_n\) denotes the finite recursion map, define its distribution by \((F_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q)\). The product specifies independence of initialization and the entire innovation path while allowing arbitrary dependence within \(Q\). Thus this definition applies before specializing to iid noise and makes the exact inputs determining the output distribution explicit.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Its initial law}{Its initial law}}


At index zero the output map is first-coordinate projection. Since \(Q\) has mass one, the first marginal of \(\mu\otimes Q\) is exactly \(\mu\). This proves that the output law starts from the supplied initial law.



For a measurable initial-state set \(A\), the probability of the zero-step output lying in \(A\) is \((\mu\otimes Q)(A\times\Omega_Q)=\mu(A)Q(\Omega_Q)=\mu(A)\). The noise source's unit mass is the only normalization needed here. This direct marginal computation proves that the product construction preserves the specified initialization.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Law identification under independence}{Law identification under independence}}


Independence gives the joint input law \(P_{(X,W)}=P_X\otimes P_W\). Push that identity through the measurable recursion output and use pushforward composition. The output law is therefore the pushforward of this product input law under the recursion map at the specified index.



Independence means that the joint law of initialization and complete noise path factors into their two marginals. Applying the same measurable recursion to this equality yields equality of output laws. In particular the result does not require the input variables to have a product-space representation; their factorized joint distribution is sufficient.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Law identification for independent identical innovations}{Law identification for independent identical innovations}}


Joint independence of the innovations and their common law \(\eta\) identify the entire noise-path law with \(\bigotimes_n\eta\). Substitute this identity into the independent-initialization law theorem. Countability of the time index supplies path-valued measurability.



For iid innovations with common law \(\eta\), every finite cylinder event factors as a product of \(\eta\)-probabilities. These cylinder probabilities identify the complete path law as \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\). Substituting this law in the independent-input result shows that the algorithm's distribution is determined by \(F\), \(\mu\) and \(\eta\), independently of the probability-space representation of those inputs.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Uniqueness of the resulting marginal laws}{Uniqueness of the resulting marginal laws}}


Apply the independent-input pushforward formula on both probability spaces. Equality of their initial laws and the shared innovation law makes the right sides identical, so their iterate laws agree. The theorem compares distributions without coupling the two constructions.



Both realizations are measurable images of the same abstract input probability \(\mu\otimes\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\). Hence each measurable output set receives the same probability in both. The conclusion is equality of their distributions at a fixed finite iteration, even though their random variables live on different spaces and no joint coupling between them was specified.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Equality with the ULA recursion}{Equality with the ULA recursion}}


Induct on the index with \(F(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}z\). At zero both recursions return the same state. If they agree at index \(n\), their successor equations apply the same function to the same state and innovation, so they agree at index \(n+1\). This identifies the generic noise-driven recursion with ULA pointwise.



With the Euler update \(F(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z\), both pointwise constructions have the same initial clause and the same successor clause. Applying the induction hypothesis inside this common function proves equality at the next step. The argument is purely deterministic, so it can be reused with every supplied innovation sequence before assigning Gaussian laws.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Measurability of the ULA update}{Measurability of the ULA update}}


Lipschitzness makes \(\nabla U\) continuous. The joint ULA update is obtained from coordinate projections and that gradient by scalar multiplication and addition, hence is measurable. This discharges the generic recursion's update-measurability premise.



The map \((x,z)\mapsto x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z\) is jointly continuous because the gradient is Lipschitz and hence continuous. Coordinate projections, addition and scalar multiplication preserve continuity. Thus its measurability does not require a step restriction, strong convexity, or Gaussianity; those hypotheses enter only the later moment and convergence estimates.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The ULA law under independent identical innovations}{The ULA law under independent identical innovations}}


Use measurability of the Gaussian Euler update in the generic iid law theorem, then rewrite the generic recursion using equality of the two Gaussian Euler recursions. The result identifies the ULA recursion with the product-noise pushforward law.



Apply the general iid realization formula to the measurable Euler update. The deterministic recursion equality identifies its output with the Euler chain. Its time-\(n\) law is therefore the pushforward of the independent initialization and iid innovation path. This equality compares numerical chains on different probability-space extensions by their distributions.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The square-integrable chain has the same law}{The square-integrable chain has the same law}}


The \(L^2\) chain representative agrees almost everywhere with the pointwise ULA recursion of the input representatives. Apply the pointwise law theorem and transfer pushforwards across that almost-everywhere equality. Quotient representative choices therefore do not change the algorithm law.



At every fixed finite index, the square-integrable recursion and its ordinary pointwise update agree almost surely. Almost-surely equal measurable random variables have the same pushforward law. The pointwise iid theorem therefore transfers to the square-integrable realization. This transfer ensures that analytic \(L^2\) estimates apply to the algorithm law, without depending on choices of representative random functions.
