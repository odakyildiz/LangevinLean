\author{LangevinLean Project}
\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Let \(\mathcal X\) (states) and \(\mathcal Z\) (noise values) be measurable spaces, let \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) carry the product \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the coordinate maps \(w\mapsto w_k\), and let \(F:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\to\mathcal X\) be an update rule. The recursion driven by a noise sequence \(w=(w_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}\) is


\[\Phi_0(x,w)=x,\qquad\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F\bigl(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n\bigr).\]


For a measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal X\) and a measure \(Q\) on \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) we define the \emph{chain law} \(\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)=(\Phi_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q)\), the image of the product measure under \(\Phi_n\); here \(f_\#\nu(A)=\nu(f^{-1}(A))\) denotes the image (pushforward) of a measure. We prove that \(\Phi_n\) is measurable when \(F\) is, that \(\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)=\mu\), and that every realisation of the recursion on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\), with an initial state \(X\) independent of the noise sequence \(W\), has law \(\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W)\), where \(P_X\) is the law of \(X\). If the noise variables \(W_n\) are independent with common law \(\eta\), then \(P_W\) is the infinite product \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\), so the law of the \(n\)-th iterate depends only on \(F\), \(P_X\) and \(\eta\). Two realisations on different probability spaces with the same input laws therefore have the same output laws.




The main application is the unadjusted Langevin algorithm on \(\mathbb R^d\), with update \(F(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z\), for both the pointwise recursion and its realisation in \(L^2\). Throughout, \(\sqrt{\cdot}\) is the nonnegative square root, with the convention \(\sqrt x=0\) for \(x<0\); the identities of this section hold for every real step \(h\), and the convention only affects \(h<0\), which never occurs in the estimates. Consequently the law of the algorithm does not depend on how the initial state is coupled with other random variables, provided it is independent of the noise; this is what allows the coupling argument to choose the initial coupling freely.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The recursion driven by a noise sequence}{The recursion driven by a noise sequence}}


\textbf{Definition.} For sets \(\mathcal X,\mathcal Z\), a map \(F:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\to\mathcal X\), an initial state \(x\in\mathcal X\) and a noise sequence \(w=(w_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}\in\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\), define \(\Phi_n(x,w)\in\mathcal X\) by recursion on \(n\):


\[\Phi_0(x,w)=x,\qquad\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F\bigl(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n\bigr).\]


By induction, \(\Phi_n(x,w)\) depends only on \(x\) and on \(w_0,\dots,w_{n-1}\): this is clear for \(n=0\), and \(\Phi_{n+1}\) uses \(\Phi_n\) and the single additional coordinate \(w_n\). No measurability or probability enters the definition; laws are assigned to the inputs \((x,w)\) only below.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The successor recursion}{The successor recursion}}


\textbf{Claim.} For every \(F\), \(x\), \(w\) and \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)\).




\textbf{Proof.} This is the second clause of the definition. \(\square\)




The \((n+1)\)-st state is obtained from the \(n\)-th state with the noise value \(w_n\), and \(\Phi_n(x,w)\) depends only on \(w_0,\dots,w_{n-1}\); the noise used in a step is therefore not yet used by the current state.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Measurability of the iterates}{Measurability of the iterates}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \(\mathcal X,\mathcal Z\) be measurable spaces and let \(F:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\to\mathcal X\) be measurable for the product \(\sigma\)-algebra on \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\). Then for every \(n\in\mathbb N\) the map \((x,w)\mapsto\Phi_n(x,w)\) is measurable from \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) to \(\mathcal X\).




\textbf{Proof.} Induction on \(n\).




\emph{Base case.} \(\Phi_0(x,w)=x\) is the first coordinate projection, which is measurable for the product \(\sigma\)-algebra by definition of that \(\sigma\)-algebra.




\emph{Inductive step.} Assume \(\Phi_n\) is measurable. The map \((x,w)\mapsto w_n\) is the composition of the second projection with the coordinate map \(w\mapsto w_n\), and both are measurable (the latter by definition of the product \(\sigma\)-algebra on \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\)). A map into a product \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\) is measurable if both of its components are, so \((x,w)\mapsto(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)\) is measurable. Composing with the measurable \(F\) shows that \(\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)\) is measurable. \(\square\)




Only joint measurability of \(F\) is used, not continuity. Measurability of \(\Phi_n\) is what makes the image measures below well defined.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The chain law as an image of a product measure}{The chain law as an image of a product measure}}


\textbf{Definition.} Let \(\mu\) be a measure on \(\mathcal X\), \(Q\) a measure on \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) and \(n\in\mathbb N\). The law of the \(n\)-th iterate with initial law \(\mu\) and noise-path law \(Q\) is the image of the product measure under \(\Phi_n\):


\[\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)=(\Phi_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q),\qquad\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)(A)=(\mu\otimes Q)\bigl(\{(x,w):\Phi_n(x,w)\in A\}\bigr)\]


for measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) (when \(F\) is measurable, the set on the right is measurable by the previous step). Under \(\mu\otimes Q\) the two coordinates \(x\) and \(w\) are independent with laws \(\mu\) and \(Q\); the coordinates of \(w\) may be dependent under \(Q\). The definition involves only \(F\), \(\mu\) and \(Q\), not a probability space on which the algorithm is run.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The initial law}{The initial law}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \(\mu\) be a measure on \(\mathcal X\) and \(Q\) a probability measure on \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\). Then \(\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)=\mu\).




\textbf{Proof.} Since \(\Phi_0(x,w)=x\), the map \(\Phi_0\) is the first projection. For measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) the preimage is the rectangle \(A\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\), and the product measure of a rectangle is the product of the measures of its sides:


\[\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)(A)=(\mu\otimes Q)\bigl(A\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\bigr)=\mu(A)\,Q(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})=\mu(A)\cdot1=\mu(A).\]


Two measures that agree on all measurable sets are equal. \(\square\)




The hypothesis \(Q(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})=1\) is used in the third equality; for a general finite \(Q\) the result would be \(Q(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})\,\mu\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{The law of an independent realisation}{The law of an independent realisation}}


Recall that random variables \(X:\Omega\to\mathcal X\) and \(W:\Omega\to\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\) are independent if \(P(X\in A,\ W\in B)=P(X\in A)\,P(W\in B)\) for all measurable \(A\), \(B\); equivalently, the joint law of \((X,W)\) is the product of the marginal laws, \(P_{(X,W)}=P_X\otimes P_W\) (two measures on a product space that agree on measurable rectangles agree, by the uniqueness of extension from a \(\pi\)-system).




\textbf{Claim.} Let \(P\) be a probability measure on \(\Omega\), \(F\) measurable, \(X:\Omega\to\mathcal X\) and \(W:\Omega\to\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) almost-everywhere measurable and independent. Then for every \(n\),


\[P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W),\]


where \(\Phi_n(X,W)\) is the random variable \(\omega\mapsto\Phi_n(X(\omega),W(\omega))\).




\textbf{Proof.} The random variable \(\Phi_n(X,W)\) is the composition of \(\omega\mapsto(X(\omega),W(\omega))\) with \(\Phi_n\). The image of a measure under a composition is the iterated image, \((\Phi_n\circ(X,W))_\#P=(\Phi_n)_\#\bigl((X,W)_\#P\bigr)\); this holds for the measurable \(\Phi_n\) (previous measurability step) and the almost-everywhere measurable pair \((X,W)\). By independence \((X,W)_\#P=P_X\otimes P_W\). Hence


\[P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=(\Phi_n)_\#\bigl(P_X\otimes P_W\bigr)=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W).\qquad\square\]


Only independence of \(X\) from the whole noise sequence is used; the coordinates of \(W\) may depend on each other.

\end{enumerate}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Independent identically distributed innovations}{Independent identically distributed innovations}}


Recall that \(W_0,W_1,\dots\) are (mutually) independent if for all finite sets \(J\subseteq\mathbb N\) and measurable \(B_j\subseteq\mathcal Z\), \(P(W_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J)=\prod_{j\in J}P(W_j\in B_j)\). For a probability measure \(\eta\) on \(\mathcal Z\), the infinite product \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\) is the unique probability measure on \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) with \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}(\{w:w_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J\})=\prod_{j\in J}\eta(B_j)\) for all such \(J\) and \(B_j\).




\textbf{Claim.} Let \(P\) be a probability measure, \(F\) measurable, \(X:\Omega\to\mathcal X\) and \(W_k:\Omega\to\mathcal Z\) (\(k\in\mathbb N\)) almost-everywhere measurable, with \(X\) independent of the sequence \(W=(W_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}\), the \(W_k\) mutually independent, and \(P_{W_k}=\eta\) for every \(k\). Then for every \(n\),


\[P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n\bigl(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\bigr).\]


\textbf{Proof.} \emph{Path law.} The map \(\omega\mapsto(W_k(\omega))_{k}\) into \(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\) is almost-everywhere measurable, because each coordinate is and the index set \(\mathbb N\) is countable. For finite \(J\) and measurable \(B_j\), independence and \(P_{W_j}=\eta\) give


\[P_W\bigl(\{w:w_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J\}\bigr)=\prod_{j\in J}P(W_j\in B_j)=\prod_{j\in J}\eta(B_j),\]


so by the uniqueness of the infinite product, \(P_W=\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\).




\emph{Conclusion.} The previous step, applied to \(X\) and the sequence \(W\), gives \(P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W)=\mathcal L_n(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})\). \(\square\)

\begin{enumerate}


\item \texttt{hp} is the path law. Mathlib's \texttt{i\allowbreak{}Indep\allowbreak{}Fun.\allowbreak{}map\_\allowbreak{}fun\_\allowbreak{}eq\_\allowbreak{}infinite\allowbreak{}Pi\_\allowbreak{}map\ensuremath{{}_{0}}'} states that, for countably many independent almost-everywhere measurable variables, the law of the sequence is the infinite product of the individual laws; \texttt{simp\_\allowbreak{}rw [hlaw] at hp} replaces every individual law by \(\eta\).

\item The final \texttt{rw} applies \texttt{noise\allowbreak{}Driven\allowbreak{}Chain\_\allowbreak{}law\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}indep} to \(X\) and the sequence, whose almost-everywhere measurability is \texttt{aemeasurable\_\allowbreak{}pi\_\allowbreak{}iff.\allowbreak{}mpr h\allowbreak{}W} (a map into a countable product is almost-everywhere measurable when all coordinates are), and then substitutes \texttt{hp}.

\end{enumerate}
\end{leanbox}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Equal input laws give equal output laws}{Equal input laws give equal output laws}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \((\Omega,P)\) and \((\Omega',P')\) be probability spaces and \(F\) measurable. Let \(X,(W_k)\) on \(\Omega\) and \(X',(W'_k)\) on \(\Omega'\) satisfy the hypotheses of the previous step with the same noise law \(\eta\), and assume \(P_X=P'_{X'}\). Then for every \(n\), \(\Phi_n(X,W)\) under \(P\) and \(\Phi_n(X',W')\) under \(P'\) have the same law.




\textbf{Proof.} By the previous step applied on each space,


\[P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n\bigl(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\bigr)=\mathcal L_n\bigl(P'_{X'},\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\bigr)=P'_{\Phi_n(X',W')},\]


where the middle equality substitutes \(P_X=P'_{X'}\). \(\square\)




No relation between the two probability spaces is required. In the coupling argument this is applied to realisations of the algorithm on extensions of a probability space that carry different couplings of the initial state with a stationary reference; all of them produce the same law of the algorithm.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Equality with the ULA recursion}{Equality with the ULA recursion}}


For \(d\in\mathbb N\), \(U:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) and \(h\in\mathbb R\), the ULA update is


\[F_h(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z,\qquad x,z\in\mathbb R^d,\]


and the ULA chain with initial state \(x\) and innovations \(w=(w_k)\) is \(X_0=x\), \(X_{k+1}=F_h(X_k,w_k)\), as defined in the section on the noise-driven ULA chain.




\textbf{Claim.} For all \(d\), \(U\), \(h\), \(x\), \(w\) and \(n\), \(\Phi_n(x,w)\) computed with \(F=F_h\) equals the \(n\)-th ULA iterate \(X_n\).




\textbf{Proof.} Induction on \(n\). For \(n=0\) both are \(x\). If \(\Phi_n(x,w)=X_n\), then \(\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F_h(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)=F_h(X_n,w_n)=X_{n+1}\), by the successor clauses of the two recursions and the induction hypothesis. \(\square\)

\section{\texorpdfstring{Measurability of the ULA update}{Measurability of the ULA update}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \(d\in\mathbb N\), \(h\in\mathbb R\), \(L\ge0\), and let \(U:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) have an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient. Then \(F_h:\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d\) is measurable for the product of the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebras.




\textbf{Proof.} A Lipschitz map is continuous, and a continuous map between metric spaces is Borel measurable; hence \(\nabla U\) is measurable. The projections \((x,z)\mapsto x\) and \((x,z)\mapsto z\) are measurable, so \((x,z)\mapsto h\nabla U(x)\) and \((x,z)\mapsto\sqrt{2h}\,z\) are measurable (compositions and multiplication by constants). On \(\mathbb R^d\), which is second countable, the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra of \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\) is the product of the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebras, so the sum and difference of two measurable \(\mathbb R^d\)-valued maps are measurable. Therefore \(F_h(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z\) is measurable. \(\square\)




The Lipschitz hypothesis is used only through continuity of \(\nabla U\); no condition on \(h\) or on convexity is required.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The law of the ULA chain}{The law of the ULA chain}}


\textbf{Claim.} Let \(P\) be a probability measure, \(d\in\mathbb N\), \(h\in\mathbb R\), \(L\ge0\), \(U:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) with \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient, and let \(X\) and \(W_k\) (\(k\in\mathbb N\)) be almost-everywhere measurable \(\mathbb R^d\)-valued random variables with \(X\) independent of \((W_k)_k\), the \(W_k\) mutually independent and \(P_{W_k}=\eta\) for all \(k\). Then the \(n\)-th ULA iterate \(X_n\) with initial state \(X\) and innovations \(W_k\) satisfies


\[P_{X_n}=\mathcal L_n\bigl(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\bigr)\qquad\text{with }F=F_h.\]


\textbf{Proof.} By the previous step \(F_h\) is measurable, so the independent identically distributed case applies to \(F=F_h\) and gives \(P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})\). By the identification step, \(\Phi_n(X(\omega),W(\omega))=X_n(\omega)\) for every \(\omega\). \(\square\)




When \(\eta\) is the standard Gaussian law on \(\mathbb R^d\), \(\mathcal L_n(\mu,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})\) is the law of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm after \(n\) steps started from \(\mu\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{The square-integrable chain has the same law}{The square-integrable chain has the same law}}


Let \(\mathcal G[X]=[\nabla U\circ X]\) be the gradient acting on \(L^2=L^2(P;\mathbb R^d)\). The ULA chain in \(L^2\), with initial class \(X\) and innovation classes \(W_k\), is \(\mathcal X_0=X\), \(\mathcal X_{k+1}=\mathcal X_k-h\,\mathcal G(\mathcal X_k)+\sqrt{2h}\,W_k\). The law of a class is the law of any representative; it does not depend on the representative, because almost-everywhere equal functions have the same image measure.




\textbf{Claim.} Let \(P\) be a probability measure, \(d\), \(h\), \(L\), \(U\) as in the previous step, \(X\in L^2\) and \(W_k\in L^2\) (\(k\in\mathbb N\)), with (representatives satisfying) \(X\) independent of \((W_k)_k\), the \(W_k\) mutually independent and \(P_{W_k}=\eta\). Then for every \(n\), the law of \(\mathcal X_n\) is \(\mathcal L_n(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})\).




\textbf{Proof.} By the corresponding result for the chain in \(L^2\) \cite[Section~3.13.16]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}, any representative of \(\mathcal X_n\) agrees \(P\)-almost everywhere with \(\omega\mapsto X_n(\omega)\), the pointwise ULA iterate started at \(X(\omega)\) with innovations \(W_k(\omega)\). (Induction on \(n\): \(\mathcal G(\mathcal X_n)\) is represented by \(\nabla U\circ\mathcal X_n\), and a countable union of null sets is null.) Almost-everywhere equal maps have equal images of \(P\), since their preimages of every measurable set differ by a null set. Every element of \(L^2\) has an almost-everywhere measurable representative, so the previous step applies to \(X\) and \((W_k)\) and gives \(P_{X_n}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})\). \(\square\)
