For measurable \(F(x,z)\), the recursion \(X_{n+1}=F(X_n,Z_n)\) has a law determined by the initial law and the full noise law when the initialization and noise are independent.


\section{Define the recursion and its canonical law}


Set \(\Phi_0(x,w)=x\), \(\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)\). Induction proves measurability: the next step composes measurable \(F\) with the previous state and coordinate evaluation. Define \(\operatorname{chainLaw}_n=\Phi_{n\#}(\mu\otimes Q)\). For probability noise law \(Q\), the time-zero law is \(\mu\).

\section{Use independence to identify any realization}


If \(X_0\) is independent of the noise path \(W\), then \(\operatorname{Law}(X_0,W)=\operatorname{Law}(X_0)\otimes\operatorname{Law}(W)\). Push this equality through \(\Phi_n\). For iid noise with marginal \(\eta\), the path law is \(\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\). Hence two realizations with equal initial law, equal noise marginals, iid noise and the stated initial independence have identical output laws at every finite time.

\section{Specialize to pointwise and L\ensuremath{{}^{2}} ULA}


With \(F(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}z\), induction identifies the generic recursion with ulaChain. Gradient Lipschitzness gives measurability of \(F\). The generic iid law theorem therefore applies to ULA; its \(L^2\) version first uses the almost-everywhere identity between the lifted chain and the pointwise recursion, which leaves pushforward laws unchanged.
