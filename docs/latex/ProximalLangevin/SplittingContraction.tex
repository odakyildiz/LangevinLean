Assume \(N>0\); let \(g_1\) be differentiable and \(m\)-strongly convex with \(L_1\)-Lipschitz gradient, and let \(g_2\) be convex and bounded below. Suppose \(0<m\le L_1\), \(\lambda>0\), and \(0\le\gamma\le2/(m+L_1)\). Jensen’s inequality transfers pairwise proximal nonexpansiveness to the averaged state map. In two updates driven by the same innovation, the additive perturbations cancel before proximity. The smooth gradient-step contraction therefore also bounds the full splitting update and its finite iterates.



Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.7]{LangevinLean2026IPLA}; \cite[Section~3.10]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Nonexpansiveness of the averaged pair map}{Nonexpansiveness of the averaged pair map}}


Jensen’s inequality for the averaged parameter difference and the exact latent squared scaling give

\[\|\operatorname{Pair}_P(z)-\operatorname{Pair}_P(w)\|^2\le N^{-1}\sum_i\|P(J_i z)-P(J_i w)\|^2.\]

If \(P\) is nonexpansive, each squared summand is at most \(\|J_i(z-w)\|^2\). Their sum is \(N\|z-w\|^2\), so the averaged map is nonexpansive.



Explicitly put \(A_i z=(\theta,\sqrt N z_i)\) and \(\Delta_i=P(A_i z)-P(A_i w)\). The parameter inequality is \(N\|\overline\Delta_\theta\|^2\le\sum_i\|\Delta_{i,\theta}\|^2\), while the latent differences are rescaled by \(N^{-1/2}\). Thus

\[\|\operatorname{Pair}P(z)-\operatorname{Pair}P(w)\|^2\le\frac1N\sum_i\|\Delta_i\|^2.\]

Nonexpansiveness of \(P\) and identity \(\sum_i\|A_i(z-w)\|^2=N\|z-w\|^2\) give the unit full-state Lipschitz bound. Positivity of \(N\) justifies division and rescaling. The argument compares two averaged outputs and does not assume that either minimizes a shared-state proximal objective.




Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.1.1]{LangevinLean2026IPLA}; \cite[Section~3.1.2]{LangevinLean2026IPLA}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Contraction with the same innovation}{Contraction with the same innovation}}


The lifted smooth potential has curvature \(Nm\) and gradient constant \(NL_1\). At step \(\gamma/N\) its gradient update contracts by \(1-m\gamma\ge0\). For the same innovation the noisy forward difference is this deterministic difference. Composition with the nonexpansive pairwise proximal map yields

\[\|T_\lambda(z,\xi)-T_\lambda(w,\xi)\|\le(1-m\gamma)\|z-w\|.\]

Induction gives factor \((1-m\gamma)^n\). Writing \(q=1-m\gamma\), the step restriction ensures \(q\ge0\). Starting from \(e_0=\|z-w\|\), the induction step is \(e_{k+1}\le q e_k\le q^{k+1}e_0\). Equivalently, with \(q=1-m\gamma\) the discrepancies satisfy \(e_{k+1}\le q e_k\) and are bounded at time n by \(q^n e_0\).



Only equality of the innovations is used, so this stability statement holds for deterministic sequences as well as Gaussian ones. Convergence to a Gibbs law additionally requires a stationary reference and a local-error estimate.
