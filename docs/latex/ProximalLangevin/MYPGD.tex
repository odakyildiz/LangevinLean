Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


Consider finite-second-moment latent-only and full-noise chains with the same deterministic \(r\)-contractive map \(D\) and noise amplitude \(a\). The one-step coupling gives a squared Wasserstein recurrence. From a common initial law, geometric summation bounds their separation uniformly over finite iteration counts. For the smoothed gradient map this yields an explicit radius depending on parameter dimension and particle count.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The transition estimate after the coupling infimum}{The transition estimate after the coupling infimum}}


For every finite-cost input coupling the transition estimate is \(W_2(T_x\mu,T\nu)^2\le r^2\operatorname{cost}(c)^2+a^2d_\theta\). Finite input second moments give \(W_2(\mu,\nu)<\infty\), so taking the coupling infimum yields \(W_2(T_x\mu,T\nu)^2\le r^2W_2(\mu,\nu)^2+a^2d_\theta\).



The additive term \(a^2d_\theta\) is independent of the chosen coupling. Since \(W_2(\mu,\nu)<\infty\), the infimum is finite and the equivalent kernel notation gives

\[W_2(\mu P_x,\nu P)^2\le r^2W_2(\mu,\nu)^2+a^2d_\theta.\]

The infimum replaces the squared input cost by \(W_2(\mu,\nu)^2\), with nonnegative coefficient \(r^2\). One finite input coupling already proves a finite output cost. Thus neither finiteness nor the recurrence assumes attainment of the optimal coupling.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The geometric comparison from a common initial law}{The geometric comparison from a common initial law}}


Measurable transitions preserve probability, and their linear-growth moment bounds preserve \(L^2\) at every finite iteration. Starting both chains from \(\mu\) gives zero initial transport by the diagonal coupling. Under \(0\le r<1\), geometric summation yields \(W_2(\rho_n^x,\rho_n)^2\le a^2d_\theta/(1-r^2)\).



For recurrence \(E_{k+1}\le r^2E_k+a^2d_\theta\) with zero initial value, induction gives \(E_k\le a^2d_\theta\sum_{j<k}r^{2j}\). The condition \(r<1\) makes \(1-r^2\) positive, so the finite geometric sum is bounded by its infinite-series value. This proves the stated uniform bound while retaining finite moments at each iteration.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Contraction of the smoothed gradient step}{Contraction of the smoothed gradient step}}


The smoothed gradient step contracts by \(r=1-m\gamma\) under \(0<m\le L_\eta\) and \(0<\gamma\le2/(m+L_\eta)\). For each input coupling, use a common standard Gaussian vector for the latent-only and full transitions. The exact omitted-noise variance gives

\[
W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_{k+1},\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_{k+1})^2
 \le r^2 W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_k,\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_k)^2
       +\frac{2\gamma d_\theta}{N}.
\]

Take the input coupling infimum at each step. From the same initial law, \(0\le r<1\) and \(1-r^2=m\gamma(2-m\gamma)\ge m\gamma\) give

\[
W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_n,\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_n)^2
 \le\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}.
\]

For \(V_\eta=g_1+g_2^\eta\), the envelope results give curvature \(m\) and gradient constant \(L_\eta=L_1+\eta^{-1}\). Lifting gives \(Nm,NL_\eta\), so the step \(\gamma/N\) has contraction \(r=1-m\gamma\). Restriction \(0<\gamma\le2/(m+L_\eta)\) and \(m\le L_\eta\) imply \(0\le m\gamma\le1\) and thus \(0\le r<1\).



With \(r=1-m\gamma\) and \(a^2=2\gamma/N\), the denominator in the geometric sum is \(1-r^2=m\gamma(2-m\gamma)\). Inequality \(m\gamma\le1\) bounds it below by \(m\gamma>0\). Cancellation of the physical step gives the squared radius \(2d_\theta/(Nm)\). This is an upper bound on the difference between two finite-particle laws. It asserts neither that removing noise worsens the algorithm nor that a mean-field limit has been taken.
