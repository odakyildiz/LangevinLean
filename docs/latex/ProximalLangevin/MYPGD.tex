Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


The one-step noise-removal coupling yields a squared Wasserstein recurrence between the actual latent-only and full-noise chains. Finite moments and probability mass propagate through both transitions. From a common initialization, geometric summation bounds their finite-particle separation, and the true smoothed forward contraction supplies an explicit parameter-dimension floor.




Finite-moment latent-only and full Gaussian chains use the same deterministic \(r\)-contractive map \(D\) and amplitude \(a\). Their actual Markov laws are compared at every iteration.


\section{\texorpdfstring{Pass from every input coupling to actual transport}{Pass from every input coupling to actual transport}}


The preceding coupling supplies \(W_2(T_x\mu,T\nu)^2\le r^2\operatorname{cost}(c)^2+a^2d_\theta\) for every finite input coupling. Finite second moments prove \(W_2(\mu,\nu)<\infty\), so the infimum lemma yields \(W_2(T_x\mu,T\nu)^2\le r^2W_2(\mu,\nu)^2+a^2d_\theta\).




The preceding estimate holds for every finite-cost input coupling, with the same additive \(a^2d_\theta\). Finite second moments first ensure \(W_2(\mu,\nu)<\infty\). The infimum lemma then gives \[W_2(\mu P_x,\nu P)^2\le r^2W_2(\mu,\nu)^2+a^2d_\theta.\] The existence of one finite-cost coupling also proves finiteness of the output distance. An optimal coupling is unnecessary: the argument uses a common bound for the entire family of admissible couplings.





Because the same upper bound holds for every input coupling, taking its cost infimum replaces the coupling square by \(W_2(\mu,\nu)^2\). The coefficient \(r^2\) is nonnegative, so this operation preserves order. Finite input moments ensure that the infimum is finite, and one finite coupling already supplies a finite output cost. No optimal coupling needs to be chosen.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Iterate from a common initialization}{Iterate from a common initialization}}


Measurability preserves probability mass, and the one-step moment lemmas preserve \(L^2\) by induction. Since both chains start from \(\mu\), their initial transport distance is zero. For \(0\le r<1\), the geometric recurrence gives \(W_2(\rho_n^x,\rho_n)^2\le a^2d_\theta/(1-r^2)\), along with finiteness at every time.






Starting from the same initial law gives zero initial distance via the diagonal coupling. Induction on \(E_{k+1}\le r^2E_k+a^2d_\theta\) gives \(E_k\le a^2d_\theta\sum_{j<k}r^{2j}\). The strict condition \(r<1\) makes the denominator \(1-r^2\) positive and bounds the finite sum by its infinite geometric value. The estimate is uniform over finite iteration counts.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The genuine smoothed forward map contracts}{The genuine smoothed forward map contracts}}


The common deterministic smoothed step contracts by \(r=1-m\gamma\) when \(0<m\le L_\eta\) and \(0<\gamma\le2/(m+L_\eta)\). Couple any two current laws, share their latent Gaussian noise, and use the same full Gaussian vector for the MYIPLA step. The exact noise identity proves the actual one-step squared transport estimate


\[
W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_{k+1},\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_{k+1})^2
 \le r^2 W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_k,\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_k)^2
       +\frac{2\gamma d_\theta}{N}.
\]


The proof constructs each output coupling, establishes its finite moments, and takes the infimum over input couplings. Starting from the same initial law, \(0\le r<1\) and \(1-r^2=m\gamma(2-m\gamma)\ge m\gamma\) give


\[
W_2(\rho^{\mathrm{PGD}}_n,\rho^{\mathrm{IPLA}}_n)^2
 \le\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}.
\]


This is a conservative comparison radius, not a claim that removing parameter noise worsens the algorithm. The estimate follows from the two actual recursions and their genuine independent Gaussian transitions.




For \(V_\eta=g_1+g_2^\eta\), the earlier Moreau results give differentiability, strong convexity \(m\) and score Lipschitz constant \(L_\eta=L_1+\eta^{-1}\). Lifting gives constants \(Nm,NL_\eta\), and the Euler step \(\gamma/N\) consequently contracts with \(r=1-m\gamma\). The admissible step \(0<\gamma\le2/(m+L_\eta)\), with \(m\le L_\eta\), proves \(0\le m\gamma\le1\), hence \(0\le r<1\).





With \(r=1-m\gamma\) and \(a^2=2\gamma/N\), the denominator is \(1-r^2=m\gamma(2-m\gamma)\). The admissible step implies \(m\gamma\le1\), so this denominator is at least \(m\gamma>0\). Canceling the step yields the conservative squared radius \(2d_\theta/(Nm)\). This is a comparison between two finite-particle algorithms, not a claim of a mean-field limit.
