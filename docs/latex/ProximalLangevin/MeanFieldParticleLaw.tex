Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


At each fixed time the tagged particle pair and its reference copy are defined on the same probability space. Both have finite second moments, and the measure-preserving copy has exactly the original mean-field law. Their joint distribution is consequently an admissible finite-cost coupling. The coordinate \(L^2\) estimate bounds its cost and yields the one-time quadratic Wasserstein rate for the constructed particle process.



\section{\texorpdfstring{The unchanged law of a measure-\allowbreak{}preserving copy}{The unchanged law of a measure-preserving copy}}


The copy representative is \(X(\omega_i)\) almost surely. Product evaluation pushes \(Q^{\otimes N}\) to \(Q\). Composition of pushforwards therefore gives \(\mathcal L_{Q^{\otimes N}}(C_iX)=\mathcal L_Q(X)\).



Almost-sure replacement of representatives preserves this law. Thus the reference coordinate in the tagged coupling has precisely the original mean-field distribution.



\section{\texorpdfstring{The coupling of the tagged pair and its reference copy}{The coupling of the tagged pair and its reference copy}}


On the product space couple particle pair \((\Theta^N,X_i^N)\) with the corresponding copy of \((\theta,X)\). Both belong to \(L^2\), so their quadratic coupling cost is finite. Pair reconstruction and Minkowski give

\[W_2(\mathcal L(\Theta^N,X_i^N),\mathcal L(\theta,X))\le\|\Theta^N-\theta\|_2+\|X_i^N-X_i\|_2.\]

The particle pair \((\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N})\) and copied reference have the required marginal laws. Their \(L^2\) moments justify the quadratic cost, whose root is bounded by the sum of coordinate \(L^2\) differences. For latent coordinate \(i\), the same reconstruction estimate in \(L^2\) gives the displayed inequality. Transport, as the infimum over couplings, is no larger. Its finiteness is thus proved before real conversion.




Earlier results used here: \cite[Section~3.17.12]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.13]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.3]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.17.4]{LangevinLean2026ULASC}; \cite[Section~3.1.1]{LangevinLean2026IPLA}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The one-\allowbreak{}time law estimate for the specified particle equation}{The one-time law estimate for the specified particle equation}}


The pair \((\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N})\) has finite second moment, and its reference copy has the law of \(Y_t=(\theta_t,X_t)\). The explicit coupling and coordinate comparison give

\[
W_2\!\left(\mathcal L(\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N}),\mathcal L(\theta_t,X_t)\right)
 \le\frac{C_T}{\sqrt N},\qquad 0\le t\le T.
\]

For every positive \(N\), tag, and time, the conclusion retains the empirical-gradient integral equation, independent copied initialization, and copied latent Brownian input plus scaled parameter Brownian input. Thus the law estimate refers to those specified processes.



At each fixed time the coupling proves finite \(W_2\) and bound \(C_T/\sqrt N\), uniformly over the stated interval. This is convergence of tagged one-time laws. A pathwise strong stochastic equation, expected path-supremum bound, or gradient-flow partial differential equation is not asserted by this result.
