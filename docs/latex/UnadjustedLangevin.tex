Draft mathematical exposition; these notes may contain mistakes.


The development has two convergence routes. Under positive strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient, common-noise coupling gives an explicit quadratic-Wasserstein bound for Gaussian ULA. Under a target log-Sobolev inequality and gradient smoothness, positive-time Gaussian interpolation gives an entropy recurrence from finite initial KL. The supporting arguments construct the laws, moments, density regularity, and endpoint limits used by these conclusions.



The Wasserstein argument compares a numerical chain with a stationary continuous reference under common noise, whereas the entropy argument follows the actual positive-time Gaussian interpolation. Both proofs first derive a local estimate and then iterate a scalar recurrence. Their hypotheses serve different purposes: strong convexity yields deterministic distance contraction, while a log-Sobolev inequality turns Fisher dissipation into entropy contraction without requiring convexity of the potential.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The strongly convex Wasserstein route}{The strongly convex Wasserstein route}}


The imported modules prove Gibbs normalization and score moments, construct independent Brownian coordinates and \(L^2\) Langevin solutions, establish Gibbs stationarity by invariant Metropolis approximation, and assemble a stationary coupling reference for every finite initial coupling. Infimizing the resulting cost gives

\[W_2(P_h^k\mu,\pi)\le(1-mh)^kW_2(\mu,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{hd}\]

under differentiability, \(m>0\) strong convexity, \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient, \(0<h\le2/(m+L)\), and finite initial \(W_2\). The alternative stable branch and the theorem for an implemented iid-Gaussian \(L^2\) recursion are imported as well.



The strongly convex route controls \(W_2(\mu_k,\pi)\) by a geometric initialization term and a step-dependent floor. Its proof first constructs the stationary diffusion, then couples the actual Gaussian chain to that diffusion. Strong convexity is used both for contraction and for the target's Gaussian tails.


\section{\texorpdfstring{The conditional entropy route and verification}{The conditional entropy route and verification}}


The entropy route instead assumes a log-Sobolev inequality and finite initial relative entropy. It derives a discrete affine recurrence from a positive-time interpolation, with endpoint limits supplying the initial value. Convexity of the potential is not required for that argument; the two convergence routes therefore have distinct hypotheses and conclusions.
