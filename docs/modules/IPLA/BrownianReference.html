Langevin integral equations on successive intervals

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. A globally Lipschitz drift gives a unique square-integrable solution of the Langevin integral equation on each finite interval. Starting each interval at the preceding terminal value gives a consistent sequence of segments. Their Brownian increments also define the Euler innovations. Stationarity is an explicit premise of this intermediate comparison result and is proved separately for the reference used in the Gaussian convergence theorem.

This file exports shared Brownian segment and reference constructions and contains no duplicated local declarations. The imported Brownian-reference layer has an explicit all-time Gibbs-law premise; the complete Gaussian ULA development later proves stationarity.

Mathematical statement and proof

On each interval [kh,(k+1)h][kh,(k+1)h][kh,(k+1)h], solve the drift integral equation in L2L^2L2 from the preceding terminal value:

Yk,t−Yk,0=−∫0t∇V(Yk,s)ds+2(Bkh+t−Bkh).Y_{k,t}-Y_{k,0}=-\int_0^t\nabla V(Y_{k,s})ds+\sqrt2\bigl(B_{kh+t}-B_{kh}\bigr).Yk,t​−Yk,0​=−∫0t​∇V(Yk,s​)ds+2​(Bkh+t​−Bkh​).

Lipschitz continuity gives existence, uniqueness, and mean-square continuity, so adjacent segments agree at their common endpoint. The Brownian noise term at the endpoint, normalized by 2h\sqrt{2h}2h​, gives the Euler innovation. Its second moment gives the interval noise bound 2dt\sqrt{2d}\sqrt t2d​t​.

If every reference-time law is the Gibbs law, the score-moment estimate gives ∥∇V(Yk,t)∥L2≤Ld\|\nabla V(Y_{k,t})\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Ld}∥∇V(Yk,t​)∥L2​≤Ld​ uniformly over intervals. These integral, joining, noise, and drift bounds permit the same local comparison at every step. The segment construction alone does not prove the all-time Gibbs-law premise; that stationarity assertion is supplied independently.
