One-dimensional Prékopa–Leindler inequality

References and publication status for this development

Let the two one-dimensional densities be continuous, strictly positive, and have finite positive integrals. Their cumulative distribution functions define an increasing transport map. Its derivative, together with the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality and change of variables, proves the normalized Prékopa–Leindler inequality. Rescaling the densities proves the inequality for arbitrary finite positive masses.

01Differentiability and strict increase of the cumulative integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let fff be continuous, strictly positive, and integrable on R\mathbb RR, with ∫f=1\int f=1∫f=1. Its cumulative distribution function is F(x)=∫(−∞,x]fF(x)=\int_{(-\infty,x]}fF(x)=∫(−∞,x]​f. Splitting the integral at zero gives

F(y)−F(0)=∫0yf(t)dtF(y)-F(0)=\int_0^y f(t)dtF(y)−F(0)=∫0y​f(t)dt

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives F′(x)=f(x)F'(x)=f(x)F′(x)=f(x). Thus the cumulative function is differentiable and continuous. For y>xy>xy>x, the difference F(y)−F(x)=∫xyf(t)dtF(y)-F(x)=\int_x^y f(t)dtF(y)−F(x)=∫xy​f(t)dt is positive because a positive continuous function has positive integral on a nonempty interval. Consequently the cumulative function is strictly increasing. The infinite lower limit is constant in the local differentiation argument.

02The cumulative range and the open unit interval §

Mathematical statement and proof

Integrability gives F(x)→0F(x)\to0F(x)→0 as x→−∞x\to-\inftyx→−∞. For any u∈(0,1)u\in(0,1)u∈(0,1), the endpoint limits supply two finite arguments whose cumulative values lie below and above uuu. 03The increasing transport map and its derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

For cumulative distribution functions F,GF,GF,G, define T=G−1∘FT=G^{-1}\circ FT=G−1∘F. Both have range (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), so G(T(x))=F(x)G(T(x))=F(x)G(T(x))=F(x). Strict monotonicity makes GGG injective, and G′(T(x))=g(T(x))>0G'(T(x))=g(T(x))>0G′(T(x))=g(T(x))>0 permits the inverse-function theorem. The chain rule gives

T′(x)=f(x)g(T(x))>0.T'(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(T(x))}>0.T′(x)=g(T(x))f(x)​>0.

In particular TTT is strictly increasing. The identity G(T(x))=F(x)G(T(x))=F(x)G(T(x))=F(x) equates the cumulative probabilities at corresponding arguments. Its differentiated form is g(T(x))T′(x)=f(x)g(T(x))T'(x)=f(x)g(T(x))T′(x)=f(x). The denominator is positive, so division is justified and yields the derivative and density change-of-variables relation. Both the map and its derivative follow from the density assumptions.

For positive continuous unit-mass densities, F(x)=∫−∞xfF(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xfF(x)=∫−∞x​f and G(y)=∫−∞ygG(y)=\int_{-\infty}^ygG(y)=∫−∞y​g are differentiable with strictly positive derivatives, hence strictly increasing, and their limits give range (0,1)(0,1)(0,1). The cumulative identities and strict monotonicity declarations justify this change of variables on the whole real line.

05The inequality for arbitrary positive finite masses §

Mathematical statement and proof

A strictly positive integrable function on a nonzero measure space has positive integral. Write A=∫f>0A=\int f>0A=∫f>0, B=∫g>0B=\int g>0B=∫g>0, and C=A1−tBt>0C=A^{1-t}B^t>0C=A1−tBt>0. Apply the normalized result to f/Af/Af/A, g/Bg/Bg/B, and h/Ch/Ch/C. The identity log⁡(f/A)=log⁡f−log⁡A\log(f/A)=\log f-\log Alog(f/A)=logf−logA verifies the rescaled interpolation inequality. If hhh is also nonnegative and integrable, the extended conclusion becomes 1≤(∫h)/C1\le(\int h)/C1≤(∫h)/C. Multiplication by CCC gives

exp⁡ ⁣((1−t)log⁡∫f+tlog⁡∫g)≤∫h.\exp\!\left((1-t)\log\int f+t\log\int g\right)\le\int h.exp((1−t)log∫f+tlog∫g)≤∫h.

Thus normalization of fff and ggg changes the interpolation factor by A1−tBtA^{1-t}B^tA1−tBt, and multiplication by that positive finite factor recovers the original statement. The logarithms occur only at positive numbers and therefore also define the formula at endpoint weights. The positivity argument applies to the one-point measure in dimension zero as well.
