Take the product of a finite-cost initial coupling and a probability space carrying the noise. The initial pair is then independent of the full noise sequence, and its two marginals are preserved. The numerical recursion uses only its first initial coordinate and the noise, so its output law depends on the prescribed first marginal and not on the dependence chosen in the coupling.

This file re-exports the shared product realization and its law-identification theorem. A product of an initial coupling and a noise source realizes every needed coupling while fixing a common algorithm output law.

01Independence of initialization and the noise source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use probability space (H×H)×S(H\times H)\times S(H×H)×S with measure c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q. The two initial variables are the coordinates of ccc, and the innovations depend only on SSS. Product independence makes the initial pair independent of the noise. The first recursion applies x↦x−h∇V(x)+2hξkx\mapsto x-h\nabla V(x)+\sqrt{2h}\xi_kx↦x−h∇V(x)+2h​ξk​. Its output is the measurable pushforward of μ0⊗Q\mu_0\otimes Qμ0​⊗Q, so it depends on μ0\mu_0μ0​ and not on coupling ccc. Finite coupling cost and the target second moment give the required L2L^2L2 initial variables.

Writing the initial pair as X0,Y0X_0,Y_0X0​,Y0​ and the noise coordinate as SSS, under c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q the pair (X0,S)(X_0,S)(X0​,S) has law μ0⊗Q\mu_0\otimes Qμ0​⊗Q. The numerical recursion therefore has a fixed law across choices of the initial coupling. The reference starts at Y0Y_0Y0​ and uses the same noise, giving a synchronous comparison for each initial cost. A finite such comparison proves output finiteness before the transport infimum is converted to a real estimate.
