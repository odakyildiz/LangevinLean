01Pair coordinates split the squared norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

The pairing map has parameter coordinates θj\theta_jθj​ and latent coordinates xjx_jxj​. Expanding the Euclidean squared norm over the disjoint index sets gives

∥pair⁡(θ,x)∥2=∥θ∥2+∥x∥2.\|\operatorname{pair}(\theta,x)\|^2=\|\theta\|^2+\|x\|^2.∥pair(θ,x)∥2=∥θ∥2+∥x∥2.

Every output coordinate of pairing is a continuous product projection; the finite coordinate-to-Euclidean identification is continuous as well. Hence (θ,x)↦pair⁡(θ,x)(\theta,x)\mapsto\operatorname{pair}(\theta,x)(θ,x)↦pair(θ,x) is jointly continuous. These elementary identities justify the orthogonal Gaussian domination and later dominated convergence.

Mathematical statement and proof

Integrating the stronger Gaussian domination gives

0<M(θ)≤C(∫e−a∥x∥2dx)e−a∥θ∥2.0<M(\theta)\le C\left(\int e^{-a\|x\|^2}dx\right)e^{-a\|\theta\|^2}.0<M(θ)≤C(∫e−a∥x∥2dx)e−a∥θ∥2.

Monotonicity of ofReal⁡\operatorname{ofReal}ofReal transfers that maximum to the extended marginal predicate. Uniqueness is proved later from Prékopa strong convexity.
