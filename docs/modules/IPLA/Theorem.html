01Combine lifted transport with proved equilibrium concentration §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the state has at least one coordinate. The lifted geometry gives NmNmNm-strong convexity and NLNLNL-smoothness in dimension D=dθ+NdxD=d_\theta+Nd_xD=dθ​+Ndx​. The Gibbs theorem normalizes ΠN\Pi_NΠN​, while square-integrable initialization and Gibbs moments imply finite initial transport. Apply the shared Gaussian ULA theorem at step h=γ/Nh=\gamma/Nh=γ/N, using the supplied Gaussian hypotheses. The step restriction rescales exactly and gives

W2(ρk,ΠN)≤(1−mγ)kW2(μ0,ΠN)+3320Lmdx+dθ/Nγ.W_2(\rho_k,\Pi_N)\le(1-m\gamma)^kW_2(\mu_0,\Pi_N)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma.W2​(ρk​,ΠN​)≤(1−mγ)kW2​(μ0​,ΠN​)+2033​mL​dx​+dθ​/N​γ​.

The ULA output-finiteness result and the proved law identity ensure this is finite actual transport, before any real conversion. The equilibrium concentration theorem gives RMS⁡ΠN(pθ−θ∗)≤2dθ/(Nm)\operatorname{RMS}_{\Pi_N}(p_\theta-\theta_*)\le\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}RMSΠN​​(pθ​−θ∗​)≤2dθ​/(Nm)​. For any coupling of ρk\rho_kρk​ and ΠN\Pi_NΠN​, Minkowski and ∥pθ∥≤1\|p_\theta\|\le1∥pθ​∥≤1 imply the sampled parameter RMS is at most coupling RMS plus that equilibrium radius. Taking the coupling infimum and using finiteness yields

(∫∥pθz−θ∗∥2dρk)1/2≤2dθNm+(1−mγ)kW2(μ0,ΠN)+3320Lmdx+dθNγ.\left(\int\|p_\theta z-\theta_*\|^2d\rho_k\right)^{1/2}\le\sqrt{\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}}+(1-m\gamma)^kW_2(\mu_0,\Pi_N)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+\frac{d_\theta}{N}}\sqrt\gamma.(∫∥pθ​z−θ∗​∥2dρk​)1/2≤Nm2dθ​​​+(1−mγ)kW2​(μ0​,ΠN​)+2033​mL​dx​+Ndθ​​​γ​.

Stationarity, finite transport and concentration are derived here through the shared and particle theorems; they are not additional theorem hypotheses.

The proof first constructs target probability and initial finite transport from the lifted strong-convexity assumptions and L2L^2L2 initialization. Gaussian ULA then gives the state-space transport rate, with step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N, modulus NmNmNm, score constant NLNLNL, and dimension dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​. The law adapter identifies its output with the actual supplied IPLA recursion and proves the output transport distance is finite.

The projection inequality gives Rθ∗(ρk)≤W2(ρk,ΠN)+Rθ∗(ΠN)R_{\theta_*}(\rho_k)\le W_2(\rho_k,\Pi_N)+R_{\theta_*}(\Pi_N)Rθ∗​​(ρk​)≤W2​(ρk​,ΠN​)+Rθ∗​​(ΠN​); equilibrium concentration supplies the second term. The facts houtput and hmoment ensure finite values before projected RMS is rewritten as a square-root integral. Finally, the exact particle/time scaling simplifies the transport terms. Thus normalization, stationary reference and parameter concentration are derived inputs, while joint strong convexity, smoothness and the Gaussian innovation laws remain the stated assumptions.

02Include every zero-dimensional case in the public theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

The public theorem splits on whether the state coordinate index type is nonempty. In the nonempty case, instantiate the preceding theorem directly, retaining arbitrary independent dθ,dxd_\theta,d_xdθ​,dx​. If the index type is empty, its parameter part forces dθ=0d_\theta=0dθ​=0; since N>0N>0N>0, a positive dxd_xdx​ would supply a latent coordinate, so dx=0d_x=0dx​=0 as well. Normalize the Gibbs target by the same strong-convexity theorem and apply the singleton-state bound, where every error, transport distance and dimension term vanishes. This completes the displayed estimate in all dimensions. Existence and uniqueness of a permissible θ∗\theta_*θ∗​ are established separately by existsUnique_marginalMaximizer, so the maximizer binder does not demand an unproved existence assumption.
