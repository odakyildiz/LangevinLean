How the Lean proof follows this argument

laws_eq_ulaLawStep states laws P U γ L hdiff hLip initial noise k = (ULA.ulaLawStep (potential U) (γ / N))^[k] (P.map initial). Here ULA.ulaLawStep U h μ is defined in the Langevin library as (μ.prod (stdGaussian H)).map (fun p ↦ malaProposal U h p.1 p.2), where malaProposal U h x ξ = x - h • gradient U x + Real.sqrt (2 * h) • ξ is the Euler map FFF and stdGaussian H is Mathlib's standard Gaussian measure N(0,ID)\mathcal N(0,I_D)N(0,ID​); f^[k] denotes the kkk-fold iterate of a map.

The proof is one application of the library theorem ULA.hilbert_lpChain_law_eq_ulaLawStep, which proves the induction above for any square-integrable chain whose initial state is independent of the innovation path and whose innovation vectors are independent and standard Gaussian. Its arguments are:

  • the Lipschitz proof potential_gradient_lipschitz hdiff hLip with constant (N : ℝ≥0) * L;
  • hGaussian.independentInitial for the independence of the initial state and the path;
  • ULA.gaussianInnovations_vectorIndependent and ULA.gaussianInnovations_vectorLaw, which derive the independence of the vectors and their law stdGaussian from the scalar fields hGaussian.coordinateLaw and hGaussian.independent (the first paragraph of the proof); hW supplies the almost everywhere measurability of each innovation via Lp.aestronglyMeasurable.
