References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

The mathematical conclusion rests on a chain of derived inputs: normalized target, finite moments, a stationary continuous reference, exact Gaussian algorithm laws, marginal strong convexity and projected concentration. An audit of the public theorem checks that these results are connected through the stated primitive assumptions. It adds no new analytic inequality.

This executable audit imports the public IPLA library, checks the exact public theorem type, and prints axiom dependencies of the final theorem and every major mathematical bridge. It introduces no mathematical declarations.

Mathematical statement and proof

Run lake env lean IPLA/Check.lean. The #check output exposes the complete type of IPLA.theorem1; the #print axioms commands expose transitive kernel dependencies. The audit includes the Gaussian ULA endpoint, constructed Brownian source and stationary reference, Gibbs normalization and moments, finite transport, the Gaussian algorithm-law identity, exact parameter marginal, Prékopa theorem, marginal strong convexity, unique maximizer, sharp concentration, independent dimensions and scaling, and the zero-dimensional case. The permitted foundational dependencies are propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. An explicit theorem hypothesis such as Gaussian independence is part of its statement; an unproved additional axiom would instead appear in this audit.

Each #check command asks Lean to elaborate the complete theorem type, retaining every displayed assumption. Each #print axioms command reports the transitive foundational dependencies of that theorem. Compile this module after the library build to inspect that report; these commands check the existing proofs and do not supply new mathematical hypotheses or conclusions.

The distinction relevant to the mathematical statement is between an explicit hypothesis and an unproved assertion. Gaussian independence and positive curvature are hypotheses of the theorem; normalization and concentration are conclusions proved from them. Checking the dependency chain ensures that an intermediate conditional reference theorem has not been mistaken for a completed construction. It does not enlarge the scope of any stated result or remove any of its assumptions.
