Equilibrium concentration of the parameter marginal

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

The parameter Gibbs potential is NΦN\PhiNΦ. Since its curvature is proportional to particle count, the continuous strongly convex Gibbs concentration inequality gives a parameter second moment inversely proportional to that count. The exact density is proportional to M(θ)N=e−NΦ(θ)M(\theta)^N=e^{-N\Phi(\theta)}M(θ)N=e−NΦ(θ); its potential has curvature NmNmNm in dimension dθd_\thetadθ​. The resulting equilibrium root mean square radius is 2dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}2dθ​/(Nm)​. This is the available concentration estimate with factor two, derived without a separate concentration assumption.

Statement and proof

01The scaled marginal potential and its Gibbs measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Multiply the strong-convexity inequality for Φ\PhiΦ by NNN. The affine terms and quadratic term are all multiplied by NNN. Thus NΦN\PhiNΦ has strong-convexity constant NmNmNm. Since M>0M>0M>0, the exponential identity is

e−NΦ(θ)=eNlog⁡M(θ)=M(θ)N.e^{-N\Phi(\theta)}=e^{N\log M(\theta)}=M(\theta)^N.e−NΦ(θ)=eNlogM(θ)=M(θ)N.

The equality e−NΦ=MNe^{-N\Phi}=M^Ne−NΦ=MN holds at every parameter because MMM is positive and finite. The unnormalized measures therefore coincide, as do their normalized measures. The Gibbs concentration theorem can consequently be applied to the parameter target with curvature NmNmNm.

The finite-real representation of the original extended marginal identifies this weight exactly with the one defining marginalTarget. Its normalized law is therefore the shared Gibbs measure of NΦN\PhiNΦ.

Formal correspondence: scaled_marginalPotential_strongConvex : Multiplying the actual marginal potential by N multiplies its proved strong-convexity constant by N . marginalTarget_eq_gibbsMeasure : The normalized powered marginal density is exactly the Gibbs law of the proved N -scaled negative log marginal.

02The parameter second-moment estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

A marginal-likelihood maximizer θ∗\theta_*θ∗​ minimizes Φ\PhiΦ and therefore NΦN\PhiNΦ. The continuous-potential Gibbs estimate applies without requiring differentiability of Φ\PhiΦ and yields

∫∥θ−θ∗∥2dπNθ(θ)≤2dθNm.\int\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2d\pi_N^\theta(\theta)\le\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}.∫∥θ−θ∗​∥2dπNθ​(θ)≤Nm2dθ​​.

Its assumptions hold for NΦN\PhiNΦ by continuity, the proved NmNmNm-strong convexity, and the minimizing-point property. This estimate has factor two and needs only these assumptions. The dimension is dθd_\thetadθ​ because the latent variables have already been integrated out. Their dimension does not enter this equilibrium parameter moment.

Formal correspondence: marginalTarget_secondMoment_le : Exact finite second-moment bound about any parameter maximizing the actual latent marginal. Its existence and uniqueness were proved separately.

03The projected moment under the lifted Gibbs law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The projection equality pθ#ΠN=πNθp_{\theta\#}\Pi_N=\pi_N^\thetapθ#​ΠN​=πNθ​ and the pushforward integral formula give

∫∥pθz−θ∗∥2dΠN(z)≤2dθNm.\int\|p_\theta z-\theta_*\|^2d\Pi_N(z)\le\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}.∫∥pθ​z−θ∗​∥2dΠN​(z)≤Nm2dθ​​.

The projected variable belongs to L2L^2L2, and subtracting θ∗\theta_*θ∗​ preserves square integrability. The right-hand moment bound is nonnegative, so taking square roots gives

RMS⁡ΠN(pθ−θ∗)≤2dθ/(Nm).\operatorname{RMS}_{\Pi_N}(p_\theta-\theta_*)\le\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}.RMSΠN​​(pθ​−θ∗​)≤2dθ​/(Nm)​.

For clarity, apply the pushforward identity to g(θ)=∥θ−θ∗∥2g(\theta)=\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2g(θ)=∥θ−θ∗​∥2. It states ∫g(pθz)dΠN=∫g(θ)dπNθ\int g(p_\theta z)d\Pi_N=\int g(\theta)d\pi_N^\theta∫g(pθ​z)dΠN​=∫g(θ)dπNθ​ and thus transfers the same moment estimate to the full-state Gibbs law without bounding latent deviations. Minkowski’s inequality can then add this equilibrium radius to a full-state coupling error. Joint differentiability and positive strong convexity suffice; the gradient-Lipschitz assumption is not used in this concentration step.

Powering the marginal density by NNN multiplies its negative log potential by NNN, so its modulus becomes NmNmNm. The actual marginal maximizer minimizes that potential. The dilation concentration theorem used in this module bounds its parameter second moment by 2dθ/(Nm)2d_\theta/(Nm)2dθ​/(Nm), and the exact parameter pushforward identity gives the same moment under the lifted Gibbs law. The final square-root estimate uses the proved finite second moment to interpret projected L2L^2L2 error as an ordinary integral. The stronger dθ/(Nm)d_\theta/(Nm)dθ​/(Nm) constant is separately proved in the proximal sharp-concentration extension.

Formal correspondence: gibbsTarget_parameter_secondMoment_le : Transfer the proved marginal moment bound to the actual scaled IPLA Gibbs measure using the exact parameter-marginal identity. gibbsTarget_parameter_concentration : The equilibrium concentration input in IPLA Theorem 1 is a proved theorem of the original joint assumptions. No gradient-Lipschitz or marginal assumption is needed for this statistical term.
