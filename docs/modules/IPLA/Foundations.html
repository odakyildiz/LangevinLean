Normalization, moments and score moments of the lifted target

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

Gaussian domination of a strongly convex Gibbs density implies a finite positive partition function and finite second moments. The gradient regularity also bounds the equilibrium squared score. Apply these facts to VNV_NVN​, whose differentiability and NmNmNm-strong convexity follow from the assumptions on UUU. The resulting moments justify the transport and drift-integral estimates.

01Normalization and the second moment of the Gibbs target §

Mathematical statement and proof

The target is ΠN(dz)=ZN−1e−VN(z) dz\Pi_N(dz)=Z_N^{-1}e^{-V_N(z)}\,dzΠN​(dz)=ZN−1​e−VN​(z)dz. Assume N>0N>0N>0 and m>0m>0m>0, so that Nm>0Nm>0Nm>0. Differentiability and strong convexity imply a quadratic lower bound. Gaussian integrability then gives 0<ZN<∞0<Z_N<\infty0<ZN​<∞, and normalization gives ΠN(RD)=1\Pi_N(\mathbb R^D)=1ΠN​(RD)=1. The same domination proves ∫∥z∥2dΠN<∞\int\|z\|^2d\Pi_N<\infty∫∥z∥2dΠN​<∞, equivalently square integrability of the identity in L2(ΠN)L^2(\Pi_N)L2(ΠN​).

In detail, completing the affine part of the strong-convexity lower bound gives VN(z)≥a∥z∥2−bV_N(z)\ge a\|z\|^2-bVN​(z)≥a∥z∥2−b with a>0a>0a>0. The density is consequently at most ebe−a∥z∥2e^b e^{-a\|z\|^2}ebe−a∥z∥2. Multiplication by ∥z∥2\|z\|^2∥z∥2 preserves integrability of this Gaussian majorant. The strictly positive exponential has positive integral under Euclidean volume, so ZN>0Z_N>0ZN​>0. Division by this positive finite integral proves both the probability property and the claimed second moment.

The geometry has already proved that VNV_NVN​ is differentiable and NmNmNm-strongly convex. Its quadratic lower bound dominates e−VNe^{-V_N}e−VN​ and its norm-weighted powers by integrable Gaussian functions, giving a finite positive partition and finite second moment. The first declaration identifies the named target with the shared normalized Gibbs definition; the next two apply the normalization and moment theorems with modulus Nm>0Nm>0Nm>0. Thus target mass one and finite moments are consequences of the original joint assumptions.

Formal correspondence: gibbsTarget_eq_gibbsMeasure : IPLA's target is exactly the general normalized Gibbs density. gibbsTarget_isProbability : Normalization of the lifted target follows from strong convexity of the original joint potential; no separate Gibbs normalization hypothesis. gibbsTarget_memLp : The lifted target has a finite second moment from the original joint strong-convexity assumption. Gradient Lipschitz continuity is not needed.

02Square integrability from finite quadratic transport §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let XXX be a measurable initialization satisfying W2(X#P,ΠN)<∞W_2(X_\#P,\Pi_N)<\inftyW2​(X#​P,ΠN​)<∞. The Gibbs target has a finite second moment. Choose a coupling with finite quadratic cost and integrate ∥x∥2≤2∥x−y∥2+2∥y∥2\|x\|^2\le2\|x-y\|^2+2\|y\|^2∥x∥2≤2∥x−y∥2+2∥y∥2. Its first marginal gives the initial second moment, while its second marginal gives the target moment. Both terms on the right have finite integral, hence X∈L2(P)X\in L^2(P)X∈L2(P).

Finiteness of the Wasserstein distance guarantees a finite-cost coupling; attainment of the infimum is unnecessary. This argument permits finite initial transport distance to replace a separate initial second-moment assumption.

Formal correspondence: initial_memLp_of_finite_transport : An actual measurable initialization at finite W₂ from the lifted Gibbs measure automatically belongs to L². This justifies the L² representation used by the main analytic theorem without an independent moment assumption.

03The lifted Gibbs score second moment §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a strongly convex potential with ℓ\ellℓ-Lipschitz gradient, the Gibbs score estimate is ∫∥∇V∥2dπ≤ℓdim⁡H\int\|\nabla V\|^2d\pi\le\ell\dim H∫∥∇V∥2dπ≤ℓdimH. The substitutions V=VNV=V_NV=VN​, ℓ=NL\ell=NLℓ=NL, and dim⁡H=dθ+Ndx\dim H=d_\theta+Nd_xdimH=dθ​+Ndx​ therefore give

∫∥∇VN(z)∥2dΠN(z)≤NL(dθ+Ndx).\int\|\nabla V_N(z)\|^2d\Pi_N(z)\le NL(d_\theta+Nd_x).∫∥∇VN​(z)∥2dΠN​(z)≤NL(dθ​+Ndx​).

The pair-potential assumptions imply all regularity hypotheses for this application. Since the gradient includes the parameter and every particle coordinate, its squared moment involves the full-state dimension. In the abbreviation NLDNL DNLD, the dimension is D=dθ+NdxD=d_\theta+Nd_xD=dθ​+Ndx​. The later drift-integral estimate combines this moment with a step of length γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N. The parameter concentration estimate is a separate result and involves only the parameter dimension. No Hessian assumption or additional score-moment assumption is used.

Finite initial Wasserstein distance to a target with finite second moment implies finite initial second moment via a finite-cost coupling and ∥X∥2≤2∥X−Y∥2+2∥Y∥2\|X\|^2\le2\|X-Y\|^2+2\|Y\|^2∥X∥2≤2∥X−Y∥2+2∥Y∥2. The next theorem applies the shared Gibbs-score estimate to the actual lifted score with constant NLNLNL, obtaining EΠN∥∇VN∥2≤NL(dθ+Ndx).\mathbb E_{\Pi_N}\|\nabla V_N\|^2\le NL(d_\theta+Nd_x).EΠN​​∥∇VN​∥2≤NL(dθ​+Ndx​). The dimension substitution uses finrank_state; a separate Hessian or score-moment premise is unnecessary.

Formal correspondence: gibbsTarget_score_moment : The lifted Gibbs score moment also follows from the original joint potential assumptions; no separate score hypothesis or Hessian is introduced.
