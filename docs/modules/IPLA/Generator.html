Physical-time generator and Gibbs integration identity

For scaled state zzz, physical IPLA time is NNN times the ordinary lifted Langevin time. The generator is consequently N−1(Δ−∇VN⋅∇)N^{-1}(\Delta-\nabla V_N\cdot\nabla)N−1(Δ−∇VN​⋅∇).

01Identify the generator on a twice differentiable test function §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define ANφ=N−1LVNφ\mathcal A_N\varphi=N^{-1}\mathcal L_{V_N}\varphiAN​φ=N−1LVN​​φ, where LV\mathcal L_VLV​ is the shared Langevin generator. For C2C^2C2 test functions the shared coordinate definition equals the Laplacian minus the gradient inner product, hence

ANφ(z)=1N(Δφ(z)−⟨∇VN(z),∇φ(z)⟩).\mathcal A_N\varphi(z)=\frac1N\left(\Delta\varphi(z)-\langle\nabla V_N(z),\nabla\varphi(z)\rangle\right).AN​φ(z)=N1​(Δφ(z)−⟨∇VN​(z),∇φ(z)⟩).

This is a direct identity of operators on the scaled state; no invariant-measure conclusion is built into its definition.

02Derive integrability and infinitesimal invariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

For compactly supported C2C^2C2 φ\varphiφ, the shared Gibbs integration-by-parts theorem proves that LVNφ\mathcal L_{V_N}\varphiLVN​​φ is integrable and has Gibbs integral zero. Its potential hypotheses follow from differentiability and Lipschitzness of the joint gradient via the geometry module. Multiplication by the constant N−1N^{-1}N−1 preserves integrability and gives

∫ANφ dΠN=1N∫LVNφ dΠN=0.\int\mathcal A_N\varphi\,d\Pi_N=\frac1N\int\mathcal L_{V_N}\varphi\,d\Pi_N=0.∫AN​φdΠN​=N1​∫LVN​​φdΠN​=0.

The theorem also returns the probability property, derived from positive strong convexity. Explicit integrability rules out a zero caused by the default integral value of a nonintegrable function. Full time-evolution stationarity is supplied separately by the shared Langevin construction.

For a C2C^2C2 compactly supported test function, the shared Gibbs integration identity gives ∫[Δφ−⟨∇VN,∇φ⟩]dΠN=0\int[\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla V_N,\nabla\varphi\rangle]d\Pi_N=0∫[Δφ−⟨∇VN​,∇φ⟩]dΠN​=0. Physical IPLA time multiplies this generator by N−1N^{-1}N−1, so linearity of the integral preserves zero. The theorem also proves integrability of the generator before asserting this identity, using the compact support and the proved lifted score regularity. This distinguishes an actual invariance calculation from a formal integral expression whose integrability has not been justified.
