Assumptions and scope of the entropy arguments

References and publication status for this development

Unpublished proof

The IPLA LSI proof presented here is unpublished. It applies the ULA entropy theorem to the joint particle Gibbs measure, proves the joint entropy estimate and its normalization by particle number, and bounds the parameter-marginal entropy by data processing. The papers below provide the ULA and IPLA results used in the argument.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices
  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2

The conditional entropy theorem assumes interpolation densities, their weak equations, and the differentiability and integrability needed for entropy calculus. The Gaussian interpolation theorem constructs these properties under its stated smoothness, logarithmic Sobolev, step, and initial-entropy hypotheses. The assumption check records the different scopes and confirms that finiteness and projection are used with their hypotheses.

Mathematical statement and proof

For the conditional entropy result, smoothness and a lifted logarithmic Sobolev inequality are supplemented by explicit density and weak-equation regularity. The recurrence follows from those data, but that result alone does not prove their existence. The separate Gaussian interpolation construction supplies them for the unconditional theorem. The dependency check retains this distinction, including the finite-divergence requirement before real conversion and the same-map projection requirement.

Run lake env lean IPLA/LSI/Check.lean. The audited chain covers positive-denominator scaling, the actual Gaussian algorithm-law identity, finite KL, derived one-step and iterated bounds, normalized and NαN\alphaNα-LSI specializations, and parameter data processing, together with the four finite-initial-KL theorems of UnconditionalTheorem.lean. The conditional theorems among them assume an LSI of the actual lifted target and the displayed RegularInterpolationData; the finite-initial-KL theorems assume only smoothness, the lifted-target LSI, the step restriction, and finite initial joint KL. The audit checks that every listed theorem uses only Lean's usual foundational axioms and no added mathematical axiom.

Each #print axioms command reports the transitive foundational dependencies of that theorem. Compile this module after the library build to inspect that report; these commands check the existing proofs and do not supply new mathematical hypotheses or conclusions.
