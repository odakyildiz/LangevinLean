The Wasserstein distance is the infimum of quadratic coupling costs. An estimate in terms of an arbitrary initial coupling therefore gives an estimate in terms of the initial Wasserstein distance, provided the comparison process exists for each finite-cost coupling and the numerical output law remains the same. No optimal coupling is required.

This module re-exports the shared finite-coupling and reference-family interface. It contains no separate definitions or proofs; the imported ULA module develops the passage from a coupled initial RMS to the genuine initial W2W_2W2​ term.

01The infimum over finite-cost initial couplings §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each finite-cost coupling ccc of μ0\mu_0μ0​ and π\piπ, assume an L2L^2L2 realization of the initial pair and a common independent Euler noise. Suppose its initial L2L^2L2 discrepancy is bounded by cost⁡(c)\operatorname{cost}(c)cost(c) and all realizations have the same numerical output law μk\mu_kμk​. The synchronous estimate gives W2(μk,π)≤qkcost⁡(c)+bW_2(\mu_k,\pi)\le q^k\operatorname{cost}(c)+bW2​(μk​,π)≤qkcost(c)+b for every ccc. Taking the infimum gives upper bound qkW2(μ0,π)+bq^kW_2(\mu_0,\pi)+bqkW2​(μ0​,π)+b.

Indeed, for each ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0 choose an initial coupling of cost at most W2(μ0,π)+εW_2(\mu_0,\pi)+\varepsilonW2​(μ0​,π)+ε. The same estimate applies, and the left side and additive term are independent of the choice. Letting ε\varepsilonε decrease to zero proves the claim. A single finite-cost coupling already proves output finiteness before real-valued transport is used. The Gaussian product construction establishes these assumed realizations and their common numerical marginal.
