Finite-dimensional Prékopa–Leindler with Gaussian tails

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2

The dimension induction requires a class of functions stable under marginalization. Continuous positive functions with Gaussian upper tails have exactly the needed properties: every coordinate slice is integrable, its integral varies continuously, and the resulting marginal again has Gaussian tails. This closes the induction without assuming marginal continuity or integrability independently.

Positive continuous weights dominated by an integrable Gaussian form a class closed under integrating out one coordinate. This supplies all continuity and integrability facts needed to iterate the one-dimensional transport proof.

01Gaussian domination proves integrability and positive integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write Coordinates(n)=Rn\mathrm{Coordinates}(n)=\mathbb R^nCoordinates(n)=Rn. A Gaussian weight satisfies continuity, strict positivity, and f(x)≤Ce−a∑ixi2f(x)\le Ce^{-a\sum_i x_i^2}f(x)≤Ce−a∑i​xi2​, with C,a>0C,a>0C,a>0. The Euclidean Gaussian integral is finite, and the coordinate identification preserves volume, so domination proves f∈L1f\in L^1f∈L1. Positivity on the whole nonzero volume space proves ∫f>0\int f>0∫f>0. Multiplying by c>0c>0c>0 preserves these properties and changes the envelope constant to cCcCcC.

Formal correspondence: GaussianWeight : The class of positive continuous weights with a Gaussian upper bound.

A Gaussian envelope with coefficient a>0a>0a>0 has finite integral on Rn\mathbb R^nRn, as follows by the product of one-dimensional Gaussian integrals. Since the function is nonnegative, domination proves its absolute integrability. Strict positivity then makes its integral positive. Multiplication by a positive scalar preserves all three properties and changes only the envelope constant, a fact needed when strong convexity supplies an exponential prefactor.

02A coordinate slice and its marginal are integrable and continuous §

Mathematical statement and proof

The insertion map (s,x)↦(s,x1,…,xn)(s,x)\mapsto(s,x_1,\ldots,x_n)(s,x)↦(s,x1​,…,xn​) is continuous coordinate by coordinate. Define f1(x)=∫Rf(s,x)dsf_1(x)=\int_\mathbb R f(s,x)dsf1​(x)=∫R​f(s,x)ds. Since ∑i(s,x)i2=s2+∑ixi2≥s2\sum_i(s,x)_i^2=s^2+\sum_i x_i^2\ge s^2∑i​(s,x)i2​=s2+∑i​xi2​≥s2, every slice is bounded by Ce−as2Ce^{-as^2}Ce−as2, which is integrable independently of xxx. Slice continuity supplies measurability and domination proves slice integrability. If xj→xx_j\to xxj​→x, joint continuity gives pointwise convergence in sss, and the same Gaussian bound allows dominated convergence. Hence f1f_1f1​ is continuous.

Write a point in Rn+1\mathbb R^{n+1}Rn+1 as cons⁡(z,x)\operatorname{cons}(z,x)cons(z,x), where z∈Rz\in\mathbb Rz∈R is the coordinate being integrated. The coordinate-insertion continuity theorem checks its first and remaining coordinates separately. A Gaussian weight has constants C,a>0C,a>0C,a>0 satisfying

0<f(cons⁡(z,x))≤Cexp⁡ ⁣[−a(z2+∑i=1nxi2)]≤Ce−az2.0<f(\operatorname{cons}(z,x))\le C\exp\!\left[-a\left(z^2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2\right)\right]\le C e^{-az^2}.0<f(cons(z,x))≤Cexp[−a(z2+i=1∑n​xi2​)]≤Ce−az2.

The last inequality drops a nonnegative sum of squares, so the majorant is independent of xxx. It is integrable in zzz by the one-dimensional Gaussian integral theorem. The declaration slice_integrable uses positivity to identify the absolute value of the slice with the slice itself, continuity to obtain measurability, and this majorant to prove integrability.

Now let xm→xx_m\to xxm​→x. For every fixed zzz, continuity of fff and of coordinate insertion gives f(cons⁡(z,xm))→f(cons⁡(z,x))f(\operatorname{cons}(z,x_m))\to f(\operatorname{cons}(z,x))f(cons(z,xm​))→f(cons(z,x)). The same Gaussian majorant controls every term and the limit. Dominated convergence therefore gives convergence of their real integrals, proving continuity of the first marginal. In the Lean invocation of continuous_of_dominated, the four obligations are slice measurability, the uniform almost-everywhere bound, integrability of that bound, and pointwise continuity in the remaining coordinates. Those are all established before the induction later treats the marginal as another Gaussian weight.

Formal correspondence: continuous_cons : Continuity of inserting the first real coordinate. firstMarginal : Integrate out the first coordinate of a weight.

The crucial majorant is independent of the remaining coordinates. Therefore a sequence of such coordinates changes only the integrand, not the integrable bound used in dominated convergence. Each slice is positive and continuous in the integrated coordinate, so its integral is positive and finite. The argument proves joint analytic control needed to regard the first marginal as a function in the same class.

03Coordinate integration preserves Gaussian tails and total integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Retaining the full Gaussian envelope and factoring its exponent gives

f1(x)≤C(∫Re−as2ds)e−a∑ixi2.f_1(x)\le C\left(\int_\mathbb R e^{-as^2}ds\right)e^{-a\sum_i x_i^2}.f1​(x)≤C(∫R​e−as2ds)e−a∑i​xi2​.

The new constant is positive and finite, and integrating the positive slice makes f1f_1f1​ positive everywhere. Together with continuity, this proves that f1f_1f1​ is again a Gaussian weight. The coordinate splitting equivalence preserves product volume. Its integral formula and Fubini, justified by the Gaussian integrability already proved, then show ∫f1=∫f\int f_1=\int f∫f1​=∫f.

Retaining the remaining-coordinate Gaussian factor gives a sharper conclusion than slice integrability alone. The new envelope constant is the old constant multiplied by ∫e−as2ds\int e^{-as^2}ds∫e−as2ds, while the decay coefficient aaa is unchanged. Fubini then identifies the marginal's total integral with the original integral because Gaussian domination has already established absolute integrability. These facts make repeated coordinate integration legitimate.

04Induct on the dimension §

Mathematical statement and proof

For n=0n=0n=0, the coordinate space is a singleton with volume one; each integral equals its function value and the result is the pointwise premise. For dimension n+1n+1n+1, fix remaining-coordinate vectors x,yx,yx,y. The one-dimensional theorem applied to the slices s↦f(s,x)s\mapsto f(s,x)s↦f(s,x), r↦g(r,y)r\mapsto g(r,y)r↦g(r,y), and u↦h(u,(1−t)x+ty)u\mapsto h(u,(1-t)x+ty)u↦h(u,(1−t)x+ty) gives the pointwise Prékopa inequality for f1,g1,h1f_1,g_1,h_1f1​,g1​,h1​. These marginals are Gaussian weights, so the induction hypothesis applies in dimension nnn. Replace their integrals by those of the original weights to obtain

(∫f)1−t(∫g)t≤∫h.\left(\int f\right)^{1-t}\left(\int g\right)^t\le\int h.(∫f)1−t(∫g)t≤∫h.

The formal statement writes positive powers using exponentials and logarithms, including t=0t=0t=0 and t=1t=1t=1.

The induction splits Rd+1\mathbb R^{d+1}Rd+1 into its first coordinate and ddd remaining coordinates. For fixed remaining coordinates, apply the proved one-dimensional inequality to the first-coordinate slices. Their integrals define marginal functions that satisfy the same interpolation inequality in ddd dimensions. Gaussian domination proves slice integrability, continuity of those marginal functions by dominated convergence, and Gaussian tails for them. The induction hypothesis applies, and Fubini identifies the iterated integrals with the original total integrals. Dimension zero is the one-point base case.

Formal correspondence: prekopa_leindler : The finite-dimensional Prékopa--Leindler inequality. The induction uses the proved real-line transport theorem and integrates one coordinate at a time.

At the induction step the original pointwise hypothesis holds for every pair of first coordinates and every pair of remaining-coordinate vectors. Fixing the latter pair gives exactly the one-dimensional interpolation premise. Its conclusion is the corresponding premise for the marginal functions. Applying the induction hypothesis integrates the remaining coordinates, and the total-integral identities replace each iterated integral by its original multidimensional value.
