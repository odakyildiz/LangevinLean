Strong convexity of the negative log marginal likelihood

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

Joint strong convexity gives a parameter quadratic term in the pointwise interpolation inequality. Applying Prékopa–Leindler to the latent variables retains this term, so −log⁡M-\log M−logM is strongly convex. More precisely, define M(θ)=∫e−U(θ,x)dxM(\theta)=\int e^{-U(\theta,x)}dxM(θ)=∫e−U(θ,x)dx and Φ(θ)=−log⁡M(θ)\Phi(\theta)=-\log M(\theta)Φ(θ)=−logM(θ). The positive finite marginal and the finite-dimensional integral inequality show that Φ\PhiΦ has curvature mmm. This also proves uniqueness of the marginal-likelihood maximizer.

01Definition and minimizing points of the marginal potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

The latent integral MMM is positive, finite, and continuous. Therefore Φ=−log⁡M\Phi=-\log MΦ=−logM is real-valued and continuous, since the logarithm is continuous away from zero. If θ∗\theta_*θ∗​ maximizes the extended marginal likelihood, its finite real representation gives Φ(θ∗)≤Φ(θ)\Phi(\theta_*)\le\Phi(\theta)Φ(θ∗​)≤Φ(θ) for every θ\thetaθ by monotonicity of the logarithm and negation.

The condition 0<M(θ)<∞0<M(\theta)<\infty0<M(θ)<∞ holds at every parameter. Thus if θ∗\theta_*θ∗​ maximizes MMM, one has log⁡M(θ)≤log⁡M(θ∗)\log M(\theta)\le\log M(\theta_*)logM(θ)≤logM(θ∗​), which is equivalent to global minimality of Φ\PhiΦ. This argument compares function values and does not differentiate through the latent integral.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

marginalIntegral is the ordinary latent integral and marginalPotential its negative logarithm. marginalIntegral_pos uses integral_exp_pos with the proved integrable marginal weight. marginalPotential_continuous composes continuity of the integral with the logarithm, using strict positivity to exclude its singular point. marginalMaximizer_minimizes_potential first rewrites the extended marginal densities as finite ordinary integrals, then applies monotonicity of logarithm and reverses the sign. This makes the statistical maximizer exactly a minimizer of the marginal potential.

Formal correspondence: marginalIntegral : The real latent partition. Positivity and finiteness were proved from the joint assumptions in MarginalConcentration.lean . marginalPotential : The actual marginal negative log-likelihood, with the observations fixed. marginalMaximizer_minimizes_potential : The statistical marginal maximizer minimizes the actual negative log-likelihood. All real conversions use proved positive finite partitions.

02Gaussian-dominated weights in product coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let fθ(x)=e−U(θ,x)f_\theta(x)=e^{-U(\theta,x)}fθ​(x)=e−U(θ,x) in ordinary latent product coordinates. The Gaussian bound gives fθ(x)≤Ce−m∥x∥2/4f_\theta(x)\le Ce^{-m\|x\|^2/4}fθ​(x)≤Ce−m∥x∥2/4; continuity and strict positivity therefore put each slice in the class required by Prékopa–Leindler. The Euclidean coordinate identification preserves volume, so its integral is M(θ)M(\theta)M(θ). Pairing respects differences and affine combinations:

pair⁡(θ,x)−pair⁡(θ′,y)=pair⁡(θ−θ′,x−y),\operatorname{pair}(\theta,x)-\operatorname{pair}(\theta',y)=\operatorname{pair}(\theta-\theta',x-y),pair(θ,x)−pair(θ′,y)=pair(θ−θ′,x−y),pair⁡(aθ+bθ′,ax+by)=apair⁡(θ,x)+bpair⁡(θ′,y).\operatorname{pair}(a\theta+b\theta',ax+by)=a\operatorname{pair}(\theta,x)+b\operatorname{pair}(\theta',y).pair(aθ+bθ′,ax+by)=apair(θ,x)+bpair(θ′,y).

Consequently the joint strong-convexity inequality can be applied to (θ,x)(\theta,x)(θ,x) and (θ′,y)(\theta',y)(θ′,y). Their squared distance separates into parameter and latent terms. Discarding only the nonnegative latent term retains the full parameter curvature in the integral inequality.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

latentWeight expresses the latent Gibbs weight in ordinary product coordinates. latentWeight_gaussian supplies continuity, positivity and Gaussian domination, with marginal_weight_uniform_bound supplying the final estimate. integral_latentWeight uses the measure-preserving coordinate equivalence PiLp.volume_preserving_toLp to identify its integral with marginalIntegral. The identities pair_sub and pair_affine are checked coordinatewise by separating the parameter and latent cases. They justify applying joint strong convexity at the interpolated pair.

Formal correspondence: latentWeight : A latent slice written in ordinary product coordinates for applying the finite-dimensional Prékopa--Leindler theorem.

03Prékopa–Leindler with the parameter quadratic term §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 with a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1, and set θr=aθ+bθ′\theta_r=a\theta+b\theta'θr​=aθ+bθ′ and c=(mab/2)∥θ−θ′∥2c=(mab/2)\|\theta-\theta'\|^2c=(mab/2)∥θ−θ′∥2. Strong convexity of UUU and the orthogonal pair norm imply

aU(θ,x)+bU(θ′,y)≥U(θr,ax+by)+c,aU(\theta,x)+bU(\theta',y)\ge U(\theta_r,ax+by)+c,aU(θ,x)+bU(θ′,y)≥U(θr​,ax+by)+c,

Here the additional term (mab/2)∥x−y∥2(mab/2)\|x-y\|^2(mab/2)∥x−y∥2 has been discarded because it is nonnegative. Exponentiation gives the Prékopa premise with third weight e−cfθre^{-c}f_{\theta_r}e−cfθr​​, which is a positive constant multiple of a Gaussian-dominated slice. The integral inequality yields M(θ)aM(θ′)b≤e−cM(θr)M(\theta)^aM(\theta')^b\le e^{-c}M(\theta_r)M(θ)aM(θ′)b≤e−cM(θr​). Positivity permits logarithms and negation, giving

Φ(aθ+bθ′)≤aΦ(θ)+bΦ(θ′)−mab2∥θ−θ′∥2.\Phi(a\theta+b\theta')\le a\Phi(\theta)+b\Phi(\theta')-\frac{mab}{2}\|\theta-\theta'\|^2.Φ(aθ+bθ′)≤aΦ(θ)+bΦ(θ′)−2mab​∥θ−θ′∥2.

For the alternative parameter notation θr=aθ+bϑ\theta_r=a\theta+b\varthetaθr​=aθ+bϑ under a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1, the same pointwise step is

U(θr,ax+by)≤aU(θ,x)+bU(ϑ,y)−mab2∥θ−ϑ∥2,U(\theta_r,ax+by)\le aU(\theta,x)+bU(\vartheta,y)-\tfrac{mab}{2}\|\theta-\vartheta\|^2,U(θr​,ax+by)≤aU(θ,x)+bU(ϑ,y)−2mab​∥θ−ϑ∥2,

The multiplier is e−ce^{-c}e−c with c=mab∥θ−ϑ∥2/2c=mab\|\theta-\vartheta\|^2/2c=mab∥θ−ϑ∥2/2. Thus its integral conclusion is M(θ)aM(ϑ)b≤e−cM(θr)M(\theta)^aM(\vartheta)^b\le e^{-c}M(\theta_r)M(θ)aM(ϑ)b≤e−cM(θr​). In the first notation, setting c=(mab/2)∥θ−θ′∥2c=(mab/2)\|\theta-\theta'\|^2c=(mab/2)∥θ−θ′∥2 gives the exponential inequality e−ce−U(θr,ax+by)≥e−aU(θ,x)e−bU(θ′,y)e^{-c}e^{-U(\theta_r,ax+by)}\ge e^{-aU(\theta,x)}e^{-bU(\theta',y)}e−ce−U(θr​,ax+by)≥e−aU(θ,x)e−bU(θ′,y), whose Prékopa conclusion is e−cM(θr)≥M(θ)aM(θ′)be^{-c}M(\theta_r)\ge M(\theta)^aM(\theta')^be−cM(θr​)≥M(θ)aM(θ′)b. Taking logarithms gives log⁡M(θr)≥alog⁡M(θ)+blog⁡M(θ′)+c\log M(\theta_r)\ge a\log M(\theta)+b\log M(\theta')+clogM(θr​)≥alogM(θ)+blogM(θ′)+c. Negation proves strong convexity with modulus mmm and the stated quadratic coefficient 1/21/21/2. All latent integrals are positive and finite, so every transformation is a real-valued one.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

marginalPotential_strongConvex constructs the defining convexity inequality at arbitrary nonnegative weights a, b summing to one. hf, hg and hh provide Gaussian domination of the three latent weights. hpoint follows from hsc.2 after pair_affine and pair_sub; the nonnegative latent squared distance is discarded while the parameter curvature term c is retained. Prekopa.prekopa_leindler integrates this pointwise inequality. integral_latentWeight identifies the marginal integrals, hz proves strict positivity, and Real.log_le_log turns the integrated inequality into strong convexity of the negative logarithm.

Every marginal integral is already finite and positive, so logarithms preserve the inequality. Negating gives F(θr)≤aF(θ)+bF(ϑ)−cF(\theta_r)\le aF(\theta)+bF(\vartheta)-cF(θr​)≤aF(θ)+bF(ϑ)−c, exactly mmm-strong convexity of F=−log⁡MF=-\log MF=−logM. The code's hpoint is the weight inequality, hpl its Prékopa integral consequence, and hlog its logarithmic conversion. Strict convexity for m>0m>0m>0 then proves uniqueness of the previously constructed maximizer.

Formal correspondence: marginalPotential_strongConvex : Integrating out the latent variables preserves precisely the original strong-convexity constant. This is the derived Prékopa theorem for IPLA.

04Existence and uniqueness of the marginal maximizer §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume m>0m>0m>0. The established mmm-strong convexity makes Φ\PhiΦ strictly convex. Two marginal-likelihood maximizers would both minimize Φ\PhiΦ. If they were distinct, the midpoint inequality would contradict minimality. Existence has already followed from continuity and decay of the marginal, so there is ∃!θ∗\exists!\theta_*∃!θ∗​ satisfying the original maximization condition.

Specifically, the midpoint value would be at most the common minimum minus (m/8)∥θ−θ′∥2(m/8)\|\theta-\theta'\|^2(m/8)∥θ−θ′∥2. This subtraction is strictly positive for distinct parameters because m>0m>0m>0. The contradiction proves uniqueness without requiring a derivative of the marginal potential.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

marginalMaximizer_unique applies .strictConvexOn hm to the already proved marginal strong convexity. eq_of_isMinOn then identifies two minimizers, after marginalMaximizer_minimizes_potential translates each maximizer premise. existsUnique_marginalMaximizer takes existence from exists_marginalMaximizer and supplies uniqueness by this result. The existential conclusion therefore comes with both attainment and uniqueness.

Formal correspondence: marginalMaximizer_unique : Joint strong convexity implies uniqueness of the marginal maximizer. existsUnique_marginalMaximizer : The marginal maximizer exists uniquely under exactly the original joint potential assumptions.
