Interacting particle Langevin: transport and entropy convergence

References and publication status for this development

Published source and unpublished extension

Source: Theorem 1, equation (34), and Appendix B of arXiv:2409.07101v2 (27 May 2025), using the ULA estimate of Dalalyan and Karagulyan. The independent parameter- and latent-dimension extension and its proof presented here are unpublished; equal dimensions recover the paper’s displayed bound. The assumption audit explains the joint-potential smoothness condition.

  • Alex Glyn-Davies, Connor Duffin, Ieva Kazlauskaite, Mark Girolami, and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Statistical Finite Elements via Interacting Particle Langevin Dynamics. SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 13(3), 1200–1227. Audited version: arXiv:2409.07101v2
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv

The same Gaussian interacting-particle recursion admits two kinds of estimates. Joint strong convexity gives a quadratic-transport bound and parameter root mean square error about a marginal-likelihood maximizer. A logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the coupled particle target gives relative-entropy bounds for the joint and projected laws. The hypotheses, initial quantities, and particle normalizations differ and are stated separately.

The root module imports the complete particle library. It exports the strongly convex transport/RMS theorem together with the separate conditional lifted-LSI entropy route. It adds no declarations of its own; the linked dependency modules provide the detailed mathematical and Lean proofs.

Statement and proof

01The strongly convex transport and parameter estimates §

Mathematical statement and proof

In scaled coordinates the summed potential has explicit curvature and gradient constants. These regularity properties normalize its Gibbs law and permit a stationary Langevin coupling for the full-state transport bound. Latent integration independently identifies the parameter Gibbs marginal and proves its concentration about the unique marginal-likelihood maximizer. Projection and Minkowski’s inequality then add equilibrium concentration to the transport estimate, giving the equilibrium, transient, and discretization terms in the parameter bound.

The imported chain begins with VN(θ,z)=∑iU(θ,Nzi)V_N(\theta,z)=\sum_iU(\theta,\sqrt Nz_i)VN​(θ,z)=∑i​U(θ,N​zi​), proves dimension dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​, convexity NmNmNm and smoothness NLNLNL, identifies the Gaussian algorithm with step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N, and applies shared Gaussian ULA transport. In parallel, Gaussian domination and proved Prékopa–Leindler identify the parameter target and prove its NmNmNm-strong convexity. The final projection estimate adds equilibrium radius 2dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}2dθ​/(Nm)​, transient transport (1−mγ)kW2(μ0,ΠN)(1-m\gamma)^kW_2(\mu_0,\Pi_N)(1−mγ)kW2​(μ0​,ΠN​), and Euler bias (33/20)(L/m)dx+dθ/Nγ(33/20)(L/m)\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma(33/20)(L/m)dx​+dθ​/N​γ​. 02The joint and parameter entropy estimates §

Mathematical statement and proof

The entropy estimate concerns the relative entropy of the current joint law with respect to the lifted Gibbs target. Applying data processing to both laws gives the projected parameter-law divergence. It does not by itself give concentration about a deterministic maximizing parameter. The conditional interpolation theorem and the Gaussian interpolation construction establish their respective entropy estimates under the distinct assumptions stated in each result.

The LSI imports instead assume an LSI of constant β\betaβ for the actual ΠN\Pi_NΠN​ and the regular interpolation data needed by the shared entropy proof. They prove finite actual KL and Hk≤e−βγk/NH0+8γN(dθ+Ndx)L2/βH_k\le e^{-\beta\gamma k/N}H_0+8\gamma N(d_\theta+Nd_x)L^2/\betaHk​≤e−βγk/NH0​+8γN(dθ​+Ndx​)L2/β, then derive normalized and parameter-projection consequences.
