The actual mean-field force is Lipschitz in W₂

For a pair potential VVV with LLL-Lipschitz gradient, define bV(θ,μ)=∫∇θV(θ,x)μ(dx)b_V(\theta,\mu)=\int\nabla_\theta V(\theta,x)\mu(dx)bV​(θ,μ)=∫∇θ​V(θ,x)μ(dx). The measures are probability laws with finite second moments. Every Bochner expectation and every finite real transport distance is justified.

01A Lipschitz observable preserves L² and controls expectation differences §

Mathematical statement and proof

Center an observable ϕ\phiϕ at ϕ(0)\phi(0)ϕ(0); its norm growth is bounded by K∥x∥K\|x\|K∥x∥, so ϕ(X)∈L2⊂L1\phi(X)\in L^2\subset L^1ϕ(X)∈L2⊂L1. Given any finite-cost coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, pushforward integration and Cauchy–Schwarz give

∥∫ϕ dμ−∫ϕ dν∥≤K(∫∥x−y∥2dc)1/2.\left\|\int\phi\,d\mu-\int\phi\,d\nu\right\|\le K\left(\int\|x-y\|^2dc\right)^{1/2}.​∫ϕdμ−∫ϕdν​≤K(∫∥x−y∥2dc)1/2.

Taking the actual coupling infimum yields the bound KW2(μ,ν)K W_2(\mu,\nu)KW2​(μ,ν), with finiteness supplied by the second moments.

The nonzero value φ(0)\varphi(0)φ(0) must be retained when proving integrability. Write φ(X)=[φ(X)−φ(0)]+φ(0)\varphi(X)=[\varphi(X)-\varphi(0)]+\varphi(0)φ(X)=[φ(X)−φ(0)]+φ(0). The first term is in L2L^2L2 because its Lipschitz map vanishes at zero; the second is a constant in L2L^2L2 on a probability space. This proves memLp_lipschitz_observable, including the offset.

For the transport estimate, take any coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν. Its marginal identities pull the given second moments onto its two coordinate random variables. Applying the observable lemma to both coordinates proves their L2L^2L2 membership and hence their Bochner integrability. The fact hid then changes variables using those marginals and combines the two integrals:

∫φ dμ−∫φ dν=∫[φ(x)−φ(y)] dc(x,y).\int\varphi\,d\mu-\int\varphi\,d\nu=\int[\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)]\,dc(x,y).∫φdμ−∫φdν=∫[φ(x)−φ(y)]dc(x,y).

The successive inequalities in hfamily are the integral triangle inequality, the probability-space comparison ∥F∥1≤∥F∥2\|F\|_1\le\|F\|_2∥F∥1​≤∥F∥2​, and the pointwise Lipschitz bound ∥φ(x)−φ(y)∥≤K∥x−y∥\|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\|\le K\|x-y\|∥φ(x)−φ(y)∥≤K∥x−y∥. They give the same upper bound K cost⁡(c)K\,\operatorname{cost}(c)Kcost(c) for every coupling. Taking the infimum in the extended nonnegative reals proves hbound. Finite input second moments already imply finite W2W_2W2​, so the product KW2KW_2KW2​ is finite and the final conversion to real numbers preserves the inequality.

02Project the true joint gradient to its parameter block §

Mathematical statement and proof

The bounded linear map PθP_\thetaPθ​ keeps the parameter coordinates. Its squared norm is a sub-sum of the full Euclidean square, hence ∥Pθz∥≤∥z∥\|P_\theta z\|\le\|z\|∥Pθ​z∥≤∥z∥. Define ϕθ(x)=Pθ∇V(θ,x)\phi_\theta(x)=P_\theta\nabla V(\theta,x)ϕθ​(x)=Pθ​∇V(θ,x), then define the mean-field force as its actual Bochner integral. These definitions use the existing joint potential, with no arbitrary force field substituted for its gradient.

03Control joint parameter and latent perturbations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Decompose a joint pair difference into its two coordinate inclusions, giving ∥(θ,x)−(θ′,x′)∥≤∥θ−θ′∥+∥x−x′∥\|(\theta,x)-(\theta',x')\|\le\|\theta-\theta'\|+\|x-x'\|∥(θ,x)−(θ′,x′)∥≤∥θ−θ′∥+∥x−x′∥. Gradient Lipschitzness and the nonexpansive parameter projection imply

∥ϕθ(x)−ϕθ′(x′)∥≤L(∥θ−θ′∥+∥x−x′∥).\|\phi_\theta(x)-\phi_{\theta'}(x')\|\le L(\|\theta-\theta'\|+\|x-x'\|).∥ϕθ​(x)−ϕθ′​(x′)∥≤L(∥θ−θ′∥+∥x−x′∥).

Fixing θ=θ′\theta=\theta'θ=θ′ proves that the latent observable itself is LLL-Lipschitz.

04Separate the parameter change from the law change §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a differentiable potential VVV, define

bV(θ,μ)=∫∇θV(θ,x) μ(dx). b_V(\theta,\mu)=\int \nabla_\theta V(\theta,x)\,\mu(dx). bV​(θ,μ)=∫∇θ​V(θ,x)μ(dx).

Assume ∇V\nabla V∇V is LLL-Lipschitz and μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν are probability laws with finite second moment. Coordinate projection has norm at most one, so the integrand is Lipschitz and grows at most linearly. Its Bochner expectation is therefore integrable. For any finite-cost coupling, compare its two integrands, apply the norm-of-integral inequality, and use Cauchy–Schwarz. Taking the infimum of actual coupling costs gives

∥bV(θ,μ)−bV(θ′,ν)∥≤L(∥θ−θ′∥+W2(μ,ν)). \|b_V(\theta,\mu)-b_V(\theta',\nu)\| \le L\bigl(\|\theta-\theta'\|+W_2(\mu,\nu)\bigr). ∥bV​(θ,μ)−bV​(θ′,ν)∥≤L(∥θ−θ′∥+W2​(μ,ν)).

With the genuine split envelope V=g1+g2ηV=g_1+g_2^\etaV=g1​+g2η​, the proved constant is L1+1/ηL_1+1/\etaL1​+1/η. The paper’s 1/η1/\eta1/η constant applies to a full envelope, not to this split expression in general. This is a corrected W₂ version of Lemma C.1: its right-hand side is weaker than the paper’s W₁ statement, and the distinction is explicit. Propositions C.2–C.3 are not consequences formalized by this lemma alone.

Fix an arbitrary coupling (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) of the two latent laws. Split the force difference into a parameter change at fixed law and a law change at fixed parameter. Nonexpansive parameter projection and the full score's LLL-Lipschitz property give ∥∇θU(θ,x)−∇θU(ϑ,y)∥≤L(∥θ−ϑ∥+∥x−y∥)\|\nabla_\theta U(\theta,x)-\nabla_\theta U(\vartheta,y)\|\le L(\|\theta-\vartheta\|+\|x-y\|)∥∇θ​U(θ,x)−∇θ​U(ϑ,y)∥≤L(∥θ−ϑ∥+∥x−y∥). Integrability of this observable follows from the latent second moments. Integrate, use Cauchy–Schwarz on E∥X−Y∥\mathbb E\|X-Y\|E∥X−Y∥, and take the coupling infimum to obtain ∥b(θ,μ)−b(ϑ,ν)∥≤L∥θ−ϑ∥+LW2(μ,ν).\|b(\theta,\mu)-b(\vartheta,\nu)\|\le L\|\theta-\vartheta\|+LW_2(\mu,\nu).∥b(θ,μ)−b(ϑ,ν)∥≤L∥θ−ϑ∥+LW2​(μ,ν). The Moreau specialization substitutes the already proved score constant L1+η−1L_1+\eta^{-1}L1​+η−1.
