Sharp parameter concentration and the IPLA RMS bound

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Apply the continuous-potential concentration estimate to the parameter marginal, whose curvature has factor NNN from particle count. For a differentiable mmm-strongly convex pair potential UUU with m>0m>0m>0, Prékopa–Leindler gives the marginal curvature. Its NNN-power target has curvature NmNmNm, so its parameter second moment is at most dθ/(Nm)d_\theta/(Nm)dθ​/(Nm). This improves the equilibrium term in the earlier estimate while keeping the transport and discretization terms unchanged.

01Sharp concentration of the parameter marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

For continuous aaa-strongly convex potential fff with minimizer x⋆x_\starx⋆​, the dilation argument gives

∫∥x−x⋆∥2 π(dx)≤da,π(dx)∝e−f(x)dx. \int\|x-x_\star\|^2\,\pi(dx)\le\frac d a, \qquad\pi(dx)\propto e^{-f(x)}dx. ∫∥x−x⋆​∥2π(dx)≤ad​,π(dx)∝e−f(x)dx.

Only continuity, strong convexity, and a minimizing point are needed; no nonsmooth integration-by-parts formula is assumed. Prékopa–Leindler gives strong convexity of the parameter marginal potential. Raising the marginal likelihood to its NNN-th power multiplies that curvature by NNN. Thus

W2(Πλ,Nθ,δθλ,⋆)≤dθNm. W_2(\Pi_{\lambda,N}^{\theta},\delta_{\theta_{\lambda,\star}}) \le\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta}{Nm}}. W2​(Πλ,Nθ​,δθλ,⋆​​)≤Nmdθ​​​.

This is the factor in the source’s equilibrium estimate. The earlier radius 2dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}2dθ​/(Nm)​ was a valid weaker bound.

The parameter density is proportional to M(θ)NM(\theta)^NM(θ)N and has negative log potential NF(θ)NF(\theta)NF(θ). Its curvature is NmNmNm, with minimizer θ∗\theta_*θ∗​. The sharp theorem in dimension dθd_\thetadθ​ gives ∫∥θ−θ∗∥2dΠN≤dθ/(Nm)\int\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2d\Pi_N\le d_\theta/(Nm)∫∥θ−θ∗​∥2dΠN​≤dθ​/(Nm). In the equivalent notation, density e−NΦe^{-N\Phi}e−NΦ has curvature NmNmNm, so dimension dθd_\thetadθ​ gives second moment dθ/(Nm)d_\theta/(Nm)dθ​/(Nm). The exact projection identity transfers that same moment to the full Gibbs law; its nonnegative square root is the equilibrium root mean square radius.

02The Gaussian particle estimate with sharp equilibrium radius §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume N>0N>0N>0, γ>0\gamma>0γ>0, γ≤2/(m+L)\gamma\le2/(m+L)γ≤2/(m+L), independent Gaussian innovations, and L2L^2L2 initialization. The scaled Gaussian Euler estimate, parameter projection, and the sharp equilibrium moment give

RMS⁡n≤dθNm+(1−mγ)nW2(ρ0,ΠN)+3320Lmdx+dθ/Nγ.\operatorname{RMS}_n\le\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta}{Nm}}+(1-m\gamma)^nW_2(\rho_0,\Pi_N)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma.RMSn​≤Nmdθ​​​+(1−mγ)nW2​(ρ0​,ΠN​)+2033​mL​dx​+dθ​/N​γ​.

For any coupling of the current state and Gibbs target, projection does not increase quadratic cost. Minkowski adds the Gibbs parameter error about the center. Taking the coupling infimum gives transport plus the sharp radius, and substitution yields the displayed estimate. If the parameter dimension is zero its error is zero; if the state is a singleton its transport is zero. Thus the same inequality holds for arbitrary nonnegative parameter and latent dimensions.

For D=dθ+NdxD=d_\theta+Nd_xD=dθ​+Ndx​, the lifted state has score constant NLNLNL, convexity NmNmNm and step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N. The shared Gaussian ULA theorem controls its W2W_2W2​ error, while the parameter projection has norm at most one. The projected RMS therefore is at most the sharp equilibrium scale dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}dθ​/(Nm)​ plus that transport error. The nonempty-dimensional case proves this combination directly; the final declaration splits off zero-dimensional cases so that no unspoken positive-dimension premise remains.
