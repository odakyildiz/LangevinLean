The finite-particle parameter receives a common Brownian input of amplitude 2/N\sqrt{2/N}2/N​. Constructing it from a product copy of the actual source preserves Brownian laws and gives an exact L2L^2L2 norm. On a fixed horizon its magnitude has an explicit coefficient times N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2, matching the empirical-force fluctuation scale.

On Q⊗NQ^{\otimes N}Q⊗N, N>0N>0N>0, use copy zero of a supplied dθd_\thetadθ​-coordinate Brownian source for the common parameter input. The actual noise amplitude is 2/N\sqrt{2/N}2/N​.

01Pull back Brownian coordinates and define their scaled L² noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

Coordinate evaluation on the finite product is measure preserving, so each pulled-back scalar process retains its standard Brownian law. Define At=N−1/22(Btθ−B0θ)A_t=N^{-1/2}\sqrt2(B_t^\theta-B_0^\theta)At​=N−1/22​(Btθ​−B0θ​) as an actual L2L^2L2 element. The Brownian-noise construction proves continuity in L2L^2L2 and value zero at time zero; scalar multiplication preserves both facts.

Choose copy zero on the finite iid product, which exists because N>0N>0N>0. Coordinate evaluation is measure preserving, so pulling back each supplied standard Brownian coordinate preserves its Brownian law. Multiply its Langevin noise 2Wt\sqrt2W_t2​Wt​ by N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2, producing the actual common parameter input. The L2L^2L2-valued Brownian construction proves continuity in time and zero initial value; neither property is assumed for a newly introduced noise process.

Formal correspondence: empiricalParameter_preBrownian : Each coordinate of the common parameter source has the actual Brownian law after pullback to the finite iid product. empiricalParameterNoise : The actual parameter Brownian input, with the particle scaling.

A coordinate of the finite product has exactly the original source law. Pulling the parameter Brownian paths through that coordinate therefore preserves their scalar Brownian distributions. The vector construction sums their finite second moments and multiplies by 2/N\sqrt{2/N}2/N​. Continuity in L2L^2L2 and zero initial value are inherited from the constructed Brownian increment process and fixed scalar multiplication.

02Derive the exact norm and its N-independent horizon coefficient §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coordinate second moments sum to dθtd_\theta tdθ​t. Consequently

∥At∥2=2dθtN≤2dθTN,0≤t≤T.\|A_t\|_2=\frac{\sqrt{2d_\theta}\sqrt t}{\sqrt N}\le\frac{\sqrt{2d_\theta}\sqrt T}{\sqrt N},\qquad0\le t\le T.∥At​∥2​=N​2dθ​​t​​≤N​2dθ​​T​​,0≤t≤T.

The theorem follows by the exact Brownian norm identity and monotonicity of the square root. The canonical specialization uses the already constructed Gaussian time-indicator Brownian source, so it supplies a concrete instance rather than postulating one.

Each parameter Brownian coordinate has variance ttt, so the vector Langevin noise has squared L2L^2L2 norm 2dθt2d_\theta t2dθ​t. Scalar multiplication by N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2 gives the exact identity ∥AN(t)∥2=2dθtN.\|A_N(t)\|_2=\frac{\sqrt{2d_\theta}\sqrt t}{\sqrt N}.∥AN​(t)∥2​=N​2dθ​​t​​. Monotonicity of the square root gives the uniform horizon bound with T\sqrt TT​. The canonical corollary merely substitutes the already constructed Gaussian time-indicator Brownian source, thereby discharging existence of a source with these moments.

Formal correspondence: empiricalParameterNoise_norm : The exact Brownian second-moment scale of the actual parameter input. empiricalParameterNoise_bound : A finite-horizon bound with an explicit coefficient independent of N . canonical_empiricalParameterNoise_bound : A concrete source exists: the Brownian coordinates are the already constructed independent Gaussian time-indicator processes.

The squared norm is (2/N)∑j=1dθE∣Btj−B0j∣2=2dθt/N(2/N)\sum_{j=1}^{d_\theta}\mathbb E|B_t^j-B_0^j|^2=2d_\theta t/N(2/N)∑j=1dθ​​E∣Btj​−B0j​∣2=2dθ​t/N. Its nonnegative square root gives the exact formula. For t≤Tt\le Tt≤T, monotonicity yields the horizon coefficient 2dθT\sqrt{2d_\theta T}2dθ​T​, independent of NNN. This is the additional-input constant inserted into the scaled Grönwall comparison.
