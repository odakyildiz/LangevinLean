Gaussian sequence representations of the splitting laws

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Mutual independence of the scalar standard Gaussian coordinates specifies an independent sequence of standard state-space Gaussian vectors. Independence of initialization gives a product law for all recursive inputs. The measurable splitting map therefore has the same output distribution whether the Gaussian sequence is represented by a scalar product measure, a vector product measure, or another realization with the same input laws.

Statement and proof

01Independent standard Gaussian vectors from scalar coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scalar coordinate distributions and their mutual independence give ξk∼N(0,I)\xi_k\sim\mathcal N(0,I)ξk​∼N(0,I) as a full vector. Disjoint time-coordinate groups give independence across kkk. Together with initialization independence, the innovation-sequence law is N(0,I)⊗N\mathcal N(0,I)^{\otimes\mathbb N}N(0,I)⊗N.

Indeed every finite set of vector coordinates has the product of its scalar Gaussian laws. These finite-coordinate distributions determine the vector-sequence product measure. Applying the general independent-innovation recursion result identifies the finite-time splitting laws without requiring a stationary distribution.

The hypothesis GaussianInnovations specifies laws and independence of all scalar coordinates over all times, as well as independence of initialization from the whole path. gaussianInnovations_vectorLaw groups a finite set of independent standard scalar Gaussians into a standard state-space Gaussian; gaussianInnovations_vectorIndependent groups the time slices into an independent vector family. The measurable recursion can then be pushed forward from the product of the initial law and this iid path law. Equality follows for the actual PIPGLA iterate at every finite time; it requires no invariant target.

Formal correspondence: pipglaLaws_eq_canonicalGaussian : The full iid vector Gaussian algorithm law follows from the original scalar-coordinate Gaussian hypotheses.

02Equality of scalar and vector sequence representations §

Mathematical statement and proof

The map grouping scalar coordinates into time-indexed vectors is measurable. Its pushforward takes the scalar Gaussian product measure to the vector Gaussian product measure. Applying the same measurable recursion to these equal input distributions gives the same output law at time nnn.

Consequently any two probability-space realizations with the same initial law and independent standard Gaussian sequence have equal splitting laws. The concrete representatives and the choice of auxiliary product space have no effect on this distributional conclusion.

There are two descriptions of the same noise source: a countable product of scalar coordinates, packed into vectors, or a countable product of standard vector Gaussians. The first law identity uses noisePath_law directly; the second uses vector iid laws. To compare two probability spaces, rewrite both output laws in the vector representation and substitute the equal initial laws. The ambient probability spaces and their concrete random-variable representatives therefore disappear from the conclusion.

Formal correspondence: pipglaLaws_eq_canonicalNoisePath : An equivalent canonical law uses the repository's single countable product of scalar Gaussian coordinates, grouped into vector innovations. pipglaLaws_eq_of_initialLaw_eq : Gaussian realizations on different probability spaces give identical actual PIPGLA output laws when the initialization laws agree.
