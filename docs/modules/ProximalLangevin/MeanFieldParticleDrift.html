01Coordinate inclusions and nonexpansive projections §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the coordinate product state and the latent inclusion Ix(x)=(0,x)I_x(x)=(0,x)Ix​(x)=(0,x). The Euclidean square proves ∥Ixx∥=∥x∥\|I_xx\|=\|x\|∥Ix​x∥=∥x∥, while the latent projection has norm at most one. For any bounded linear pointwise contraction CCC, integrating ∥C(X)∥2≤∥X∥2\|C(X)\|^2\le\|X\|^2∥C(X)∥2≤∥X∥2 proves ∥C∘X∥2≤∥X∥2\|C\circ X\|_2\le\|X\|_2∥C∘X∥2​≤∥X∥2​.

03Define the gradient and empirical particle drift §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the actual gradient ∇V\nabla V∇V to each pair J(Θ,Xi)J(\Theta,X_i)J(Θ,Xi​). The parameter force is the empirical average and each latent force is its own gradient block:

AN(z)=(1N∑i∇θV(Θ,Xi), (∇xV(Θ,Xi))i).\mathcal A_N(z)=\left(\frac1N\sum_i\nabla_\theta V(\Theta,X_i),\ (\nabla_xV(\Theta,X_i))_i\right).AN​(z)=(N1​i∑​∇θ​V(Θ,Xi​), (∇x​V(Θ,Xi​))i​).

Almost everywhere composition and finite-sum identities verify these exact coordinate formulas. The definition does not replace the empirical parameter force by a mean-field expectation.

04Dimension-free Lipschitz control constructs the actual solution §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each coordinate difference is bounded by the product-state norm. The joint-pair estimate therefore gives ∥∇V(Jiz)−∇V(Jiw)∥2≤2L∥z−w∥\|\nabla V(J_i z)-\nabla V(J_i w)\|_2\le2L\|z-w\|∥∇V(Ji​z)−∇V(Ji​w)∥2​≤2L∥z−w∥. Averaging NNN parameter terms and dividing by N>0N>0N>0 preserves this bound; the maximum of the latent terms also preserves it. Hence Lip⁡(AN)≤2L\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal A_N)\le2LLip(AN​)≤2L, independently of NNN.
