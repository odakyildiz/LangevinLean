02Where each algorithm places its noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

PIPGLA, v3 equations (11)–(14), first takes the noisy smooth g1g_1g1​ step and then applies the averaged pairwise proximal map. In scaled coordinates it is

yn=zn−γN∇ ⁣(∑ig1(Jizn))+2γNξn,zn+1=Pair⁡Pλ(yn). y_n=z_n-\frac\gamma N\nabla\!\left(\sum_i g_1(J_i z_n)\right) +\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{N}}\xi_n, \qquad z_{n+1}=\operatorname{Pair}_{P_\lambda}(y_n). yn​=zn​−Nγ​∇(i∑​g1​(Ji​zn​))+N2γ​​ξn​,zn+1​=PairPλ​​(yn​).

PIPULA is the special MYIPLA update with g1=0g_1=0g1​=0 and λ=γ\lambda=\gammaλ=γ, followed by Gaussian noise. Both distinctions are encoded in separate definitions and proved reduction identities. The PIPGLA proximity step occurs after the noise; its target in the v3 convergence analysis is the original nonsmooth Gibbs model. The MYIPLA theorem cannot be reused merely by renaming this composition.

For clarity, the expanded MYIPLA definition itself is

TMY(z,ξ)=(1−γ/η)z−γN∇Fg1(z)+γηPair⁡P(z)+2γ/Nξ.T_{\mathrm{MY}}(z,\xi)=(1-\gamma/\eta)z-\frac\gamma N\nabla F_{g_1}(z)+\frac\gamma\eta\operatorname{Pair}_P(z)+\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\xi.TMY​(z,ξ)=(1−γ/η)z−Nγ​∇Fg1​​(z)+ηγ​PairP​(z)+2γ/N​ξ.

These are exact definitions of pointwise maps. The residual-gradient theorem below proves when this expanded map equals a smoothed Euler step.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.

  • pipulaUpdate: PIPULA: pairwise proximity, followed by noise.
  • myiplaUpdate: MYIPLA, with independent smoothing and time steps.
  • pipglaUpdate: PIPGLA: the noisy smooth forward step is followed by the pairwise proximal map. The common parameter is averaged only at the end.

06Residual proximity is exactly an Euler step §

Mathematical statement and proof

Given any map PPP on one joint parameter-particle pair, define its particle action by applying PPP separately to each JizJ_i zJi​z, averaging the resulting parameter coordinates, and dividing each resulting physical particle coordinate by N\sqrt NN​. The common parameter is not optimized independently for each particle and then kept as NNN separate state variables; the averaging is part of the algorithm.

With P=PλP=P_\lambdaP=Pλ​, MYIPLA in v3 equations (9)–(10) is exactly the noisy Euler step for Vλ=g1+g2λV_\lambda=g_1+g_2^\lambdaVλ​=g1​+g2λ​. Indeed, the pairwise residual-gradient identity gives

Pair⁡P(z)=z−λN∇ ⁣(∑ig2λ(Jiz)). \operatorname{Pair}_P(z)=z-\frac\lambda N\nabla\!\left(\sum_i g_2^\lambda(J_i z)\right). PairP​(z)=z−Nλ​∇(i∑​g2λ​(Ji​z)).

Substituting this into the expanded MYIPLA formula cancels the extra multiples of zzz, leaving

zn+1=zn−γN∇Vλ(zn)+2γN ξn. z_{n+1}=z_n-\frac\gamma N\nabla\mathcal V_\lambda(z_n) +\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{N}}\,\xi_n. zn+1​=zn​−Nγ​∇Vλ​(zn​)+N2γ​​ξn​.

The coordinate proofs also recover parameter noise 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​ and physical-particle noise 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. The full recursion identity follows by induction on the iteration count.

The recursion declarations include the initial and successor identities for each supplied innovation sequence. All pointwise identities concern the explicit state update, before any stochastic convergence theorem is applied.

For P(x)=x−λr(x)P(x)=x-\lambda r(x)P(x)=x−λr(x), the parameter coordinate of the pairwise map is its original parameter minus λ\lambdaλ times the average parameter residual. Each latent coordinate similarly loses λ/N\lambda/\sqrt Nλ/N​ times its pair residual. These are exactly the blocks of (λ/N)∇VN(r)(\lambda/N)\nabla V_N(r)(λ/N)∇VN​(r), using the previously proved lifted-gradient identities. Add ∇g1+∇e=∇(g1+e)\nabla g_1+\nabla e=\nabla(g_1+e)∇g1​+∇e=∇(g1​+e) under differentiability and set the proximal scale to the Euler scale to identify the MYIPLA update with scaledUpdate. Induction then identifies the entire pointwise chain, not merely its first step.
