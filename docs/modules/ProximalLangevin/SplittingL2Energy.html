Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Apply the deterministic energy estimate to L2L^2L2 random vectors. Lipschitz growth makes the scalar potential values integrable, and square integrability makes all squared-distance terms integrable. Weighted Young inequalities control the smooth local defect without losing the squared contraction. Integrated convex supports retain the perturbation-force pairing in a form that can be combined with the expected potential difference.

02Integrability of potential and quadratic terms §

Mathematical statement and proof

For globally KKK-Lipschitz scalar function fff, subtract f(0)f(0)f(0). The centered growth is at most K∥X∥K\|X\|K∥X∥, so f(X)∈L2⊂L1f(X)\in L^2\subset L^1f(X)∈L2⊂L1 on a probability space. The L2L^2L2 norm satisfies ∥X∥22=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP\|X\|_2^2=\int\|X(\omega)\|^2dP∥X∥22​=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP.

For observable fff, the direct bound is ∣f(X)∣≤K∥X∥+∣f(0)∣|f(X)|\le K\|X\|+|f(0)|∣f(X)∣≤K∥X∥+∣f(0)∣. Thus an L2L^2L2 state gives f(X)∈L2⊂L1f(X)\in L^2\subset L^1f(X)∈L2⊂L1. Centering fff justifies the composition estimate. The L2L^2L2 inner product and identity ⟨X,X⟩=∥X∥2\langle X,X\rangle=\|X\|^2⟨X,X⟩=∥X∥2 give ∥X∥L22=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP\|X\|_{L^2}^2=\int\|X(\omega)\|^2dP∥X∥L22​=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP. More generally, bound ∣f(x)∣≤∣f(0)∣+K∥x∥|f(x)|\le|f(0)|+K\|x\|∣f(x)∣≤∣f(0)∣+K∥x∥ makes composition with an L2L^2L2 state an L2L^2L2 function. Squared differences of L2L^2L2 states are also integrable. Hence the potential and quadratic expectations below are finite, and linearity may be applied without subtraction of infinite quantities.

Formal correspondence: integrable_lipschitz_compLp : Every globally Lipschitz scalar observable of an L2L^2L2 random variable is integrable. lp_norm_sq_eq_integral : Exact second-moment identity for actual L2L^2L2 random variables.

03The integrated proximal energy inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume ∥T(x)−y∥2≤∥x−y∥2−2h(f(x)−f(y))+C\|T(x)-y\|^2\le\|x-y\|^2-2h(f(x)-f(y))+C∥T(x)−y∥2≤∥x−y∥2−2h(f(x)−f(y))+C and Lipschitz maps T,fT,fT,f. Substitute S,Y∈L2S,Y\in L^2S,Y∈L2 pointwise on a probability-one representative event. The Lipschitz composition represents T(S)T(S)T(S) almost surely. Each term is integrable by the preceding growth and moment bounds. Integral monotonicity and linearity therefore give

∥T(S)−Y∥22≤∥S−Y∥22−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.\|T(S)-Y\|_2^2\le\|S-Y\|_2^2-2h(\mathbb E f(S)-\mathbb E f(Y))+C.∥T(S)−Y∥22​≤∥S−Y∥22​−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.

This is the same energy inequality in L2L^2L2. The additive constant is unchanged because the source has mass one. The statement concerns the random output of the actual composed map, as identified by its almost-sure representative.

Apply the deterministic premise at (S(ω),Y(ω))(S(\omega),Y(\omega))(S(ω),Y(ω)). The representative facts for lpDrift and subtraction identify its left side almost everywhere with ∥T(S)−Y∥2\|T(S)-Y\|^2∥T(S)−Y∥2. The local facts hn, hfS, hfY prove integrability of both quadratic norms and both potential values. Only then does integral_mono_ae integrate the inequality and distribute its finite terms: ∥T(S)−Y∥22≤∥S−Y∥22−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.\|T(S)-Y\|_2^2\le\|S-Y\|_2^2-2h(\mathbb Ef(S)-\mathbb Ef(Y))+C.∥T(S)−Y∥22​≤∥S−Y∥22​−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C. The constant remains CCC because PPP has mass one.

04Integrated convex support and the terminal-state difference §

Mathematical statement and proof

The support inequality for a differentiable convex perturbation gives ⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y)\langle S-Y,\nabla e(Y)\rangle\le e(S)-e(Y)⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y) pointwise. Integrability permits the same inequality for the L2L^2L2 inner product. For a third vector ZZZ, identity S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z)S-Z=(S-Y)+(Y-Z)S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z) leaves additional pairing ⟨Y−Z,∇e(Y)⟩L2\langle Y-Z,\nabla e(Y)\rangle_{L^2}⟨Y−Z,∇e(Y)⟩L2​.

Explicitly, for convex differentiable eee the inequality is ⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y)\langle S-Y,\nabla e(Y)\rangle\le e(S)-e(Y)⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y). The gradient composition and state difference belong to L2L^2L2 under the stated bounds, so Cauchy–Schwarz makes their pairing integrable. Integration consequently replaces the reference-force pairing by an expected potential difference, while retaining the endpoint-difference pairing whose future-noise part is shown to vanish separately.

The pointwise supporting-plane inequality is e(S)−e(Y)≥⟨∇e(Y),S−Y⟩e(S)-e(Y)\ge\langle\nabla e(Y),S-Y\ranglee(S)−e(Y)≥⟨∇e(Y),S−Y⟩. Potential integrability follows from value Lipschitzness; inner-product integrability follows from both vectors belonging to L2L^2L2, with score Lipschitzness ensuring ∇e(Y)∈L2\nabla e(Y)\in L^2∇e(Y)∈L2. Integration gives the first declaration. For an endpoint ZZZ, write S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z)S-Z=(S-Y)+(Y-Z)S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z) and use linearity of the inner product. This produces the energy difference plus the reference-increment cross term, precisely the two contributions used in splitting_cross_energy_le.

Formal correspondence: lp_convex_gradient_support : The actual convex gradient supports every square-integrable comparison variable after integrating the pointwise supporting-plane inequality. lp_convex_gradient_cross : Endpoint comparison split into the convex energy difference and a reference-increment cross term, all in the genuine L2L^2L2 inner product.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.2.1.
