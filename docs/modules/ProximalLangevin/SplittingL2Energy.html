Quadratic Hilbert identities and integrated convex energy

The pointwise proximal estimate is lifted to actual L2L^2L2 random variables. All scalar energy expectations are proved integrable before distributing integrals or comparing them.

02Justify potential and quadratic expectations §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a globally KKK-Lipschitz scalar fff, center at f(0)f(0)f(0); its growth is at most K∥X∥K\|X\|K∥X∥. Hence f(X)∈L2⊂L1f(X)\in L^2\subset L^1f(X)∈L2⊂L1 on a probability space. The actual Hilbert-space L2L^2L2 inner product gives ∥X∥22=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP\|X\|_2^2=\int\|X(\omega)\|^2dP∥X∥22​=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP, without any arbitrary representative-dependent value.

For a value-Lipschitz observable fff, ∣f(X)∣≤K∥X∥+∣f(0)∣|f(X)|\le K\|X\|+|f(0)|∣f(X)∣≤K∥X∥+∣f(0)∣; hence an L2L^2L2 state on a probability space gives f(X)∈L2⊂L1f(X)\in L^2\subset L^1f(X)∈L2⊂L1. The first proof implements this by centering fff at zero. The second identity uses the definition of the L2L^2L2 inner product and ⟨X,X⟩=∥X∥2\langle X,X\rangle=\|X\|^2⟨X,X⟩=∥X∥2: ∥X∥L22=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP\|X\|_{L^2}^2=\int\|X(\omega)\|^2dP∥X∥L22​=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP. These facts ensure that the energy expressions below are ordinary finite Bochner integrals.

03Integrate the actual deterministic energy premise §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose ∥T(x)−y∥2≤∥x−y∥2−2h(f(x)−f(y))+C\|T(x)-y\|^2\le\|x-y\|^2-2h(f(x)-f(y))+C∥T(x)−y∥2≤∥x−y∥2−2h(f(x)−f(y))+C, with T,fT,fT,f Lipschitz. Apply it pointwise to S,Y∈L2S,Y\in L^2S,Y∈L2. The composition representative agrees almost everywhere with T(S)T(S)T(S), and every term is integrable. Monotonicity and linearity of the integral yield

∥T(S)−Y∥22≤∥S−Y∥22−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.\|T(S)-Y\|_2^2\le\|S-Y\|_2^2-2h(\mathbb E f(S)-\mathbb E f(Y))+C.∥T(S)−Y∥22​≤∥S−Y∥22​−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.

Apply the deterministic premise at (S(ω),Y(ω))(S(\omega),Y(\omega))(S(ω),Y(ω)). The representative facts for lpDrift and subtraction identify its left side almost everywhere with ∥T(S)−Y∥2\|T(S)-Y\|^2∥T(S)−Y∥2. The local facts hn, hfS, hfY prove integrability of both quadratic norms and both potential values. Only then does integral_mono_ae integrate the inequality and distribute its finite terms: ∥T(S)−Y∥22≤∥S−Y∥22−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C.\|T(S)-Y\|_2^2\le\|S-Y\|_2^2-2h(\mathbb Ef(S)-\mathbb Ef(Y))+C.∥T(S)−Y∥22​≤∥S−Y∥22​−2h(Ef(S)−Ef(Y))+C. The constant remains CCC because PPP has mass one.

04Integrate convex supports and split endpoint differences §

Mathematical statement and proof

Convex differentiability gives ⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y)\langle S-Y,\nabla e(Y)\rangle\le e(S)-e(Y)⟨S−Y,∇e(Y)⟩≤e(S)−e(Y) pointwise. Integrate to obtain the same inequality for the L2L^2L2 inner product. For a third variable ZZZ, write S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z)S-Z=(S-Y)+(Y-Z)S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z). This splits the cross term into the convex energy difference plus ⟨Y−Z,∇e(Y)⟩L2\langle Y-Z,\nabla e(Y)\rangle_{L^2}⟨Y−Z,∇e(Y)⟩L2​, whose future-noise part can later be canceled.

The pointwise supporting-plane inequality is e(S)−e(Y)≥⟨∇e(Y),S−Y⟩e(S)-e(Y)\ge\langle\nabla e(Y),S-Y\ranglee(S)−e(Y)≥⟨∇e(Y),S−Y⟩. Potential integrability follows from value Lipschitzness; inner-product integrability follows from both vectors belonging to L2L^2L2, with score Lipschitzness ensuring ∇e(Y)∈L2\nabla e(Y)\in L^2∇e(Y)∈L2. Integration gives the first declaration. For an endpoint ZZZ, write S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z)S-Z=(S-Y)+(Y-Z)S−Z=(S−Y)+(Y−Z) and use linearity of the inner product. This produces the energy difference plus the reference-increment cross term, precisely the two contributions used in splitting_cross_energy_le.
