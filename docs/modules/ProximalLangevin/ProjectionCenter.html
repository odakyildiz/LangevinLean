Changing the center of a projected quadratic error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Let μ\muμ be a probability measure on a real normed space EEE, T:E→FT:E\to FT:E→F a continuous linear map into a real normed space FFF, and a∈Fa\in Fa∈F. The projected quadratic error of μ\muμ about the center aaa is

Rμ(a)=(∫E∥Tx−a∥2 μ(dx))1/2∈[0,∞],R_\mu(a)=\Bigl(\int_E\|Tx-a\|^2\,\mu(dx)\Bigr)^{1/2}\in[0,\infty],Rμ​(a)=(∫E​∥Tx−a∥2μ(dx))1/2∈[0,∞],

with the convention Rμ(a)=∞R_\mu(a)=\inftyRμ​(a)=∞ when x↦Txx\mapsto Txx↦Tx is not measurable for μ\muμ. By Minkowski's inequality, Rμ(a)≤Rμ(b)+∥b−a∥R_\mu(a)\le R_\mu(b)+\|b-a\|Rμ​(a)≤Rμ​(b)+∥b−a∥ for any two centers. Hence a finite error about one center implies a finite error about every center, and then the inequality holds between the ordinary square-root integrals. The result is used with TTT the projection onto the parameter block, to move the center from the smoothed marginal maximizer to the original one.

Statement and proof

01Minkowski's inequality for the change of center §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let μ\muμ be a probability measure on EEE, T:E→FT:E\to FT:E→F continuous and linear, and a,b∈Fa,b\in Fa,b∈F. Then

Rμ(a)≤Rμ(b)+∥b−a∥in [0,∞],(1)R_\mu(a)\le R_\mu(b)+\|b-a\|\qquad\text{in }[0,\infty],\qquad(1)Rμ​(a)≤Rμ​(b)+∥b−a∥in [0,∞],(1)

and if Rμ(b)<∞R_\mu(b)<\inftyRμ​(b)<∞, then Rμ(a)<∞R_\mu(a)<\inftyRμ​(a)<∞.

Proof. For every xxx, Tx−a=(Tx−b)+(b−a)Tx-a=(Tx-b)+(b-a)Tx−a=(Tx−b)+(b−a). Minkowski's inequality in L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ), which holds for functions with values in a normed space and with norms in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞], gives

Rμ(a)=∥(T⋅−b)+(b−a)∥L2(μ)≤∥T⋅−b∥L2(μ)+∥b−a∥L2(μ),R_\mu(a)=\bigl\|(T\cdot-b)+(b-a)\bigr\|_{L^2(\mu)}\le\|T\cdot-b\|_{L^2(\mu)}+\|b-a\|_{L^2(\mu)},Rμ​(a)=​(T⋅−b)+(b−a)​L2(μ)​≤∥T⋅−b∥L2(μ)​+∥b−a∥L2(μ)​,

where in the last term b−ab-ab−a denotes the constant function. Its L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ) norm is (∫∥b−a∥2 dμ)1/2=∥b−a∥ μ(E)1/2=∥b−a∥\bigl(\int\|b-a\|^2\,d\mu\bigr)^{1/2}=\|b-a\|\,\mu(E)^{1/2}=\|b-a\|(∫∥b−a∥2dμ)1/2=∥b−a∥μ(E)1/2=∥b−a∥, because μ(E)=1\mu(E)=1μ(E)=1. This proves (1). If Rμ(b)<∞R_\mu(b)<\inftyRμ​(b)<∞, the right-hand side of (1) is finite, hence so is Rμ(a)R_\mu(a)Rμ​(a). If x↦Txx\mapsto Txx↦Tx is not measurable, both errors are infinite and (1) holds trivially. □\square□

For a finite measure of total mass MMM the constant term would be M ∥b−a∥\sqrt M\,\|b-a\|M​∥b−a∥; the coefficient 111 uses M=1M=1M=1. The inequality is proved before any finiteness is known, so finiteness about aaa is a conclusion, not a hypothesis.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The quadratic error is ULA.Hilbert.projectedRMS μ T b, defined in the unadjusted Langevin development as eLpNorm (fun x ↦ T x - b) 2 μ. Here eLpNorm f 2 μ is the L2L^2L2 seminorm with values in ℝ≥0∞ =[0,∞]=[0,\infty]=[0,∞]; it equals (∫−∥f∥2 dμ)1/2(\int^-\|f\|^2\,d\mu)^{1/2}(∫−∥f∥2dμ)1/2 for almost-everywhere strongly measurable fff and ∞\infty∞ otherwise. The map T : E →L[ℝ] F is a continuous linear map and [IsProbabilityMeasure μ] states μ(E)=1\mu(E)=1μ(E)=1.

In projectedRMS_center_le, heq is the identity of functions T⋅−a=(T⋅−b)+(b−a)T\cdot-a=(T\cdot-b)+(b-a)T⋅−a=(T⋅−b)+(b−a). After unfolding, eLpNorm_add_le with exponent 2≥12\ge12≥1 is Minkowski's inequality, and the simpa call evaluates the norm of the constant function with eLpNorm_const' (it equals ∥c∥ μ(E)1/2\|c\|\,\mu(E)^{1/2}∥c∥μ(E)1/2) and ofReal_norm, using μ(E)=1\mu(E)=1μ(E)=1. projectedRMS_center_finite applies ne_top_of_le_ne_top to (1): the right-hand side is finite because hb states Rμ(b)≠∞R_\mu(b)\ne\inftyRμ​(b)=∞ and ENNReal.ofReal_ne_top states that ∥b−a∥\|b-a\|∥b−a∥ is finite.

02The inequality for square-root integrals §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let μ\muμ, TTT, a,ba,ba,b be as in the previous step and assume Rμ(b)<∞R_\mu(b)<\inftyRμ​(b)<∞. Then both integrals below are finite and

(∫∥Tx−a∥2 μ(dx))1/2≤(∫∥Tx−b∥2 μ(dx))1/2+∥b−a∥.\Bigl(\int\|Tx-a\|^2\,\mu(dx)\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\Bigl(\int\|Tx-b\|^2\,\mu(dx)\Bigr)^{1/2}+\|b-a\| .(∫∥Tx−a∥2μ(dx))1/2≤(∫∥Tx−b∥2μ(dx))1/2+∥b−a∥.

Proof. By the previous step Rμ(a)<∞R_\mu(a)<\inftyRμ​(a)<∞ as well. The inequality (1) of the previous step is then an inequality between finite nonnegative numbers, hence an inequality of real numbers Rμ(a)≤Rμ(b)+∥b−a∥R_\mu(a)\le R_\mu(b)+\|b-a\|Rμ​(a)≤Rμ​(b)+∥b−a∥. Since Rμ(a)R_\mu(a)Rμ​(a) and Rμ(b)R_\mu(b)Rμ​(b) are finite, the functions x↦∥Tx−a∥2x\mapsto\|Tx-a\|^2x↦∥Tx−a∥2 and x↦∥Tx−b∥2x\mapsto\|Tx-b\|^2x↦∥Tx−b∥2 are integrable and Rμ(c)=(∫∥Tx−c∥2 dμ)1/2R_\mu(c)=\bigl(\int\|Tx-c\|^2\,d\mu\bigr)^{1/2}Rμ​(c)=(∫∥Tx−c∥2dμ)1/2 for c=a,bc=a,bc=a,b. □\square□

The finiteness hypothesis is necessary for the statement to have its intended meaning: if the second moment about bbb were infinite, the integrals would not be finite and the displayed real numbers would not represent the quadratic errors.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

ULA.Hilbert.projectedRMSReal μ T a is the real number (projectedRMS μ T a).toReal; ENNReal.toReal sends ∞\infty∞ to 000, which is why finiteness must be proved before converting. In projectedRMSReal_center_le, h applies the monotonicity ENNReal.toReal_mono to (1), using that its right-hand side is finite (ENNReal.add_ne_top); ENNReal.toReal_add and ENNReal.toReal_ofReal convert the sum and the constant term. sqrt_integral_center_le first obtains ha (finiteness about a) from projectedRMS_center_finite, and then rewrites both real errors with ULA.Hilbert.projectedRMSReal_eq_sqrt_integral, which identifies a finite error with the square root of the Bochner integral ∫ x, ‖T x - a‖ ^ 2 ∂μ.
