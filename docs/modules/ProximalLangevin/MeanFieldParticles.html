References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

For a potential with globally LLL-Lipschitz gradient, compare a constructed mean-field solution with actual finite-particle coordinate solutions on the iid product source. The coefficient is chosen before all particle counts and the norm is the supremum of timewise L2L^2L2 errors.

02Copies inherit the actual equation by bounded linearity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The copy map RN\mathcal R_NRN​ is bounded linear, so it commutes with the Bochner time integral and preserves differences. Apply it to the mean-field equation to get the exact copied-reference forcing. Define

CT=2[(C+2Lb)Ψ(2L,T)+b].C_T=2\big[(C+2Lb)\Psi(2L,T)+b\big].CT​=2[(C+2Lb)Ψ(2L,T)+b].

Here CCC bounds the actual centered force and b/Nb/\sqrt Nb/N​ bounds the extra parameter input. The factor two converts the coordinate maximum norm into the sum of parameter and one latent error.

03Construct the particle solution and use the exact iid residual §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each N>0N>0N>0, Picard constructs the actual particle solution ZZZ with copied latent input and any continuous extra parameter input AAA bounded by b/Nb/\sqrt Nb/N​. The copied mean-field reference has drift residual norm exactly ∥CV(Yt)∥2/N≤C/N\|\mathcal C_V(Y_t)\|_2/\sqrt N\le C/\sqrt N∥CV​(Yt​)∥2​/N​≤C/N​. Apply the forcing-comparison theorem to the 2L2L2L-Lipschitz particle drift. Both tagged coordinate errors are at most the product-state norm, hence

∥ΘtN−θt∥2+∥Xti,N−Xti∥2≤CT/N,0≤t≤T.\|\Theta_t^N-\theta_t\|_2+\|X_t^{i,N}-X_t^i\|_2\le C_T/\sqrt N,\qquad0\le t\le T.∥ΘtN​−θt​∥2​+∥Xti,N​−Xti​∥2​≤CT​/N​,0≤t≤T.

The theorem retains the constructed integral equation and exact iid initial state. It does not assume the final rate.

Continuity of the single-copy centered force along the constructed mean-field path implies a finite bound CCC on the compact interval [0,T][0,T][0,T]. Construct the actual particle process by Picard with copied latent input and the extra parameter input ANA_NAN​. The copied reference equation follows by commuting its bounded linear copy map through the time integral. Its drift defect is at most C/NC/\sqrt NC/N​, by the exact iid residual theorem, and ∥AN∥≤b/N\|A_N\|\le b/\sqrt N∥AN​∥≤b/N​.

The scaled integral-forcing theorem, with particle Lipschitz constant 2L2L2L, yields ∥Zt−Rt∥≤gronwallBound⁡(0,2L,C+2Lb,T)+bN.\|Z_t-R_t\|\le\frac{\operatorname{gronwallBound}(0,2L,C+2Lb,T)+b}{\sqrt N}.∥Zt​−Rt​∥≤N​gronwallBound(0,2L,C+2Lb,T)+b​. Each parameter or tagged-latent difference is bounded by the maximum product norm; their sum is therefore at most twice this expression. This accounts for the factor two in meanFieldParticleConstant. The reference and CCC are chosen before NNN, making the coefficient independent of particle count.

04Construct both systems with actual Brownian inputs §

Mathematical statement and proof

The actual centered reference force is a continuous L²-valued function of time. Compactness of [0,T][0,T][0,T] supplies a finite C≥0C\ge0C≥0 such that

sup⁡0≤t≤T∥∇θV(Yt)−EQ∇θV(Yt)∥2≤C. \sup_{0\le t\le T}\| \nabla_\theta V(Y_t)-\mathbb E_Q\nabla_\theta V(Y_t)\|_2\le C. 0≤t≤Tsup​∥∇θ​V(Yt​)−EQ​∇θ​V(Yt​)∥2​≤C.

This CCC is chosen before the quantifier over all particle counts; it depends on the reference potential, initial law and horizon, not on NNN. Subtract the common latent input and the extra parameter Brownian input in the actual integral equations. The proof applies Grönwall to the remaining drift integral, so it never differentiates Brownian noise. Define

Ψ(a,T)={(eaT−1)/a,a>0,T,a=0,CT=2[(C+2Lb)Ψ(2L,T)+b]. \Psi(a,T)=\begin{cases}(e^{aT}-1)/a,&a>0,\\T,&a=0,\end{cases} \qquad C_T=2\big[(C+2Lb)\Psi(2L,T)+b\big]. Ψ(a,T)={(eaT−1)/a,T,​a>0,a=0,​CT​=2[(C+2Lb)Ψ(2L,T)+b].

For every N>0N>0N>0, the theorem constructs the actual particle integral solution with the prescribed iid initialization and proves, for every tagged particle iii,

sup⁡0≤t≤T(∥ΘtN−θt∥2+∥Xti,N−Xti∥2)≤CTN. \sup_{0\le t\le T}\left( \|\Theta_t^N-\theta_t\|_2+\|X_t^{i,N}-X_t^i\|_2\right) \le\frac{C_T}{\sqrt N}. 0≤t≤Tsup​(∥ΘtN​−θt​∥2​+∥Xti,N​−Xti​∥2​)≤N​CT​​.

For the actual split-smoothed potential V=g1+g2ηV=g_1+g_2^\etaV=g1​+g2η​, use the derived constant L=L1+1/ηL=L_1+1/\etaL=L1​+1/η. The coefficient can depend on η\etaη; uniformity in smoothing is not claimed. This is a constructed iid propagation-of-chaos replacement in uniform timewise L² norm. It does not move the time supremum inside the expectation, as in printed Proposition C.2; the expected path-supremum norm is stronger; the proved uniform timewise L² estimate does not establish it.
