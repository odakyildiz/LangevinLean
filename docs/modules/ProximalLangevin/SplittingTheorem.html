01Convert the pathwise scale comparison into RMS §

Mathematical statement and proof

Drive the λ\lambdaλ- and η\etaη-chains by the same initial variable and innovations. Their state distance is at most K∣λ−η∣/(mγ)K|\lambda-\eta|/(m\gamma)K∣λ−η∣/(mγ). Both chains have finite second moments. The parameter projection is nonexpansive, so Minkowski gives

RMS⁡λ(a)≤RMS⁡η(a)+K∣λ−η∣mγ.\operatorname{RMS}_\lambda(a)\le\operatorname{RMS}_\eta(a)+\frac{K|\lambda-\eta|}{m\gamma}.RMSλ​(a)≤RMSη​(a)+mγK∣λ−η∣​.

The proof works with the actual L2L^2L2 realizations and then identifies their pushforward integrals.

Here is the conversion from a pathwise comparison to the two displayed law integrals. On the supplied probability space let XXX and YYY be the actual L2L^2L2 PIPGLA iterates with proximal parameters λ\lambdaλ and η\etaη, the same initialization, and the same innovations. The pathwise parameter-sensitivity theorem gives

∥X(ω)−Y(ω)∥≤c:=K∣λ−η∣mγalmost surely.\|X(\omega)-Y(\omega)\|\le c:=\frac{K|\lambda-\eta|}{m\gamma}\quad\text{almost surely}.∥X(ω)−Y(ω)∥≤c:=mγK∣λ−η∣​almost surely.

The transient term vanishes because both initial states agree. The full-measure representative equalities for the two chains and their subtraction justify this substitution in hpoint. Since QQQ has total mass one, integrating the uniform bound gives ∥X−Y∥L2≤c\|X-Y\|_{L^2}\le c∥X−Y∥L2​≤c; this is hXY.

Define A=PθX−θA=P_\theta X-\thetaA=Pθ​X−θ and B=PθY−θB=P_\theta Y-\thetaB=Pθ​Y−θ as L2L^2L2 variables. Linearity gives A−B=Pθ(X−Y)A-B=P_\theta(X-Y)A−B=Pθ​(X−Y), and the projection norm bound gives ∥A−B∥2≤∥X−Y∥2\|A-B\|_2\le\|X-Y\|_2∥A−B∥2​≤∥X−Y∥2​. Thus ∥A∥2≤∥B∥2+c\|A\|_2\le\|B\|_2+c∥A∥2​≤∥B∥2​+c, the inequality hn. Finally, hnorm first identifies each actual law as the pushforward of its L2L^2L2 iterate, then changes variables in the quadratic integral and uses ∥A∥22=∥A∥2\sqrt{\|A\|_2^2}=\|A\|_2∥A∥22​​=∥A∥2​. All these norms are finite by construction. Substituting those two identities into hn proves the stated RMS comparison.

02Combine equal-step transport and original-center projection §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set the comparison scale to η=γ\eta=\gammaη=γ. The equal-step law theorem and the original-center projection inequality give

RMS⁡λ(θU)≤K∣λ−γ∣mγ+γK2m+dθNm+(1−mγ)nW2(ρ0,ΠN,γ)2+γNCN.\begin{aligned}\operatorname{RMS}_\lambda(\theta_U)\le{}&\frac{K|\lambda-\gamma|}{m\gamma}+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma K^2}{m}}+\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta}{Nm}}\\&+\sqrt{(1-m\gamma)^nW_2(\rho_0,\Pi_{N,\gamma})^2+\frac\gamma N C_N}.\end{aligned}RMSλ​(θU​)≤​mγK∣λ−γ∣​+mγK2​​+Nmdθ​​​+(1−mγ)nW2​(ρ0​,ΠN,γ​)2+Nγ​CN​​.​

Here CNC_NCN​ is the explicit finite coefficient from the endpoint module. It is independent of the time step. Every positive λ\lambdaλ is covered, with the displayed parameter-comparison cost; a vanishing error as γ→0\gamma\to0γ→0 additionally requires controlling that cost. This is the proved replacement rate, not the paper’s printed heat-flow constants.

The equal-step transport theorem controls the actual law at proximal scale γ\gammaγ (with the lifted smoothing scale prescribed in its statement). The projection theorem adds the sharp equilibrium RMS and the original-center displacement. To handle a different positive proximal parameter λ\lambdaλ, compare the two pointwise runs under the same innovations: their uniform difference is at most K∣λ−γ∣/(mγ)K|\lambda-\gamma|/(m\gamma)K∣λ−γ∣/(mγ). Integrating this deterministic bound yields the same RMS difference because the probability has mass one. The final result adds this scale-comparison term to the already proved equal-step error; the parameter corridor then supplies its explicit simpler upper bound.
