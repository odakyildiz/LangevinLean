Finite moments of the splitting recursion justify parameter root mean square errors about any deterministic center. Projection converts full-state transport to the smoothed Gibbs law into parameter error about its maximizer. The finite potential-gap estimate then changes that center to the original marginal maximizer. The resulting inequality retains the transport distance until its quantitative splitting estimate is substituted.

Let ρn\rho_nρn​ be the actual PIPGLA law with any positive proximal parameter λ\lambdaλ, and compare it with the Gibbs target for Vη=g1+g2ηV_\eta=g_1+g_2^\etaVη​=g1​+g2η​, η>0\eta>0η>0. This module does not assume the algorithm already has a transport convergence rate.

Statement and proof

01State and projected second moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The splitting law is the pushforward of its L2L^2L2 iterate. Change of variables gives z↦z∈L2(ρn)z\mapsto z\in L^2(\rho_n)z↦z∈L2(ρn​). Bounded parameter projection and subtraction of a constant give θ(z)−a∈L2(ρn)\theta(z)-a\in L^2(\rho_n)θ(z)−a∈L2(ρn​) for every aaa.

Indeed the L2L^2L2 state is sent into L2L^2L2 by the linear projection. A deterministic center is an L2L^2L2 constant under a probability measure, so their difference has finite second moment. These moment conclusions hold for every positive proximal scale before the more restrictive convergence-step condition is imposed.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.6.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The law equals the pushforward of lpPipglaChain, so the identity observable under that law has a finite second moment. The continuous parameter projection preserves L2L^2L2, and subtracting a constant center also preserves it because the law is a probability. These two declarations establish finiteness before the later comparison writes a real square-root integral.

Formal correspondence: pipglaLaws_state_memLp : Every actual output law has a finite state second moment, without Gaussianity or a convergence assumption. pipglaLaws_parameter_memLp : The actual parameter error has finite second moment about every center.

pipglaLaws_state_memLp: the intermediate fact is X, in source order; the source steps apply or rewrite pipglaLaws_eq_lpLaw, memLp_map_measure_iff, fun_prop, Lp.memLp.

02Transport, equilibrium concentration, and center displacement §

Mathematical statement and proof

The smoothed target has sharp equilibrium radius dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}dθ​/(Nm)​, and the uniform gap gives ∥θU−θη∥≤ηK2/m\|\theta_U-\theta_\eta\|\le\sqrt{\eta K^2/m}∥θU​−θη​∥≤ηK2/m​. Projection of a coupling and Minkowski’s inequality therefore yield

RMS⁡ρn(θU)≤ηK2/m+dθ/(Nm)+W2(ρn,ΠN,η).\operatorname{RMS}_{\rho_n}(\theta_U)\le\sqrt{\eta K^2/m}+\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}+W_2(\rho_n,\Pi_{N,\eta}).RMSρn​​(θU​)≤ηK2/m​+dθ​/(Nm)​+W2​(ρn​,ΠN,η​).

For algorithm law ρn\rho_nρn​ and reference ΠN,η\Pi_{N,\eta}ΠN,η​, finite second moments ensure finite transport. The intermediate estimate is

Rθη(ρn)≤W2(ρn,ΠN,η)+dθ/(Nm).R_{\theta_\eta}(\rho_n)\le W_2(\rho_n,\Pi_{N,\eta})+\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}.Rθη​​(ρn​)≤W2​(ρn​,ΠN,η​)+dθ​/(Nm)​.

Changing the center by its deterministic displacement gives

RθU(ρn)≤W2(ρn,ΠN,η)+dθ/(Nm)+ηK2/m.R_{\theta_U}(\rho_n)\le W_2(\rho_n,\Pi_{N,\eta})+\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}+\sqrt{\eta K^2/m}.RθU​​(ρn​)≤W2​(ρn​,ΠN,η​)+dθ​/(Nm)​+ηK2/m​.

For each coupling the projection does not increase its quadratic cost, and the reference parameter error is bounded by dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}dθ​/(Nm)​. Taking the coupling infimum gives the transport contribution. The center change is at most ηK2/m\sqrt{\eta K^2/m}ηK2/m​ by the finite smoothing gap. These operations use the same smoothed target, and the subsequent splitting transport theorem supplies the remaining numerical bound.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.21.2; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.6; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.2; IPLA-SC, Section 3.5.1.
