Energy estimates for averaged pairwise proximal outputs

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

The pairwise algorithm averages the proposed parameter outputs and rescales the proposed latent outputs. It generally differs from a proximal minimizer of one objective on the shared state. Nevertheless Jensen’s inequality bounds its squared parameter error, and the latent squared errors satisfy exact scaling identities. Summing the single-pair proximal inequalities therefore gives the required full-state energy estimate for the stated algorithm.

The scaled state is z=(θ,x1/N,…,xN/N)z=(\theta,x_1/\sqrt N,\ldots,x_N/\sqrt N)z=(θ,x1​/N​,…,xN​/N​), with N>0N>0N>0, and Jiz=(θ,xi)J_i z=(\theta,x_i)Ji​z=(θ,xi​). The actual algorithm averages proximal parameter outputs; this module proves its energy inequality directly.

01Averaging squares in the shared parameter §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each parameter coordinate, Cauchy–Schwarz gives N(N−1∑ibi)2≤∑ibi2N(N^{-1}\sum_i b_i)^2\le\sum_i b_i^2N(N−1∑i​bi​)2≤∑i​bi2​. Each latent coordinate instead has an exact squared scaling identity after multiplication by NNN, using (N)2=N(\sqrt N)^2=N(N​)2=N. Adding all coordinate inequalities gives

∥Pair⁡P(z)−a∥2≤1N∑i∥P(Jiz)−Jia∥2.\|\operatorname{Pair}_P(z)-a\|^2\le\frac1N\sum_i\|P(J_i z)-J_i a\|^2.∥PairP​(z)−a∥2≤N1​i∑​∥P(Ji​z)−Ji​a∥2.N∣1N∑ipi,θ,j−aθ,j∣2≤∑i∣pi,θ,j−aθ,j∣2.N\left|\frac1N\sum_i p_{i,\theta,j}-a_{\theta,j}\right|^2\le\sum_i|p_{i,\theta,j}-a_{\theta,j}|^2.N​N1​i∑​pi,θ,j​−aθ,j​​2≤i∑​∣pi,θ,j​−aθ,j​∣2.

The first declaration is the scalar Jensen inequality Nbˉ2≤∑ibi2N\bar b^2\le\sum_i b_i^2Nbˉ2≤∑i​bi2​, with bˉ=N−1∑ibi\bar b=N^{-1}\sum_i b_ibˉ=N−1∑i​bi​. It follows by multiplying the average-of-squares inequality by positive NNN; hn supplies exactly that sign and the nonzero denominator.

The local identity hid inside hθ moves that constant through the average, and scalar Jensen gives

This is hx, using (N)2=N(\sqrt N)^2=N(N​)2=N. Expand the two Euclidean squared norms into finite coordinate sums, exchange the parameter and particle summations, and add the parameter inequalities to the exact latent identities. The result is N∥Pair⁡P(z)−a∥2≤∑i∥pi−Aia∥2N\|\operatorname{Pair}P(z)-a\|^2\le\sum_i\|p_i-A_i a\|^2N∥PairP(z)−a∥2≤∑i​∥pi​−Ai​a∥2; division by N>0N>0N>0 proves the displayed bound. 02Summation of the pairwise proximal energy inequalities §

Mathematical statement and proof

The averaged pairwise output need not minimize the shared-state proximal objective. The valid comparison is instead

∥Pair⁡P(z)−a∥2≤1N∑i=1N∥P(Jiz)−Jia∥2. \|\operatorname{Pair}_P(z)-a\|^2 \le\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\|P(J_i z)-J_i a\|^2. ∥PairP​(z)−a∥2≤N1​i=1∑N​∥P(Ji​z)−Ji​a∥2.

Apply the single-pair energy inequality to each term. The identities ∑i∥Ji(z−a)∥2=N∥z−a∥2\sum_i\|J_i(z-a)\|^2=N\|z-a\|^2∑i​∥Ji​(z−a)∥2=N∥z−a∥2 and G2=N−1∑ig2∘JiG_2=N^{-1}\sum_i g_2\circ J_iG2​=N−1∑i​g2​∘Ji​ give

∥Pair⁡Pλ(z)−a∥2≤∥z−a∥2−2λ(G2(z)−G2(a))+λ2C. \|\operatorname{Pair}_{P_\lambda}(z)-a\|^2 \le\|z-a\|^2-2\lambda\bigl(G_2(z)-G_2(a)\bigr)+\lambda^2C. ∥PairPλ​​(z)−a∥2≤∥z−a∥2−2λ(G2​(z)−G2​(a))+λ2C.

This estimate uses the minimizing property only for each individual pair. It is the deterministic energy statement required in the splitting analysis; it is not by itself a stochastic convergence theorem.

A counterexample to shared-state proximal optimality has N=2N=2N=2, g2(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣g_2(\theta,x)=|\theta-x|g2​(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣, proximal scale one, common parameter 111, and physical latent values (0,4)(0,4)(0,4). The pair minimizers are (1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2) and (2,3)(2,3)(2,3), so averaging their parameter components gives (5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3). The feasible shared-state competitor (1,1,3)(1,1,3)(1,1,3) has objective 3/23/23/2, strictly below the pairwise output value 51/3251/3251/32.

To derive the valid estimate, apply the pair inequality at AizA_i zAi​z against AiyA_i yAi​y and sum its NNN instances. The identity ∑i∥Ai(z−y)∥2=N∥z−y∥2\sum_i\|A_i(z-y)\|^2=N\|z-y\|^2∑i​∥Ai​(z−y)∥2=N∥z−y∥2 controls the initial squared distances. Divide by positive NNN; the potential term becomes N−1∑i[f(Aiz)−f(Aiy)]N^{-1}\sum_i[f(A_i z)-f(A_i y)]N−1∑i​[f(Ai​z)−f(Ai​y)]. Equivalently the initial sum is N∥z−a∥2N\|z-a\|^2N∥z−a∥2 and the potential sum is N[G2(z)−G2(a)]N[G_2(z)-G_2(a)]N[G2​(z)−G2​(a)], whose division by N>0N>0N>0 gives the displayed full-state expression. Jensen supplies the parameter inequality, exact latent scaling supplies the other coordinates, and the averaged subgradient bounds supply the remainder.

Formal correspondence: pairwise_prox_energy_le : The actual averaged pairwise proximal update satisfies the lifted energy estimate for the original nonsmooth potential. This proves the bridge needed in v3 Corollary A.13 directly, without a false prox -of-a-sum identity. The bound only needs one bounded subgradient at each pair input. In particular the paper's bound on the least-norm subgradient supplies this hypothesis whenever that minimal section is available.
