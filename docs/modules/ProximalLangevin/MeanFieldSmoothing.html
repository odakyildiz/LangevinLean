Finite smoothing comparison for actual mean-field equations

For this module the convex bounded-below g2g_2g2​ is additionally differentiable, with L2L_2L2​-Lipschitz gradient and global value Lipschitz constant KKK. The finite-horizon comparison uses the same initialization and additive input for the original and split-smoothed systems.

01At differentiability points subgradients equal the gradient §

Mathematical statement and proof

A supporting vector sss makes x↦f(x)−⟨s,x⟩x\mapsto f(x)-\langle s,x\ranglex↦f(x)−⟨s,x⟩ globally minimized at the support point ppp. Its derivative there is zero, so s=∇f(p)s=\nabla f(p)s=∇f(p). Apply this to the genuine proximal residual to obtain the exact identity ∇fη(x)=∇f(Pη(x))\nabla f^\eta(x)=\nabla f(P_\eta(x))∇fη(x)=∇f(Pη​(x)).

  • IsSubgradient.eq_gradient: A supporting subgradient of a differentiable function is its actual gradient.
  • gradient_moreau_eq_gradient_prox: The actual Moreau score is the original smooth score at the true proximal point.

02Smoothness gives a finite score defect §

Mathematical statement and proof

Compose ∇f\nabla f∇f, which is L2L_2L2​-Lipschitz, with the nonexpansive map PηP_\etaPη​. The envelope gradient therefore has constant L2L_2L2​, independent of η\etaη. The actual proximal displacement bound ∥x−Pη(x)∥≤ηK\|x-P_\eta(x)\|\le\eta K∥x−Pη​(x)∥≤ηK gives

∥∇f(x)−∇fη(x)∥≤L2∥x−Pη(x)∥≤ηL2K.\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f^\eta(x)\|\le L_2\|x-P_\eta(x)\|\le\eta L_2K.∥∇f(x)−∇fη(x)∥≤L2​∥x−Pη​(x)∥≤ηL2​K.

This is a finite inequality, without a Taylor remainder.

When the original convex function is differentiable, a subgradient at a point equals its gradient; evaluate support along both signed directions and take the directional derivative. Proximal optimality therefore gives ∇fη(x)=∇f(Pηx)\nabla f^\eta(x)=\nabla f(P_\eta x)∇fη(x)=∇f(Pη​x). If ∇f\nabla f∇f is L2L_2L2​-Lipschitz and fff is KKK-Lipschitz, then ∥∇fη(x)−∇f(x)∥≤L2∥Pηx−x∥≤ηL2K.\|\nabla f^\eta(x)-\nabla f(x)\|\le L_2\|P_\eta x-x\|\le\eta L_2K.∥∇fη(x)−∇f(x)∥≤L2​∥Pη​x−x∥≤ηL2​K. This finite score defect is the numerical forcing in the smoothing comparison.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.

  • gradient_moreau_defect_le: The uniform score defect is derived from proximal optimality and displacement. Its coefficient is independent of the smoothing parameter.

03Recover component regularity from the regular sum §

Mathematical statement and proof

If U=f+gU=f+gU=f+g and fff are differentiable, then g=U−fg=U-fg=U−f is differentiable. Gradient linearity gives ∇g=∇U−∇f\nabla g=\nabla U-\nabla f∇g=∇U−∇f, so gradient constants add: Lip⁡(∇g)≤LU+Lf\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla g)\le L_U+L_fLip(∇g)≤LU​+Lf​. This provides a legitimate L2L_2L2​ when only the original sum and the smooth part were specified.

05Construct both actual systems and quantify the smoothing error §

Mathematical statement and proof

For this result, additionally assume g2g_2g2​ is differentiable with an L2L_2L2​-Lipschitz gradient, while retaining convexity, the lower bound and the global value Lipschitz constant KKK. The supporting subgradient at a differentiability point must equal the actual gradient. Proximal optimality therefore gives the exact identity

∇g2η(v)=∇g2(Pη(v)). \nabla g_2^\eta(v)=\nabla g_2(P_\eta(v)). ∇g2η​(v)=∇g2​(Pη​(v)).

Nonexpansiveness of PηP_\etaPη​ preserves the smooth-score constant L2L_2L2​, and ∥v−Pη(v)∥≤ηK\|v-P_\eta(v)\|\le\eta K∥v−Pη​(v)∥≤ηK gives the finite score defect

∥∇g2(v)−∇g2η(v)∥≤ηL2K. \|\nabla g_2(v)-\nabla g_2^\eta(v)\|\le\eta L_2K. ∥∇g2​(v)−∇g2η​(v)∥≤ηL2​K.

No asymptotic expansion or integrated remainder is used. If the original sum U=g1+g2U=g_1+g_2U=g1​+g2​ is differentiable with LUL_ULU​-Lipschitz gradient and g1g_1g1​ has constant L1L_1L1​, the component regularity is derived with L2=LU+L1L_2=L_U+L_1L2​=LU​+L1​.

The development constructs original and split-smoothed L² integral solutions with the same initialization and additive input. Their input cancels. The actual drift operators differ by at most 2ηL2K2\eta L_2K2ηL2​K, and their common gradient constant is at most L1+L2L_1+L_2L1​+L2​, independently of η\etaη. Grönwall gives

sup⁡0≤t≤T∥Yt−Zt∥L2≤2ηL2K Ψ(2(L1+L2),T),Ψ(a,T)={(eaT−1)/a,a>0,T,a=0. \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|Y_t-Z_t\|_{L^2} \le 2\eta L_2K\,\Psi(2(L_1+L_2),T), \qquad \Psi(a,T)=\begin{cases}(e^{aT}-1)/a,&a>0,\\T,&a=0.\end{cases} 0≤t≤Tsup​∥Yt​−Zt​∥L2​≤2ηL2​KΨ(2(L1​+L2​),T),Ψ(a,T)={(eaT−1)/a,T,​a>0,a=0.​

Specialize the shared initialization to a constant parameter and the shared input to the latent-only Brownian construction to obtain the usual deterministic-parameter mean-field systems. The proved norm is the supremum over time of L² norms. It is not the expectation of a pathwise supremum in Proposition C.3. This is a consistent split-smoothing replacement with explicit assumptions and a finite linear-in-η\etaη norm bound.

Both systems are constructed with the same continuous additive input. Subtracting their equations cancels that input and leaves a differentiable integral error. One drift contributes its global Lipschitz constant times the current difference; the other contributes the uniform Moreau score defect just proved. Grönwall then bounds the actual process difference by a coefficient times η\etaη on the finite horizon. The component-regularity declarations justify the smoothness constants of each summand from the stated assumptions on the regular sum before this perturbation argument is applied.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.
