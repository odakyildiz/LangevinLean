PIPULA for the full envelope and its smoothed marginal maximizer

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

PIPULA applies the full-potential pairwise proximal map and then adds Gaussian noise. The residual identity makes it Euler Langevin for the full Moreau envelope at the same scale. The envelope has reduced curvature mγ=m/(1+mγ)m_\gamma=m/(1+m\gamma)mγ​=m/(1+mγ). Assume the original potential UUU is mmm-strongly convex and bounded below, with m,γ>0m,\gamma>0m,γ>0. No global Lipschitz assumption is imposed on UUU. The resulting estimate concerns the marginal maximizer of UγU^\gammaUγ and retains the scale dependence of the generic Euler error.

01The full-envelope curvature and admissible step §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set mγ=m/(1+mγ)>0m_\gamma=m/(1+m\gamma)>0mγ​=m/(1+mγ)>0. The inequality γmγ=mγ/(1+mγ)≤1\gamma m_\gamma=m\gamma/(1+m\gamma)\le1γmγ​=mγ/(1+mγ)≤1 gives γ(mγ+γ−1)≤2\gamma(m_\gamma+\gamma^{-1})\le2γ(mγ​+γ−1)≤2, equivalently γ≤2/(mγ+γ−1)\gamma\le2/(m_\gamma+\gamma^{-1})γ≤2/(mγ​+γ−1). Positivity of the denominators justifies the multiplications and divisions.

More specifically, mγ/(1+mγ)<1m\gamma/(1+m\gamma)<1mγ/(1+mγ)<1 implies γmγ+1≤2\gamma m_\gamma+1\le2γmγ​+1≤2. Since the envelope gradient constant is 1/γ1/\gamma1/γ, this is precisely the required Euler restriction. The contraction factor is 1−mγγ=1/(1+mγ)>01-m_\gamma\gamma=1/(1+m\gamma)>01−mγ​γ=1/(1+mγ)>0. These facts follow from strong convexity of the original potential and envelope regularity, without a global Lipschitz bound on the original potential’s values.

Formal correspondence: fullSmoothingModulus : Strong convexity after smoothing the entire potential. pipula_admissible_step : With smoothing equal to the time step, the envelope always meets the proved Gaussian ULA step restriction.

02Equality of the proximal-noise and envelope Euler laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

In the MYIPLA update set g1=0g_1=0g1​=0 and η=γ\eta=\gammaη=γ. Proximal optimality gives Pγ=I−γ∇UγP_\gamma=I-\gamma\nabla U^\gammaPγ​=I−γ∇Uγ, so the resulting recursion is IPLA for UγU^\gammaUγ.

The pointwise identity Pγ(v)=v−γ∇Uγ(v)P_\gamma(v)=v-\gamma\nabla U^\gamma(v)Pγ​(v)=v−γ∇Uγ(v), applied to every pair, gives the scaled Euler drift. Adding the same Gaussian vector after proximity gives the PIPULA update. Equality at initialization and at each successor proves equality of all finite iterates; their measurable pushforwards therefore agree as probability laws.

Formal correspondence: pipulaLaws : The probability law defined by the paper's pairwise PIPULA recursion. pipulaLaws_eq_iplaLaws : Exact equality between the displayed PIPULA law and IPLA for the genuine full Moreau envelope.

03The proved estimate about the smoothed marginal center §

Mathematical statement and proof

The update specialization g1=0g_1=0g1​=0 and λ=γ\lambda=\gammaλ=γ identifies PIPULA with full-envelope IPLA. The source’s claim about error relative to the original marginal maximizer requires additional analysis: its proximal inner-product deficit μ\muμ gives envelope-gradient monotonicity coefficient μ/γ\mu/\gammaμ/γ because the residual is divided by γ\gammaγ. A scale-uniform discretization estimate and the source’s rate relative to the original marginal maximizer are not established by substituting smoothness 1/γ1/\gamma1/γ into a generic Euler bound.

The proved estimate instead assumes a bounded-below mmm-strongly convex full potential. For γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 its envelope curvature is mγ=m/(1+mγ)m_\gamma=m/(1+m\gamma)mγ​=m/(1+mγ) and its gradient constant is 1/γ1/\gamma1/γ. The generic error term is (33/20)(1/(γmγ))dx+dθ/Nγ(33/20)(1/(\gamma m_\gamma))\sqrt{d_x+d_\theta/N}\sqrt\gamma(33/20)(1/(γmγ​))dx​+dθ​/N​γ​, which diverges as γ↓0\gamma\downarrow0γ↓0. It therefore does not reproduce the claimed rate O(γ)O(\sqrt\gamma)O(γ​) relative to the original marginal maximizer.

Write mγ=m/(1+mγ)m_\gamma=m/(1+m\gamma)mγ​=m/(1+mγ), let ΠN,γ\Pi_{N,\gamma}ΠN,γ​ be the envelope Gibbs law, and let θγ\theta_\gammaθγ​ be its marginal maximizer. Sharp concentration and the Gaussian particle estimate give

The theorem uses gradient constant γ−1\gamma^{-1}γ−1. For a separately denoted envelope scale, the curvature is mη=m/(1+mη)m_\eta=m/(1+m\eta)mη​=m/(1+mη) rather than mmm and the gradient constant is 1/η1/\eta1/η; the required restriction is γ≤2/(mη+1/η)\gamma\le2/(m_\eta+1/\eta)γ≤2/(mη​+1/η). At equal proximal and Euler scales, the preceding algebra proves it automatically. The equilibrium radius uses this reduced curvature, and the error coefficient uses (1/γ)/mγ(1/\gamma)/m_\gamma(1/γ)/mγ​. Consequently the valid finite-step estimate is about the smoothed center and does not prove vanishing error as γ↓0\gamma\downarrow0γ↓0. A uniform conclusion relative to the original marginal maximizer would require a stronger envelope analysis.
