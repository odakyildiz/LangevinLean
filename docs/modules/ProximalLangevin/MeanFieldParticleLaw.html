One-time Wasserstein convergence for tagged particles

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

At each fixed time the tagged particle pair and its reference copy are defined on the same probability space. Both have finite second moments, and the measure-preserving copy has exactly the original mean-field law. Their joint distribution is consequently an admissible finite-cost coupling. The coordinate L2L^2L2 estimate bounds its cost and yields the one-time quadratic Wasserstein rate for the constructed particle process.

The constructed particle/reference pair supplies an actual finite-cost coupling of tagged one-time laws. This module transfers the proved timewise L2L^2L2 rate to W2W_2W2​, keeping the original integral equations and initialization.

01The unchanged law of a measure-preserving copy §

Mathematical statement and proof

The copy representative is X(ωi)X(\omega_i)X(ωi​) almost surely. Product evaluation pushes Q⊗NQ^{\otimes N}Q⊗N to QQQ. Composition of pushforwards therefore gives LQ⊗N(CiX)=LQ(X)\mathcal L_{Q^{\otimes N}}(C_iX)=\mathcal L_Q(X)LQ⊗N​(Ci​X)=LQ​(X).

Almost-sure replacement of representatives preserves this law. Thus the reference coordinate in the tagged coupling has precisely the original mean-field distribution.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

Formal correspondence: iidCopyCLM_law : An actual iid L2L^2L2 copy has exactly the original law.

02The coupling of the tagged pair and its reference copy §

Mathematical statement and proof

On the product space couple particle pair (ΘN,XiN)(\Theta^N,X_i^N)(ΘN,XiN​) with the corresponding copy of (θ,X)(\theta,X)(θ,X). Both belong to L2L^2L2, so their quadratic coupling cost is finite. Pair reconstruction and Minkowski give

W2(L(ΘN,XiN),L(θ,X))≤∥ΘN−θ∥2+∥XiN−Xi∥2.W_2(\mathcal L(\Theta^N,X_i^N),\mathcal L(\theta,X))\le\|\Theta^N-\theta\|_2+\|X_i^N-X_i\|_2.W2​(L(ΘN,XiN​),L(θ,X))≤∥ΘN−θ∥2​+∥XiN​−Xi​∥2​.

The particle pair (ΘtN,Xti,N)(\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N})(ΘtN​,Xti,N​) and copied reference have the required marginal laws. Their L2L^2L2 moments justify the quadratic cost, whose root is bounded by the sum of coordinate L2L^2L2 differences. For latent coordinate iii, the same reconstruction estimate in L2L^2L2 gives the displayed inequality. Transport, as the infimum over couplings, is no larger. Its finiteness is thus proved before real conversion.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.12; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

tagged_particle_wasserstein_le defines X as the tagged particle pair and R as its reference independent copy. ULA.wasserstein2_le_Lp_cost and its real version bound transport by this explicit square-integrable coupling. iidCopyCLM_law identifies the reference marginal with the original one-time mean-field law. lpJointPair_difference_le bounds the coupling cost by the parameter and tagged-latent errors, and iidMeanFieldCopies_joint identifies the corresponding copied pair. Finiteness is returned before the numerical bound.

03The one-time law estimate for the specified particle equation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The pair (ΘtN,Xti,N)(\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N})(ΘtN​,Xti,N​) has finite second moment, and its reference copy has the law of Yt=(θt,Xt)Y_t=(\theta_t,X_t)Yt​=(θt​,Xt​). The explicit coupling and coordinate comparison give

W2 ⁣(L(ΘtN,Xti,N),L(θt,Xt))≤CTN,0≤t≤T. W_2\!\left(\mathcal L(\Theta_t^N,X_t^{i,N}),\mathcal L(\theta_t,X_t)\right) \le\frac{C_T}{\sqrt N},\qquad 0\le t\le T. W2​(L(ΘtN​,Xti,N​),L(θt​,Xt​))≤N​CT​​,0≤t≤T.

For every positive NNN, tag, and time, the conclusion retains the empirical-gradient integral equation, independent copied initialization, and copied latent Brownian input plus scaled parameter Brownian input. Thus the law estimate refers to those specified processes.

At each fixed time the coupling proves finite W2W_2W2​ and bound CT/NC_T/\sqrt NCT​/N​, uniformly over the stated interval. This is convergence of tagged one-time laws. A pathwise strong stochastic equation, expected path-supremum bound, or gradient-flow partial differential equation is not asserted by this result.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

particleBrownianInput combines the copied latent noise with the empirical parameter noise. exists_canonical_meanField_particle_law_comparison obtains the constructed mean-field and particle solutions from exists_canonical_meanField_particle_comparison. For each positive particle count, it rewrites the integral equation using particleBrownianInput. tagged_particle_wasserstein_le then converts the pathwise root second-moment comparison into finite transport for every tagged pair at each time. Its final bound is the same finite-horizon inverse-square-root particle estimate. It does not infer a time-uniform comparison or an invariant law.
