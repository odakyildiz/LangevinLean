01Identify actual algorithm laws in the comparison §

Mathematical statement and proof

Rewrite both output laws as the Gaussian Markov recursions of the shared smoothed drift. The initialization pushforward has finite second moment because its representative belongs to L2L^2L2. The iterated noise-removal theorem now proves finite transport distance and W2(ρnPGD,ρnIPLA)2≤2dθ/(Nm)W_2(\rho_n^{\rm PGD},\rho_n^{\rm IPLA})^2\le2d_\theta/(Nm)W2​(ρnPGD​,ρnIPLA​)2≤2dθ​/(Nm).

02Transfer parameter RMS through a nonexpansive projection §

Mathematical statement and proof

The parameter map has operator norm at most one. The projected RMS transport inequality yields RMS⁡PGD(θ)≤W2(ρnPGD,ρnIPLA)+RMS⁡IPLA(θ)\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm PGD}(\theta)\le W_2(\rho_n^{\rm PGD},\rho_n^{\rm IPLA})+\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm IPLA}(\theta)RMSPGD​(θ)≤W2​(ρnPGD​,ρnIPLA​)+RMSIPLA​(θ). MYIPLA finite moments and the finite comparison imply the MYPGD moment is finite before passing to real square-root integrals. Taking the square root of the comparison gives the added radius 2dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}2dθ​/(Nm)​.

Finite second moments of both algorithm laws give finite transport. Projecting a coupling onto the common parameter, whose operator norm is at most one, gives RMYPGD(θ∗)≤RMYIPLA(θ∗)+W2(ρnMYPGD,ρnMYIPLA).R_{\mathrm{MYPGD}}(\theta_*)\le R_{\mathrm{MYIPLA}}(\theta_*)+W_2(\rho_n^{\mathrm{MYPGD}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{MYIPLA}}).RMYPGD​(θ∗​)≤RMYIPLA​(θ∗​)+W2​(ρnMYPGD​,ρnMYIPLA​). The actual-law comparison bounds the squared last term by 2dθ/(Nm)2d_\theta/(Nm)2dθ​/(Nm); nonnegative square roots therefore bound it by 2dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{2d_\theta/(Nm)}2dθ​/(Nm)​. The final theorem inserts the already proved original-center MYIPLA estimate, retaining its smoothing, equilibrium and discretization contributions.

03Insert the proved original-center MYIPLA theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the parameter-projection transport inequality to the two actual laws, then use the proved MYIPLA original-center theorem. With Lη=L1+1/ηL_\eta=L_1+1/\etaLη​=L1​+1/η and the assumptions above,

(E∥θnPGD−θ⋆∥2)1/2≤2dθNm+ηK2m+dθNm+(1−mγ)nW2(ρ0,ΠN,η)+3320Lηmdx+dθNγ. \begin{aligned} \left(\mathbb E\|\theta^{\mathrm{PGD}}_n-\theta_\star\|^2\right)^{1/2} \le{}&\sqrt{\frac{2d_\theta}{Nm}} +\sqrt{\frac{\eta K^2}{m}}+\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta}{Nm}}\\ &+(1-m\gamma)^nW_2(\rho_0,\Pi_{N,\eta})\\ &+\frac{33}{20}\frac{L_\eta}{m} \sqrt{d_x+\frac{d_\theta}{N}}\sqrt\gamma. \end{aligned} (E∥θnPGD​−θ⋆​∥2)1/2≤​Nm2dθ​​​+mηK2​​+Nmdθ​​​+(1−mγ)nW2​(ρ0​,ΠN,η​)+2033​mLη​​dx​+Ndθ​​​γ​.​

Every term is finite under the primitive assumptions, and every law in the statement is the actual defined algorithm law. This is a genuine finite-particle replacement for Appendix B’s Theorem B.1. It has a Gibbs initial-law transient and an explicit conservative comparison term; it does not reproduce the paper’s mean-field metric, constants, or stronger Taylor smoothing expression.
