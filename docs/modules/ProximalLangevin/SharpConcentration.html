Sharp Gibbs concentration by dilation

Let FFF be continuous and qqq-strongly convex on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of dimension ddd, with q>0q>0q>0 and global minimizer zzz. Gaussian domination supplies the integrals used below.

02Take the sharp limit and normalize §

Mathematical statement and proof

Multiply the dilation inequality by rdr^drd and define Φ(r)=rd[Z+(q/2)(1−r2)M]\Phi(r)=r^d[Z+(q/2)(1-r^2)M]Φ(r)=rd[Z+(q/2)(1−r2)M]. Then Φ(r)≤Z=Φ(1)\Phi(r)\le Z=\Phi(1)Φ(r)≤Z=Φ(1) for 0<r<10<r<10<r<1. Its left difference quotients at one are nonnegative. Differentiating only this scalar polynomial expression gives Φ′(1)=dZ−qM≥0\Phi'(1)=dZ-qM\ge0Φ′(1)=dZ−qM≥0, so M≤dZ/qM\le dZ/qM≤dZ/q. The partition function ZZZ is finite and strictly positive, as proved by strong-convexity Gaussian domination. The actual Gibbs normalization gives

∫∥x−z∥2π(dx)=M/Z≤d/q,π(dx)=Z−1e−F(x)dx.\int\|x-z\|^2\pi(dx)=M/Z\le d/q,\qquad\pi(dx)=Z^{-1}e^{-F(x)}dx.∫∥x−z∥2π(dx)=M/Z≤d/q,π(dx)=Z−1e−F(x)dx.

The proof differentiates no potential and remains valid for continuous nonsmooth strongly convex FFF.

Here hZ proves Z>0Z>0Z>0, and hpart identifies the extended partition function with ofReal⁡Z\operatorname{ofReal}ZofRealZ. The density change in heq therefore gives ∫∥x−z∥2 dπ(x)=Z−1M.\int\|x-z\|^2\,d\pi(x)=Z^{-1}M.∫∥x−z∥2dπ(x)=Z−1M. Positive normalization is proved from the potential assumptions, rather than supplied as a moment or probability premise.
