Exact algebraic corrections and an averaged-proximity counterexample

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

The examples below establish specific algebraic facts used to qualify the source arguments: the sign of a quadratic proximal expansion, scaling of a squared norm, the inverse-step factor in residual monotonicity, and failure of shared-state proximal optimality for averaged pairwise outputs. The convergence results use the finite potential-gap and summed energy inequalities proved above. Each example has only the scope stated for its own assumptions.

This module contains explicit mathematical certificates. Each claim is limited to its stated algebraic example or implication; these certificates do not claim that every stronger result in the reference paper is false.

01The second-order sign in the quadratic proximal expansion §

Mathematical statement and proof

The proximal equation p+λ∇g2(p)=xp+\lambda\nabla g_2(p)=xp+λ∇g2​(p)=x gives a positive second-order coefficient in the scalar quadratic example:

prox⁡u2/2λ(x)=x1+λ=x−λx+λ2x−λ3x1+λ. \operatorname{prox}_{u^2/2}^\lambda(x) =\frac{x}{1+\lambda} =x-\lambda x+\lambda^2x-\frac{\lambda^3x}{1+\lambda}. proxu2/2λ​(x)=1+λx​=x−λx+λ2x−1+λλ3x​.

The negative coefficient printed in the source leaves error at least λ2\lambda^2λ2 at x=1x=1x=1 and therefore cannot be its third-order expansion. The quadratic is not globally value Lipschitz, so this calculation diagnoses the displayed algebra rather than refuting a full theorem under that assumption.

Almost-everywhere twice differentiability and the stated moment condition also do not provide the uniform integrable third-order remainder needed for the claimed order O(λ)O(\lambda)O(λ) for displacement of the original marginal maximizer. The proved finite-gap result gives λK2/m\sqrt{\lambda K^2/m}λK2/m​; this difference in established scope does not prove the stronger assertion false. For f(u)=u2/2f(u)=u^2/2f(u)=u2/2 the minimizer equation is (1+λ)p=x(1+\lambda)p=x(1+λ)p=x. Division gives the exact resolvent and its positive λ2x\lambda^2xλ2x term with the displayed third-order remainder.

Formal correspondence: quadratic_prox_minimizes : The proximal point of u ↦ u²/2 is x/(1+lam) . The difference of objectives is a nonnegative square; no differentiability of an argmin map is assumed. quadratic_prox_expansion : The second-order coefficient in the proximal expansion has a positive sign. This exact identity diagnoses the opposite sign in v1 equation (25) and v3 equation (22). negative_second_order_residual : The negative second-order term printed in v1 equation (25) / v3 equation (22) leaves an error of order lam² , already for the scalar quadratic.

02Squared scaling and the unit recurrence ratio §

Mathematical statement and proof

Multiplication of a vector by NNN multiplies its squared norm by N2N^2N2, by norm homogeneity and squaring. Separately, an affine recurrence with unit ratio has finite sum nnn and therefore a linear-in-iteration additive bound, rather than a bounded geometric sum. These identities determine the particle powers and the strict positive-denominator condition needed in an energy or transport estimate.

Norm homogeneity gives ∥Ng∥2=N2∥g∥2\|Ng\|^2=N^2\|g\|^2∥Ng∥2=N2∥g∥2. The numerical case N=2,∥g∥=1N=2,\|g\|=1N=2,∥g∥=1 disproves linear squared-gradient scaling. Substituting λ=γ/(1−μγ)\lambda=\gamma/(1-\mu\gamma)λ=γ/(1−μγ) gives 1−q=01-q=01−q=0. Lean’s total real division has 1/0=01/0=01/0=0; the numerical certificate 1≰0+1/(1−1)1\not\le0+1/(1-1)1≤0+1/(1−1) shows why an unguarded formula at that endpoint cannot express an error floor.

Formal correspondence: squared_gradient_scaling : Scaling a gradient by N scales its squared norm by N² , not N . This corrects v1 Corollary A.5; v3 Corollary A.12 already uses this scaling. linear_squared_gradient_scaling_fails : A numerical witness to the invalid NC bound from the valid C bound when the gradient is multiplied by N . endpoint_denominator_zero : The geometric-series denominator is zero at the upper endpoint allowed in v1 Corollaries A.4--A.6 and v3 Corollaries A.11--A.13. A finite error floor requires a strict inequality. endpoint_error_floor_fails : At q=1 , the recurrence e(n+1)=e(n)+1 has linear growth. Interpreting real division by zero cannot rescue the printed error floor.

04A counterexample to shared-state proximal optimality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take g(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣g(\theta,x)=|\theta-x|g(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣ and proximal scale one. The pair inputs (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) have proximal minimizers (1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2) and (2,3)(2,3)(2,3). Averaging their parameter components gives (5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3). The shared-state objective is

12(∣θ−x1∣+∣θ−x2∣)+12(θ−1)2+14(x12+(x2−4)2).\tfrac12(|\theta-x_1|+|\theta-x_2|)+\tfrac12(\theta-1)^2+\tfrac14(x_1^2+(x_2-4)^2).21​(∣θ−x1​∣+∣θ−x2​∣)+21​(θ−1)2+41​(x12​+(x2​−4)2).

The competitor (1,1,3)(1,1,3)(1,1,3) has strictly smaller objective, as follows from direct absolute-value evaluation. Each pair was allowed its own proposed parameter, whereas the shared objective permits only one. Averaging the proposals need not solve that common minimization problem.

In particular the averaged output has value 51/3251/3251/32 and the feasible comparator has value 3/2=48/323/2=48/323/2=48/32. This strict comparison disproves the shared-state proximal identity in general. The splitting proof instead sums the valid single-pair energy inequalities and applies Jensen’s inequality to the parameter average.

Formal correspondence: pairAbsObjective : The pair objective for the convex, globally Lipschitz penalty g₂(theta,x)=|theta-x| with smoothing parameter one. pairAbs_first_prox : At pair input (1,0) , the true proximal point is (1/2,1/2) . pairAbs_second_prox : At pair input (1,4) , the true proximal point is (2,3) . sharedAbsObjective : The two-particle lifted objective in physical particle coordinates. The quadratic penalty uses precisely the scaled state norm with N=2 . averaged_pair_prox_not_lifted_minimizer : Averaging the two true pairwise proximal outputs gives parameter 5/4 and particles (1/2,3) . A feasible competitor (1,1,3) has strictly smaller lifted objective. Thus the pairwise average cannot be a lifted proximal minimizer, even for a convex globally Lipschitz penalty.
