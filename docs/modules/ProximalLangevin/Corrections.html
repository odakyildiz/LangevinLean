01The quadratic proximal expansion has positive second order §

Mathematical statement and proof

On page 15, v3 equation (22) prints a negative second-order term in the proximal expansion. Differentiating p+λ∇g2(p)=xp+\lambda\nabla g_2(p)=xp+λ∇g2​(p)=x gives a positive second derivative contribution. The scalar quadratic supplies an exact certificate:

prox⁡u2/2λ(x)=x1+λ=x−λx+λ2x−λ3x1+λ. \operatorname{prox}_{u^2/2}^\lambda(x) =\frac{x}{1+\lambda} =x-\lambda x+\lambda^2x-\frac{\lambda^3x}{1+\lambda}. proxu2/2λ​(x)=1+λx​=x−λx+λ2x−1+λλ3x​.

The negative second-order expression leaves an error at least λ2\lambda^2λ2 when x=1x=1x=1, so it cannot be that quadratic's third-order expansion. This diagnoses the displayed algebra; the quadratic is not globally Lipschitz and is not by itself a counterexample to the full final theorem under A3.

Separately, twice differentiability almost everywhere and the stated moment bound do not supply a uniform integrable third-order remainder. The original O(λ)O(\lambda)O(λ) minimizer claim is therefore not reproduced here. The proved uniform-gap argument replaces it with λK2/m\sqrt{\lambda K^2/m}λK2/m​. A proof gap does not establish that the stronger conclusion is false.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.

  • quadratic_prox_minimizes: The proximal point of u ↦ u²/2 is x/(1+lam). The difference of objectives is a nonnegative square; no differentiability of an argmin map is assumed.
  • quadratic_prox_expansion: The second-order coefficient in the proximal expansion has a positive sign. This exact identity diagnoses the opposite sign in v1 equation (25) and v3 equation (22).
  • negative_second_order_residual: The negative second-order term printed in v1 equation (25) / v3 equation (22) leaves an error of order lam², already for the scalar quadratic.

02Squared scaling and the geometric endpoint §

Mathematical statement and proof

Norm homogeneity gives ∥Ng∥2=N2∥g∥2\|Ng\|^2=N^2\|g\|^2∥Ng∥2=N2∥g∥2. The numerical case N=2,∥g∥=1N=2,\|g\|=1N=2,∥g∥=1 disproves linear squared-gradient scaling. Substituting λ=γ/(1−μγ)\lambda=\gamma/(1-\mu\gamma)λ=γ/(1−μγ) gives 1−q=01-q=01−q=0. Lean’s total real division has 1/0=01/0=01/0=0; the numerical certificate 1≰0+1/(1−1)1\not\le0+1/(1-1)1≤0+1/(1−1) shows why an unguarded formula at that endpoint cannot express an error floor.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.

  • squared_gradient_scaling: Scaling a gradient by N scales its squared norm by N², not N. This corrects v1 Corollary A.5; v3 Corollary A.12 already uses this scaling.
  • linear_squared_gradient_scaling_fails: A numerical witness to the invalid NC bound from the valid C bound when the gradient is multiplied by N.
  • endpoint_denominator_zero: The geometric-series denominator is zero at the upper endpoint allowed in v1 Corollaries A.4--A.6 and v3 Corollaries A.11--A.13. A finite error floor requires a strict inequality.
  • endpoint_error_floor_fails: At q=1, the recurrence e(n+1)=e(n)+1 has linear growth. Interpreting real division by zero cannot rescue the printed error floor.

04A shared parameter prevents the lifted-proximal identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the convex pair function g(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣g(\theta,x)=|\theta-x|g(θ,x)=∣θ−x∣ and unit proximal parameter. The actual pair objectives show that inputs (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) have minimizers (1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2) and (2,3)(2,3)(2,3). Averaging their parameter outputs gives (5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3)(5/4,1/2,3). The shared lifted objective is

12(∣θ−x1∣+∣θ−x2∣)+12(θ−1)2+14(x12+(x2−4)2).\tfrac12(|\theta-x_1|+|\theta-x_2|)+\tfrac12(\theta-1)^2+\tfrac14(x_1^2+(x_2-4)^2).21​(∣θ−x1​∣+∣θ−x2​∣)+21​(θ−1)2+41​(x12​+(x2​−4)2).

Direct absolute-value inequalities certify the two pair minima. Exact arithmetic shows that the competitor (1,1,3)(1,1,3)(1,1,3) has strictly smaller shared objective than the averaged output. Therefore averaged pairwise proximity is not in general the proximal map of the lifted sum.

The final counterexample keeps a single parameter shared across two pair objectives. Minimizing each pair separately allows two different parameter choices; averaging them afterwards need not solve the single shared-parameter minimization problem. The explicit absolute-value objectives make each pair minimizer and the failed shared optimality test computable. This is why the main splitting proof uses summed pairwise energy and Jensen, rather than treating its averaged map as a lifted proximal minimizer.

Correspondence with the displayed declarations.

  • pairAbsObjective: The pair objective for the convex, globally Lipschitz penalty g₂(theta,x)=|theta-x| with smoothing parameter one.
  • pairAbs_first_prox: At pair input (1,0), the true proximal point is (1/2,1/2).
  • pairAbs_second_prox: At pair input (1,4), the true proximal point is (2,3).
  • sharedAbsObjective: The two-particle lifted objective in physical particle coordinates. The quadratic penalty uses precisely the scaled state norm with N=2.
  • averaged_pair_prox_not_lifted_minimizer: Averaging the two true pairwise proximal outputs gives parameter 5/4 and particles (1/2,3). A feasible competitor (1,1,3) has strictly smaller lifted objective. Thus the pairwise average cannot be a lifted proximal minimizer, even for a convex globally Lipschitz penalty.
