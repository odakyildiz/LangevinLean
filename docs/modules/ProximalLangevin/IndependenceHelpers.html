Independence under grouping of random variables

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Independent source variables may be grouped into an initialized past and a future increment by factoring their joint distributions and reassociating product measures. The results below use joint independence of the specified blocks. Pairwise independence of three separate variables would not imply the required independence of one variable from a pair.

Statement and proof

01An independent family with two indices §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume X⊥YX\perp YX⊥Y and define Zfalse=XZ_{\rm false}=XZfalse​=X and Ztrue=YZ_{\rm true}=YZtrue​=Y. To verify independence of this two-element family, consider its finite index subsets. The empty subset uses probability mass one, and each singleton gives an identity. For the subset of both indices the condition is exactly P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B)P(X\in A,Y\in B)=P(X\in A)P(Y\in B)P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B).

Thus two-variable independence is equivalent to the factorization required for the corresponding two-element independent family. No additional independence condition is introduced by this indexing.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

An independent family is defined by factorization for every finite set of indices. For a Boolean index there are four possibilities: neither variable, only XXX, only YYY, or both. The singleton identities are tautological; the empty family has probability one because μ\muμ is a probability; the two-variable case is exactly the assumed independence of XXX and YYY. The two membership case splits in Lean exhaust these four possibilities explicitly.

Formal correspondence: iIndepFun_bool_of_indepFun : Two independent random variables form an independent family indexed by Bool . The probability assumption supplies the empty-family identity.

iIndepFun_bool_of_indepFun: the cases are separated by by_cases hf : false ∈ s; the intermediate facts include hs, hi, in source order; the source steps apply or rewrite iIndepFun_iff_measure_inter_preimage_eq_mul, Bool.forall_bool, h.measure_inter_preimage_eq_mul.

02Reassociation of a jointly independent triple §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume measurable variables satisfy X⊥(Y,Z)X\perp(Y,Z)X⊥(Y,Z) and Y⊥ZY\perp ZY⊥Z. Their triple law factors as μX⊗(μY⊗μZ)\mu_X\otimes(\mu_Y\otimes\mu_Z)μX​⊗(μY​⊗μZ​). Product reassociation gives (μX⊗μY)⊗μZ(\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y)\otimes\mu_Z(μX​⊗μY​)⊗μZ​, while projection gives μ(X,Y)=μX⊗μY\mu_{(X,Y)}=\mu_X\otimes\mu_Yμ(X,Y)​=μX​⊗μY​. Hence (X,Y)⊥Z(X,Y)\perp Z(X,Y)⊥Z.

Indeed XXX is independent of the entire pair (Y,Z)(Y,Z)(Y,Z), and YYY and ZZZ are independent. These hypotheses give μX⊗(μY⊗μZ)\mu_X\otimes(\mu_Y\otimes\mu_Z)μX​⊗(μY​⊗μZ​), whose reassociation is (μX⊗μY)⊗μZ(\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y)\otimes\mu_Z(μX​⊗μY​)⊗μZ​. This proves independence of (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) and ZZZ. The first two product marginals are the joint law of (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y), so the conclusion is (X,Y)⊥Z(X,Y)\perp Z(X,Y)⊥Z. Equality of measurable pushforward measures proves the statement also for path-valued variables. The hypotheses are stronger than mere pairwise independence.
