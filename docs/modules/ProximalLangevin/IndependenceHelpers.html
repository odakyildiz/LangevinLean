01Two independent variables form a Boolean family §

Mathematical statement and proof

Given X⊥YX\perp YX⊥Y, define Zfalse=XZ_{\rm false}=XZfalse​=X, Ztrue=YZ_{\rm true}=YZtrue​=Y. The finite-family intersection criterion has four possible index subsets: the empty set, either singleton, or both indices. Probability mass one handles the empty case, the singletons are identities, and the two-element case is exactly P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B)P(X\in A,Y\in B)=P(X\in A)P(Y\in B)P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B).

An independent family is defined by factorization for every finite set of indices. For a Boolean index there are four possibilities: neither variable, only XXX, only YYY, or both. The singleton identities are tautological; the empty family has probability one because μ\muμ is a probability; the two-variable case is exactly the assumed independence of XXX and YYY. The two membership case splits in Lean exhaust these four possibilities explicitly.

02Regroup a jointly independent triple §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume X⊥(Y,Z)X\perp(Y,Z)X⊥(Y,Z) and Y⊥ZY\perp ZY⊥Z, with measurable variables. The joint pushforward is μX⊗(μY⊗μZ)\mu_X\otimes(\mu_Y\otimes\mu_Z)μX​⊗(μY​⊗μZ​). Use the measurable product-associativity equivalence to rewrite it as (μX⊗μY)⊗μZ(\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y)\otimes\mu_Z(μX​⊗μY​)⊗μZ​. Projection also proves μ(X,Y)=μX⊗μY\mu_{(X,Y)}=\mu_X\otimes\mu_Yμ(X,Y)​=μX​⊗μY​, so (X,Y)⊥Z(X,Y)\perp Z(X,Y)⊥Z. The joint independence premise is essential.

The hypotheses say that XXX is independent of the whole pair (Y,Z)(Y,Z)(Y,Z), and that YYY and ZZZ are independent. Therefore the triple law is μX⊗(μY⊗μZ)\mu_X\otimes(\mu_Y\otimes\mu_Z)μX​⊗(μY​⊗μZ​). Product associativity rewrites it as (μX⊗μY)⊗μZ(\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y)\otimes\mu_Z(μX​⊗μY​)⊗μZ​, proving independence of (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) and ZZZ. The proof uses equality of pushforward measures under the measurable associator, so this is joint independence; pairwise independence of three unrelated variables would not suffice.
