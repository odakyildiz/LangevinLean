Local smooth drift defect and the future cross term

Let a continuous Hilbert-valued reference satisfy Yt−Y0=−∫0tg(Ys)ds+BtY_t-Y_0=-\int_0^t g(Y_s)ds+B_tYt​−Y0​=−∫0t​g(Ys​)ds+Bt​, with ∥g(Yt)∥≤G\|g(Y_t)\|\le G∥g(Yt​)∥≤G and ∥Bt∥≤σt\|B_t\|\le\sigma\sqrt t∥Bt​∥≤σt​. Only the smooth-part Lipschitz constant enters the local defect.

01Integrate the smooth drift increment §

Mathematical statement and proof

The integral equation implies ∥Yt−Y0∥≤Gt+σt\|Y_t-Y_0\|\le Gt+\sigma\sqrt t∥Yt​−Y0​∥≤Gt+σt​. If aaa is LLL-Lipschitz and 0≤t≤h0\le t\le h0≤t≤h, then ∥a(Yt)−a(Y0)∥≤L(Gh+σh)\|a(Y_t)-a(Y_0)\|\le L(Gh+\sigma\sqrt h)∥a(Yt​)−a(Y0​)∥≤L(Gh+σh​). Integrating over an interval of length hhh yields

∥∫0h(a(Yt)−a(Y0))dt∥≤Lh(Gh+σh).\left\|\int_0^h(a(Y_t)-a(Y_0))dt\right\|\le Lh(Gh+\sigma\sqrt h).​∫0h​(a(Yt​)−a(Y0​))dt​≤Lh(Gh+σh​).

For the integral solution Yt−Y0=−∫0tg(Ys)ds+BtY_t-Y_0=-\int_0^t g(Y_s)ds+B_tYt​−Y0​=−∫0t​g(Ys​)ds+Bt​, assume ∥g(Ys)∥≤G\|g(Y_s)\|\le G∥g(Ys​)∥≤G and ∥Bt∥≤νt\|B_t\|\le\nu\sqrt t∥Bt​∥≤νt​ in the Hilbert space under consideration. Then ∥Yt−Y0∥≤tG+νt\|Y_t-Y_0\|\le tG+\nu\sqrt t∥Yt​−Y0​∥≤tG+νt​. If only the smooth part aaa is LLL-Lipschitz, ∥∫0h(a(Yt)−a(Y0))dt∥≤Lh(Gh+νh).\left\|\int_0^h(a(Y_t)-a(Y_0))dt\right\|\le Lh(Gh+\nu\sqrt h).​∫0h​(a(Yt​)−a(Y0​))dt​≤Lh(Gh+νh​). The code first bounds the integrand uniformly for 0≤t≤h0\le t\le h0≤t≤h, using monotonicity of ttt and t\sqrt tt​, and then applies the interval-integral norm inequality. The Lipschitz constant of the full drift ensures regularity but does not enter this numerical bound.

02Cancel future noise before estimating the remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

Subtract the reference equations at t,ht,ht,h and use adjacent-interval additivity:

Yt−Yh=∫thg(Ys)ds−(Bh−Bt).Y_t-Y_h=\int_t^h g(Y_s)ds-(B_h-B_t).Yt​−Yh​=∫th​g(Ys​)ds−(Bh​−Bt​).

If ∥b∥≤K\|b\|\le K∥b∥≤K and ⟨b,Bh−Bt⟩=0\langle b,B_h-B_t\rangle=0⟨b,Bh​−Bt​⟩=0, the noise term cancels exactly. Cauchy–Schwarz and the drift bound give ⟨Yt−Yh,b⟩≤(h−t)GK\langle Y_t-Y_h,b\rangle\le(h-t)GK⟨Yt​−Yh​,b⟩≤(h−t)GK. This avoids an order-h−t\sqrt{h-t}h−t​ loss.

Subtract the two integral equations and split the integral at ttt: Yt−Yh=∫thg(Ys)ds−(Bh−Bt).Y_t-Y_h=\int_t^h g(Y_s)ds-(B_h-B_t).Yt​−Yh​=∫th​g(Ys​)ds−(Bh​−Bt​). Pair this identity with bbb. The explicit orthogonality assumption cancels the future-noise contribution before any norm estimate, leaving ⟨Yt−Yh,b⟩≤∥∫thg(Ys)ds∥∥b∥≤(h−t)GK.\langle Y_t-Y_h,b\rangle\le\left\|\int_t^h g(Y_s)ds\right\|\|b\|\le(h-t)GK.⟨Yt​−Yh​,b⟩≤​∫th​g(Ys​)ds​∥b∥≤(h−t)GK. In the probabilistic application this is an inner product in L2L^2L2, so orthogonality means the expected inner product is zero; it is not a samplewise cancellation of Brownian increments.
