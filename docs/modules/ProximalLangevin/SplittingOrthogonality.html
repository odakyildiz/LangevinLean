Derive Brownian future orthogonality from the actual past

Let the scalar Brownian coordinates have independent full paths, measurable evaluations, and initialization independent of the whole Brownian family. The actual Langevin integral solution is measurable from initialization and the stopped noise past.

01Assemble independent vector past and shifted future §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each scalar Brownian path has independent shifted future Bi(T+s)−Bi(T)B_i(T+s)-B_i(T)Bi​(T+s)−Bi​(T) and stopped past Bi(min⁡(t,T))B_i(\min(t,T))Bi​(min(t,T)). Encode these as a Boolean pair for each coordinate. Full-path independence makes the coordinate rows independent; flatten the rows and Boolean columns, then take the disjoint sets of past and future indices. Regrouping proves independence of the entire vector path families.

The stopped past records each coordinate path up to time ttt, while the future block records increments after ttt. Brownian independent increments give this separation within each scalar coordinate. Independence of the whole coordinate paths permits regrouping the coordinatewise pairs into one vector past and one vector future. This is a statement about the generated product sigma-algebras, so it remains valid when the future vector is paired with any measurable function of the entire past.

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the pointwise vector Bt=2(B(t+)−B(0))\mathcal B_t=\sqrt2(B(t_+)-B(0))Bt​=2​(B(t+​)−B(0)), using the nonnegative-time conversion t+=max⁡(t,0)t_+=\max(t,0)t+​=max(t,0). 03Add the independent initialization to the stopped past §

Mathematical statement and proof

The stopped noise path and a future increment are independent. Initialization is independent of their joint pair, so the regrouping lemma proves that initialization together with the stopped past is independent of the future. An auxiliary result then compares sigma-algebras: every variable almost everywhere strongly measurable for the natural initialization/noise past inherits this independence. Almost everywhere replacement transfers the result to actual L2L^2L2 representatives.

The initialized natural past contains both the initial state and the stopped Brownian path. Independence of initialization from the entire Brownian source, together with past/future Brownian independence, permits the triple regrouping (initial,past)∣future(\text{initial},\text{past})\mid\text{future}(initial,past)∣future. An almost-strongly-measurable past variable can be replaced by a measurable representative without changing its law or independence. This is the role of the representative arguments: independence is transferred through almost-everywhere equality, rather than assumed for the later integral solution.

04Centering and independence imply Hilbert orthogonality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Every scalar Brownian coordinate has mean zero. Commute the finite coordinate projections through the Bochner integral to get EBt=0\mathbb E B_t=0EBt​=0, then subtract and scale for EBt=0\mathbb E\mathcal B_t=0EBt​=0. For independent L2L^2L2 variables X,WX,WX,W, bilinear integration gives ⟨X,W⟩L2=⟨EX,EW⟩\langle X,W\rangle_{L^2}=\langle\mathbb E X,\mathbb E W\rangle⟨X,W⟩L2​=⟨EX,EW⟩. If WWW is centered this is zero.

05Apply adaptedness of the constructed integral solution §

Mathematical statement and proof

The invariant-subspace construction for the actual integral equation proves that YtY_tYt​ is measurable for the natural past. A Lipschitz observable b(Yt)b(Y_t)b(Yt​) remains past measurable and in L2L^2L2. The preceding independence and zero-mean results therefore prove

⟨b(Yt),Bh−Bt⟩L2=0,0≤t≤h.\langle b(Y_t),\mathcal B_h-\mathcal B_t\rangle_{L^2}=0,\qquad0\le t\le h.⟨b(Yt​),Bh​−Bt​⟩L2​=0,0≤t≤h.

This essential cancellation is a conclusion of the Brownian and integral-equation construction.

The constructed solution is adapted to the initialized natural past, so the Lipschitz observable b(Yt)b(Y_t)b(Yt​) is measurable there. The future Brownian increment is independent of this past and has zero mean. Both variables belong to L2L^2L2, hence their inner product is integrable, and independence factors its expectation as E⟨b(Yt),Bh−Bt⟩=⟨Eb(Yt),E(Bh−Bt)⟩=0.\mathbb E\langle b(Y_t),B_h-B_t\rangle=\left\langle\mathbb E b(Y_t),\mathbb E(B_h-B_t)\right\rangle=0.E⟨b(Yt​),Bh​−Bt​⟩=⟨Eb(Yt​),E(Bh​−Bt​)⟩=0. The L2L^2L2 inner product is precisely this integral. Thus the final orthogonality input of the splitting proof follows from adaptedness, centering and the constructed Brownian law.
