Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

MYPGD retains the Moreau residual drift and latent Gaussian noise of MYIPLA while removing its parameter Gaussian component. Equality of the update maps identifies the expanded proximal recursion with the masked-noise Euler recursion. The independent Gaussian-sequence assumptions then identify its laws with the corresponding iterated Markov transition, to which the finite-particle noise comparison applies.

01The recursion with latent-projected innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Replace the innovation ξ\xiξ in MYIPLA by PxξP_x\xiPx​ξ. This defines MYPGD. The recursion is MYIPLA driven by sequence (Pxξk)k(P_x\xi_k)_k(Px​ξk​)k​ because initialization and successor updates agree.

The deterministic terms are identical at every step; only the supplied innovation is projected. Induction therefore proves equality of the pointwise chains before any distributional assumptions are made. Their laws are subsequently the measurable pushforwards of this same recursion.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

02The common deterministic smoothed gradient map §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let Fη=∑i(g1+g2η)(θ,xi)F_\eta=\sum_i(g_1+g_2^\eta)(\theta,x_i)Fη​=∑i​(g1​+g2η​)(θ,xi​) and h=γ/Nh=\gamma/Nh=γ/N. The residual-envelope identity gives the update

Xk+1=Xk−h∇Fη(Xk)+2h Pxξk. X_{k+1}=X_k-h\nabla F_\eta(X_k)+\sqrt{2h}\,P_x\xi_k. Xk+1​=Xk​−h∇Fη​(Xk​)+2h​Px​ξk​.

The parameter coordinate has no additive Gaussian term, while each physical latent coordinate has coefficient 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. Finite-second-moment initialization and independent standard Gaussian coordinates, independent of initialization, define the transition laws. The unused parameter Gaussians may remain on the probability space for comparison with MYIPLA; no initial independent-particle assumption is required.

The bound Lip⁡(I−(γ/N)∇Fη)≤1+∣γ/N∣ NLη\operatorname{Lip}(I-(\gamma/N)\nabla F_\eta)\le1+|\gamma/N|\,NL_\etaLip(I−(γ/N)∇Fη​)≤1+∣γ/N∣NLη​ ensures continuity and measurable finite-moment updates without imposing contraction. In particular the deterministic map is D(z)=z−(γ/N)∇Fη(z)D(z)=z-(\gamma/N)\nabla F_\eta(z)D(z)=z−(γ/N)∇Fη​(z), and the physical latent noise coefficient is 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. The stricter step restriction is required only for the quantitative contractive comparison.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.4.

03The Markov transition from independent Gaussian coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Mutual independence of all scalar Gaussian innovations gives independent standard vector innovations, independent of initialization. Consequently the two supplied chains have the Markov transitions D+aPxξD+aP_x\xiD+aPx​ξ and D+aξD+a\xiD+aξ.

The common deterministic map is D(z)=z−(γ/N)∇VN,η(z)D(z)=z-(\gamma/N)\nabla V_{N,\eta}(z)D(z)=z−(γ/N)∇VN,η​(z). MYPGD adds 2γ/NPxξ\sqrt{2\gamma/N}P_x\xi2γ/N​Px​ξ and MYIPLA adds 2γ/Nξ\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\xi2γ/N​ξ. At time kkk the current state is a measurable function of initialization and preceding innovations, so the next Gaussian vector is independent of it. Projection gives the latent-only kernel; retention of the full vector gives the MYIPLA kernel. Induction identifies their supplied laws with the iterated transition laws used in the comparison theorem.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.12; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6; ULA-SC, Section 3.41.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.41.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.2.
