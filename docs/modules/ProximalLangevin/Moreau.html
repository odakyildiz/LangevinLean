Moreau envelope: gradient, smoothness and finite bias

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Let fff be finite, convex, and bounded below on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and let λ>0\lambda>0λ>0. Set P=PλP=P_\lambdaP=Pλ​, fλ(x)=Qλ,x(Px)f^\lambda(x)=Q_{\lambda,x}(P x)fλ(x)=Qλ,x​(Px), and r(x)=(x−Px)/λr(x)=(x-Px)/\lambdar(x)=(x−Px)/λ. Comparison of minimizing objectives at neighboring inputs gives a two-sided quadratic remainder, proving that the envelope gradient is the scaled proximal residual. A global Lipschitz bound on the original function also bounds the residual and the uniform difference between the original function and its envelope.

01Value bounds and the exact residual §

Mathematical statement and proof

The envelope is the minimum objective value. Using competitor p=xp=xp=x gives fλ(x)≤f(x)f^\lambda(x)\le f(x)fλ(x)≤f(x). If f≥bf\ge bf≥b, nonnegativity of the quadratic term gives fλ≥bf^\lambda\ge bfλ≥b. These comparisons prove that the envelope is finite and satisfies the same lower bound at every input.

The residual at a minimizing point is a subgradient of the original function. Identification with the derivative of the envelope requires the two neighboring-objective comparisons below; it does not follow from the value bounds alone.

Formal correspondence: moreau : Moreau envelope, evaluated at the selected true proximal point. residual : The proximal residual, which is the actual Moreau gradient on its domain. moreau_le : The Moreau envelope lies below the original potential. le_moreau : The Moreau envelope is above every common lower bound of the potential.

02Global supports and quadratic upper remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the residual support at PxP xPx with competitor PyP yPy. Addition of the two quadratic penalties leaves a nonnegative squared norm, giving fλ(x)+⟨r(x),y−x⟩≤fλ(y)f^\lambda(x)+\langle r(x),y-x\rangle\le f^\lambda(y)fλ(x)+⟨r(x),y−x⟩≤fλ(y). In the other direction, use PxP xPx as competitor for the objective at yyy. Expanding its penalty gives upper remainder ∥y−x∥2/(2λ)\|y-x\|^2/(2\lambda)∥y−x∥2/(2λ).

For z=ax+byz=ax+byz=ax+by, apply the global envelope supports based at zzz to the two endpoints. Multiply by weights a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 satisfying a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1. Their linear terms cancel, proving convexity. The lower comparison uses the support of the original function at PλxP_\lambda xPλ​x, whereas the upper comparison uses only the old minimizer as an admissible competitor. Its quadratic remainder is ∥y−x∥2/(2λ)\|y-x\|^2/(2\lambda)∥y−x∥2/(2λ).

Formal correspondence: moreau_support : The proximal residual supports the Moreau envelope globally. moreau_upper : Quadratic upper Taylor bound for the Moreau envelope. moreau_convex : Convexity of the envelope is a consequence of its global supports.

03Nonexpansiveness and the derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define g2λ(x)=Qλ,x(Pλ(x))g_2^\lambda(x)=Q_{\lambda,x}(P_\lambda(x))g2λ​(x)=Qλ,x​(Pλ​(x)) and rλ(x)=λ−1(x−Pλ(x))r_\lambda(x)=\lambda^{-1}(x-P_\lambda(x))rλ​(x)=λ−1(x−Pλ​(x)). The residual support gives the lower comparison and the old minimizer as competitor gives the upper comparison:

0≤g2λ(y)−g2λ(x)−⟨rλ(x),y−x⟩≤∥y−x∥22λ. 0\le g_2^\lambda(y)-g_2^\lambda(x) -\langle r_\lambda(x),y-x\rangle \le\frac{\|y-x\|^2}{2\lambda}. 0≤g2λ​(y)−g2λ​(x)−⟨rλ​(x),y−x⟩≤2λ∥y−x∥2​.

The upper remainder is o(∥y−x∥)o(\|y-x\|)o(∥y−x∥), so ∇g2λ=rλ\nabla g_2^\lambda=r_\lambda∇g2λ​=rλ​. The global supports also give convexity. Nonexpansiveness of I−PλI-P_\lambdaI−Pλ​ yields

∥∇g2λ(x)−∇g2λ(y)∥≤λ−1∥x−y∥. \|\nabla g_2^\lambda(x)-\nabla g_2^\lambda(y)\| \le\lambda^{-1}\|x-y\|. ∥∇g2λ​(x)−∇g2λ​(y)∥≤λ−1∥x−y∥.

For y=x+hy=x+hy=x+h, the remainder divided by ∥h∥\|h\|∥h∥ is bounded by ∥h∥/(2λ)\|h\|/(2\lambda)∥h∥/(2λ), which tends to zero. Thus the Fréchet derivative is h↦⟨rλ(x),h⟩h\mapsto\langle r_\lambda(x),h\rangleh↦⟨rλ​(x),h⟩. The residual-map inequality gives gradient Lipschitz constant 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ, hence continuity of the gradient. These facts justify smooth Langevin analysis for the envelope even when the original function is nonsmooth.

The key derivative estimate is the two-sided remainder 0≤fλ(x+h)−fλ(x)−⟨rλ(x),h⟩≤∥h∥22λ.0\le f^\lambda(x+h)-f^\lambda(x)-\langle r_\lambda(x),h\rangle\le\frac{\|h\|^2}{2\lambda}.0≤fλ(x+h)−fλ(x)−⟨rλ​(x),h⟩≤2λ∥h∥2​. Dividing by ∥h∥\|h\|∥h∥ makes the upper bound ∥h∥/(2λ)→0\|h\|/(2\lambda)\to0∥h∥/(2λ)→0. The declaration moreau_hasGradientAt packages this Fréchet remainder estimate, moreau_differentiable forgets the derivative value, and gradient_moreau identifies the derivative's Riesz vector with the residual. The continuity of that residual, already obtained from nonexpansiveness of I−PλI-P_\lambdaI−Pλ​, upgrades differentiability to C1C^1C1.

Formal correspondence: prox_lipschitz : The selected proximal mapping is nonexpansive. residual_lipschitz : The actual envelope residual has the sharp 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ Lipschitz constant. moreau_hasGradientAt : The Moreau envelope is differentiable, with gradient equal to its proximal residual. moreau_differentiable : Differentiability of the actual Moreau envelope. gradient_moreau : Exact formula for the actual Moreau gradient. moreau_contDiff : The envelope is continuously differentiable, even when fff is nonsmooth.

04Bounded residual and a sharp uniform gap §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume fff is convex, bounded below, and globally KKK-Lipschitz, with K≥0K\ge0K≥0 and λ>0\lambda>0λ>0. At input xxx, write p=Pλ(x)p=P_\lambda(x)p=Pλ​(x) and r=(x−p)/λr=(x-p)/\lambdar=(x−p)/λ. The optimality condition gives r∈∂f(p)r\in\partial f(p)r∈∂f(p). Test this support at p+rp+rp+r and apply the Lipschitz bound:

∥r∥2≤f(p+r)−f(p)≤K∥r∥. \|r\|^2\le f(p+r)-f(p)\le K\|r\|. ∥r∥2≤f(p+r)−f(p)≤K∥r∥.

For r=0r=0r=0 the residual bound is immediate; otherwise cancellation of its positive norm gives ∥r∥≤K\|r\|\le K∥r∥≤K. From x−p=λrx-p=\lambda rx−p=λr one obtains ∥x−p∥≤λK\|x-p\|\le\lambda K∥x−p∥≤λK.

To bound the value gap, competitor p=xp=xp=x gives fλ(x)≤f(x)f^\lambda(x)\le f(x)fλ(x)≤f(x). Set d=∥p−x∥d=\|p-x\|d=∥p−x∥ and subtract the minimizing objective value:

f(x)−fλ(x)=f(x)−f(p)−d22λ≤Kd−d22λ=λK22−(d−λK)22λ≤λK22. \begin{aligned} f(x)-f^\lambda(x) &=f(x)-f(p)-\frac{d^2}{2\lambda}\\ &\le Kd-\frac{d^2}{2\lambda}\\ &=\frac{\lambda K^2}{2}-\frac{(d-\lambda K)^2}{2\lambda} \le\frac{\lambda K^2}{2}. \end{aligned} f(x)−fλ(x)​=f(x)−f(p)−2λd2​≤Kd−2λd2​=2λK2​−2λ(d−λK)2​≤2λK2​.​

The squared remainder has a positive denominator because λ>0\lambda>0λ>0. Together with the upper comparison this proves 0≤f(x)−fλ(x)≤λK2/20\le f(x)-f^\lambda(x)\le\lambda K^2/20≤f(x)−fλ(x)≤λK2/2 at every input. For U=g1+fU=g_1+fU=g1​+f and Vλ=g1+fλV_\lambda=g_1+f^\lambdaVλ​=g1​+fλ the same estimate becomes Vλ≤U≤Vλ+λK2/2V_\lambda\le U\le V_\lambda+\lambda K^2/2Vλ​≤U≤Vλ​+λK2/2. Exponentiation and latent integration then compare marginal potentials by the same finite gap. No asymptotic expansion or interchange of an unbounded remainder with integration is required.

Formal correspondence: residual_norm_le : The Moreau gradient has norm at most the Lipschitz constant of fff. prox_displacement_le : A Lipschitz potential moves each proximal point by at most λK\lambda KλK. moreau_lower_of_lipschitz : Sharp uniform approximation bound for a KKK-Lipschitz potential. moreau_gap_of_lipschitz : The pointwise Moreau correction is nonnegative and at most λK2/2\lambda K^2/2λK2/2.
