Grönwall comparison with an extra continuous noise input

In a Banach space let FFF be LLL-Lipschitz. Compare actual integral equations with a common input BBB, one extra continuous input AAA, and a reference forcing GGG satisfying a finite drift defect.

01Subtract the extra input before applying integral Grönwall §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose ∥At∥≤a\|A_t\|\le a∥At​∥≤a, ∥F(Yt)−Gt∥≤C\|F(Y_t)-G_t\|\le C∥F(Yt​)−Gt​∥≤C, and

Xt−x=−∫0tF(Xs)ds+Bt+At,Yt−x=−∫0tGsds+Bt.X_t-x=-\int_0^tF(X_s)ds+B_t+A_t,\qquad Y_t-x=-\int_0^tG_sds+B_t.Xt​−x=−∫0t​F(Xs​)ds+Bt​+At​,Yt​−x=−∫0t​Gs​ds+Bt​.

Set Dt=Xt−Yt−AtD_t=X_t-Y_t-A_tDt​=Xt​−Yt​−At​. The common input cancels and ∥Dt∥≤∫0t[L∥Ds∥+La+C]ds\|D_t\|\le\int_0^t[L\|D_s\|+La+C]ds∥Dt​∥≤∫0t​[L∥Ds​∥+La+C]ds. Grönwall yields ∥Xt−Yt∥≤(C+La)Ψ(L,T)+a\|X_t-Y_t\|\le(C+La)\Psi(L,T)+a∥Xt​−Yt​∥≤(C+La)Ψ(L,T)+a on [0,T][0,T][0,T]. The proof uses continuous integral equations and never differentiates the additional input.

The inputs B,AB,AB,A may be continuous Brownian paths in L2L^2L2; differentiating them would be invalid. Instead set Dt=Xt−Yt−AtD_t=X_t-Y_t-A_tDt​=Xt​−Yt​−At​. Subtracting the two actual integral equations cancels BBB and AAA, leaving Dt=−∫0t[F(Xs)−Gs]ds,D0=0.D_t=-\int_0^t[F(X_s)-G_s]ds,\quad D_0=0.Dt​=−∫0t​[F(Xs​)−Gs​]ds,D0​=0. Continuity of the integrands proves that this remainder has derivative −F(Xt)+Gt-F(X_t)+G_t−F(Xt​)+Gt​.

If FFF is LLL-Lipschitz, ∥At∥≤a\|A_t\|\le a∥At​∥≤a and ∥F(Yt)−Gt∥≤C\|F(Y_t)-G_t\|\le C∥F(Yt​)−Gt​∥≤C, then ∥Dt′∥≤L∥Dt∥+C+La.\|D'_t\|\le L\|D_t\|+C+La.∥Dt′​∥≤L∥Dt​∥+C+La. The right-derivative Grönwall theorem gives ∥Dt∥≤gronwallBound⁡(0,L,C+La,T)\|D_t\|\le\operatorname{gronwallBound}(0,L,C+La,T)∥Dt​∥≤gronwallBound(0,L,C+La,T) uniformly on [0,T][0,T][0,T]. Adding back AtA_tAt​ produces the final +a+a+a. In Lean, hDI identifies the differentiable integral remainder, hderiv proves its derivative, and hb establishes this exact scalar forcing.

02Factor a common inverse sampling scale §

Mathematical statement and proof

The explicit zero-initial-error Grönwall function is linear in its forcing coefficient, including its L=0L=0L=0 branch. Thus GB⁡(0,L,C/r,T)=GB⁡(0,L,C,T)/r\operatorname{GB}(0,L,C/r,T)=\operatorname{GB}(0,L,C,T)/rGB(0,L,C/r,T)=GB(0,L,C,T)/r. If r>0r>0r>0, ∥At∥≤a/r\|A_t\|\le a/r∥At​∥≤a/r and ∥F(Yt)−Gt∥≤C/r\|F(Y_t)-G_t\|\le C/r∥F(Yt​)−Gt​∥≤C/r, substitute into the preceding theorem and factor:

∥Xt−Yt∥≤(C+La)Ψ(L,T)+ar.\|X_t-Y_t\|\le\frac{(C+La)\Psi(L,T)+a}{r}.∥Xt​−Yt​∥≤r(C+La)Ψ(L,T)+a​.

The zero-initial-error Grönwall response is linear in its forcing: G(0,L,C/r,T)=G(0,L,C,T)/rG(0,L,C/r,T)=G(0,L,C,T)/rG(0,L,C/r,T)=G(0,L,C,T)/r, also in the L=0L=0L=0 branch. Apply the preceding theorem with forcing C/rC/rC/r and extra-input bound a/ra/ra/r, where r>0r>0r>0. Factor (C+La)/r(C+La)/r(C+La)/r and use that homogeneity to obtain the common 1/r1/r1/r factor in the conclusion. In the particle application r=Nr=\sqrt Nr=N​, so the entire coefficient is independent of NNN.
