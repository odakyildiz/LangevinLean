02The residual characterizes the minimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

A vector sss is a subgradient of g2g_2g2​ at ppp when g2(p)+⟨s,y−p⟩≤g2(y)g_2(p)+\langle s,y-p\rangle\le g_2(y)g2​(p)+⟨s,y−p⟩≤g2​(y) for every yyy. The exact proximal optimality condition is

p=Pλ(x)⟺x−pλ∈∂g2(p). p=P_\lambda(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{x-p}{\lambda}\in\partial g_2(p). p=Pλ​(x)⟺λx−p​∈∂g2​(p).

For necessity, compare the minimizing objective at ppp with its value along p+t(y−p)p+t(y-p)p+t(y−p), use convexity, and let positive ttt become arbitrarily small. For sufficiency, insert the supporting-hyperplane inequality into the difference of the two quadratic objectives. This proves the global minimum directly, without assuming differentiability of g2g_2g2​.

Apply monotonicity of subgradients to the residuals at two inputs. It gives firm nonexpansiveness,

∥Pλ(x)−Pλ(y)∥2≤⟨x−y,Pλ(x)−Pλ(y)⟩. \|P_\lambda(x)-P_\lambda(y)\|^2 \le\langle x-y,P_\lambda(x)-P_\lambda(y)\rangle. ∥Pλ​(x)−Pλ​(y)∥2≤⟨x−y,Pλ​(x)−Pλ​(y)⟩.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives nonexpansiveness of PλP_\lambdaPλ​. Expanding the square gives nonexpansiveness of I−PλI-P_\lambdaI−Pλ​ as well. Those are derived geometric facts, not premises stored in a proximal-map structure.

To see the necessity calculation explicitly, fix yyy, put d=y−pd=y-pd=y−p, and compare the proximal objective at ppp with the competitor p+tdp+tdp+td, 0<t≤10<t\le10<t≤1. Convexity bounds f(p+td)f(p+td)f(p+td) by (1−t)f(p)+tf(y)(1-t)f(p)+tf(y)(1−t)f(p)+tf(y). After expanding the square and dividing by positive ttt, f(y)−f(p)≥⟨x−pλ,d⟩−t2λ∥d∥2.f(y)-f(p)\ge\left\langle\frac{x-p}{\lambda},d\right\rangle-\frac{t}{2\lambda}\|d\|^2.f(y)−f(p)≥⟨λx−p​,d⟩−2λt​∥d∥2. Apply subgradient monotonicity to (x−p)/λ(x-p)/\lambda(x−p)/λ and (y−q)/λ(y-q)/\lambda(y−q)/λ. Multiplying by λ\lambdaλ gives ∥p−q∥2≤⟨x−y,p−q⟩\|p-q\|^2\le\langle x-y,p-q\rangle∥p−q∥2≤⟨x−y,p−q⟩. 04Compact attainment and the chosen map §

Mathematical statement and proof

For λ>0\lambda>0λ>0, define

Qλ,x(p)=g2(p)+∥p−x∥22λ,Pλ(x)=argmin⁡pQλ,x(p). Q_{\lambda,x}(p)=g_2(p)+\frac{\|p-x\|^2}{2\lambda},\qquad P_\lambda(x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_p Q_{\lambda,x}(p). Qλ,x​(p)=g2​(p)+2λ∥p−x∥2​,Pλ​(x)=pargmin​Qλ,x​(p).

A finite-valued convex function on a finite-dimensional real space is continuous. If g2≥bg_2\ge bg2​≥b, then outside a sufficiently large ball the quadratic term makes Qλ,x(p)Q_{\lambda,x}(p)Qλ,x​(p) exceed Qλ,x(x)=g2(x)Q_{\lambda,x}(x)=g_2(x)Qλ,x​(x)=g2​(x). A continuous function attains its minimum on that closed ball, and the same point therefore minimizes it globally. The proof explicitly constructs a suitable radius from 2λ(g2(x)−b)+1\sqrt{2\lambda(g_2(x)-b)}+12λ(g2​(x)−b)​+1.

The Lean definition chooses a minimizer when one exists and assigns zero outside that domain. All mathematical endpoints establish existence from convexity, the lower bound, and positive λ\lambdaλ; their conclusions do not depend on that fallback value. Strict convexity of the quadratic, expressed through the variational characterization below, gives uniqueness.

The existence argument first uses finite-dimensional convexity of a finite-valued function to obtain continuity. If f≥bf\ge bf≥b, a radius R>2λ(f(x)−b)R>\sqrt{2\lambda(f(x)-b)}R>2λ(f(x)−b)​ ensures Qλ,x(p)>f(x)=Qλ,x(x)Q_{\lambda,x}(p)>f(x)=Q_{\lambda,x}(x)Qλ,x​(p)>f(x)=Qλ,x​(x) whenever ∥p−x∥>R\|p-x\|>R∥p−x∥>R. A minimum on the compact closed ball is therefore global. The chosen-map theorem invokes this existence result under its explicit assumptions, then uniqueness identifies all minimizers. Consequently later occurrences of prox denote the actual unique minimizer on the positive-parameter domain.
