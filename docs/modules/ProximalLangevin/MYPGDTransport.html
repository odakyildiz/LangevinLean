A coupling of latent-only and full Gaussian transitions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Let rrr-Lipschitz map DDD define the common deterministic update. Compare D(x)+aPxξD(x)+aP_x\xiD(x)+aPx​ξ and D(y)+aξD(y)+a\xiD(y)+aξ using the same independent standard Gaussian vector. The omitted parameter block has a known variance, and its mixed expectation with the input discrepancy is zero. For probability input laws with finite second moments, this constructs a finite-cost coupling of the two transition laws.

01Finite moments of Lipschitz images and finite coupling costs §

Mathematical statement and proof

For map DDD, the bound ∥D(x)−D(0)∥≤r∥x∥\|D(x)-D(0)\|\le r\|x\|∥D(x)−D(0)∥≤r∥x∥ and addition of its constant value at zero show preservation of L2L^2L2. If two L2L^2L2 vectors are defined on one probability space, their joint distribution is a coupling. Its quadratic cost bounds W2W_2W2​ and yields W2(L(X),L(Y))2≤E∥X−Y∥2W_2(\mathcal L(X),\mathcal L(Y))^2\le\mathbb E\|X-Y\|^2W2​(L(X),L(Y))2≤E∥X−Y∥2.

A joint realization (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) is thus an admissible candidate in the infimum defining W2W_2W2​. The same finite squared separation proves transport finiteness and the numerical upper bound. The map need not vanish at zero because its constant part has finite second moment under a probability measure.

A globally Lipschitz map DDD need not vanish at zero. The first lemma writes D(X)=[D(X)−D(0)]+D(0)D(X)=[D(X)-D(0)]+D(0)D(X)=[D(X)−D(0)]+D(0): the centered map preserves L2L^2L2, and the constant belongs to L2L^2L2 because the measure is a probability. For the transport lemma, convert X,YX,YX,Y to their L2L^2L2 classes. Their joint realization is an admissible coupling of their pushforwards, so W2(X#P,Y#P)2≤∥X−Y∥L2(P)2=∫∥X−Y∥2dP.W_2(X_\#P,Y_\#P)^2\le\|X-Y\|_{L^2(P)}^2=\int\|X-Y\|^2dP.W2​(X#​P,Y#​P)2≤∥X−Y∥L2(P)2​=∫∥X−Y∥2dP. The representative equalities hx, hy prove that these pushforwards are the original laws, and the finite norm bound proves finite Wasserstein distance before squaring its real value.

Formal correspondence: memLp_lipschitz_comp : Globally Lipschitz maps preserve finite quadratic moments on a probability space. wasserstein_sq_le_integral_pair : Direct finite-cost transport estimate for an actual L2L^2L2 pair.

02Measurability and moments of the two Gaussian transitions §

Mathematical statement and proof

The transition maps are Tx(u,ξ)=D(u)+aPxξT_x(u,\xi)=D(u)+aP_x\xiTx​(u,ξ)=D(u)+aPx​ξ and T(u,ξ)=D(u)+aξT(u,\xi)=D(u)+a\xiT(u,ξ)=D(u)+aξ. Continuity of DDD, linearity of PxP_xPx​, and continuity of addition make them jointly measurable. On μ⊗N(0,I)\mu\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)μ⊗N(0,I) both summands belong to L2L^2L2.

The squared norm of a sum is at most twice the sum of squared norms, so both output distributions have finite second moments. This integrability also justifies expansion and Fubini in the subsequent exact mixed-term calculation. The product distribution explicitly separates the input from the fresh noise.

Both updates use a fresh Z∼N(0,I)Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)Z∼N(0,I), independent of the input: D(x)+aPxZD(x)+aP_xZD(x)+aPx​Z and D(x)+aZD(x)+aZD(x)+aZ. Their measurability follows by composing continuous DDD, the coordinate projections, addition and scalar multiplication. For moment propagation, comp_fst pulls the L2L^2L2 drift onto the input–Gaussian product and comp_snd pulls the Gaussian noise onto that same space. The sum is in L2L^2L2; memLp_map_measure_iff then transfers this fact to the output law. Thus the next coupling argument has actual finite-moment marginals.

Formal correspondence: latentGaussianUpdate : The deterministic map followed by latent-only Gaussian noise. fullGaussianUpdate : The same map followed by full Gaussian noise.

03Extension of an input coupling by independent Gaussian noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

Given any finite-cost input coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, extend it independently to c⊗N(0,I)c\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)c⊗N(0,I). The output marginals are the latent-only and full Gaussian transitions. Their difference is D(x)−D(y)−aPθξD(x)-D(y)-aP_\theta\xiD(x)−D(y)−aPθ​ξ. The zero mixed-term identity gives squared cost Ec∥D(x)−D(y)∥2+a2dθ\mathbb E_c\|D(x)-D(y)\|^2+a^2d_\thetaEc​∥D(x)−D(y)∥2+a2dθ​, at most r2cost⁡(c)2+a2dθr^2\operatorname{cost}(c)^2+a^2d_\thetar2cost(c)2+a2dθ​ by Lipschitz continuity.

The first marginal uses law μ\muμ and latent Gaussian noise, while the second uses ν\nuν and full Gaussian noise. In random-vector notation the difference is D(X)−D(Y)−aPθξD(X)-D(Y)-aP_\theta\xiD(X)−D(Y)−aPθ​ξ. Independence gives mean square at most r2E∥X−Y∥2+a2dθr^2\mathbb E\|X-Y\|^2+a^2d_\thetar2E∥X−Y∥2+a2dθ​. Thus the same bound holds for every finite-cost input coupling, which permits the Wasserstein infimum without choosing an optimal coupling.

Start from any input coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, and work on c⊗N(0,I)c\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)c⊗N(0,I). Define F=D(X)+aPxZF=D(X)+aP_xZF=D(X)+aPx​Z and H=D(Y)+aZH=D(Y)+aZH=D(Y)+aZ. The facts hFlaw and hHlaw check separately that their marginals are the desired two Markov transitions; this ensures the constructed pair is a coupling of the actual algorithms.

The cancellation in hid is F−H=D(X)−D(Y)−aPθZ.F-H=D(X)-D(Y)-aP_\theta Z.F−H=D(X)−D(Y)−aPθ​Z. By the product Gaussian identity, its mean square is Ec∥D(X)−D(Y)∥2+a2dθ\mathbb E_c\|D(X)-D(Y)\|^2+a^2d_\thetaEc​∥D(X)−D(Y)∥2+a2dθ​. If DDD is rrr-Lipschitz, the first term is bounded by r2Ec∥X−Y∥2r^2\mathbb E_c\|X-Y\|^2r2Ec​∥X−Y∥2. Finally hnorm identifies the latter integral with the square of the coupling cost. All three random variables are proved square integrable before any of these integral identities is used.
