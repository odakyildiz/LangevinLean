For N>0N>0N>0, independent centered square-integrable Hilbert-valued vectors have zero mixed expected inner products. Expanding the finite squared sum and integrating termwise therefore gives an exact 1/N1/N1/N variance factor for their sample mean. The finite product measure supplies the required independence. Applying the identity to the parameter-gradient observable gives the empirical mean-field force fluctuation.

01The centered off-diagonal expectations §

Mathematical statement and proof

View the supplied L2L^2L2 functions as L2L^2L2 vectors. Cauchy–Schwarz makes each pairwise inner product integrable, so finite expansion gives

E∥∑iXi∥2=∑i,jE⟨Xi,Xj⟩.\mathbb E\left\|\sum_iX_i\right\|^2=\sum_{i,j}\mathbb E\langle X_i,X_j\rangle.E​i∑​Xi​​2=i,j∑​E⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩.

For i≠ji\ne ji=j, independence and zero means give ⟨EXi,EXj⟩=0\langle\mathbb E X_i,\mathbb E X_j\rangle=0⟨EXi​,EXj​⟩=0. The diagonal terms are E∥Xi∥2\mathbb E\|X_i\|^2E∥Xi​∥2. For distinct indices i≠ji\ne ji=j, centering alone would not remove covariance; independence is required for this factorization. Products of L2L^2L2 norms are integrable, which justifies the complete termwise identity.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.11.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

Expand the Hilbert square as ∥∑iXi∥2=∑i,j⟨Xi,Xj⟩\|\sum_iX_i\|^2=\sum_{i,j}\langle X_i,X_j\rangle∥∑i​Xi​∥2=∑i,j​⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩. Each mixed product is integrable by the L2L^2L2 inner-product bound, which justifies moving the finite double sum through the integral. For i≠ji\ne ji=j, independence factors E⟨Xi,Xj⟩\mathbb E\langle X_i,X_j\rangleE⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩ into ⟨EXi,EXj⟩=0\langle\mathbb EX_i,\mathbb EX_j\rangle=0⟨EXi​,EXj​⟩=0. For i=ji=ji=j, it is E∥Xi∥2\mathbb E\|X_i\|^2E∥Xi​∥2. The local facts hint, hcross and hexpand establish these prerequisites and identities separately. Centering without independence would leave the off-diagonal covariances.

Formal correspondence: integrable_inner_of_memLp : Every inner product of two L2L^2L2 random variables is integrable. integral_norm_sum_sq_centered_independent : Centered independent Hilbert-valued samples have exactly additive second moments. Independence is used for each mixed term.

02The variance of an independent sample mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let N>0N>0N>0 and let ϕ\phiϕ be square-integrable under probability μ\muμ. On μ⊗N\mu^{\otimes N}μ⊗N the coordinate samples are independent. Center ϕ\phiϕ by its expectation, expand the squared sample mean, and cancel its off-diagonal pairings. This gives

Eμ⊗N∥1N∑i=1Nϕ(Xi)−Eμϕ∥2=1NEμ∥ϕ−Eμϕ∥2. \mathbb E_{\mu^{\otimes N}} \left\|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(X_i)-\mathbb E_\mu\phi\right\|^2 =\frac1N\mathbb E_\mu\|\phi-\mathbb E_\mu\phi\|^2. Eμ⊗N​​N1​i=1∑N​ϕ(Xi​)−Eμ​ϕ​2=N1​Eμ​∥ϕ−Eμ​ϕ∥2.

The root mean square sampling factor is N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2. This variance identity is an ingredient in particle comparison, not by itself a propagation-of-chaos theorem. Exchangeability alone would not cancel covariances.

Indeed let Ci=ϕ(Xi)−EϕC_i=\phi(X_i)-\mathbb E\phiCi​=ϕ(Xi​)−Eϕ. Its average is N−1∑iCiN^{-1}\sum_iC_iN−1∑i​Ci​, the expected squared sum is NE∥C1∥2N\mathbb E\|C_1\|^2NE∥C1​∥2, and the averaging prefactor is N−2N^{-2}N−2. Their product is N−1E∥C1∥2N^{-1}\mathbb E\|C_1\|^2N−1E∥C1​∥2. Taking square roots gives 1/N1/\sqrt N1/N​. Independence here comes from the finite product construction, rather than an assumption about interacting particle states.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

On the explicit product probability P=μ⊗NP=\mu^{\otimes N}P=μ⊗N, set C(a)=φ(a)−EμφC(a)=\varphi(a)-\mathbb E_\mu\varphiC(a)=φ(a)−Eμ​φ and Xi(ω)=C(ωi)X_i(\omega)=C(\omega_i)Xi​(ω)=C(ωi​). Measure-preserving coordinate evaluation proves that each XiX_iXi​ has the same finite second moment, zero mean and law, and the product construction proves their joint independence. The exact centered-mean identity is 1N∑iφ(ωi)−Eφ=1N∑iXi(ω).\frac1N\sum_i\varphi(\omega_i)-\mathbb E\varphi=\frac1N\sum_iX_i(\omega).N1​i∑​φ(ωi​)−Eφ=N1​i∑​Xi​(ω). Thus the previous variance identity gives EP∥1N∑iφ(ωi)−Eφ∥2=Eμ∥φ−Eφ∥2N.\mathbb E_P\left\|\frac1N\sum_i\varphi(\omega_i)-\mathbb E\varphi\right\|^2=\frac{\mathbb E_\mu\|\varphi-\mathbb E\varphi\|^2}{N}.EP​​N1​i∑​φ(ωi​)−Eφ​2=NEμ​∥φ−Eφ∥2​. In the code, hXi, hind, hmean, hvar and hid verify each of these assertions. Taking the nonnegative square root gives the RMS version.

03The empirical parameter-gradient variance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume N>0N>0N>0, fix θ\thetaθ, and put ϕ(x)=∇θU(θ,x)\phi(x)=\nabla_\theta U(\theta,x)ϕ(x)=∇θ​U(θ,x). Joint-gradient Lipschitzness gives affine growth of this observable in its latent input, so it is square-integrable under every finite-second-moment law μ\muμ. Its expectation is bU(θ,μ)b_U(\theta,\mu)bU​(θ,μ). The independent-sample identity therefore gives

E∥1N∑i∇θU(θ,Xi)−bU(θ,μ)∥2=1NE∥∇θU(θ,X)−bU(θ,μ)∥2.\mathbb E\left\|\frac1N\sum_i\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X_i)-b_U(\theta,\mu)\right\|^2=\frac1N\mathbb E\|\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X)-b_U(\theta,\mu)\|^2.E​N1​i∑​∇θ​U(θ,Xi​)−bU​(θ,μ)​2=N1​E∥∇θ​U(θ,X)−bU​(θ,μ)∥2.

This is the exact variance of the empirical parameter force. Its finite variance is derived from the gradient and latent-moment assumptions rather than postulated as an additional sampling-error bound.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

For φ(x)=∇θU(θ,x)\varphi(x)=\nabla_\theta U(\theta,x)φ(x)=∇θ​U(θ,x), the parameter projection is nonexpansive and the full score is LLL-Lipschitz, so φ\varphiφ is LLL-Lipschitz in the latent variable. A latent law with finite second moment therefore makes this observable square integrable. Its Bochner mean is exactly meanFieldDrift, so the general iid identity applies without assuming a separate variance bound for this particular gradient observable.

Formal correspondence: iid_parameterGradient_variance : Exact iid empirical parameter-gradient variance for the actual joint potential. The copies are independent under the displayed finite product.
