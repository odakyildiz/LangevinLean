Exact iid variance and the empirical parameter force

References and publication status for this development

The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

On a probability space, square-integrable Hilbert-valued variables have integrable inner products. On the actual finite product law, independent centered samples yield an exact 1/N1/N1/N variance identity.

01Cancel every off-diagonal covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Realize the supplied L2L^2L2 functions as L2L^2L2 elements; Cauchy–Schwarz gives integrable pairwise inner products. Expand

E∥∑iXi∥2=∑i,jE⟨Xi,Xj⟩.\mathbb E\left\|\sum_iX_i\right\|^2=\sum_{i,j}\mathbb E\langle X_i,X_j\rangle.E​i∑​Xi​​2=i,j∑​E⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩.

For i≠ji\ne ji=j, independence factors the expectation into ⟨EXi,EXj⟩=0\langle\mathbb E X_i,\mathbb E X_j\rangle=0⟨EXi​,EXj​⟩=0. The diagonal terms are E∥Xi∥2\mathbb E\|X_i\|^2E∥Xi​∥2. Finite sums and established integrability justify the expansion.

Expand the Hilbert square as ∥∑iXi∥2=∑i,j⟨Xi,Xj⟩\|\sum_iX_i\|^2=\sum_{i,j}\langle X_i,X_j\rangle∥∑i​Xi​∥2=∑i,j​⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩. Each mixed product is integrable by the L2L^2L2 inner-product bound, which justifies moving the finite double sum through the integral. For i≠ji\ne ji=j, independence factors E⟨Xi,Xj⟩\mathbb E\langle X_i,X_j\rangleE⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩ into ⟨EXi,EXj⟩=0\langle\mathbb EX_i,\mathbb EX_j\rangle=0⟨EXi​,EXj​⟩=0. For i=ji=ji=j, it is E∥Xi∥2\mathbb E\|X_i\|^2E∥Xi​∥2. The local facts hint, hcross and hexpand establish these prerequisites and identities separately. Centering without independence would leave the off-diagonal covariances.

  • integrable_inner_of_memLp: Every inner product of two L² random variables is integrable.
  • integral_norm_sum_sq_centered_independent: Centered independent Hilbert-valued samples have exactly additive second moments. Independence is used for each mixed term.

02Use the actual finite product for an exact sample-mean variance §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a square-integrable observable ϕ\phiϕ and a probability law μ\muμ, the finite product measure μ⊗N\mu^{\otimes N}μ⊗N constructs the independent samples. The proof centers ϕ\phiϕ, expands the squared Hilbert norm, and uses independence and zero mean to cancel every off-diagonal inner product. The exact result is

Eμ⊗N∥1N∑i=1Nϕ(Xi)−Eμϕ∥2=1NEμ∥ϕ−Eμϕ∥2. \mathbb E_{\mu^{\otimes N}} \left\|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(X_i)-\mathbb E_\mu\phi\right\|^2 =\frac1N\mathbb E_\mu\|\phi-\mathbb E_\mu\phi\|^2. Eμ⊗N​​N1​i=1∑N​ϕ(Xi​)−Eμ​ϕ​2=N1​Eμ​∥ϕ−Eμ​ϕ∥2.

Taking square roots proves the N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2 sampling factor. The actual parameter-gradient observable is square integrable under finite-P₂ latent laws, so the formula applies to the mean-field force.

The source’s exchangeability premise alone does not cancel these covariances. A quantitative chaos estimate needs controlled initialization, such as the actual iid construction used here. This exact sampling identity is a proved ingredient; by itself it is not a propagation-of-chaos theorem.

On the explicit product probability P=μ⊗NP=\mu^{\otimes N}P=μ⊗N, set C(a)=φ(a)−EμφC(a)=\varphi(a)-\mathbb E_\mu\varphiC(a)=φ(a)−Eμ​φ and Xi(ω)=C(ωi)X_i(\omega)=C(\omega_i)Xi​(ω)=C(ωi​). Measure-preserving coordinate evaluation proves that each XiX_iXi​ has the same finite second moment, zero mean and law, and the product construction proves their joint independence. The exact centered-mean identity is 1N∑iφ(ωi)−Eφ=1N∑iXi(ω).\frac1N\sum_i\varphi(\omega_i)-\mathbb E\varphi=\frac1N\sum_iX_i(\omega).N1​i∑​φ(ωi​)−Eφ=N1​i∑​Xi​(ω). Thus the previous variance identity gives EP∥1N∑iφ(ωi)−Eφ∥2=Eμ∥φ−Eφ∥2N.\mathbb E_P\left\|\frac1N\sum_i\varphi(\omega_i)-\mathbb E\varphi\right\|^2=\frac{\mathbb E_\mu\|\varphi-\mathbb E\varphi\|^2}{N}.EP​​N1​i∑​φ(ωi​)−Eφ​2=NEμ​∥φ−Eφ∥2​. In the code, hXi, hind, hmean, hvar and hid verify each of these assertions. Taking the nonnegative square root gives the RMS version.

  • iid_empirical_rms: The same exact identity written as an RMS sampling error.

03Specialize the variance to a true gradient observable §

Mathematical statement and proof

Fix θ\thetaθ and let ϕ(x)=∇θU(θ,x)\phi(x)=\nabla_\theta U(\theta,x)ϕ(x)=∇θ​U(θ,x). A Lipschitz joint gradient makes this observable Lipschitz, hence square integrable under any finite-second-moment latent law μ\muμ. Its expectation is exactly bU(θ,μ)b_U(\theta,\mu)bU​(θ,μ). Substitute into the iid formula to obtain

E∥1N∑i∇θU(θ,Xi)−bU(θ,μ)∥2=1NE∥∇θU(θ,X)−bU(θ,μ)∥2.\mathbb E\left\|\frac1N\sum_i\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X_i)-b_U(\theta,\mu)\right\|^2=\frac1N\mathbb E\|\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X)-b_U(\theta,\mu)\|^2.E​N1​i∑​∇θ​U(θ,Xi​)−bU​(θ,μ)​2=N1​E∥∇θ​U(θ,X)−bU​(θ,μ)∥2.

For φ(x)=∇θU(θ,x)\varphi(x)=\nabla_\theta U(\theta,x)φ(x)=∇θ​U(θ,x), the parameter projection is nonexpansive and the full score is LLL-Lipschitz, so φ\varphiφ is LLL-Lipschitz in the latent variable. A latent law with finite second moment therefore makes this observable square integrable. Its Bochner mean is exactly meanFieldDrift, so the general iid identity applies without assuming a separate variance bound for this particular gradient observable.
