Concrete independent initialization and Brownian paths

Given a latent probability law μ\muμ with finite second moment and a deterministic θ0\theta_0θ0​, use the product Q=μ⊗QBQ=\mu\otimes Q_BQ=μ⊗QB​, where QBQ_BQB​ is the canonical joint Brownian source. This constructs all initialization and source hypotheses from primitive data.

01Product source and its measurable Brownian evaluations §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source is the actual product law μ⊗QB\mu\otimes Q_Bμ⊗QB​. Brownian coordinates use only its second factor, so measure-preserving second projection transfers their standard Brownian laws. Coordinate measurability assembles into full-path measurability. The joint path pushforward remains exactly its canonical product law, proving independence of the complete coordinate paths after initialization is added.

The named probability instance follows from the product measure identity Q(Ω)=μ(X)QB(ΩB)=1⋅1=1Q(\Omega)=\mu(X)Q_B(\Omega_B)=1\cdot1=1Q(Ω)=μ(X)QB​(ΩB​)=1⋅1=1. This records normalization of the constructed source before any law or expectation is used.

02Build the square-integrable pair initialization §

Mathematical statement and proof

The map x↦(θ0,x)x\mapsto(\theta_0,x)x↦(θ0​,x) is nonexpansive because its parameter difference vanishes. The latent first-factor variable has finite second moment under the product law. Lipschitz composition therefore proves that (θ0,X0)(\theta_0,X_0)(θ0​,X0​) is square integrable, and its actual L2L^2L2 representative defines the initialization.

03Retain the exact parameter, latent law and source independence §

Mathematical statement and proof

The representative equals (θ0,ω1)(\theta_0,\omega_1)(θ0​,ω1​) almost everywhere, so its parameter projection is const⁡θ0\operatorname{const}\theta_0constθ0​, and its latent pushforward is exactly μ\muμ. The whole initialization depends only on the first factor while all Brownian paths depend only on the second. Product independence, followed by the almost everywhere representative substitution, proves independence of initialization and the entire Brownian family.
