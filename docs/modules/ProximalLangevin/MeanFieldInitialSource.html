Initialization independent of the Brownian family

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Let μ\muμ be a latent probability law with finite second moment and let θ0\theta_0θ0​ be a deterministic parameter. Use Q=μ⊗QBQ=\mu\otimes Q_BQ=μ⊗QB​, where QBQ_BQB​ carries the constructed joint Brownian coordinates. The first coordinate gives the latent initialization and the second gives independent full Brownian paths. The pair initialization is square-integrable and retains the prescribed parameter and latent law. Thus the input assumptions for the mean-field and particle equations follow from these initial data.

01The product measure and Brownian evaluations §

Mathematical statement and proof

The probability space has measure μ⊗QB\mu\otimes Q_Bμ⊗QB​. Every Brownian path depends only on the second coordinate, whose projection preserves its source measure. Its scalar Brownian laws, full-coordinate independence, and measurable evaluations are therefore unchanged. The mass identity is Q(Ω)=μ(X)QB(ΩB)=1⋅1=1Q(\Omega)=\mu(X)Q_B(\Omega_B)=1\cdot1=1Q(Ω)=μ(X)QB​(ΩB​)=1⋅1=1.

Coordinate evaluation measurability also gives measurability of the grouped full paths. Adding the independent latent factor does not change their joint pushforward distribution.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.32.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.11; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.19; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.20; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.21.

02The square-integrable pair initialization §

Mathematical statement and proof

The map x↦(θ0,x)x\mapsto(\theta_0,x)x↦(θ0​,x) is measurable and nonexpansive as a function of its latent input. The first product coordinate has the supplied finite second moment. Hence (θ0,X0)(\theta_0,X_0)(θ0​,X0​) is square-integrable and defines an L2L^2L2 initialization.

The initialized pair (θ0,X0)(\theta_0,X_0)(θ0​,X0​) has squared norm ∥θ0∥2+∥X0∥2\|\theta_0\|^2+\|X_0\|^2∥θ0​∥2+∥X0​∥2. Its parameter term is a finite constant and its latent term has finite expectation. Thus it is an element of the joint L2L^2L2 space with the prescribed coordinates.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.1.

03The prescribed marginals and full-noise independence §

Mathematical statement and proof

The pair representative equals (θ0,ω1)(\theta_0,\omega_1)(θ0​,ω1​) almost surely. Its parameter projection is const⁡θ0\operatorname{const}\theta_0constθ0​, and its latent law is μ\muμ. Initialization is a function of the first product coordinate, whereas every Brownian path is a function of the second. Their full families are therefore independent.

For the pointwise pair (θ0,x)(\theta_0,x)(θ0​,x) this factorization is immediate from the product measure. It retains parameter θ0\theta_0θ0​ and latent law μ\muμ. The first coordinate still has that law and parameter θ0\theta_0θ0​ is deterministic. Replacing the initialization by an almost-surely equal square-integrable representative preserves both its law and independence from the complete Brownian family.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.11; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.19; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.1.
