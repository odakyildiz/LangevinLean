Brownian particle dynamics, Gibbs invariance, and convergence of position laws

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

This section connects the specified Brownian kinetic diffusion to the physical particle system and proves Proposition 3 for its output law. The initial position is an arbitrary probability law with finite second moment. Standard Gaussian velocity and the specified Brownian source are independent of that position; velocity scaling gives the required physical thermal law. The Gibbs law is a proved stationary target, and the Wasserstein distance is the infimum over all couplings.

The constructed physical KIPLD process has the intended force, thermal initialization, and stationary target. Its law-level contraction and parameter projection separate dynamical mixing from finite-particle statistical error.

02Verify the independent thermal initialization §

Mathematical statement and proof

The complete source law theorem supplies the initial phase law N(0,I)⊗ν\mathcal N(0,I)\otimes\nuN(0,I)⊗ν. Push it through velocity multiplication by N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2. The result is precisely the independent thermal-velocity and position law; the velocity and position remain independent. The selected flow’s time-zero identity and the almost-everywhere scalar-multiplication rule identify the L2L^{2}L2 representatives with this pushforward. No initialization-law hypothesis is imposed on the final theorem.

The thermal initialization is the explicit inverse-square-root-particle Gaussian pushforward. Its independence from position and the entire Brownian source is proved by the product construction. This is precisely the phase/source law needed to transfer stationarity.

03Cancel the clock factors and prove stationary mixing §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted potential has curvature NmNmNm and smoothness NLNLNL. Multiplication by NNN transforms m+L≤γ2m+L\le\gamma^2m+L≤γ2 into Nm+NL≤(Nγ)2Nm+NL\le(\sqrt N\gamma)^2Nm+NL≤(N​γ)2, so the normalized Gibbs mixing theorem applies. Its exponent is −(Nm)/(Nγ) (T/N)=−mT/γ-(Nm)/(\sqrt N\gamma)\,(T/\sqrt N)=-mT/\gamma−(Nm)/(N​γ)(T/N​)=−mT/γ. The resulting estimate is W2(νT,πZ)≤2e−mT/γW2(ν,πZ)W_2(\nu_T,\pi_Z)\le\sqrt2e^{-mT/\gamma}W_2(\nu,\pi_Z)W2​(νT​,πZ​)≤2​e−mT/γW2​(ν,πZ​). The constructed theorem proves Gibbs stationarity internally. Separate moment and probability theorems prove that all real Wasserstein quantities are finite.

The clock transform turns the process into the shared unit-temperature diffusion for the sum potential. Curvature and friction scaling cancel in the physical-time mixing exponent. Since the target position is unchanged, the resulting convergence bound is directly a particle position estimate.

04The physical force in the integral equation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The drift lifted to L2L^{2}L2 represents pointwise application of the true sum gradient. The almost-everywhere scalar multiplication and composition identities show that N−1G(∇VN)=G(F)N^{-1}\mathcal G(\nabla V_N)=\mathcal G(F)N−1G(∇VN​)=G(F), where F=∇VN/NF=\nabla V_N/NF=∇VN​/N is the previously verified particle force. Apply the exact kinetic clock theorem to the selected constructed solution. The result is the full physical-time integral equation with friction γ\gammaγ, the force FFF, and input N−1/2Bt/NN^{-1/2}B_{t/\sqrt N}N−1/2Bt/N​​. The shared Brownian scaling theorem identifies its thermal amplitude as 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​.

The average-potential gradient identity rewrites the transformed drift as the parameter-average and latent-pair force. Consequently the constructed physical path solves the intended KIPLD equation. This identification links the law-level mixing theorem to the stated particle dynamics.

Mathematical statement and proof

The parameter projection has operator norm at most one, so pushing any coupling through it cannot increase its quadratic cost. Taking the infimum over initial couplings bounds the parameter Wasserstein distance by the full position distance. The initial independent coupling ν⊗πZ\nu\otimes\pi_Zν⊗πZ​ bounds that infimum by (E∥Z0−Zˉ∗∥2)1/2(\mathbb E\|Z_0-\bar Z_*\|^2)^{1/2}(E∥Z0​−Zˉ∗​∥2)1/2, giving precisely the printed proposition. A second estimate adds the already proved sharp equilibrium concentration radius dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}dθ​/(Nm)​ by the L2L^{2}L2 triangle inequality, giving a direct parameter RMS bound about the unique marginal-likelihood maximizer.

Nonexpansive parameter projection transfers the sampling transport error. The powered Gibbs marginal contributes the sharp statistical radius around the likelihood maximizer. Their sum yields a continuous-time parameter RMS bound with separate dynamical and finite-particle contributions.
