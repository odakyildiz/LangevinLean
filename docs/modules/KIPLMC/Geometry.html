The actual scaled particle force and its geometry

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The audit uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and sections, including the stated particle-dependent OBABO rate. KIPLMC1 reuses the shared kinetic exponential integrator with its exact Cholesky-generated correlated Gaussian law; KIPLMC2 uses the checked O-B-A-B-O scheme. Physical-time Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both actual Gaussian parameter RMS theorems, exact clock and algorithm conjugacies, sharp MMLE concentration, explicit accuracy budgets, and the logistic analytic example are proved. The internal OBABO proof gives a stronger direct estimate and recovers the supplied coarse rate with an explicit uniform constant. Continuous solutions use the canonical continuous-L² model; empirical experiments are recorded separately.

The shared IPLA state stores z=(θ,X1/N,…,XN/N)z=(\theta,X^1/\sqrt N,\ldots,X^N/\sqrt N)z=(θ,X1/N​,…,XN/N​) in a Euclidean coordinate space with independent parameter and latent dimensions. This module keeps physical time, scaling velocities by the same spatial map. In this convention the kinetic temperature is 1/N1/N1/N, the potential is the particle average UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​, and the force is its actual gradient. It is equivalent to the manuscript's additional time change but avoids reimplementing the shared kinetic step.

01Define the average and prove that its gradient is the force §

Mathematical statement and proof

State reuses the scaled Euclidean IPLA state. The existing lifted potential is VN(z)=∑iU(θ,Nzi)V_N(z)=\sum_iU(\theta,\sqrt Nz_i)VN​(z)=∑i​U(θ,N​zi​). Define UˉN=VN/N\bar U_N=V_N/NUˉN​=VN​/N and F=∇VN/NF=\nabla V_N/NF=∇VN​/N. To prove ∇UˉN=F\nabla\bar U_N=F∇UˉN​=F, compare their inner products against an arbitrary vector. The derivative rule for a scalar multiple gives DUˉN[w]=N−1DVN[w]D\bar U_N[w]=N^{-1}DV_N[w]DUˉN​[w]=N−1DVN​[w], and the inner-product characterization of gradients identifies the two vectors. Lean uses ext_inner_right, the proved differentiability of the lifted potential, and HasFDerivAt.const_mul. For N>0N>0N>0, cancellation of the nonzero particle number gives VN=NUˉNV_N=N\bar U_NVN​=NUˉN​.

02Average the lifted strong convexity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The existing particle geometry proves VNV_NVN​ is NmNmNm-strongly convex whenever the pair potential is mmm-strongly convex. Multiply its convex-combination inequality by N−1>0N^{-1}>0N−1>0: the function terms become UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​, and the quadratic correction N−1(Nm)∥x−y∥2/2N^{-1}(Nm)\|x-y\|^2/2N−1(Nm)∥x−y∥2/2 becomes m∥x−y∥2/2m\|x-y\|^2/2m∥x−y∥2/2. Lean constructs the strong-convexity structure directly, obtains the lifted inequality, multiplies it by the nonnegative inverse particle number, and uses field_simp and ring to cancel constants. The proof requires positive particle count but imposes no artificial equality of the parameter and latent dimensions.

03Recover the mean parameter force and unaveraged latent force §

Mathematical statement and proof

The parameter block of ∇VN\nabla V_N∇VN​ is the sum of pair parameter gradients, so Fθ=N−1∑i∇θU(θ,Xi)F_\theta=N^{-1}\sum_i\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X^i)Fθ​=N−1∑i​∇θ​U(θ,Xi). Each latent block of ∇VN\nabla V_N∇VN​ is N∇xU\sqrt N\nabla_xUN​∇x​U. Dividing by NNN and undoing the spatial scaling gives NFi=∇xU(θ,Xi)\sqrt N F_i=\nabla_xU(\theta,X^i)N​Fi​=∇x​U(θ,Xi). The parameter proof simplifies with the existing gradient-coordinate theorem. The latent proof expands coordinate scalar multiplication, groups the coefficient into (N)2N−1(\sqrt N)^2N^{-1}(N​)2N−1, rewrites (N)2=N(\sqrt N)^2=N(N​)2=N, and cancels the nonzero NNN. These identities verify the actual force used by both particle algorithms.

04The force retains the pair smoothness constant §

Mathematical statement and proof

The shared lifted gradient has Lipschitz constant NLNLNL. Since F=∇VN/NF=\nabla V_N/NF=∇VN​/N, its difference satisfies ∥F(x)−F(y)∥≤N−1(NL)∥x−y∥=L∥x−y∥\|F(x)-F(y)\|\le N^{-1}(NL)\|x-y\|=L\|x-y\|∥F(x)−F(y)∥≤N−1(NL)∥x−y∥=L∥x−y∥. Lean starts from the existing lifted Lipschitz estimate, rewrites norms of scalar multiples with the nonnegative inverse particle count, multiplies the inequality by that inverse, and cancels NNN. This proves that the physical-time scaled force has a smoothness constant independent of the number of particles, a key feature of the favorable KIPLMC1 scaling.
