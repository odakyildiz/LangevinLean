The scaled particle potential and its gradient

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

02The force is the gradient of the averaged potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let UUU be differentiable on Rdθ×Rdx\mathbb R^{d_\theta}\times\mathbb R^{d_x}Rdθ​×Rdx​ and let N≥0N\ge0N≥0. Then UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ is differentiable and

∇UˉN(z)=FN(z)for every z∈ZN.\nabla\bar U_N(z)=F_N(z)\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal Z_N.∇UˉN​(z)=FN​(z)for every z∈ZN​.

If N>0N>0N>0, then moreover VN(z)=N UˉN(z)V_N(z)=N\,\bar U_N(z)VN​(z)=NUˉN​(z) for every z∈ZNz\in\mathcal Z_Nz∈ZN​.

Proof. Each JiJ_iJi​ is linear, hence differentiable with derivative JiJ_iJi​ at every point, so by the chain rule z↦U(Jiz)z\mapsto U(J_iz)z↦U(Ji​z) is differentiable with derivative w↦DU(Jiz)[Jiw]w\mapsto DU(J_iz)[J_iw]w↦DU(Ji​z)[Ji​w]. A finite sum of differentiable functions is differentiable; hence VNV_NVN​ is differentiable, which is the differentiability statement of the particle geometry (IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3). By the rule for constant multiples, UˉN=N−1VN\bar U_N=N^{-1}V_NUˉN​=N−1VN​ is differentiable and

DUˉN(z)[w]=N−1 DVN(z)[w](w∈ZN).(1)D\bar U_N(z)[w]=N^{-1}\,DV_N(z)[w]\qquad(w\in\mathcal Z_N).\qquad(1)DUˉN​(z)[w]=N−1DVN​(z)[w](w∈ZN​).(1)

Fix zzz and an arbitrary w∈ZNw\in\mathcal Z_Nw∈ZN​. By the defining property of ∇UˉN(z)\nabla\bar U_N(z)∇UˉN​(z), identity (1), the defining property of ∇VN(z)\nabla V_N(z)∇VN​(z) and linearity of the inner product in its first argument,

⟨∇UˉN(z),w⟩=DUˉN(z)[w]=N−1DVN(z)[w]=N−1⟨∇VN(z),w⟩=⟨FN(z),w⟩.\langle\nabla\bar U_N(z),w\rangle=D\bar U_N(z)[w]=N^{-1}DV_N(z)[w]=N^{-1}\langle\nabla V_N(z),w\rangle=\langle F_N(z),w\rangle.⟨∇UˉN​(z),w⟩=DUˉN​(z)[w]=N−1DVN​(z)[w]=N−1⟨∇VN​(z),w⟩=⟨FN​(z),w⟩.

Hence u=∇UˉN(z)−FN(z)u=\nabla\bar U_N(z)-F_N(z)u=∇UˉN​(z)−FN​(z) satisfies ⟨u,w⟩=0\langle u,w\rangle=0⟨u,w⟩=0 for every www. Taking w=uw=uw=u gives ∥u∥2=0\|u\|^2=0∥u∥2=0, so u=0u=0u=0.

For the second statement, N>0N>0N>0 means that the real number NNN is nonzero, so N⋅N−1=1N\cdot N^{-1}=1N⋅N−1=1 and

N UˉN(z)=N(N−1VN(z))=(N⋅N−1) VN(z)=VN(z).□N\,\bar U_N(z)=N\bigl(N^{-1}V_N(z)\bigr)=(N\cdot N^{-1})\,V_N(z)=V_N(z).\qquad\squareNUˉN​(z)=N(N−1VN​(z))=(N⋅N−1)VN​(z)=VN​(z).□

When N=0N=0N=0 both sides of the gradient identity vanish, so that identity needs no hypothesis on NNN. The force FNF_NFN​ is thus a gradient field, which is what allows the kinetic theory for a potential fff to be applied with f=UˉNf=\bar U_Nf=UˉN​; the identity VN=NUˉNV_N=N\bar U_NVN​=NUˉN​ converts between the averaged normalization and the sum normalization used for the rescaled dynamics later in the report.

03Averaging preserves the strong convexity constant §

Mathematical statement and proof

Strong convexity. Let m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R. A function fff on a real inner product space EEE is mmm-strongly convex if for all x,y∈Ex,y\in Ex,y∈E and all a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 with a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1,

f(ax+by)≤af(x)+bf(y)−ab m2 ∥x−y∥2.(1)f(ax+by)\le af(x)+bf(y)-ab\,\frac m2\,\|x-y\|^2.\qquad(1)f(ax+by)≤af(x)+bf(y)−ab2m​∥x−y∥2.(1)

For m=0m=0m=0 this is convexity. For differentiable fff and m>0m>0m>0 it is equivalent to the monotonicity condition ⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩≥m∥x−y∥2\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle\ge m\|x-y\|^2⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩≥m∥x−y∥2, which is the form of Assumption H1 in the paper; this equivalence is not used below.

Claim. Let N>0N>0N>0 and m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, and let UUU be mmm-strongly convex on Rdθ×Rdx\mathbb R^{d_\theta}\times\mathbb R^{d_x}Rdθ​×Rdx​. Then UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ is mmm-strongly convex on ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​.

Proof. We first recall why VNV_NVN​ is (Nm)(Nm)(Nm)-strongly convex (IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3). Let x,y∈ZNx,y\in\mathcal Z_Nx,y∈ZN​ and a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 with a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1. Linearity of JiJ_iJi​ gives Ji(ax+by)=aJix+bJiyJ_i(ax+by)=aJ_ix+bJ_iyJi​(ax+by)=aJi​x+bJi​y and Jix−Jiy=Ji(x−y)J_ix-J_iy=J_i(x-y)Ji​x−Ji​y=Ji​(x−y), so (1) for UUU at the points JixJ_ixJi​x and JiyJ_iyJi​y reads

U(Ji(ax+by))≤aU(Jix)+bU(Jiy)−ab m2 ∥Ji(x−y)∥2.U(J_i(ax+by))\le aU(J_ix)+bU(J_iy)-ab\,\frac m2\,\|J_i(x-y)\|^2.U(Ji​(ax+by))≤aU(Ji​x)+bU(Ji​y)−ab2m​∥Ji​(x−y)∥2.

Summing over i=1,…,Ni=1,\dots,Ni=1,…,N and using the norm identity ∑i∥Ji(x−y)∥2=N∥x−y∥2\sum_i\|J_i(x-y)\|^2=N\|x-y\|^2∑i​∥Ji​(x−y)∥2=N∥x−y∥2 from the first step,

VN(ax+by)≤aVN(x)+bVN(y)−ab Nm2 ∥x−y∥2.(2)V_N(ax+by)\le aV_N(x)+bV_N(y)-ab\,\frac{Nm}2\,\|x-y\|^2.\qquad(2)VN​(ax+by)≤aVN​(x)+bVN​(y)−ab2Nm​∥x−y∥2.(2)

Since N>0N>0N>0, the number N−1N^{-1}N−1 is positive, and multiplying (2) by it preserves the inequality. The three function values become values of UˉN=N−1VN\bar U_N=N^{-1}V_NUˉN​=N−1VN​, and the last term becomes

N−1⋅ab Nm2 ∥x−y∥2=ab m2 ∥x−y∥2,N^{-1}\cdot ab\,\frac{Nm}2\,\|x-y\|^2=ab\,\frac m2\,\|x-y\|^2,N−1⋅ab2Nm​∥x−y∥2=ab2m​∥x−y∥2,

because N−1N=1N^{-1}N=1N−1N=1. The result is (1) for f=UˉNf=\bar U_Nf=UˉN​. □\square□

No sign condition on mmm and no relation between dθd_\thetadθ​ and dxd_xdx​ is needed. The factor NNN gained by adding NNN copies of UUU is removed exactly by the average, so the strong convexity constant of UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ does not depend on the number of particles, although the dimension dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​ of ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​ does.
