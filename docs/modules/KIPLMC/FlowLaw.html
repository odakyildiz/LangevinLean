Wasserstein contraction of the physical-time particle position laws

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

This section turns the integral equation of the particle dynamics into a statement about probability laws. The randomness is described by a source: an initial velocity v0v_0v0​ and a continuous input path BBB on [0,T][0,T][0,T], with a joint law QQQ. Starting from an initial position z0z_0z0​ drawn from a law μ\muμ independently of the source, one solves the kinetic integral equation for the force ∇UˉN=FN\nabla\bar U_N=F_N∇UˉN​=FN​ and records the position at time ttt; the law of this position is denoted PtQμP^Q_t\muPtQ​μ. For a Brownian source with amplitude 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​ this is the position law of the kinetic particle diffusion, but the results hold for every source law.

We prove that PtQμP^Q_t\muPtQ​μ is a probability measure and that, for γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 and γ2≥m+L\gamma^2\ge m+Lγ2≥m+L,

W2(PtQμ,PtQν)≤2 e−mt/γ W2(μ,ν)W_2\bigl(P^Q_t\mu,P^Q_t\nu\bigr)\le\sqrt2\,e^{-mt/\gamma}\,W_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(PtQ​μ,PtQ​ν)≤2​e−mt/γW2​(μ,ν)

for all initial laws with W2(μ,ν)<∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<∞, where W2W_2W2​ is the Wasserstein distance defined as an infimum over all couplings. The constants are those of the pair potential and do not depend on NNN. No stationary law is used; the comparison with the Gibbs target is made in the section on the Brownian particle flow.

01The space of continuous input paths §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For T∈RT\in\mathbb RT∈R let CT=C([0,T];ZN)\mathcal C_T=C([0,T];\mathcal Z_N)CT​=C([0,T];ZN​) be the space of continuous paths B:[0,T]→ZNB:[0,T]\to\mathcal Z_NB:[0,T]→ZN​ with the uniform norm ∥B∥∞=sup⁡t∈[0,T]∥B(t)∥\|B\|_\infty=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|B(t)\|∥B∥∞​=supt∈[0,T]​∥B(t)∥ (for T<0T<0T<0 the interval is empty and CT\mathcal C_TCT​ has exactly one element). It carries the Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra of this norm, generated by the open sets. A source is a pair (v0,B)∈ZN×CT(v_0,B)\in\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal C_T(v0​,B)∈ZN​×CT​, and a source law is a probability measure QQQ on ZN×CT\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal C_TZN​×CT​ with the product of the Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebras.

With the Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra, every continuous map from CT\mathcal C_TCT​, or from a product containing it, into a metric space with its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra is measurable. The flow maps of the next step are continuous in the source, so they are measurable and the laws below are well defined. Measurability alone imposes no relation between the source and the initial position; that relation is fixed by forming product measures.

02The position law of the particle equation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Let N>0N>0N>0, L,γ≥0L,\gamma\ge0L,γ≥0, let UUU be differentiable with LLL-Lipschitz gradient, and let T≥0T\ge0T≥0. For an initial position z0z_0z0​ and a source (v0,B)(v_0,B)(v0​,B), let Y=Ψ(z0,v0,B)Y=\Psi(z_0,v_0,B)Y=Ψ(z0​,v0​,B) be the continuous solution of

For t∈Rt\in\mathbb Rt∈R, the position law with initial law μ\muμ on ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​ and source law QQQ is

PtQμ=Ψt#(μ⊗Q),Ψt(z0,(v0,B))=position component of Yt.P^Q_t\mu=\Psi_t{}_\#(\mu\otimes Q),\qquad\Psi_t(z_0,(v_0,B))=\text{position component of }Y_t.PtQ​μ=Ψt​#​(μ⊗Q),Ψt​(z0​,(v0​,B))=position component of Yt​.

Claim. If moreover t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T] and μ,Q\mu,Qμ,Q are probability measures, then PtQμP^Q_t\muPtQ​μ is a probability measure.

Proof. The solution depends Lipschitz continuously on the initial state and on the input in the uniform norm on [0,T][0,T][0,T] (Grönwall's inequality for the difference of two integral equations with a Lipschitz drift); in particular Ψt\Psi_tΨt​ is continuous for t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T] and hence measurable (Kinetic, Section 3.9.4). The product μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q is a probability measure, and the pushforward of a probability measure by a measurable map has total mass 111. □\square□

The force here is ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​, which equals the particle force FNF_NFN​ by the geometry section; its Lipschitz constant LLL is the one proved for ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​ in the dynamics section. With B=2γ/N WB=\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\,WB=2γ/N​W for a Brownian motion WWW and an initial velocity independent of WWW, PtQμP^Q_t\muPtQ​μ is the position law of the scaled particle diffusion at time ttt.

03Wasserstein contraction of the position laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Wasserstein distance. For probability measures μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν on ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​, a coupling is a probability measure π\piπ on ZN×ZN\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_NZN​×ZN​ with marginals μ\muμ and ν\nuν, and

W2(μ,ν)=inf⁡π(∫∥x−y∥2 π(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞],W_2(\mu,\nu)=\inf_\pi\Bigl(\int\|x-y\|^2\,\pi(dx,dy)\Bigr)^{1/2}\in[0,\infty],W2​(μ,ν)=πinf​(∫∥x−y∥2π(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞],

the infimum over all couplings. The product μ⊗ν\mu\otimes\nuμ⊗ν is always a coupling, so the set of couplings is nonempty.

Claim. Let N>0N>0N>0, m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, L,γ≥0L,\gamma\ge0L,γ≥0 with m≤Lm\le Lm≤L, γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 and m+L≤γ2m+L\le\gamma^2m+L≤γ2, and let UUU be differentiable and mmm-strongly convex with LLL-Lipschitz gradient. Let T≥0T\ge0T≥0, t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T], let μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν be probability measures on ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​ with W2(μ,ν)<∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<∞, and let QQQ be a source law. Then

W2(PtQμ,PtQν)≤2 e−mt/γ W2(μ,ν).W_2\bigl(P^Q_t\mu,P^Q_t\nu\bigr)\le\sqrt2\,e^{-mt/\gamma}\,W_2(\mu,\nu).W2​(PtQ​μ,PtQ​ν)≤2​e−mt/γW2​(μ,ν).

Proof. This is the law-level kinetic contraction theorem (Kinetic, Section 3.9.6) for the potential f=UˉNf=\bar U_Nf=UˉN​, which is differentiable, mmm-strongly convex and has LLL-Lipschitz gradient by the geometry and dynamics sections. Its argument is the following. Let π\piπ be a coupling of μ\muμ and ν\nuν with finite cost. On the probability space ((ZN×ZN)×(ZN×CT),π⊗Q)\bigl((\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_N)\times(\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal C_T),\pi\otimes Q\bigr)((ZN​×ZN​)×(ZN​×CT​),π⊗Q), let (X0,Y0)(X_0,Y_0)(X0​,Y0​) be the pair of initial positions and (v0,B)(v_0,B)(v0​,B) the common source. The first marginal of π\piπ is μ\muμ and the source is independent of (X0,Y0)(X_0,Y_0)(X0​,Y0​), so (X0,(v0,B))(X_0,(v_0,B))(X0​,(v0​,B)) has law μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q and Xt=Ψt(X0,v0,B)X_t=\Psi_t(X_0,v_0,B)Xt​=Ψt​(X0​,v0​,B) has law PtQμP^Q_t\muPtQ​μ; likewise Yt=Ψt(Y0,v0,B)Y_t=\Psi_t(Y_0,v_0,B)Yt​=Ψt​(Y0​,v0​,B) has law PtQνP^Q_t\nuPtQ​ν. Hence the law of (Xt,Yt)(X_t,Y_t)(Xt​,Yt​) is a coupling of the two output laws.

The two solutions have the same input, which cancels in their difference, and the same initial velocity v0v_0v0​. By the synchronous contraction of the dynamics section (KIPLMC, Section 3.11.3), ∥Xt−Yt∥≤2e−mt/γ∥X0−Y0∥\|X_t-Y_t\|\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}\|X_0-Y_0\|∥Xt​−Yt​∥≤2​e−mt/γ∥X0​−Y0​∥ at every sample point. Squaring, integrating and taking square roots,

W2(PtQμ,PtQν)≤(E∥Xt−Yt∥2)1/2≤2e−mt/γ(∫∥x−y∥2 π(dx,dy))1/2.W_2\bigl(P^Q_t\mu,P^Q_t\nu\bigr)\le\bigl(\mathbb E\|X_t-Y_t\|^2\bigr)^{1/2}\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}\Bigl(\int\|x-y\|^2\,\pi(dx,dy)\Bigr)^{1/2}.W2​(PtQ​μ,PtQ​ν)≤(E∥Xt​−Yt​∥2)1/2≤2​e−mt/γ(∫∥x−y∥2π(dx,dy))1/2.

Taking the infimum over π\piπ gives the claim; the output distance is finite because the right side is. □\square□

The common source is what makes the comparison possible without any knowledge of its law: the input is shared by the two copies. No optimal coupling and no stationary law are needed.
