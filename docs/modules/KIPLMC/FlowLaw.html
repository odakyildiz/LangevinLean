Wasserstein contraction of the physical-time particle position laws

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the O-B-A-B-O splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L². Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

The scaled configuration space is Rdθ+Ndx\mathbb R^{d_\theta+Nd_x}Rdθ​+Ndx​, with potential UˉN=VN/N\bar U_N=V_N/NUˉN​=VN​/N. Its gradient is the physical-time particle force. The original pair potential's strong convexity and gradient Lipschitz constants pass to UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ unchanged. Consequently the shared kinetic flow theorem applies with the original m,L,γm,L,\gammam,L,γ, independently of the number of particles. This section constructs a probability law by an independent product and pushforward, and proves contraction of those laws. It does not assume Gibbs stationarity or identify an arbitrary forcing law with Brownian motion.

The physical-time integral solution defines a measurable position endpoint map. Extending every initial plan by a common independent source gives the Wasserstein contraction of its output laws.

Statement and proof

01Use the Borel structure of continuous forcing paths §

Mathematical statement and proof

The velocity input belongs to the space of continuous forcing paths on the compact horizon, equipped with the uniform norm and its Borel structure. Together with the initial velocity this gives a measurable source space. An independent product with the initial position law then specifies the joint initialization and forcing law; measurability alone does not assert independence.

The continuous forcing space carries the Borel structure of its uniform norm. The selected solution depends continuously on the initial phase and forcing. Endpoint evaluation is therefore measurable, making the physical-time random-map construction a well-defined probability law.

Earlier results used here: Kinetic, Section 3.2.2.

02The physical-time position law §

Mathematical statement and proof

For an initial position law μ\muμ and a joint source law QQQ of velocity and continuous input, form μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q. Evolve each pair with the selected kinetic integral flow for ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​ and project to its position at time ttt. The resulting pushforward is the particle position law. It is the kinetic position-law construction with the proved Lipschitz gradient of UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​. The normalization of the forcing subtracts its value at zero, so every continuous source path gives the correct initial condition. The Brownian specialization will choose the physical amplitude 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​; the definition deliberately admits any source law.

For 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T0≤t≤T, the endpoint is measurable because the selected flow depends continuously on its initial state and forcing. The product of probability inputs is a probability measure, and a measurable pushforward preserves total mass. The physical position law therefore has total mass one.

Initialization and the velocity/forcing source are independent product factors. Solving the average-force integral equation and projecting position defines the particle flow law. Its force is the proved gradient of the physical-time average potential.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.2.2; Kinetic, Section 3.9.6.

03Transfer the infimum over initial couplings without a new stochastic axiom §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν have finite W2W_2W2​, and use the same source distribution QQQ, independent of position for each evolution. If γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 and γ2≥m+L\gamma^2\ge m+Lγ2≥m+L, then

W2(PtQμ,PtQν)≤2e−mt/γW2(μ,ν).W_2(P_t^Q\mu,P_t^Q\nu)\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}W_2(\mu,\nu).W2​(PtQ​μ,PtQ​ν)≤2​e−mt/γW2​(μ,ν).

The argument applies the independent-source kinetic law theorem to UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​. The three analytic inputs are proved earlier: differentiability, strong convexity with parameter mmm, and smoothness with constant LLL. The theorem extends every initial coupling by the common independent source, proves its output marginals, bounds its cost using the integral-equation contraction, and takes the infimum. Thus the result concerns the pushforward laws. Finiteness of the output transport cost follows from the bound and the explicit finite initial-cost hypothesis so the displayed Wasserstein distance is a finite real number. To replace the second output by the Gibbs target requires the separate stationarity theorem; that stationarity hypothesis is not concluded by this law-to-law comparison.

Adjoin a common independent source to every initial position plan. The shared-noise path estimate bounds its output cost, and the exact marginal identities recover the independently defined particle laws. Infimizing yields the law-to-law Wasserstein contraction with no optimizer assumption.
