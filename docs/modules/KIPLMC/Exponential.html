The KIPLMC1 exponential update in parameter and latent coordinates

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

04The sequence generated by a given innovation sequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Given an initial phase state Y0∈ZN×ZNY_0\in\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_NY0​∈ZN​×ZN​ and an innovation sequence (ζn)n≥0(\zeta_n)_{n\ge0}(ζn​)n≥0​ in ZN×ZN\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_NZN​×ZN​, the KIPLMC1 sequence is defined by recursion:

Y0 given,Yn+1=K(Yn,ζn)(n≥0).Y_0\text{ given},\qquad Y_{n+1}=K(Y_n,\zeta_n)\qquad(n\ge0).Y0​ given,Yn+1​=K(Yn​,ζn​)(n≥0).

By the recursion theorem for the natural numbers this defines YnY_nYn​ uniquely for every nnn, for every choice of Y0Y_0Y0​ and (ζn)(\zeta_n)(ζn​); the innovation ζn\zeta_nζn​ is used to produce Yn+1Y_{n+1}Yn+1​. Writing Yn=(vn,zn)Y_n=(v_n,z_n)Yn​=(vn​,zn​), the previous two steps express (vn+1,zn+1)(v_{n+1},z_{n+1})(vn+1​,zn+1​) in the coordinates θn\theta_nθn​, Xni=Nzn,iX^i_n=\sqrt Nz_{n,i}Xni​=N​zn,i​, VnθV^\theta_nVnθ​, Vnxi=Nvn,iV^{x_i}_n=\sqrt Nv_{n,i}Vnxi​​=N​vn,i​ of Algorithm 1.

No probabilistic assumption enters the definition: YnY_nYn​ is a deterministic function of Y0,ζ0,…,ζn−1Y_0,\zeta_0,\dots,\zeta_{n-1}Y0​,ζ0​,…,ζn−1​. The algorithm is obtained by taking Y0Y_0Y0​ random and (ζn)(\zeta_n)(ζn​) independent of Y0Y_0Y0​, independent across nnn, each with the correlated Gaussian law of the innovation; the law of YnY_nYn​ is then determined in the section on chain laws, and its distance to the target is bounded in the section on the KIPLMC1 error bound.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

kiplmc1Chain U γ h z noise is a function ℕ → State N dθ dx × State N dθ dx defined by structural recursion on the natural number: the case 0 returns the initial pair z, and the case n + 1 returns kiplmc1Step U γ h (kiplmc1Chain U γ h z noise n) (noise n). The argument noise : ℕ → State N dθ dx × State N dθ dx is the innovation sequence (ζn)(\zeta_n)(ζn​). The definition is accepted by Lean's termination checker because the recursive call is on n; no existence assumption is involved. The section on chain laws proves that it coincides with the generic noise-driven recursion of the unadjusted Langevin report, for which the probabilistic results are proved.
