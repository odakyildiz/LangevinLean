Time rescaling of the correlated-Gaussian KIPLMC1 transition

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the O-B-A-B-O splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L². Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

The same positions and a velocity multiplication by √N convert physical-time KIPLMC1 into the shared unit-temperature exponential integrator for the sum potential. This file proves the covariance, Gaussian measure, update, initialization, and complete iterated-law identities. Both correlated noise components and their mutual covariance are transported explicitly.

The two Gaussian response coordinates scale by different clock powers. Their full covariance, spatial product law, initialization, and iterated transition identities establish an exact probabilistic conjugacy.

01The Brownian clock changes each correlated block diagonally §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write c>0c>0c>0, γ′=cγ\gamma^\prime=c\gammaγ′=cγ, and h′=h/ch^\prime=h/ch′=h/c. A time change contributes a Brownian factor c−1/2c^{-1/2}c−1/2; the position response contributes another factor c−1c^{-1}c−1. The resulting map on the two-component Gaussian block is Dc(v,x)=(c−1/2v,c−3/2x).D_c(v,x)=(c^{-1/2}v,c^{-3/2}x).Dc​(v,x)=(c−1/2v,c−3/2x). The argument constructs this diagonal map as a continuous linear map on Euclidean two-space. The coordinate theorem verifies the factors directly. The two response coordinates are ψ0\psi_0ψ0​ and ψ1\psi_1ψ1​, so the subsequent proof can use the general response-scaling identity.

The velocity response contains one Brownian integration factor, whereas the position response has an additional time primitive. Their different powers of the clock scale are therefore unavoidable. A single common scalar multiplication would not transform the two-component innovation correctly.

02The covariance transformation and the Gaussian measure identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The covariance is the Gram integral Cij(γ,h)=∫0hψi(γ,t)ψj(γ,t) dtC_{ij}(\gamma,h)=\int_0^h\psi_i(\gamma,t)\psi_j(\gamma,t)\,dtCij​(γ,h)=∫0h​ψi​(γ,t)ψj​(γ,t)dt, with i,j∈{0,1}i,j\in\{0,1\}i,j∈{0,1}. The exact response identity ψi(cγ,t)=c−iψi(γ,ct)\psi_i(c\gamma,t)=c^{-i}\psi_i(\gamma,ct)ψi​(cγ,t)=c−iψi​(γ,ct), followed by the substitution s=cts=cts=ct, gives Cij(cγ,h/c)=c−(i+j+1)Cij(γ,h).C_{ij}(c\gamma,h/c)=c^{-(i+j+1)}C_{ij}(\gamma,h).Cij​(cγ,h/c)=c−(i+j+1)Cij​(γ,h). These are precisely the entries of DcCDcTD_c C D_c^\mathsf TDc​CDcT​, including the off-diagonal velocity-position covariance. The argument rewrites both response factors, moves their constants outside the integral, performs the interval-integral change of variables, and reduces the four finite index cases using (c)2=c(\sqrt c)^2=c(c​)2=c.

Both measures are centered Gaussian measures. The mean of their linear pushforward is zero by commutation of the Bochner integral with the continuous linear map. Their covariance bilinear forms agree on the standard orthonormal basis by the displayed Gram identity, so Gaussian uniqueness proves (Dc)#Gγ,h=Gcγ,h/c.(D_c)_\#\mathcal G_{\gamma,h}=\mathcal G_{c\gamma,h/c}.(Dc​)#​Gγ,h​=Gcγ,h/c​. No Gaussian covariance or distributional scaling is introduced as a premise.

The off-diagonal covariance acquires the product of the two different diagonal scale factors. Checking that entry is essential: it verifies that the common Brownian forcing is preserved. Gaussian uniqueness then upgrades the covariance calculation to an equality of measures.

03Transport the independent spatial blocks without losing correlation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The particle noise measure is the finite product of these two-component Gaussian blocks over the parameter and latent coordinate set. The argument applies the proved scalar Gaussian pushforward to each independent block, uses the exact product-measure mapping identity, and checks that assembling blocks into velocity and position vectors commutes with DcD_cDc​. Thus the full particle innovation obeys the same clock law. Independence between distinct spatial blocks is retained, while each block retains its exact velocity-position correlation.

The diagonal block transformation is applied independently at each spatial coordinate. Product pushforward preserves independence between blocks. Because each whole block is transformed together, its internal response correlation is retained exactly.

04Conjugate the exponential update §

Mathematical statement and proof

For c=Nc=\sqrt Nc=N​, multiply velocity by ccc and leave position fixed. Under the changed clock, the force becomes ∇VN\nabla V_N∇VN​, the friction is cγc\gammacγ, and the step is h/ch/ch/c. The exact deterministic exponential response conjugacy has already been proved. What remains is the additive innovation: 2cγ Dcξ=(c2γ/N ξv,2γ/N ξx).\sqrt{2c\gamma}\,D_c\xi=\bigl(c\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\,\xi_v,\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\,\xi_x\bigr).2cγ​Dc​ξ=(c2γ/N​ξv​,2γ/N​ξx​). One proves these two scalar identities from the positive square roots and the particle amplitude identity, then rewrites the shared exponential map. The resulting equality is an identity of the defined updates for every phase state and every supplied correlated innovation.

The transformed force is the gradient of the sum potential and the transformed velocity has unit temperature. The response scaling and thermal amplitude identities together identify both coordinates of the update. Position remains the original scaled particle position.

05Pass the clock identity through the Markov laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

The generic innovation-mapping lemma proves that replacing the noise law by R#QR_\#QR#​Q is exactly substituting RξR\xiRξ in each transition. Its proof is an induction on the iterated Markov measure: at each step, the product law is pushed through (z,ξ)↦(z,Rξ)(z,\xi)\mapsto(z,R\xi)(z,ξ)↦(z,Rξ), then through the measurable update. The phase conjugacy theorem applies this fact together with the proved update identity and the exact Gaussian block scaling. Consequently the entire KIPLMC1 Gaussian Markov law, pushed through (v,x)↦(Nv,x)(v,x)\mapsto(\sqrt N v,x)(v,x)↦(N​v,x), is exactly the shared unit-temperature KLMC Markov law for VNV_NVN​ at (Nγ,h/N)(\sqrt N\gamma,h/\sqrt N)(N​γ,h/N​). The equality includes the noise measure and every iteration, rather than merely comparing update formulas.

One-step transition equality extends by induction to all iterated laws. At each successor step, product pushforward transports the fresh innovation measure and the update together. Thus the law identity includes the full source distribution, not only a deterministic formula.

06The thermal initialization and the unchanged position marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

The physical velocity target is the explicit Gaussian pushforward N−1/2N(0,I)N^{-1/2}\mathcal N(0,I)N−1/2N(0,I). Multiplication by N\sqrt NN​ returns the standard Gaussian law. Since the position is left fixed, the independent thermal initialization becomes N(0,I)⊗ν\mathcal N(0,I)\otimes\nuN(0,I)⊗ν for every initial position law ν\nuν. One proves this using the product-measure pushforward and the thermal velocity normalization identity. Finally, applying the position projection to the full iterated-law identity proves that the KIPLMC1 position law equals the unit-temperature KLMC position law at the changed clock. This supplies the exact law transfer needed to import the shared kinetic error estimate without duplicating its analysis.

The independent thermal initialization is transported to the independent standard-Gaussian initialization required by the shared theorem. Projecting the full chain-law identity onto position removes the velocity rescaling. This allows the unit-temperature sampling estimate to be imported without changing the observable.
