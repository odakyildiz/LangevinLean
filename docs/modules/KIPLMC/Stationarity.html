Gibbs invariance of the physical-time particle phase law

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

The invariant density calculation is proved as an identity of phase laws: the complete physical Brownian phase process, started at its normalized Gaussian-times-Gibbs target, has that same target distribution at every nonnegative time.

The exact clock conjugacy transfers the full unit-temperature stationary phase law to the physical particle temperature. Position is unchanged, so the stationary parameter marginal remains the powered likelihood.

01Transport the proved full phase stationarity through the exact clock §

Mathematical statement and proof

The constructed selected flow for the sum potential preserves N(0,I)⊗πZ\mathcal N(0,I)\otimes\pi_ZN(0,I)⊗πZ​, by the shared kinetic stationarity theorem. Push this endpoint law through D1/N(v,z)=(v/N,z)D_{1/\sqrt N}(v,z)=(v/\sqrt N,z)D1/N​​(v,z)=(v/N​,z). The product pushforward is exactly N(0,N−1I)⊗πZ\mathcal N(0,N^{-1}I)\otimes\pi_ZN(0,N−1I)⊗πZ​, namely the kinetic Gibbs target. The physical KIPLD flow was defined by this very time and velocity change. The almost-everywhere identity for scalar multiplication identifies its L2L^{2}L2 phase representative with the pushed constructed representative. This proves invariance of the whole phase law, including velocity–position independence, rather than only the position marginal or a formal zero-generator calculation.

The unit-temperature kinetic flow has the full Gaussian-times-Gibbs stationary phase law. The proved clock-and-velocity conjugacy transports that law to thermal covariance I/NI/NI/N and the same position Gibbs marginal. Hence the physical KIPLD integral solution is stationary under its phase target.
