Measurable particle transitions and the laws of their recursions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

The pathwise algorithm maps become probability transitions by integrating them against independent fresh-noise measures. This section reuses the generic Markov-law construction and independence argument already proved for ULA. For KIPLMC1 it accepts a specified correlated phase innovation law; KIPLMC2 uses an explicit pair of independent standard Gaussian vectors for its two OU half steps. Law equality is kept distinct from convergence rates.

Both explicit particle recursions are identified with their independent-noise Markov iterates. The exponential transition retains correlated response pairs, while OBABO retains two distinct independent thermostat noises.

Statement and proof

01The OBABO noise law and measurability of both step maps §

Mathematical statement and proof

The thermostat noise law is the product of two standard Gaussian measures on the particle Euclidean space. Its product probability property gives total mass one. Lipschitzness of the particle force implies continuity. Unfolding the exponential or OBABO map shows that each is built from this continuous force, coordinate projections, addition and scalar multiplication, so each uncurried state–noise map is continuous and hence measurable. The argument supplies force continuity first, applies measurability of continuous maps, and uses continuity of each operation in the finite composition.

The two thermostat noises form an independent product of Gaussian vector measures. Continuity of the force makes each finite-stage update measurable. Both particle algorithms therefore define probability transitions by pushing forward the state/noise product.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.12.1; Kinetic, Section 3.20.1.

02Define numerical laws by independent-noise pushforwards §

Mathematical statement and proof

The KIPLMC1 law sequence applies the shared Markov recursion with the exponential step, an initial phase measure μ\muμ, and a supplied innovation measure η\etaη. The KIPLMC2 law sequence uses the OBABO step and its fixed two-Gaussian noise measure. Each successor law is the pushforward of the product of the current-state law and the fresh-noise law. The probability theorems invoke the proved step measurability and probability of the initial and innovation measures, so every iterate is a probability measure. For KIPLMC1 the covariance specification of η\etaη remains explicit and separate; probability alone does not identify the intended correlated Gaussian law.

At each step take a fresh innovation independent of the present state and apply the specified map. Integrating this update against its innovation measure defines one transition. Repeated pushforward gives the numerical laws without assuming an underlying chain with the desired estimates.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.33.11; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

03The explicit exponential recursion has the Markov law §

Mathematical statement and proof

First show that the KIPLMC1 sequence equals the generic noise-driven recursion. Induction is exact: both start at the same phase and their successor expressions use the same step and innovation number. The law theorem then assumes a measurable initial random phase, a jointly independent innovation sequence with common law η\etaη, and independence of initialization from the whole sequence. The shared generic result derives the law at every step; no additional per-step state–noise independence premise is inserted. Rewriting the explicit recursion by the proved equality yields the pushforward identity with the KIPLMC1 laws. This theorem establishes equality of the recursion law and the iterated transition law, while a quantitative sampling estimate still requires moment and coupling analysis for the specified Gaussian innovation.

The explicit recursion is a measurable function of initialization and the innovation prefix. Independence of the next block gives the one-step transition identity. Induction then identifies its law with the iterated exponential transition measure.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.20.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.12; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

04The independent-Gaussian OBABO trajectory has the constructed transition law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The KIPLMC2 sequence iterates the OBABO map with the two thermostat vectors from each supplied noise pair. If these pairs are independent across time, have the product law of two independent standard Gaussian vectors, and are independent of initialization, then the pushforward of the pathwise recursion equals the KIPLMC2 laws at every step. The generic independence theorem derives independence of the current state from the fresh pair from these global assumptions. The proof is a direct reuse of that theorem with the established measurable OBABO map. Independence within each pair is given by its product Gaussian law; it is not confused with the independent sequence of pairs. This completes the algorithm-law correspondence, while contraction and the discrete invariant-measure bias remain separate quantitative conclusions.

The same argument applies to the two-thermostat recursion, keeping the two noises distinct and independent. Their common block law is used at every step. Thus the trajectory’s pushforward agrees with the constructed OBABO Markov law.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.12; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.

05Choose the constructed kinetic Gaussian for Algorithm 1 §

Mathematical statement and proof

The generic law identity accepts a specified iid innovation measure. The Gaussian KIPLMC1 law sequence uses the correlated Gaussian measure constructed in the explicit correlated particle-noise construction, whose spatial pairs have the shared kinetic Gram covariance. Thus the transition and its particle scaling are explicit. The earlier iid realization theorem specializes to this measure, with ordinary independence of initialization and fresh innovations. A convergence theorem must still derive a target comparison for these laws; the definition does not assert such a bound.

For Algorithm 1 the innovation law is the constructed exponential response Gaussian, including its off-diagonal covariance. Substituting that law specializes the generic transition to the correlated-Gaussian sampler. Independent velocity and position noises would define a different law.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.
