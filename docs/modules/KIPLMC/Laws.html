Measurable particle transitions and the laws of their recursions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

Both particle algorithms are recursions Yn+1=Φ(Yn,ζn)Y_{n+1}=\Phi(Y_n,\zeta_n)Yn+1​=Φ(Yn​,ζn​) on phase space ZN×ZN\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_NZN​×ZN​, driven by fresh innovations ζn\zeta_nζn​. This section turns them into Markov chains. For a measurable update Φ\PhiΦ, an innovation law η\etaη and an initial law μ\muμ, the laws of the iterates are defined by

μ0=μ,μn+1=Φ#(μn⊗η),\mu_0=\mu,\qquad\mu_{n+1}=\Phi_\#(\mu_n\otimes\eta),μ0​=μ,μn+1​=Φ#​(μn​⊗η),

that is, μn+1\mu_{n+1}μn+1​ is the law of Φ(Y,ζ)\Phi(Y,\zeta)Φ(Y,ζ) when Y∼μnY\sim\mu_nY∼μn​ and ζ∼η\zeta\sim\etaζ∼η are independent. We prove that the KIPLMC1 update KKK and the KIPLMC2 update SSS are measurable, that the resulting μn\mu_nμn​ are probability measures, and that every realization of the recursions with an initial state independent of a sequence of independent innovations with law η\etaη has exactly the law μn\mu_nμn​ at step nnn. For KIPLMC2 the innovation law is the product of two standard Gaussian laws, one for each thermostat half-step; for KIPLMC1 it is the correlated Gaussian law constructed in the section on the innovation measure. These law identities connect the algorithms to the convergence theorems proved later; they contain no quantitative estimate.

01The thermostat noise law and measurability of both updates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. 02Markov transitions and the laws of the iterates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Let Φ:H×Z→H\Phi:H\times Z\to HΦ:H×Z→H be measurable, η\etaη a probability measure on the innovation space ZZZ and μ\muμ a probability measure on HHH. The Markov laws are μ0=μ\mu_0=\muμ0​=μ and μn+1=Φ#(μn⊗η)\mu_{n+1}=\Phi_\#(\mu_n\otimes\eta)μn+1​=Φ#​(μn​⊗η). Equivalently, μn+1=μnP\mu_{n+1}=\mu_nPμn+1​=μn​P for the Markov kernel P(y,A)=η({ζ:Φ(y,ζ)∈A})P(y,A)=\eta(\{\zeta:\Phi(y,\zeta)\in A\})P(y,A)=η({ζ:Φ(y,ζ)∈A}), since by Fubini–Tonelli

Φ#(μn⊗η)(A)=∫Hη({ζ:Φ(y,ζ)∈A}) μn(dy).\Phi_\#(\mu_n\otimes\eta)(A)=\int_H\eta\bigl(\{\zeta:\Phi(y,\zeta)\in A\}\bigr)\,\mu_n(dy).Φ#​(μn​⊗η)(A)=∫H​η({ζ:Φ(y,ζ)∈A})μn​(dy).

The KIPLMC1 laws μn(1)(μ,η)\mu^{(1)}_n(\mu,\eta)μn(1)​(μ,η) are the Markov laws for Φ=K\Phi=KΦ=K, with initial phase law μ\muμ and an innovation law η\etaη on ZN×ZN\mathcal Z_N\times\mathcal Z_NZN​×ZN​ that is left as a parameter. The KIPLMC2 laws μn(2)(μ)\mu^{(2)}_n(\mu)μn(2)​(μ) are the Markov laws for Φ((v,z),(ξ,ξ′))=S((v,z),ξ,ξ′)\Phi((v,z),(\xi,\xi'))=S((v,z),\xi,\xi')Φ((v,z),(ξ,ξ′))=S((v,z),ξ,ξ′) with η=η2\eta=\eta_2η=η2​.

Claim. Let N>0N>0N>0, L≥0L\ge0L≥0, UUU differentiable with LLL-Lipschitz gradient, γ,h∈R\gamma,h\in\mathbb Rγ,h∈R, and μ,η\mu,\etaμ,η probability measures. Then μn(1)(μ,η)\mu^{(1)}_n(\mu,\eta)μn(1)​(μ,η) and μn(2)(μ)\mu^{(2)}_n(\mu)μn(2)​(μ) are probability measures for every nnn.

Proof. Induction on nnn. For n=0n=0n=0 the law is μ\muμ. If μn\mu_nμn​ is a probability measure, then so is the product μn⊗η\mu_n\otimes\etaμn​⊗η, and the pushforward by the measurable map Φ\PhiΦ of the previous step has total mass (μn⊗η)(Φ−1(H))=(μn⊗η)(H×Z)=1(\mu_n\otimes\eta)(\Phi^{-1}(H))=(\mu_n\otimes\eta)(H\times Z)=1(μn​⊗η)(Φ−1(H))=(μn​⊗η)(H×Z)=1. □\square□

The innovation law of KIPLMC1 is kept general here so that the correlated Gaussian law can be inserted later; probability of η\etaη is the only property used in this step.

Proof. This is the Markov law theorem (ULA-SC, Section 3.33.12) for the measurable update ((v,z),(ξ,ξ′))↦S((v,z),ξ,ξ′)((v,z),(\xi,\xi'))\mapsto S((v,z),\xi,\xi')((v,z),(ξ,ξ′))↦S((v,z),ξ,ξ′) of the first step, with the induction described in the previous step. □\square□

Two different independence structures are involved. Within a step, the law η2\eta_2η2​ makes the two thermostat noises ξn\xi_nξn​ and ξn′\xi'_nξn′​ independent standard Gaussian vectors, as in Algorithm 2. Across steps, the pairs WnW_nWn​ are independent of each other and of the initial state. Within the update the two noises are not used symmetrically: ξn\xi_nξn​ affects the new position through v1/2v_{1/2}v1/2​, whereas ξn′\xi'_nξn′​ affects only the new velocity.
