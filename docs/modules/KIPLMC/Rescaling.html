Velocity and time rescaling of the exponential update

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

The physical-time particle realization can be expressed in unit-temperature kinetic coordinates by scaling velocity and the clock. This section proves the deterministic conjugacy of the exponential map and the damping identity. Gaussian covariance scaling and Brownian time-change laws remain distinct probabilistic results.

The deterministic velocity-and-clock conjugacy converts the physical average force to the unit-temperature sum-potential force. Position is fixed, while response and damping identities control the remaining phase coefficients.

Statement and proof

01Invert the velocity scale §

Mathematical statement and proof

The velocity rescaling sends (v,x)(v,x)(v,x) to (cv,x)(cv,x)(cv,x). If c≠0c\ne0c=0, applying the inverse scale yields (c−1cv,x)=(v,x)(c^{-1}cv,x)=(v,x)(c−1cv,x)=(v,x). The argument simplifies the nested scalar action and cancels the nonzero factor. Position is unchanged, so a parameter-position projection commutes with this part of the change of variables.

The velocity-only map is invertible for a nonzero scale and fixes every position observable. Hence it changes the phase coordinates without changing the parameter sample itself. Later law identities exploit exactly this unchanged position projection.

02Conjugate the shared exponential step §

Mathematical statement and proof

The exact coefficient scaling gives ψcγ,k(h/c)=ψγ,k(h)/ck\psi_{c\gamma,k}(h/c)=\psi_{\gamma,k}(h)/c^kψcγ,k​(h/c)=ψγ,k​(h)/ck. Apply a step with force ggg, friction cγc\gammacγ, duration h/ch/ch/c, initial velocity cvcvcv, and additive innovation whose velocity component is also scaled by ccc. Its result equals the velocity rescaling of a physical-time step with force g/c2g/c^2g/c2, friction γ\gammaγ, duration hhh. One proves the two scalar cancellations for the first primitive, expands the map coordinatewise, rewrites all coefficient scales, and normalizes the resulting vector identities with vector-space algebra. The innovation here is already additive; obtaining its prescribed Gaussian law under this conjugacy requires a separate covariance computation.

The response with one time primitive acquires one inverse clock factor; the position-force response acquires two. Combining those factors with the scaled velocity and force gives the deterministic step conjugacy. The noise law must be transformed separately because its two response coordinates scale differently.

Earlier results used here: Kinetic, Section 3.10.1; Kinetic, Section 3.10.3; Kinetic, Section 3.12.1.

03The invariant friction–step product and particle scale §

Mathematical statement and proof

The OU damping depends on the product γh\gamma hγh, so e−(cγ)(h/c)/2=e−γh/2e^{-(c\gamma)(h/c)/2}=e^{-\gamma h/2}e−(cγ)(h/c)/2=e−γh/2 for c≠0c\ne0c=0. The proof unfolds the damping definition, reduces equality of exponentials to equality of exponents, and cancels the nonzero factor with cancellation of nonzero denominators. For the particle choice c=Nc=\sqrt Nc=N​, the coefficient identity ((N)2)−1=N−1((\sqrt N)^2)^{-1}=N^{-1}((N​)2)−1=N−1 follows from the nonnegative square-root square theorem. Consequently the sum-potential force in rescaled time corresponds to the average-potential force in physical time.

Thermostat damping depends only on the dimensionless product of friction and step, so that product is invariant under the clock change. Choosing the scale N\sqrt NN​ converts the average force into the sum-potential force and simultaneously normalizes thermal velocity.

Earlier results used here: Kinetic, Section 3.20.5.
