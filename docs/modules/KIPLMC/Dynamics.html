Physical-time particle integral solutions and synchronous contraction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the O-B-A-B-O splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L². Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

The particle-average potential has the original pair curvature and smoothness constants, so the physical-time kinetic system can reuse the shared existence and nonlinear contraction theorems directly. The temperature changes the additive noise amplitude; the synchronous contraction does not depend on that amplitude because the common forcing cancels. The results here are explicit path statements, not stationarity statements.

The average force meets the shared kinetic existence and contraction hypotheses. Common-input cancellation explains why physical-time deterministic contraction does not deteriorate with particle number or total state dimension.

Statement and proof

01Verify differentiability and smoothness of the average §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted potential is differentiable whenever the pair potential is differentiable. Multiplying its value by the constant 1/N1/N1/N preserves differentiability, yielding differentiability of the average potential. The gradient has already been identified pointwise with the particle force. Equality at every position identifies the two vector fields. The average-gradient smoothness bound therefore gives the same Lipschitz estimate for the particle force. The result uses the original pair constant LLL, independent of NNN.

The gradient identity is essential for applying a potential-based contraction theorem: it ensures the defined force is the derivative of the stated average potential. Smoothness is then inherited with the original pair constant, while strong convexity is inherited by averaging the lifted inequality.

03The nonlinear contraction rate is independent of particle dimension §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the shared potential contraction theorem to UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​. Its required hypotheses are derived by the average-potential differentiability, strong-convexity and smoothness theorems. Rewrite each gradient in the difference equation using ∇UˉN=F\nabla\bar U_N=F∇UˉN​=F. With equal initial velocities, γ>0\gamma>0γ>0, and γ2≥m+L\gamma^2\ge m+Lγ2≥m+L, the resulting bound is ∥xt−yt∥≤2e−mt/γ∥x0−y0∥\|x_t-y_t\|\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}\|x_0-y_0\|∥xt​−yt​∥≤2​e−mt/γ∥x0​−y0​∥. Neither NNN nor dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​ appears in its rate. The theorem still explicitly requires continuous paths satisfying the noise-cancelled derivative equations; it does not replace construction of a stationary kinetic reference or taking the Wasserstein infimum.

The common additive noise cancels even though its amplitude depends on particle count. The remaining difference equation sees only the average-potential geometry. The dimension and temperature therefore affect equilibrium and discretization moments, while leaving the synchronous physical-time rate unchanged.
