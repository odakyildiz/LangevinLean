Physical-time particle integral solutions and synchronous contraction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

In the scaled coordinates ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​ of the geometry section, the kinetic interacting particle Langevin diffusion (KIPLD) of the paper reads

dzt=vt dt,dvt=(−γvt−FN(zt)) dt+2γ/N dWt,dz_t=v_t\,dt,\qquad dv_t=\bigl(-\gamma v_t-F_N(z_t)\bigr)\,dt+\sqrt{2\gamma/N}\,dW_t,dzt​=vt​dt,dvt​=(−γvt​−FN​(zt​))dt+2γ/N​dWt​,

where WWW is a standard Brownian motion on ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​, z=(θ,X1/N,…,XN/N)z=(\theta,X^1/\sqrt N,\dots,X^N/\sqrt N)z=(θ,X1/N​,…,XN/N​) and v=(Vθ,Vx1/N,…,VxN/N)v=(V^\theta,V^{x_1}/\sqrt N,\dots,V^{x_N}/\sqrt N)v=(Vθ,Vx1​/N​,…,VxN​/N​). By the coordinate formulas for FNF_NFN​, the parameter and latent components of this equation are exactly the equations (KIPLD) of the paper, in physical time.

Because FN=∇UˉNF_N=\nabla\bar U_NFN​=∇UˉN​ with UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ mmm-strongly convex and LLL-smooth, the kinetic theory for a single potential applies with the original constants. This section records the two analytic consequences used later: the integral form of the equation has a continuous solution on every finite time interval for every continuous additive input, and two solutions driven by the same input and started with the same velocity approach each other at the rate e−mt/γe^{-mt/\gamma}e−mt/γ, whenever γ2≥m+L\gamma^2\ge m+Lγ2≥m+L. Neither the dimension dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​ nor the temperature 1/N1/N1/N enters this rate. These are statements about paths; laws and stationarity are treated in later sections.

01Differentiability and smoothness of the averaged potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let UUU be differentiable. Then UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ is differentiable for every N≥0N\ge0N≥0. If moreover N>0N>0N>0, L≥0L\ge0L≥0 and ∇U\nabla U∇U is LLL-Lipschitz, then ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​ is LLL-Lipschitz.

Proof. The lifted potential VN=∑iU∘JiV_N=\sum_iU\circ J_iVN​=∑i​U∘Ji​ is differentiable as a finite sum of compositions of a differentiable function with linear maps (IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3), and a constant multiple of a differentiable function is differentiable; hence UˉN=N−1VN\bar U_N=N^{-1}V_NUˉN​=N−1VN​ is differentiable. By the gradient identity of the geometry section, ∇UˉN(z)=FN(z)\nabla\bar U_N(z)=F_N(z)∇UˉN​(z)=FN​(z) for every zzz (KIPLMC, Section 3.1.2), so the maps ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​ and FNF_NFN​ coincide. Under the additional hypotheses FNF_NFN​ is LLL-Lipschitz (KIPLMC, Section 3.1.5), hence so is ∇UˉN\nabla\bar U_N∇UˉN​. □\square□

Together with the strong convexity of UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ proved in the geometry section, UˉN\bar U_NUˉN​ satisfies the three hypotheses of the kinetic contraction theory for a potential fff: differentiability, mmm-strong convexity, and an LLL-Lipschitz gradient, with the constants of UUU.
