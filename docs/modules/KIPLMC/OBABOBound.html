KIPLMC2 position transport and parameter root-mean-square error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

The entire KIPLMC2 error estimate follows from the shared OBABO sampler, its exact particle clock change and the sharp parameter concentration theorem. Every initial law, Gaussian innovation, target transport and finite output moment is established internally. The explicit bound below has the thermal dimension factor (dθ+Ndx)/N\sqrt{(d_\theta+Nd_x)/N}(dθ​+Ndx​)/N​, and consequently also implies the supplied manuscript’s coarser particle dependence.

The KIPLMC2 sampling estimate follows from whole-law conjugacy and the shared invariant-comparator argument. Its thermal dimension factor then combines with sharp equilibrium parameter concentration.

01Transfer the independent thermal initialization and position law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The physical scaled velocity law is N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2 times a standard Gaussian, independently of the initial position ν\nuν. Multiplication by N\sqrt NN​ restores the exact independent standard-Gaussian initialization. The product pushforward identity proves this equality. Applying the already proved whole-chain Markov conjugacy and then projecting to positions gives equality of the KIPLMC2 position law and the standard-temperature OBABO position law at γ′=Nγ\gamma\prime=\sqrt N\gammaγ′=N​γ, h′=h/Nh\prime=h/\sqrt Nh′=h/N​, for the sum potential VNV_NVN​.

The velocity scaling changes only the velocity coordinate, so the position output law is unchanged. Whole-chain conjugacy identifies that output with the shared sampler under its transformed curvature, friction, and step. This permits direct transfer of the numerical theorem.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.113.3; Kinetic, Section 3.20.5.

Velocity rescaling leaves the second coordinate unchanged, so the projected law is exactly the standard-temperature position law.

02Cancel all clock factors in rate and admissibility §

Mathematical statement and proof

With a general positive clock factor ccc, curvature becomes c2m,c2Lc^2m,c^2Lc2m,c2L, friction becomes cγc\gammacγ, and the step becomes h/ch/ch/c. Hence m′h′/(3γ′)=mh/(3γ)m\prime h\prime/(3\gamma\prime)=mh/(3\gamma)m′h′/(3γ′)=mh/(3γ). The friction threshold 4L≤γ24L\le\gamma^24L≤γ2 and the step threshold 33γ3h≤m33\gamma^3h\le m33γ3h≤m are preserved by multiplying through by c2c^2c2. One proves these cancellations directly, keeping the nonzero clock factor explicit.

The contraction ratio contains one curvature factor, one step factor, and one inverse friction factor. Their clock powers cancel. The friction and stability assumptions scale by a common positive factor, so their validity is preserved by the change of units.

04Apply the OBABO sampling theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

The sum potential inherits strong convexity NmNmNm and smoothness NLNLNL from the original joint potential. Its Gibbs target is exactly the defined lifted particle target. The theorem applies the proved unit-temperature sampler bound, transports its position law through the exact conjugacy, and simplifies the rate and bias. It proves both finiteness of the output Wasserstein distance and

W2(νn,πN)≤2(1−mh3γ)n/2W2(ν,πN)+62γmhdzN(4L+Lh2).W_2(\nu_n,\pi_N)\le\sqrt2\left(1-\frac{mh}{3\gamma}\right)^{n/2}W_2(\nu,\pi_N)+\frac{6\sqrt2\gamma}{m}h\sqrt{\frac{d_z}{N}}\left(4\sqrt L+\frac{Lh}{2}\right).W2​(νn​,πN​)≤2​(1−3γmh​)n/2W2​(ν,πN​)+m62​γ​hNdz​​​(4L​+2Lh​).

The two-thermostat Gaussian laws are part of the constructed Markov recursion. No stationarity, reference-path or local-error estimate is assumed.

The numerical law, target, and all analytic assumptions have been identified before invoking the shared result. Its finite-output transport conclusion therefore transfers along with its inequality. The resulting physical-time bound applies to the two-thermostat Gaussian particle recursion.

05Project to the parameter and add the sharp concentration term §

Mathematical statement and proof

The initial finite second moment and strong convexity give a finite initial transport distance. The preceding theorem gives finite output transport. The parameter projection has norm at most one, so its RMS error around the unique MMLE is bounded by the position transport distance plus the Gibbs parameter RMS error. The latter is at most dθ/(Nm)\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}dθ​/(Nm)​. The final theorem therefore adds this third term to the KIPLMC2 sampling estimate and expresses the result as the square root of the parameter-error integral.

Project an output coupling to the common parameter and use its nonexpansiveness. The equilibrium powered marginal lies within the sharp statistical radius of the unique maximizer. Adding this radius to the numerical transport bound yields the displayed parameter RMS estimate.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.21.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.6; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.3.2; IPLA-SC, Section 3.5.1; Kinetic, Section 3.113.4.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

hπ obtains the Gibbs second moment and hfinite constructs finite initial transport. hb is the preceding physical-time sampling result and hc is parameter_concentration. hp applies IPLA.projectedRMSReal_le_wasserstein2Real_add with the parameter projection of norm at most one. hf proves that projected moment finite, allowing IPLA.projectedRMSReal_eq_sqrt_integral to recover the displayed square-root integral. The final inequality substitutes hb.2.
