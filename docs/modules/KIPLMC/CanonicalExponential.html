The KIPLMC1 parameter root-mean-square error bound

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

The KIPLMC1 Gaussian Markov laws are identified with the shared constructed KLMC laws at the changed clock. The shared kinetic theorem constructs its Brownian reference, proves exact Gibbs stationarity, bounds the local error, and takes the infimum over initial couplings. The particle geometry and sharp parameter concentration then give the paper’s three explicit error terms.

The constructed shared theorem applies to the sum potential and correlated-Gaussian chain. Exact clock cancellation and sharp parameter concentration yield the three explicit physical-time KIPLMC1 error contributions.

01The particle noise source with the Gaussian Markov law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The particle coordinate set is Fin(dθ)⊔(Fin(N)×Fin(dx))\mathrm{Fin}(d_\theta)\sqcup(\mathrm{Fin}(N)\times\mathrm{Fin}(d_x))Fin(dθ​)⊔(Fin(N)×Fin(dx​)). Its complete constructed innovation stream has the proved product law of independent spatial blocks, each with the exact correlated two-component Gaussian distribution. The argument instantiates the product-source Markov-law theorem with this Brownian source, its whole-stream law, and an independent standard Gaussian initial velocity. This identifies the kinetic position law constructed above with the position marginal of the Gaussian Markov iterate. The particle clock-rescaling theorem then gives the exact identity between the physical-time KIPLMC1 output and this same specified output for VNV_NVN​ at friction Nγ\sqrt N\gammaN​γ and step h/Nh/\sqrt Nh/N​. The position target is unchanged.

The finite particle coordinate set has cardinality dθ+Ndxd_\theta+Nd_xdθ​+Ndx​, even when the parameter and latent dimensions differ. The generic shared construction applies directly to that set. Its Brownian response law therefore matches the particle Gaussian transition without an artificial dimensional identification.

02The transformed assumptions and all particle-count cancellations §

Mathematical statement and proof

In scaled particle coordinates, the sum potential VNV_NVN​ has strong convexity NmNmNm and gradient Lipschitz constant NLNLNL. Put c=Nc=\sqrt Nc=N​, γ′=cγ\gamma^\prime=c\gammaγ′=cγ, and h′=h/ch^\prime=h/ch′=h/c. 03Derive finite transport moments for the output §

Mathematical statement and proof

The initial position law has the stated finite second moment. Strong convexity proves the corresponding moment of the normalized Gibbs target; their independent product therefore supplies a finite transport coupling. The independent product construction places this coupling on the same Gaussian/Brownian source as the chain. Its numerical and exact reference positions are L2L^{2}L2 random variables, and exact reference stationarity has already been proved from the splitting limit. Their L2L^{2}L2 discrepancy supplies a finite output transport coupling. The proof transfers this finiteness through the exact particle law identity. Thus the later real-valued Wasserstein and parameter RMS expressions are used only after their finiteness has been proved.

Finite initialization and target moments construct a finite-cost coupling. The shared constructed chain and stationary reference then give finite output transport on the source. This validates every later real-valued transport and parameter-error integral.

The shared theorem is applied to the sum potential, using its derived NmNmNm-strong convexity and NLNLNL-smoothness. 05Project to the parameter and add the sharp equilibrium concentration §

Mathematical statement and proof

The parameter projection is a continuous linear map with norm at most one. The powered Gibbs parameter marginal satisfies the proved sharp concentration bound EπN∥θ−θ⋆∥2≤dθ/(Nm)\mathbb E_{\pi_N}\|\theta-\theta_\star\|^2\le d_\theta/(Nm)EπN​​∥θ−θ⋆​∥2≤dθ​/(Nm). The transport-to-RMS inequality therefore gives (E∥θn−θ⋆∥2)1/2≤2(1−3mh4γ)nW2(ν,πN)+Lh2mdθ+NdxN+dθNm.\bigl(\mathbb E\|\theta_n-\theta_\star\|^2\bigr)^{1/2}\le\sqrt2\left(1-\frac{3mh}{4\gamma}\right)^nW_2(\nu,\pi_N)+\frac{Lh\sqrt2}{m}\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta+Nd_x}{N}}+\sqrt{\frac{d_\theta}{Nm}}.(E∥θn​−θ⋆​∥2)1/2≤2​(1−4γ3mh​)nW2​(ν,πN​)+mLh2​​Ndθ​+Ndx​​​+Nmdθ​​​. The argument invokes the output finiteness theorem, the sharp equilibrium concentration theorem, and the nonexpansive projection theorem. It rewrites the finite RMS as the displayed square root of the Bochner integral and substitutes the proved Gaussian position-law estimate. These are the initialization, discretization, and finite-particle concentration errors in the paper’s theorem.

The parameter projection is nonexpansive, so it cannot increase the transport discrepancy. Minkowski’s inequality adds the equilibrium parameter radius around the maximizer. This decomposes the parameter root-mean-square error into transient, discretization, and statistical contributions.

Mathematical statement and proof

The initial Wasserstein distance is bounded by every initial coupling cost. In particular, independent Z0∼νZ_0\sim\nuZ0​∼ν and Zˉ⋆∼πN\bar Z_\star\sim\pi_NZˉ⋆​∼πN​ give W2(ν,πN)≤(E∥Z0−Zˉ⋆∥2)1/2.W_2(\nu,\pi_N)\le\bigl(\mathbb E\|Z_0-\bar Z_\star\|^2\bigr)^{1/2}.W2​(ν,πN​)≤(E∥Z0​−Zˉ⋆​∥2)1/2. The argument constructs this independent product coupling, derives the integrability of its discrepancy from the two proved second moments, and rewrites its finite L2L^{2}L2 cost as the square-root integral. It also proves that the stated step restrictions make the geometric rate nonnegative. Multiplying the initial coupling inequality by this nonnegative rate gives exactly the paper’s printed constant C1C_1C1​; the other two constants are unchanged. Thus the Wasserstein statement and the printed independent-initialization statement are both consequences for the same sampler.

The independent initial product coupling is one admissible plan, so its root-mean-square cost bounds the infimum defining Wasserstein distance. A nonnegative transient coefficient preserves that comparison. The printed initialization constant is therefore a corollary of the stronger Wasserstein estimate.
