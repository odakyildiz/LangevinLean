The KIPLMC2 thermostat–kick–drift–kick–thermostat update

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the O-B-A-B-O splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L². Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

In scaled physical-time particle coordinates every velocity block has temperature 1/N1/N1/N. The parameter coordinates stay unchanged. Multiplying a latent coordinate by N\sqrt NN​ recovers the physical latent position and velocity and the unit-temperature latent noise used in Algorithm 2. The module proves these coordinate identities for the five-subflow map; no OBABO contraction theorem is hidden in the definitions.

The particle OBABO map retains the exact order of the five substeps in scaled coordinates. Undoing latent scaling restores the physical thermostat temperature and the original pair-gradient half kicks.

Statement and proof

01The scaled thermostat and half velocity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let δ=e−γh/2\delta=e^{-\gamma h/2}δ=e−γh/2 and σN=(1−δ2)/N\sigma_N=\sqrt{(1-\delta^2)/N}σN​=(1−δ2)/N​. The thermostat amplitude is σN\sigma_NσN​, and the KIPLMC2 update uses it with the force FFF to the shared OBABO map, and the half-step velocity records v1/2=δv+σNξ−hF(x)/2v_{1/2}=\delta v+\sigma_N\xi-hF(x)/2v1/2​=δv+σN​ξ−hF(x)/2. The algorithm uses this newly formed half velocity for its full position drift. The second force kick is evaluated at the new position, and the final OU half step receives the distinct noise ξ′\xi'ξ′. The position and velocity formulas follow by evaluating these five successive maps.

The first thermostat refresh and first force kick create the velocity used by the drift. The final force is evaluated at the newly updated position before the second thermostat refresh. Reordering either evaluation would produce a different splitting method.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.20.1; Kinetic, Section 3.20.5.

02The parameter half velocity contains the average gradient §

Mathematical statement and proof

Evaluating the half velocity on parameter index jjj gives

(v1/2)θ,j=δvθ,j+σNξθ,j−h2N∑i∇θU(θ,Xi)j.(v_{1/2})_{\theta,j}=\delta v_{\theta,j}+\sigma_N\xi_{\theta,j} -\frac{h}{2N}\sum_i\nabla_\theta U(\theta,X^i)_j.(v1/2​)θ,j​=δvθ,j​+σN​ξθ,j​−2Nh​i∑​∇θ​U(θ,Xi)j​.

The proof unfolds the half-velocity definition, distributes coordinate evaluation over vector addition, subtraction and scalar multiplication, then substitutes the parameter-average force identity. The remaining equality is scalar rational algebra, verified by distributing scalar products. This proof keeps the particle mean in the force rather than adding an assumed algorithm-equivalence premise.

The average parameter gradient is inherited from the averaged potential. It is multiplied by the half-step length because this is one of the two B stages. The full parameter force action over a step is distributed between the old and new positions.

03Undo latent scaling in the first O and B substeps §

Mathematical statement and proof

The thermostat identity is NσN=1−δ2\sqrt N\sigma_N=\sqrt{1-\delta^2}N​σN​=1−δ2​. Its proof combines square roots and cancels the positive NNN inside the argument. Multiplying the latent half velocity by N\sqrt NN​ then gives

N(v1/2)i=δ(Nvi)+1−δ2ξi−h2∇xU(θ,Xi).\sqrt N(v_{1/2})_i=\delta(\sqrt Nv_i)+\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\xi_i-\frac h2\nabla_xU(\theta,X^i).N​(v1/2​)i​=δ(N​vi​)+1−δ2​ξi​−2h​∇x​U(θ,Xi).

The coordinate proof expands multiplication through the three terms, applies the proved thermostat and force-unscaling identities, and normalizes multiplication order with expansion of the scalar polynomials. This yields exactly the physical latent OU refresh and unaveraged force kick.

The latent scaling cancels the temperature reduction in thermostat amplitude. Its physical noise is therefore the standard unit-temperature OU noise. The same scaling cancels the averaging in the latent force, giving the physical pair-gradient kick.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.20.5.

04Recover physical parameter and latent position drifts §

Mathematical statement and proof

The common position formula is z+=z+hv1/2z^+=z+h v_{1/2}z+=z+hv1/2​. Its parameter coordinate reads θ+=θ+hV1/2θ\theta^+=\theta+hV^\theta_{1/2}θ+=θ+hV1/2θ​ directly because that coordinate was not scaled; its theorem is definitional. In a latent coordinate, multiply both sides by N\sqrt NN​ and distribute: Xi+=Xi+hV1/2xiX^{i+}=X^i+hV^{x_i}_{1/2}Xi+=Xi+hV1/2xi​​. The argument rewrites the shared position identity, simplifies Euclidean coordinate operations and proves the scalar distributive equality with expansion of the scalar polynomials. Together with the half-velocity and final-velocity identities, these are the paper's complete pointwise OBABO stages. Gaussian transition laws and their numerical bias remain separate analytic assertions.

Position does not receive an independent direct noise in this splitting. Its randomness is transported by the refreshed half velocity. The parameter and latent position formulas therefore follow from the same deterministic drift stage after their respective coordinate scalings.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1.
