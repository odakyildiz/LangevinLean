The KIPLMC2 thermostat–kick–drift–kick–thermostat update

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

KIPLMC2 is the OBABO splitting scheme applied to the kinetic particle system in the scaled coordinates ZN\mathcal Z_NZN​ of the geometry section. For a step size hhh, friction γ\gammaγ and a phase state (v,z)(v,z)(v,z), velocity first, one step composes five maps: an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck half-step (O) on the velocity, a half kick (B) by the force FNF_NFN​, a full drift (A) of the position, a second half kick at the new position, and a second Ornstein–Uhlenbeck half-step with a fresh noise vector. With δ=e−γh/2\delta=e^{-\gamma h/2}δ=e−γh/2 and σN=(1−δ2)/N\sigma_N=\sqrt{(1-\delta^2)/N}σN​=(1−δ2)/N​, the result is

v1/2=δv+σNξ−h2FN(z),z+=z+hv1/2,v+=δ(v1/2−h2FN(z+))+σNξ′.v_{1/2}=\delta v+\sigma_N\xi-\tfrac h2F_N(z),\qquad z^+=z+hv_{1/2},\qquad v^+=\delta\bigl(v_{1/2}-\tfrac h2F_N(z^+)\bigr)+\sigma_N\xi'.v1/2​=δv+σN​ξ−2h​FN​(z),z+=z+hv1/2​,v+=δ(v1/2​−2h​FN​(z+))+σN​ξ′.

This section defines the step and proves these formulas, and then proves that in coordinates they are Algorithm 2 of the paper: the parameter half kick uses the averaged parameter gradient, and after multiplication by N\sqrt NN​ the latent half velocity has the unit-temperature thermostat noise 1−δ2 ξ\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\,\xi1−δ2​ξ and the single-particle gradient. All statements are identities for given noise vectors ξ,ξ′\xi,\xi'ξ,ξ′; the law of the resulting chain and its convergence are studied in later sections.
