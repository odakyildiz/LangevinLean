The physical-time integral equation under velocity and time scaling

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the OOO-BBB-AAA-BBB-OOO splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2. Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

Statement and proof

01Make velocity scaling a bounded linear map §

Mathematical statement and proof

The velocity projection multiplied by ccc, paired with the unchanged position projection, defines a continuous linear map. Its action is exactly the velocity rescaling. Linearity allows this map to pass through Bochner integrals; continuity ensures the transformed path remains continuous. Expanding the phase drift proves c−1Dc−1Rg,cγ(z)=Rc−2g,γ(Dc−1z)c^{-1}D_{c^{-1}}R_{g,c\gamma}(z)=R_{c^{-2}g,\gamma}(D_{c^{-1}}z)c−1Dc−1​Rg,cγ​(z)=Rc−2g,γ​(Dc−1​z). The two powers of c−1c^{-1}c−1 in the force come from time and velocity separately.

Velocity scaling is a bounded linear map on the full phase space. It therefore commutes with Bochner integration and preserves continuity of the constructed paths. Its position component is the identity, so physical parameter observables are unaffected.

02Change variables inside the integral equation §

Mathematical statement and proof

For 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T0≤t≤T, positivity of ccc gives t/c∈[0,T/c]t/c\in[0,T/c]t/c∈[0,T/c]. Apply the continuous linear map to the given solution identity at t/ct/ct/c, pass it through the integral, and substitute r=s/cr=s/cr=s/c. The drift identity identifies the transformed force and friction, and the additive source becomes c−1Bt/cc^{-1}B_{t/c}c−1Bt/c​. One proves equality of the complete vector-valued integrals, not merely equality of differential expressions. This theorem requires no derivative of the Brownian input.

Apply the map to the original solution equation and perform the positive time substitution inside its drift integral. The force, friction, and thermal input transform with their stated factors. This proves an identity between integral solutions, not merely between formal differential equations.
