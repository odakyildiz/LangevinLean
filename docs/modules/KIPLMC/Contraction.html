Contraction of the KIPLMC2 steps and chains

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The audit uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and sections, including the stated particle-dependent OBABO rate. KIPLMC1 reuses the shared kinetic exponential integrator with its exact Cholesky-generated correlated Gaussian law; KIPLMC2 uses the checked O-B-A-B-O scheme. Physical-time Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both actual Gaussian parameter RMS theorems, exact clock and algorithm conjugacies, sharp MMLE concentration, explicit accuracy budgets, and the logistic analytic example are proved. The internal OBABO proof gives a stronger direct estimate and recovers the supplied coarse rate with an explicit uniform constant. Continuous solutions use the canonical continuous-L² model; empirical experiments are recorded separately.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

KIPLMC2 inherits the shared OBABO contraction by using the averaged particle potential. Its gradient is exactly the defined particle force, and its strong convexity and smoothness constants remain m,Lm,Lm,L, independent of the number of particles. The thermostat temperature changes the noise amplitude; common innovations cancel exactly in a synchronous comparison.

The shared nonlinear OBABO contraction applies to the scaled particle force. Common noises, explicit recursion, and endpoint projection transfer the bound directly to particle trajectories.

01Transfer the proved one-step phase inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write E(v,x)=∥x∥2+2⟨x,v⟩/γ+∥v∥2/L\mathcal E(v,x)=\lVert x\rVert^2+2\langle x,v\rangle/\gamma+\lVert v\rVert^2/LE(v,x)=∥x∥2+2⟨x,v⟩/γ+∥v∥2/L. Under γ2≥4L\gamma^2\ge4Lγ2≥4L and 33γ3h≤m33\gamma^3h\le m33γ3h≤m, the shared theorem proves E(S(z,ξ,ξ′)−S(w,ξ,ξ′))≤(1−mh/(3γ))E(z−w)\mathcal E(S(z,\xi,\xi\prime)-S(w,\xi,\xi\prime))\le(1-mh/(3\gamma))\mathcal E(z-w)E(S(z,ξ,ξ′)−S(w,ξ,ξ′))≤(1−mh/(3γ))E(z−w). The argument supplies the proved differentiability, strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient of the average potential, then rewrites its gradient as the force. Both thermostat inputs are the same in the two copies. No Gaussian assumption is needed for this pathwise inequality.

The particle force satisfies the shared strong-smooth potential hypotheses with the original constants. Substitution into the proved OBABO phase inequality therefore gives one-step contraction for the defined particle map under common thermostat noises.

02Use the defined chain recurrence at every step §

Mathematical statement and proof

The chain is the concrete noise-driven recursion, not a sequence assumed to satisfy an estimate. Its successor identity supplies exactly the shared OBABO iteration hypotheses. Repeated contraction gives E(Zn−Z~n)≤qnE(Z0−Z~0)\mathcal E(Z_n-\widetilde Z_n)\le q^n\mathcal E(Z_0-\widetilde Z_0)E(Zn​−Zn​)≤qnE(Z0​−Z0​), where q=1−mh/(3γ)q=1-mh/(3\gamma)q=1−mh/(3γ). All noise values may be arbitrary: the result therefore applies to the Gaussian algorithm after its independent-noise law is constructed.

Use the same first and second thermostat innovations for the two trajectories at every step. Their current discrepancy satisfies the same deterministic contraction inequality, so induction gives the geometric phase bound. This is a statement about the explicit recursion.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.112.3; Kinetic, Section 3.69.1.

03Project to positions with a common initial velocity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The phase energy satisfies E(v,x)≥∥x∥2/2\mathcal E(v,x)\ge\lVert x\rVert^2/2E(v,x)≥∥x∥2/2. If the initial velocities agree, its initial value is exactly the squared initial position discrepancy. Taking square roots gives ∥Xn−X~n∥≤2qn∥X0−X~0∥\lVert X_n-\widetilde X_n\rVert\le\sqrt{2q^n}\lVert X_0-\widetilde X_0\rVert∥Xn​−Xn​∥≤2qn​∥X0​−X0​∥. The physical-time contraction does not acquire an artificial particle factor. This is the stability part of the sampling analysis; comparison with the Gibbs target also needs the separately proved discretization bias.

A common initial velocity removes its contribution to the initial phase discrepancy. The verified projection then bounds the endpoint position difference. The geometric coefficient consequently applies to position initialization without introducing a separate velocity transport term.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; Kinetic, Section 3.112.3.
