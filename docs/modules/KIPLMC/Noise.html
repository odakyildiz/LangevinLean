Correlated Gaussian velocity and position innovations in particle coordinates

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The comparison uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and its sections, including the OBABO rate that depends on the particle number. KIPLMC1 is the kinetic exponential integrator applied to the scaled particle potential, with correlated Gaussian increments represented by a Cholesky factor. KIPLMC2 uses the O-B-A-B-O splitting. The results prove diffusion convergence in physical time, Gibbs invariance, root mean square parameter estimates for both Gaussian recursions, time-rescaling identities, concentration about the marginal maximizer, and iteration counts. The logistic example is checked analytically. The OBABO argument proves a stronger estimate and then deduces the stated coarser rate with a uniform constant. The continuous solutions are continuous in L². Numerical experiments are reported separately from these estimates.

  • Paul Valsecchi Oliva and Ö. Deniz Akyildiz (2026). Kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo. Statistics and Computing 36, article 221. arXiv
  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv
  • Pierre Monmarché (2021). High-dimensional MCMC with a standard splitting scheme for the underdamped Langevin diffusion. Electronic Journal of Statistics 15(2), 4117–4166. Full paper

Velocity and position innovations within a coordinate share a Brownian source and are correlated. Distinct spatial coordinates are independent. This section constructs that joint probability measure using the kinetic library's two-dimensional Gram Gaussian, directly on the parameter-and-particle coordinate set. It works also when one of the dimensions is zero.

The innovation is built from independent spatial response blocks. Packing preserves each block’s correlation and gives the finite-dimensional Gaussian vectors used by the defined first particle algorithm.

Statement and proof

01Construct independent spatial Gaussian blocks and assemble phase vectors §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coordinate set is I={1,…,dθ}⊔({1,…,N}×{1,…,dx})I=\{1,\ldots,d_\theta\}\sqcup(\{1,\ldots,N\}\times\{1,\ldots,d_x\})I={1,…,dθ​}⊔({1,…,N}×{1,…,dx​}). For each i∈Ii\in Ii∈I, take the shared kinetic Gaussian (εi,εi′)∼N(0,Cγ,h)(\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_i\prime)\sim\mathcal N(0,C_{\gamma,h})(εi​,εi​′)∼N(0,Cγ,h​), independently across iii. Their finite product measure is the independent spatial Gaussian blocks. The assembly map collects the first entries into the velocity vector and the second entries into the position vector. The proof defines these two Euclidean vectors with the Euclidean identification and proves the map measurable coordinate by coordinate. Pushing the product measure forward gives the correlated exponential noise. Probability follows from the Gaussian probability properties and preservation of total mass under the measurable map; no normalization assumption is supplied.

For each scaled particle-space coordinate, draw one two-response Gaussian block. Independent products over coordinates preserve the within-block velocity–position covariance. Packing all first responses and all second responses produces the two vector innovations used in the update.

Earlier results used here: Kinetic, Section 3.26.3.

02Coordinate independence and within-pair covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

The product-space evaluation maps are independent, so the Gaussian blocks belonging to distinct coordinates are independent. Evaluating the assembled velocity and position at a fixed spatial coordinate recovers its original two-dimensional Gaussian block exactly. The proof composes the two pushforwards, proves equality of the composed function with the block evaluation map by checking the two possible indices, and uses the product-measure evaluation law. The covariance of the pair is therefore exactly the kinetic Gram matrix, already proved positive semidefinite in the shared library. Multiplication by 2γ/N\sqrt{2\gamma/N}2γ/N​ is performed by the particle step, not by this base noise law.

Different spatial coordinate blocks are independent because they are projections of different product factors. The two entries of a single block generally remain correlated through their common response Gram matrix. Both properties are required to recover the intended particle transition law.
