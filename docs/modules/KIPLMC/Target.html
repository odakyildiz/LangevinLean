The normalized kinetic Gibbs candidate and sharp parameter concentration

References and publication status for this development

Published source; checked diffusion and sampler proofs

The audit uses the supplied KIPLMC main.tex and sections, including the stated particle-dependent OBABO rate. KIPLMC1 reuses the shared kinetic exponential integrator with its exact Cholesky-generated correlated Gaussian law; KIPLMC2 uses the checked O-B-A-B-O scheme. Physical-time Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both actual Gaussian parameter RMS theorems, exact clock and algorithm conjugacies, sharp MMLE concentration, explicit accuracy budgets, and the logistic analytic example are proved. The internal OBABO proof gives a stronger direct estimate and recovers the supplied coarse rate with an explicit uniform constant. Continuous solutions use the canonical continuous-L² model; empirical experiments are recorded separately.

The velocity-first phase target is the product of an explicit Gaussian thermal velocity measure and the already normalized lifted Gibbs position measure. Its parameter marginal is the powered likelihood, and its concentration radius has the paper's sharp dimension coefficient. These results concern a concrete probability measure; the section deliberately does not claim invariance of that measure under a kinetic diffusion.

The phase target combines explicit thermal Gaussian velocities with the normalized lifted Gibbs position law. Its powered-likelihood parameter marginal supplies the sharp equilibrium concentration around the unique marginal maximizer.

01Construct thermal velocities as a Gaussian pushforward §

Mathematical statement and proof

Start with the standard Gaussian on the scaled particle Euclidean space and map v↦v/Nv\mapsto v/\sqrt Nv↦v/N​. thermal Gaussian velocity measure defines this measure, giving covariance I/NI/NI/N for positive NNN. The probability proof uses measurability of the scalar map, evaluates its preimage of the whole space, and obtains mass one. The definition also makes sense at N=0N=0N=0 under the convention 0−1=00^{-1}=00−1=0, but the physical target and subsequent normalization theorems require N>0N>0N>0. No unspecified Gaussian velocity law is assumed.

The velocity pushforward is a concrete centered Gaussian with covariance I/NI/NI/N for positive particle count. Its finite moments follow from Gaussian scaling. This gives both the exact thermal initialization and the equilibrium velocity factor used in the particle coupling proof.

02Normalize the product and compute its position marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

the kinetic Gibbs target is N(0,I/N)⊗πN\mathcal N(0,I/N)\otimes\pi_NN(0,I/N)⊗πN​, with velocity first. Joint strong convexity and differentiability of the original pair potential imply that the shared lifted Gibbs target πN\pi_NπN​ is a probability measure. Both factors have total mass one, so their product does as well. Mapping the product by its second projection integrates out the first factor, whose total mass is one, so the result is exactly πN\pi_NπN​. The proof uses the second marginal of a product measure and simplifies the mass scalar. This computes the normalized marginal rather than merely comparing unnormalized density expressions.

The position density is proportional to e−VNe^{-V_N}e−VN​, while the kinetic velocity density is proportional to e−N∥v∥2/2e^{-N\|v\|^2/2}e−N∥v∥2/2. Their product is the physical-temperature phase target. Product normalization immediately gives its independence structure and exact position marginal.

03Identify the powered likelihood parameter marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

Map the phase target to θ\thetaθ by composing position projection with the parameter continuous linear map. The pushforward composition theorem identifies this with first taking the position marginal and then the parameter marginal. The preceding result gives the exact lifted Gibbs position law; the shared IPLA theorem identifies its parameter marginal as pθ(y)Np_\theta(y)^Npθ​(y)N after normalization. Measurability of both maps is provided explicitly. The theorem therefore connects the concrete phase target with the same normalized marginal target used by the concentration proof.

Integrating latent particles factorizes conditionally on the common parameter: each contributes the same marginal likelihood factor. Their product is the NNN-th power of that likelihood. The normalized pushforward identity makes this statistical interpretation precise.

04Construct the unique marginal maximizer and its sharp second moment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Joint strong convexity makes the negative log marginal strongly convex after latent integration. The shared IPLA theorem supplies existence and uniqueness of its minimizer, equivalently of the marginal likelihood maximizer. For that θ∗\theta_*θ∗​, the sharp concentration theorem gives ∫∥θ−θ∗∥2dπΘ≤dθ/(Nm)\int\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2d\pi_\Theta\le d_\theta/(Nm)∫∥θ−θ∗​∥2dπΘ​≤dθ​/(Nm). These results apply to the concrete target for the target, not an abstract measure with a stated concentration field. The last theorem expresses the square-root bound in the existing extended-nonnegative transport notation as (∫∥Pθz−θ∗∥2 dπN(z))1/2≤dθ/(Nm)\left(\int\|P_\theta z-\theta_*\|^2\,d\pi_N(z)\right)^{1/2}\le\sqrt{d_\theta/(Nm)}(∫∥Pθ​z−θ∗​∥2dπN​(z))1/2≤dθ​/(Nm)​.

The parameter concentration scale is N−1/2N^{-1/2}N−1/2, with the sharp dimension coefficient dθ/m\sqrt{d_\theta/m}dθ​/m​. It describes equilibrium error relative to the likelihood maximizer. Numerical discretization and finite-time initialization errors must be added separately in an algorithmic bound.
