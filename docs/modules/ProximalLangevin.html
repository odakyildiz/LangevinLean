Proximal particle algorithms and mean-field comparisons

References and publication status for this development

Published source; unpublished corrected proofs

The algorithms are from the published paper below; the correspondence audit uses arXiv:2406.14292v3 (29 May 2025). The corrected MYIPLA, PIPGLA, and MYPGD bounds and proofs presented here are unpublished. The audit records their differences from the paper’s printed rates and constants, and the claims that remain outside the formalization.

  • Paula Cordero Encinar, Francesca Romana Crucinio, and Omer Deniz Akyildiz (2025). Proximal Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithms. Proceedings of the Forty-first Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 286, 1220–1265. Audited version: arXiv:2406.14292v3

Proximal optimality identifies the Moreau gradient and the particle update formulas. MYIPLA is a smooth Gaussian Euler scheme, PIPULA uses the full envelope’s reduced curvature, and PIPGLA is controlled by its pairwise energy inequality and stationary comparison. Mean-field equations and particle comparisons follow from Lipschitz integral equations and independent-sample variance. Finite potential gaps give the original marginal-center comparisons under their stated assumptions.

The root module gathers proximal calculus, actual particle algorithms, corrected original-center bounds and constructed mean-field comparisons into one importable Lean library. Its imported theorems retain their exact assumptions and scope.

Mathematical statement and proof

The proximal minimizer and its residual are the common definitions used throughout. The three algorithms then require different analyses: the smoothed Euler identity for MYIPLA, the full-envelope curvature for PIPULA, and the averaged pairwise energy estimate for PIPGLA. The mean-field comparison instead subtracts the empirical particle integral equation from independently copied reference equations. Each result retains its own target distribution, scale-dependent constants, finite-moment conditions, and additional regularity assumptions.

Importing ProximalLangevin makes the checked public development available. The mathematical chain is true proximal minimization, residual/Moreau calculus, exact algorithm identities, genuine finite-moment Gaussian laws, concentration and transport, then the original-center comparison. The splitting chain additionally constructs Brownian stationarity and orthogonality; the mean-field branch constructs continuous L2L^2L2 integral equations and actual iid comparisons. Run lake build ProximalLangevin +ProximalLangevin.Check. This umbrella has no local declarations; its proof content is in the linked imported modules.

This entry module assembles the named proof families through imports. The complete assumptions, intermediate estimates and final conclusions are stated in the dependency pages below. Building the entry module recompiles its imported dependencies and verifies that their interfaces agree.
