Differences and uniqueness of common-input integral solutions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Individual kinetic velocity paths contain continuous noise and need not be differentiable. Their difference under the same additive forcing is differentiable from the right, because the forcing cancels in the integral equations themselves. This section derives that assertion and proves uniqueness by Gronwall, including the initial time.

Cancellation is performed at the level of integral equations, where Brownian differentiability is unnecessary. The resulting deterministic difference equation supports both uniqueness and the transformed contraction theorem.

01Subtract the two integral equations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose Yt−Y0=−∫0tg(Ys)ds+BtY_t-Y_0=-\int_0^t g(Y_s)ds+B_tYt​−Y0​=−∫0t​g(Ys​)ds+Bt​ and Zt−Z0=−∫0tg(Zs)ds+BtZ_t-Z_0=-\int_0^t g(Z_s)ds+B_tZt​−Z0​=−∫0t​g(Zs​)ds+Bt​, with continuous paths and a Lipschitz drift. Subtraction gives

Yt−Zt=Y0−Z0−∫0t[g(Ys)−g(Zs)]ds.Y_t-Z_t=Y_0-Z_0-\int_0^t[g(Y_s)-g(Z_s)]ds.Yt​−Zt​=Y0​−Z0​−∫0t​[g(Ys​)−g(Zs​)]ds.

The two drift integrals exist on every compact interval because their integrands are continuous. First, the argument establishes interval integrability, distributes the difference through the integral, rewrites both supplied equations as expressions for the paths, and cancels the common forcing with the additive-group calculation. No derivative of the noise is taken.

The initial phase difference remains after subtraction, but the common forcing cancels identically at every time. The difference is an integral of the drift difference. This cancellation is valid for every continuous forcing path and does not use a distributional property of the noise.

02Differentiate after cancellation, including the initial endpoint §

Mathematical statement and proof

The integrand g(Ys)−g(Zs)g(Y_s)-g(Z_s)g(Ys​)−g(Zs​) is continuous. The fundamental theorem of calculus therefore differentiates the integral expression, producing the right derivative −(g(Yt)−g(Zt))-(g(Y_t)-g(Z_t))−(g(Yt​)−g(Zt​)). The identity is known only for t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T]; consequently the mathematically appropriate conclusion at zero is a derivative within [t,∞)[t,\infty)[t,∞). The proof builds an eventual neighborhood lying inside the valid time interval and transfers the derivative from the integral formula using eventual equality. This avoids imposing an arbitrary differentiable extension of the stochastic path to negative time.

The integrand is continuous because the force and the two solution paths are continuous. The fundamental theorem of calculus therefore gives an ordinary derivative in the interior and a right derivative at zero. These are exactly the derivatives used by the transformed-energy calculation.

For D=Y−ZD=Y-ZD=Y−Z, the derived derivative obeys ∥D′t∥≤L∥Dt∥\|D\prime_t\|\le L\|D_t\|∥D′t​∥≤L∥Dt​∥. Equal initial conditions give ∥D0∥=0\|D_0\|=0∥D0​∥=0. The proof passes the right-derivative theorem to its norm version of Gronwall, simplifies the zero initial bound, and uses definiteness of the norm to recover Yt=ZtY_t=Z_tYt​=Zt​. This result supplies uniqueness for any continuous additive forcing, including the forcing used in the kinetic Picard construction.
