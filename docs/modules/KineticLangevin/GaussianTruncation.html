Gaussian truncation for dependent displacement and velocity

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A Hessian difference can be small through its spatial Lipschitz bound and uniformly bounded through the Hessian norm bound. This section combines those two pointwise controls with the Gaussian velocity tail. The coupled position and velocity may be dependent; no independence premise is used.

The random multiplier has both a global operator bound and a local displacement-dependent bound. Splitting at a Gaussian velocity cutoff combines them without imposing a additional independence assumption.

01Separate the moderate velocity region from the exact Gaussian excess §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose d≤min⁡{B,Lx} vd\le\min\{B,Lx\}\,vd≤min{B,Lx}v, with all quantities nonnegative. For a cutoff a≥0a\ge0a≥0, the exact scalar estimate is d2≤L2ax2+B2(v2−a)+.d^2\le L^2ax^2+B^2(v^2-a)_+.d2≤L2ax2+B2(v2−a)+​. Put k=min⁡{B,Lx}k=\min\{B,Lx\}k=min{B,Lx}. Where v2≤av^2\le av2≤a, use k≤Lxk\le Lxk≤Lx and d2≤k2v2≤L2x2ad^2\le k^2v^2\le L^2x^2ad2≤k2v2≤L2x2a. Where v2>av^2>av2>a, write k2v2=k2a+k2(v2−a)k^2v^2=k^2a+k^2(v^2-a)k2v2=k2a+k2(v2−a), use k≤Lxk\le Lxk≤Lx in the first term and k≤Bk\le Bk≤B in the second. One proves the two squared bounds from the nonnegative minimum, splits on the cutoff comparison, rewrites the positive part in each region, and applies these two scalar comparisons. The subtraction of aaa in the tail term is retained exactly, which permits the sharp positive-part tail bound.

The two bounds on the multiplier are used in different regions. Its local Lipschitz bound controls moderate velocities, while its global uniform bound controls only the Gaussian excess. This separation avoids requiring independence between velocity and the position displacement.

02Identify Hilbert L² norms with their second moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a Hilbert-valued L² element XXX, ∥X∥L22=∫∥X(ω)∥2 dP\|X\|_{L^2}^2=\int\|X(\omega)\|^2\,dP∥X∥L22​=∫∥X(ω)∥2dP. The argument rewrites the squared norm as the real inner product of XXX with itself, unfolds the L² inner product as its Bochner integral, and rewrites the pointwise inner products as squared norms. This gives the precise identity needed to integrate the scalar cutoff estimate; it does not replace a possibly divergent moment by a totalized real expression.

The equality between a Hilbert-space norm square and its expected pointwise norm square follows from the integral inner product. Because the variable is already square integrable, every term is finite. The scalar pointwise estimate can therefore be integrated without ambiguity.

03Integrate the cutoff and use only the Gaussian velocity marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let D,X,VD,X,VD,X,V be Euclidean L² random variables on one probability space, and suppose VVV has the standard Gaussian law in dimension ppp. Assume ∥D∥≤min⁡{B,L∥X∥}∥V∥\|D\|\le\min\{B,L\|X\|\}\|V\|∥D∥≤min{B,L∥X∥}∥V∥ almost everywhere. The preceding scalar inequality integrates to ∥D∥L22≤L2a∥X∥L22+B2E(∥V∥2−a)+.\|D\|_{L^2}^2\le L^2a\|X\|_{L^2}^2+B^2\mathbb E(\|V\|^2-a)_+.∥D∥L22​≤L2a∥X∥L22​+B2E(∥V∥2−a)+​. One proves integrability of the velocity positive part directly from the L² moment, proves integrability of both terms on the right, and applies almost-everywhere monotonicity of the Bochner integral.

The Gaussian tail theorem, transferred through the velocity law, gives E(∥V∥2−a)+≤4e−(a−p)/4\mathbb E(\|V\|^2-a)_+\le4e^{-(a-p)/4}E(∥V∥2−a)+​≤4e−(a−p)/4 for a≥5pa\ge5pa≥5p. Hence ∥D∥L2≤La ∥X∥L2+2Be−(a−p)/8.\|D\|_{L^2}\le L\sqrt a\,\|X\|_{L^2}+2B e^{-(a-p)/8}.∥D∥L2​≤La​∥X∥L2​+2Be−(a−p)/8. The argument checks the exponential-square identity, compares the two nonnegative squares, and bounds the nonnegative cross term. The only probability fact used is the marginal law of VVV; arbitrary dependence between XXX, DDD, and VVV is retained throughout. This is the cutoff estimate required for a random Hessian-difference factor multiplying a stationary Gaussian velocity.

After integration, take the square root and use u+v≤u+v\sqrt{u+v}\le\sqrt u+\sqrt vu+v​≤u​+v​. This produces one term proportional to the displacement norm and one explicit Gaussian tail term. The cutoff is later chosen to absorb the former into contraction while keeping the latter exponentially small.
