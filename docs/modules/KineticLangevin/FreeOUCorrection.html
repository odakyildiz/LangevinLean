The KLMC2 Hessian correction as an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck time integral

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The fresh stochastic Hessian-difference terms are retained exactly. Their φ2\varphi _{2}φ2​ and φ3\varphi _{3}φ3​ responses combine with the initial velocity into the first and second time primitives of one free OU velocity. A bounded random Hessian acts through its proved continuous linear L2L^{2}L2 operator, so all identities remain valid on the coupled source.

The Hessian correction is identified as a time integral of the complete free-OU path. Deterministic and stochastic response identities are retained together, including the cancellation needed for the repaired comparison.

01Keep each deterministic and stochastic response on the same source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let LLL be the continuous linear pullback from the specified Brownian L2L^{2}L2 space to the coupled probability space, including the factor 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. Define Rk(t)=ψk(γ,t)V0+LNψk(a,t),Ck(t)=φk+1(γ,t)V0+LNφk+1(a,t).R_k(t)=\psi_k(\gamma,t)V_0+L N_{\psi_k}(a,t),\qquad C_k(t)=\varphi_{k+1}(\gamma,t)V_0+L N_{\varphi_{k+1}}(a,t).Rk​(t)=ψk​(γ,t)V0​+LNψk​​(a,t),Ck​(t)=φk+1​(γ,t)V0​+LNφk+1​​(a,t). Here every noise response uses exactly the same Brownian interval beginning at aaa. In particular, R0R_0R0​ is the free OU velocity and C1,C2C_1,C_2C1​,C2​ are the full φ2\varphi _{2}φ2​ and φ3\varphi _{3}φ3​ correction responses. The proof defines these L2L^{2}L2 elements and proves continuity of each RkR_kRk​ from the response coefficient and constructed convolution continuity.

The deterministic initial-velocity response and the stochastic Wiener responses are kept in one free-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path. This is necessary because the Hessian acts on their sum. Splitting them into unrelated noise variables would destroy the exact corrected-step identity.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02The exact primitive and damped-convolution identities §

Mathematical statement and proof

The deterministic response coefficients and stochastic responses satisfy the same time-integral identities. Consequently Rk+1(t)=∫0tRk(s) ds,Ck(t)=∫0te−γ(t−s)Rk(s) ds.R_{k+1}(t)=\int_0^tR_k(s)\,ds,\qquad C_k(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}R_k(s)\,ds.Rk+1​(t)=∫0t​Rk​(s)ds,Ck​(t)=∫0t​e−γ(t−s)Rk​(s)ds. One proves integrability of every continuous summand, splits each Bochner integral, moves the fixed initial velocity outside its scalar integral, and commutes the source pullback with the integral. It then substitutes the proved Wiener ψ\psiψ primitive or φ\varphiφ convolution identity. This establishes the complete correction identity, including the fresh stochastic terms absent from the displayed subtraction in the source paper.

Taking a time primitive or a damped convolution transforms each response kernel into its precise higher-order kernel. The identities are proved before applying the random Hessian. Thus every deterministic and stochastic coefficient is matched with the correct response.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

03Pass a random Hessian operator through the integrals §

Mathematical statement and proof

A uniformly bounded measurable random Hessian difference acts as a proved continuous linear map AAA on the L2L^{2}L2 space. Therefore it commutes with every time primitive and with the damped convolution. One proves this for an arbitrary continuous linear AAA; the random-Hessian construction is an application satisfying these hypotheses. The resulting full correction identities are AC1(t)=∫0te−γ(t−s)∫0sAR0(r) dr ds,AC_1(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\int_0^sAR_0(r)\,dr\,ds,AC1​(t)=∫0t​e−γ(t−s)∫0s​AR0​(r)drds, and AC2(t)=∫0te−γ(t−s)∫0s∫0rAR0(u) du dr ds.AC_2(t)=\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}\int_0^s\int_0^rAR_0(u)\,du\,dr\,ds.AC2​(t)=∫0t​e−γ(t−s)∫0s​∫0r​AR0​(u)dudrds. The proof checks continuity of every relevant L2L^{2}L2 integrand and applies the continuous linear integral-commutation theorem, then rewrites the proved primitive identities on the nonnegative integration intervals. Thus Gaussian truncation can be applied to AR0(r)AR_0(r)AR0​(r) at each time, with no independence between the random Hessian and the velocity required.

The frozen Hessian is constant with respect to local time, although random in the sample point. Its induced bounded linear operator on second-mean variables can therefore pass through the time integrals. No assumption of independence from the integrand is needed.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04The φ2+γφ3\varphi _{2}+\gamma \varphi _{3}φ2​+γφ3​ cancellation for the noises §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scalar response identity φ2+γφ3=ψ2\varphi_2+\gamma\varphi_3=\psi_2φ2​+γφ3​=ψ2​ also holds for the jointly sampled stochastic responses. One proves the corresponding equality of deterministic time-kernel L2L^{2}L2 elements by their almost-everywhere indicator representatives. It applies the same time translation, isonormal map, and spatial lift, all continuous and linear. This proves Nφ2(a,t)+γNφ3(a,t)=Nψ2(a,t).N_{\varphi_2}(a,t)+\gamma N_{\varphi_3}(a,t)=N_{\psi_2}(a,t).Nφ2​​(a,t)+γNφ3​​(a,t)=Nψ2​​(a,t). Combining this with the identical scalar coefficient relation gives the exact combined correction identity C1(t)+γC2(t)=R2(t).C_1(t)+\gamma C_2(t)=R_2(t).C1​(t)+γC2​(t)=R2​(t). This cancellation includes both fresh stochastic corrections. This permits the transformed phase estimate to use a second time primitive of the stationary free OU velocity and recover the correct quadratic time coefficient.

The coefficient relation combines terms from the velocity and position corrections before their norm is estimated. The same relation holds for the random responses because they are linear images of the deterministic kernels. This retains the cancellation omitted by a termwise deterministic-only comparison.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.6.12; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.5.
