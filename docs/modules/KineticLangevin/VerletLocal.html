Local error relative to exact Hamiltonian motion

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

To quantify sampling bias, compare the defined OOO–Verlet–OOO step with a reference that uses exact Hamiltonian motion between the same two thermostat updates. Exact Hamiltonian motion and thermostat preserve the Gibbs target separately. This section derives the local state error from the Hamiltonian integral equations. It works in any real Hilbert space, including L2L^{2}L2, where uniform velocity and force norm bounds are second-moment statements.

The defect is derived by integrating the true Hamiltonian equations. A common-noise comparison then inserts this deterministic estimate into the same weighted geometry used for OBABO contraction.

01Integrate the Hamiltonian equations instead of assuming a local error §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Hamiltonian equations are v(t)−v(0)=−∫0tg(x(s))dsv(t)-v(0)=-\int_0^t g(x(s))dsv(t)−v(0)=−∫0t​g(x(s))ds and x(t)−x(0)=∫0tv(s)dsx(t)-x(0)=\int_0^t v(s)dsx(t)−x(0)=∫0t​v(s)ds. If the force norm is at most GGG, the integral norm inequality gives ∥v(t)−v(0)∥≤Gt\|v(t)-v(0)\|\le Gt∥v(t)−v(0)∥≤Gt. Subtract tv(0)tv(0)tv(0) from the position equation; the result is the integral of the velocity increment. Integrating the derived velocity bound GsGsGs gives the Euler-position defect ∥x(t)−x(0)−tv(0)∥≤Gt2/2\|x(t)-x(0)-tv(0)\|\le Gt^2/2∥x(t)−x(0)−tv(0)∥≤Gt2/2. One proves the exact integral identities using integrability from continuity before applying either inequality.

The exact Hamiltonian solution satisfies integrated velocity and position equations. Substitute the position primitive into the force difference and use smoothness to obtain explicit integral remainders. This derives a local state error from the differential equation itself.

02Compare both Verlet components to the true endpoint §

Mathematical statement and proof

The defined drift-stage position is xV=x0+hv0−h2g(x0)/2x_V=x_0+hv_0-h^2g(x_0)/2xV​=x0​+hv0​−h2g(x0​)/2. Its difference from the Euler position has norm at most Gh2/2Gh^2/2Gh2/2; adding the exact position defect proves ∥xV−x(h)∥≤Gh2\|x_V-x(h)\|\le Gh^2∥xV​−x(h)∥≤Gh2. For velocity, subtract the frozen-force Euler value v0−hg(x0)v_0-hg(x_0)v0​−hg(x0​) from both trajectories. The exact difference is the integral of g(x(s))−g(x0)g(x(s))-g(x_0)g(x(s))−g(x0​), bounded by MCh2/2MC h^2/2MCh2/2 using Lipschitzness and the kinematics with velocity norm CCC. The Verlet difference is exactly −h(g(xV)−g(x0))/2-h(g(x_V)-g(x_0))/2−h(g(xV​)−g(x0​))/2, whose norm is at most Mh2C/2+MGh3/4Mh^2C/2+MGh^3/4Mh2C/2+MGh3/4. Combining the two gives ∥vV−v(h)∥≤MCh2+MGh3/4\|v_V-v(h)\|\le MC h^2+MGh^3/4∥vV​−v(h)∥≤MCh2+MGh3/4. Every residual is computed from the update and the exact path; none is supplied as an additional accuracy assumption.

The first half kick, drift, and final half kick are compared componentwise with those exact integrals. Terms sharing the same leading Taylor contribution cancel. The remaining bounds retain the initial velocity and force magnitudes needed for subsequent moment integration.

03Express the state defect in the contracting OBABO metric §

Mathematical statement and proof

The upper metric equivalence and completing-square Hilbert representation imply the convenient component estimate ∥(v,x)∥E≤2(∥x∥+∥v∥/M)\|(v,x)\|_E\le2(\|x\|+\|v\|/\sqrt M)∥(v,x)∥E​≤2(∥x∥+∥v∥/M​). The proof squares both nonnegative sides, expands the scalar square, and uses M2=M\sqrt M^2=MM​2=M. Inserting the two component defects yields

∥Verleth(v0,x0)−(v(h),x(h))∥E≤2h2(G+MC+MGh/4).\|\mathrm{Verlet}_h(v_0,x_0)-(v(h),x(h))\|_E\le2h^2\bigl(G+\sqrt M C+\sqrt M G h/4\bigr).∥Verleth​(v0​,x0​)−(v(h),x(h))∥E​≤2h2(G+M​C+M​Gh/4).

This estimate uses only a Lipschitz force and the explicit moment bounds; no Hessian regularity is required.

The physical component errors are inserted into the verified weighted transformation. The triangle inequality yields a phase defect in the same norm used for contraction. Keeping this common metric avoids an extra norm-conversion loss at each later iteration.

04Couple the defined OBABO step with the exact target-preserving split §

Mathematical statement and proof

Start the Hamiltonian path at the first Ornstein–Uhlenbeck half-step. Apply the same final thermostat vector to its endpoint and to the Verlet endpoint. The final additive noise cancels exactly, while the velocity defect is multiplied by δ\deltaδ. Since ∣δ∣≤1|\delta|\le1∣δ∣≤1, its norm is no larger than the Verlet velocity defect, and the position defect is unchanged. Applying the component metric bound therefore gives the same explicit local estimate for the OOO–Verlet–OOO and OOO–exact-Hamiltonian–OOO outputs. In L2L^{2}L2, an invariant Gibbs Hamiltonian reference supplies C=dC=\sqrt dC=d​ and G≤MdG\le\sqrt{Md}G≤Md​ from the Gaussian velocity marginal and Gibbs score moment theorem. The local bound itself keeps the exact path equations and norm bounds explicit so the probabilistic reference construction must verify them.

The defined OBABO step and its exact comparator use identical first and second thermostat noises. Their only difference is replacing the central exact Hamiltonian evolution by Verlet. Thus the deterministic Hamiltonian local defect directly controls the coupled stochastic step discrepancy.
