Transformed L2L^{2}L2 bounds for the random Hessian correction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The φ2\varphi _{2}φ2​ and φ3\varphi _{3}φ3​ stochastic Hessian terms are estimated together. Their exact ψ2\psi _{2}ψ2​ cancellation gives the sharp quadratic time coefficient. Gaussian truncation then supplies the source-paper random-Hessian contribution without discarding fresh-noise terms or assuming independence from the Hessian.

The random-Hessian correction acts on a free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path with a proved Gaussian marginal. Exact response cancellation and a dependence-tolerant cutoff estimate provide the sharp comparison terms.

Integrating the free velocity once gives a response proportional to time; integrating again gives the next polynomial order with its exact denominator. The Gaussian marginal provides a uniform root-mean-square radius. These estimates apply to the complete free-OU path.

02Use the full stochastic cancellation in the phase metric §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let AAA be the continuous linear random-Hessian operator and suppose ∥AR0(r)∥L2≤C\|AR_0(r)\|_{L^2}\le C∥AR0​(r)∥L2​≤C. The complete correction identities give AC1AC_1AC1​ as a damped second primitive of AR0AR_0AR0​, so ∥AC1(t)∥≤Ct2/2\|AC_1(t)\|\le Ct^2/2∥AC1​(t)∥≤Ct2/2. The exact identity C1+γC2=R2C_1+\gamma C_2=R_2C1​+γC2​=R2​, including both stochastic responses, gives ∥AC1+γAC2∥=∥AR2∥≤Ct2/2\|AC_1+\gamma AC_2\|=\|AR_2\|\le Ct^2/2∥AC1​+γAC2​∥=∥AR2​∥≤Ct2/2. The transformed phase norm combines exactly these two coordinates, hence ∥Tγ,0(AC1,AC2)∥≤22t2C.\|T_{\gamma,0}(AC_1,AC_2)\|\le\frac{\sqrt2}{2}t^2C.∥Tγ,0​(AC1​,AC2​)∥≤22​​t2C. The argument rewrites the two primitive identities, applies the two sharp integral bounds, commutes AAA with the exact correction balance, and sums the two squared component bounds. It finally checks the square of the displayed nonnegative bound. This controls the entire defined correction.

The random Hessian difference multiplies both deterministic and stochastic correction components. Combine them first through the exact response identity, then estimate the phase norm. This captures the cancellation necessary for the sharp source comparison.

03Insert the Gaussian cutoff at each free-OU time §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose every free-OU velocity marginal is standard Gaussian and assume

∥AR0(r)(ω)∥≤min⁡{B,M2∥X(ω)∥}∥R0(r)(ω)∥.\|AR_0(r)(\omega)\|\le\min\{B,M_2\|X(\omega)\|\}\|R_0(r)(\omega)\|.∥AR0​(r)(ω)∥≤min{B,M2​∥X(ω)∥}∥R0​(r)(ω)∥.

Then the truncation theorem gives the same bound at every time: ∥AR0(r)∥L2≤M2α ∥X∥L2+2Be−(α−p)/8,α≥5p.\|AR_0(r)\|_{L^2}\le M_2\sqrt\alpha\,\|X\|_{L^2}+2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8},\qquad\alpha\ge5p.∥AR0​(r)∥L2​≤M2​α​∥X∥L2​+2Be−(α−p)/8,α≥5p. Substituting this common CCC in the sharp transformed estimate retains both terms with their exact constants. The argument constructs the nonnegative common bound and applies the Gaussian truncation theorem at each time. No independence between the Hessian factor, the position discrepancy, and the velocity is required.

At every local time, the free-OU velocity is standard Gaussian by the proved initial/future independence. The truncation estimate can therefore be applied without assuming independence from the numerical displacement. Integration over local time retains its explicit cutoff and tail coefficients.

04The cutoff hypotheses for the random operator §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a measurable random operator field H(ω)H(\omega)H(ω), suppose its operator norm satisfies both ∥H(ω)∥≤B\|H(\omega)\|\le B∥H(ω)∥≤B and ∥H(ω)∥≤M2∥X(ω)∥\|H(\omega)\|\le M_2\|X(\omega)\|∥H(ω)∥≤M2​∥X(ω)∥ almost everywhere. The random-operator construction gives an L2L^{2}L2 continuous linear map with representative H(ω)V(ω)H(\omega)V(\omega)H(ω)V(ω). The argument rewrites that representative, applies the operator norm inequality, combines the two operator bounds by their minimum, and invokes Gaussian truncation for a velocity with the standard Gaussian law. Thus the required cutoff estimate follows from the uniform and Lipschitz operator bounds of a Hessian difference.

The operator difference has two simultaneous bounds: a uniform curvature-width bound and a Hessian-Lipschitz bound proportional to displacement. Their minimum gives the hypothesis of the Gaussian cutoff lemma. Both bounds concern the random Hessians in the corrected-step difference.
