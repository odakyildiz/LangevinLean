Fourth moments of actual time integrals

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Kinetic positions are time integrals of velocities in L². A fourth-moment estimate must apply to this existing L² integral, rather than to a separately postulated L4 process. The proof uses closed convex L4 balls inside L² and the geometric fact that averages remain in a closed convex set.

Statement and proof

01View an L4 ball inside L² §

Mathematical statement and proof

The set lpFourthBall(R) contains actual L² equivalence classes whose extended fourth norm is at most R. If a sequence in this set converges in L², it converges in measure. Lower semicontinuity of the extended L4 norm under convergence in measure keeps its limit in the same ball. In the metric L² space, this sequential argument proves closedness.

02Verify convexity without extra moment assumptions §

Mathematical statement and proof

For nonnegative a,b with a+b=1, the actual representative of aX+bY equals aX(ω)+bY(ω) almost everywhere. The L4 triangle inequality and homogeneity give ‖aX+bY‖₄≤a‖X‖₄+b‖Y‖₄≤R. Extended norms allow this argument even before finite fourth moments are separately established.

03Average a continuous L² path §

Mathematical statement and proof

If every velocity v(t) on [0,T] has fourth norm at most R, its time average lies in the closed convex L4 ball. The average is T⁻¹ times the actual Bochner integral in L². Homogeneity then gives ‖∫₀ᵀv(t)dt‖₄≤TR. The zero-duration case is handled separately; for positive duration Lean checks the interval measure is finite and nonzero before applying the convex averaging theorem. This directly supplies displacement fourth moments from Gaussian velocity marginals.
