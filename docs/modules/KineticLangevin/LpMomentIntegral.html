Fourth moments of Bochner time integrals in L2L^2L2

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

01The fourth-moment ball is closed in L2L^2L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For every R∈[0,∞]R\in[0,\infty]R∈[0,∞], the set B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) is closed in L2(P;E)L^2(\mathbb P;E)L2(P;E).

Proof. L2(P;E)L^2(\mathbb P;E)L2(P;E) is a metric space, so it suffices to show that B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) is sequentially closed. Let Xn∈B4(R)X_n\in\mathcal B_4(R)Xn​∈B4​(R) converge to XXX in L2L^2L2.

Convergence in measure. For ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, Chebyshev's inequality gives P(∥Xn−X∥>ε)≤ε−2∥Xn−X∥L22→0\mathbb P(\|X_n-X\|>\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon^{-2}\|X_n-X\|_{L^2}^2\to0P(∥Xn​−X∥>ε)≤ε−2∥Xn​−X∥L22​→0, so Xn→XX_n\to XXn​→X in measure.

Lower semicontinuity. A sequence converging in measure has a subsequence XnkX_{n_k}Xnk​​ converging to XXX almost everywhere. Then ∥Xnk(ω)∥4→∥X(ω)∥4\|X_{n_k}(\omega)\|^4\to\|X(\omega)\|^4∥Xnk​​(ω)∥4→∥X(ω)∥4 almost everywhere, and Fatou's lemma gives

∫∥X∥4 dP≤lim inf⁡k→∞∫∥Xnk∥4 dP≤R4.\int\|X\|^4\,d\mathbb P\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int\|X_{n_k}\|^4\,d\mathbb P\le R^4.∫∥X∥4dP≤k→∞liminf​∫∥Xnk​​∥4dP≤R4.

Hence ∥X∥L4≤R\|X\|_{L^4}\le R∥X∥L4​≤R and X∈B4(R)X\in\mathcal B_4(R)X∈B4​(R). □\square□

The set is defined inside L2L^2L2: its elements are square-integrable classes whose fourth moment is moreover at most R4R^4R4. The fourth-moment condition is never used to obtain square integrability.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

lpFourthBall P R is the set {X | eLpNorm X 4 P ≤ R} of elements of Lp E 2 P. Mathlib's eLpNorm X 4 P is the extended L4L^4L4 norm ∥X∥L4∈[0,∞]\|X\|_{L^4}\in[0,\infty]∥X∥L4​∈[0,∞], and R : ℝ≥0∞ may be infinite.

isClosed_lpFourthBall reduces closedness to sequential closedness (isSeqClosed_iff_isClosed). For a sequence Xn in the set converging to X, tendstoInMeasure_of_tendsto_Lp gives convergence in measure, and eLpNorm_le_of_tendstoInMeasure is the lower-semicontinuity step: a uniform bound on ∥Xn∥L4\|X_n\|_{L^4}∥Xn​∥L4​ passes to the limit in measure (its proof extracts an almost everywhere convergent subsequence and applies Fatou's lemma). The measurability side condition is Lp.aestronglyMeasurable.

02Convexity of the fourth-moment ball §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For every R∈[0,∞]R\in[0,\infty]R∈[0,∞], B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) is convex.

Proof. Let X,Y∈B4(R)X,Y\in\mathcal B_4(R)X,Y∈B4​(R) and a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 with a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1. The class aX+bY∈L2aX+bY\in L^2aX+bY∈L2 is represented almost everywhere by ω↦aX(ω)+bY(ω)\omega\mapsto aX(\omega)+bY(\omega)ω↦aX(ω)+bY(ω), and ∥⋅∥L4\|\cdot\|_{L^4}∥⋅∥L4​ depends only on the class. By Minkowski's inequality in L4L^4L4 (valid for extended values) and absolute homogeneity,

∥aX+bY∥L4≤∥aX∥L4+∥bY∥L4=a∥X∥L4+b∥Y∥L4≤aR+bR=(a+b)R=R.□\|aX+bY\|_{L^4}\le\|aX\|_{L^4}+\|bY\|_{L^4}=a\|X\|_{L^4}+b\|Y\|_{L^4}\le aR+bR=(a+b)R=R.\qquad\square∥aX+bY∥L4​≤∥aX∥L4​+∥bY∥L4​=a∥X∥L4​+b∥Y∥L4​≤aR+bR=(a+b)R=R.□

The computation is carried out in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞], so it needs no prior finiteness of the fourth moments; if R<∞R<\inftyR<∞, finiteness follows from the bound.

03The fourth moment of a time integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let v:R→L2(P;E)v:\mathbb R\to L^2(\mathbb P;E)v:R→L2(P;E) be continuous, T≥0T\ge0T≥0 and R∈[0,∞]R\in[0,\infty]R∈[0,∞], and suppose ∥v(t)∥L4≤R\|v(t)\|_{L^4}\le R∥v(t)∥L4​≤R for every t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T]. Then

∥∫0Tv(t) dt∥L4≤T R,\Bigl\|\int_0^Tv(t)\,dt\Bigr\|_{L^4}\le T\,R,​∫0T​v(t)dt​L4​≤TR,

where the integral is the Bochner integral in L2(P;E)L^2(\mathbb P;E)L2(P;E) and the product is taken in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞].

Proof. If T=0T=0T=0 the integral is the zero class, whose L4L^4L4 norm is 000.

Let T>0T>0T>0. The interval (0,T](0,T](0,T] has Lebesgue measure TTT, which is positive and finite, and vvv is integrable on it because it is continuous. Every v(t)v(t)v(t), t∈(0,T]t\in(0,T]t∈(0,T], lies in B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R), which is closed and convex by the two previous steps. The average of an integrable function with values in a closed convex subset CCC of a Banach space lies in CCC. (If the average vˉ\bar vvˉ were outside CCC, the Hahn–Banach separation theorem would give a continuous linear functional ℓ\ellℓ and c∈Rc\in\mathbb Rc∈R with ℓ(vˉ)>c≥ℓ(y)\ell(\bar v)>c\ge\ell(y)ℓ(vˉ)>c≥ℓ(y) for all y∈Cy\in Cy∈C; since ℓ\ellℓ commutes with the Bochner integral, ℓ(vˉ)\ell(\bar v)ℓ(vˉ) is the average of ℓ(v(t))≤c\ell(v(t))\le cℓ(v(t))≤c, a contradiction.) Hence

vˉ=1T∫0Tv(t) dt∈B4(R),that is,1T∥∫0Tv(t) dt∥L4≤R,\bar v=\frac1T\int_0^Tv(t)\,dt\in\mathcal B_4(R),\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad\frac1T\Bigl\|\int_0^Tv(t)\,dt\Bigr\|_{L^4}\le R,vˉ=T1​∫0T​v(t)dt∈B4​(R),that is,T1​​∫0T​v(t)dt​L4​≤R,

using homogeneity ∥cX∥L4=∣c∣ ∥X∥L4\|cX\|_{L^4}=|c|\,\|X\|_{L^4}∥cX∥L4​=∣c∣∥X∥L4​. Multiplying by TTT gives the claim. □\square□

The argument uses only the L2L^2L2-valued integral and the closed convex structure of B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) inside L2L^2L2. It applies, for instance, to v(r)=Vrv(r)=V_rv(r)=Vr​, whose fourth moments are known from its Gaussian law, without any assumption on the joint law of the velocities at different times.
