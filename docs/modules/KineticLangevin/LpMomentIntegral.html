Fourth moments of Bochner time integrals in L2L^{2}L2

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Kinetic position is the Bochner time integral of velocity in L2L^2L2. To estimate its fourth moment in this same setting, consider the L4L^4L4 ball as a closed convex subset of L2L^2L2. Closedness follows from convergence in measure and lower semicontinuity of the fourth norm; convexity follows from Minkowski's inequality.

A continuous L2L^2L2 path whose fourth norm is uniformly at most RRR has its time average in that ball. Multiplication by the interval length gives an L4L^4L4 bound for its existing L2L^2L2 time integral, without requiring continuity of the path in L4L^4L4.

01An L4L^{4}L4 ball as a closed subset of L2L^{2}L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

On the stated probability space, define B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) to consist of L2L^2L2 equivalence classes whose extended fourth norm is at most RRR. Hölder's inequality ensures that a finite fourth norm also gives a finite second norm, so this is a subset of the second-mean space.

If Xn∈B4(R)X_n\in\mathcal B_4(R)Xn​∈B4​(R) converges to XXX in L2L^2L2, it converges in measure. Lower semicontinuity of the extended fourth norm under this convergence gives ∥X∥4≤R\|X\|_4\le R∥X∥4​≤R, so X∈B4(R)X\in\mathcal B_4(R)X∈B4​(R). The sequential criterion in the metric L2L^2L2 space proves that the ball is closed. This allows fourth-moment constraints to be retained under time integration performed in L2L^2L2.

02Verify convexity without extra moment assumptions §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let X,Y∈B4(R)X,Y\in\mathcal B_4(R)X,Y∈B4​(R), and let a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 satisfy a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1. The representative of the linear combination agrees almost surely with the pointwise combination. Homogeneity and Minkowski's inequality for the fourth norm give

∥aX+bY∥4≤a∥X∥4+b∥Y∥4≤(a+b)R=R.\|aX+bY\|_4\le a\|X\|_4+b\|Y\|_4\le(a+b)R=R.∥aX+bY∥4​≤a∥X∥4​+b∥Y∥4​≤(a+b)R=R.

Thus B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R) is convex. This argument uses extended nonnegative norms before any separate finiteness assertion; the bound by the finite RRR supplies finiteness where needed. No independence of XXX and YYY is required.

03Average a continuous L2L^{2}L2 path §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let v:[0,T]→L2v:[0,T]\to L^2v:[0,T]→L2 be continuous and satisfy ∥v(t)∥4≤R\|v(t)\|_4\le R∥v(t)∥4​≤R throughout the interval. For T>0T>0T>0, its Bochner average T−1∫0Tv(t)dtT^{-1}\int_0^Tv(t)dtT−1∫0T​v(t)dt belongs to the closed convex set B4(R)\mathcal B_4(R)B4​(R), by the closed-convex averaging theorem. The interval has finite positive measure, as required for this normalization.

Homogeneity then gives

∥∫0Tv(t)dt∥4≤TR.\left\|\int_0^Tv(t)dt\right\|_4\le TR.​∫0T​v(t)dt​4​≤TR.

For T=0T=0T=0, the integral and the right side are zero. This bound concerns the time integral already defined in L2L^2L2. It provides a fourth-moment estimate for displacement from Gaussian velocity marginals without an independently specified L4L^4L4-valued process.
