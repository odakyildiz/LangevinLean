Position contraction for Brownian kinetic diffusions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The continuous mixing theorem is instantiated with the existing vector Brownian source. Its initial velocity distribution is arbitrary, subject only to a finite second moment, and is independent of the initial positions and Brownian noise. The output measures are terminal pushforwards of the selected kinetic integral solution, on any finite coordinate set.

The independent-source law theorem is specialized to specified Brownian forcing. It gives explicit position mixing for the constructed kinetic diffusion with any admissible square-integrable common velocity law.

Statement and proof

01Construct arbitrary initial velocity and Brownian forcing §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source is the independent product of the prescribed velocity distribution and the specified vector Brownian measure. Its first projection gives the L² initial velocity. The second projection pulls back the specified Brownian process, multiplied by the fluctuation-dissipation factor 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. Continuity and the zero initial value follow from the corresponding proved Brownian facts and linear source pullback.

The Brownian factor supplies the physical forcing independently of the prescribed velocity law. Gaussianity of that velocity is unnecessary for the common-input difference estimate. Its finite second moment is enough to construct the phase path in the relevant Hilbert space.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02The position laws and mixing for every admissible shift §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each initial position distribution, the independent-source construction solves the kinetic equation with the original gradient and Brownian input. Its terminal position pushforward defines the law. Applying the general product-source coupling theorem proves the complete arbitrary-shift exponential coefficient. The common velocity law needs no Gaussian assumption. The initial transport term is the Wasserstein distance between the initial position distributions, obtained by infimizing over couplings rather than assuming an optimizer or a prescribed coupling cost.

The general source-law theorem now specializes to the specified Brownian input. The complete shifted prefactor remains visible, including any deterioration near the singular coordinate choice. Choosing the simple zero shift yields the exponential kinetic position-mixing bound.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4.
