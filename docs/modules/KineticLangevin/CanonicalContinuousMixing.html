Position contraction for Brownian kinetic diffusions

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

01Initial velocity and Brownian forcing on one source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Setting. Let ppp be the dimension and Rp\mathbb R^pRp carry its Euclidean norm. Let (ΩW,PW)(\Omega_W,\mathbb P_W)(ΩW​,PW​) be the probability space of the ppp-dimensional Brownian motion constructed earlier: it is the product of ppp copies of the scalar construction, in which a Brownian motion is the isonormal Gaussian process t↦Wt=I(1[0,t])t\mapsto W_t=I(\mathbf 1_{[0,t]})t↦Wt​=I(1[0,t]​) on L2([0,∞))L^2([0,\infty))L2([0,∞)), built from an orthonormal basis and independent standard normal variables. Regarded as a map t↦Wt∈L2(PW;Rp)t\mapsto W_t\in L^2(\mathbb P_W;\mathbb R^p)t↦Wt​∈L2(PW​;Rp), it satisfies W0=0W_0=0W0​=0 and E∥Wt−Ws∥2=p ∣t−s∣\mathbb E\|W_t-W_s\|^2=p\,|t-s|E∥Wt​−Ws​∥2=p∣t−s∣. For t<0t<0t<0 we put Wt=W0=0W_t=W_0=0Wt​=W0​=0.

Let ρ\rhoρ be a probability measure on Rp\mathbb R^pRp with ∫∥v∥2ρ(dv)<∞\int\|v\|^2\rho(dv)<\infty∫∥v∥2ρ(dv)<∞. The source is S=Rp×ΩWS=\mathbb R^p\times\Omega_WS=Rp×ΩW​ with Qρ=ρ⊗PWQ_\rho=\rho\otimes\mathbb P_WQρ​=ρ⊗PW​. On it define the initial velocity and the forcing

Claim. QρQ_\rhoQρ​ is a probability measure; V∈L2(Qρ;Rp)V\in L^2(Q_\rho;\mathbb R^p)V∈L2(Qρ​;Rp); the map t↦Bt∈L2(Qρ;Rp)t\mapsto B_t\in L^2(Q_\rho;\mathbb R^p)t↦Bt​∈L2(Qρ​;Rp) is continuous; and B0=0B_0=0B0​=0.

Proof. A product of probability measures is a probability measure. The projection (v,w)↦v(v,w)\mapsto v(v,w)↦v maps QρQ_\rhoQρ​ to ρ\rhoρ, so E∥V∥2=∫∥v∥2ρ(dv)<∞\mathbb E\|V\|^2=\int\|v\|^2\rho(dv)<\inftyE∥V∥2=∫∥v∥2ρ(dv)<∞. The projection (v,w)↦w(v,w)\mapsto w(v,w)↦w maps QρQ_\rhoQρ​ to PW\mathbb P_WPW​; composition with it is a linear isometry from L2(PW)L^2(\mathbb P_W)L2(PW​) to L2(Qρ)L^2(Q_\rho)L2(Qρ​). Hence ∥Bt−Bs∥L2=2γ ∥Wt+−Ws+∥L2=2γ p ∣t+−s+∣\|B_t-B_s\|_{L^2}=\sqrt{2\gamma}\,\|W_{t^+}-W_{s^+}\|_{L^2}=\sqrt{2\gamma\,p\,|t^+-s^+|}∥Bt​−Bs​∥L2​=2γ​∥Wt+​−Ws+​∥L2​=2γp∣t+−s+∣​, where t+=max⁡(t,0)t^+=\max(t,0)t+=max(t,0), and this tends to 000 as s→ts\to ts→t because t↦t+t\mapsto t^+t↦t+ is continuous. Finally B0=2γ W0=0B_0=\sqrt{2\gamma}\,W_0=0B0​=2γ​W0​=0, since the isometry is linear. □\square□

Definition.
