Fix any finite-cost coupling of the initial position law and the target position law. The probability space is its explicit product with an independent standard Gaussian velocity and the specified vector Brownian source. This section constructs the numerical chain, the exact reference segments and the auxiliary exact segments on that same space, and proves every analytic segment requirement used by the quantitative KLMC theorem.

Each initial transport plan is realized together with a shared Gaussian velocity and specified Brownian source. Exact response and solution identities connect this construction to the fixed numerical output law.

01The probability space and Brownian projection §

Mathematical statement and proof

The phase variable is velocity first. The sample space contains the two coupled initial positions, one shared Gaussian velocity and one vector Brownian source. The measure is the initial-coupling product extension; probability follows from its first marginal and from the Gaussian/Brownian probability factors. The Brownian-source projection preserves its measure. Pullback along that projection gives a continuous linear map on L2L^{2}L2, and its representative theorem identifies it almost everywhere with evaluation on the product sample.

An initial transport plan is extended by an independent Gaussian velocity and Brownian source. The source projection preserves its probability law. This one extension realizes the numerical and reference processes together without altering either prescribed input marginal.

02The common Brownian input and filtered innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scaled source lift includes the physical amplitude 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. At iteration nnn, the input is the vector Brownian increment on (nh,nh+t](nh,nh+t](nh,nh+t], and the filtered input is the exponential response to that same increment. The unscaled innovation pair consists of the exact velocity and position Wiener responses at the step endpoint. Both input paths are continuous in L2L^{2}L2, and the additive input starts at zero. These follow from the source lift's continuity and linearity and the proved vector-response properties.

The common additive input is the Brownian increment multiplied by the physical thermal amplitude. The filtered response uses the exponential kernel on that same increment. Their second-mean continuity permits the deterministic existence and uniqueness theorem for integral solutions to be applied directly.

Its endpoint is the scaled velocity innovation, and its time primitive is the scaled positional innovation. The argument transports the vector Brownian equations through the continuous linear source lift and commutes that lift with Bochner integration. 04Connect Brownian L2L^{2}L2 innovations with the generic output-law construction §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^{2}L2 innovation pair has the same representatives as the pointwise specified Brownian innovation. The proof pulls back the previously established vector-response representative theorem to the coupling product and combines the two component identities. Comparing with the representatives in the independent product-law construction gives equality of the L2L^{2}L2 pairs. Thus the numerical chain analyzed here has the coupling-independent output law already proved for the defined KLMC chain.

The L2L^{2}L2 responses agree almost everywhere with the source’s pointwise innovations. Their joint law is therefore the specified correlated Gaussian law already used to define the numerical output. Thus the variables in the coupling argument have the specified numerical output law.

05Build the numerical and stationary-reference initial states §

Mathematical statement and proof

The two initial phases share exactly one Gaussian velocity variable. The numerical phase uses the first coupled position; the reference phase uses the second. The reference representative is proved to be the explicit pair (V,Xref)(V,X_\mathrm{ref})(V,Xref​). Product independence and the two marginal laws prove that its law is N(0,Id)⊗π\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\piN(0,Id​)⊗π. The finite target second moment and finite coupling cost supply the required L2L^{2}L2 initial positions.

The two initial phases share velocity and use the two positions of the supplied plan. The reference position has the Gibbs marginal, and the velocity is an independent standard Gaussian. Their phase law is therefore the specified Gaussian-times-Gibbs probability.

06Construct all three chains and segment families §

Mathematical statement and proof

The numerical chain applies the lifted original gradient and the constructed correlated innovations. The reference chain stitches exact selected kinetic segments, each driven by the specified Brownian increment for that iteration. The corresponding reference segments start from these reference states. The auxiliary exact segment starts instead from the numerical state and receives the identical additive input. The drift is the pointwise gradient lifted to L2L^{2}L2; no substitute potential on the L2L^{2}L2 space is assumed.

The numerical recursion, exact reference recursion, and auxiliary exact segments all use the same interval source. Their distinct roles are fixed by their initial states: numerical update, stationary exact continuation, and exact continuation from the numerical phase.

07Every analytic premise of the KLMC coupling theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

The construction gives continuity of auxiliary paths, reference paths and filtered inputs; exact initial states; reference endpoint matching; the defined exponential update; both common-input integral equations; the exponential mild identity; the kinematic primitive identity; and equal initial velocities. Each property follows from the definitions and preceding integral identities. The mild and kinematic identities are derived internally. The only remaining probabilistic input for a final stationary-error theorem is the stationary law of the global reference diffusion, proved separately and connected to these stitched segments by uniqueness.

Every local analytic identity follows from these definitions and the selected-flow specification. The only distributional step still needed for the final bound is stationarity of the global reference. Uniqueness transfers that stationarity to all the constructed local segments.
