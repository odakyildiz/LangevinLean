The accepted-Verlet and rejected-reversal invariant measure

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The reversed-Verlet involution supplied exact Metropolis invariance. Applying one final velocity reversal gives ordinary Verlet on acceptance and a velocity flip on rejection. This section computes the resulting two weighted pushforward measures explicitly and proves that their sum equals the Hamiltonian Gibbs measure. This identity implies bounded-test estimates and a numerical limit.

The corrected transition is described by its accepted and rejected measures. Exact invariance supplies the stationary comparison used to prove weak error bounds for uncorrected numerical Hamiltonian evolution.

01Compute the two Metropolis graph marginals §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a deterministic proposal JJJ, the old/new graph is G(z)=(z,Jz)G(z)=(z,Jz)G(z)=(z,Jz). Weighting its pushforward by a measurable acceptance function a(z,Jz)a(z,Jz)a(z,Jz) commutes with mapping the correspondingly weighted initial measure through GGG. Projecting onto the second coordinate yields J#(aπ)J_\#(a\pi)J#​(aπ). Projecting rejected mass onto the first coordinate yields (1−a)π(1-a)\pi(1−a)π. The argument applies the previously proved pushforward-with-density formula twice and composes each graph with its coordinate projection. This proves an equality of measures without an integrability premise.

The proposal graph records both the old and proposed states. Its accepted mass has symmetric first and second marginals, while the rejected part remains at the old state after the prescribed reversal. Computing both marginals explicitly proves invariance without omitting rejected probability mass.

02The Hamiltonian Gibbs measure and velocity-reversal symmetry §

Mathematical statement and proof

The measure is e−H(v,x)(μ⊗μ)(d(v,x))e^{-H(v,x)}(\mu\otimes\mu)(d(v,x))e−H(v,x)(μ⊗μ)(d(v,x)), where μ\muμ is a translation-invariant symmetric Haar factor and H(v,x)=f(x)+∥v∥2/2H(v,x)=f(x)+\|v\|^2/2H(v,x)=f(x)+∥v∥2/2. It is initially unnormalized; finiteness and normalization are independent questions. Velocity reversal preserves product Haar measure and preserves HHH exactly. The weighted pushforward identity therefore proves that it preserves this Gibbs measure. The argument verifies measurability of the exponential density and rewrites the two exact preservation identities.

The Hamiltonian density factors into a velocity Gaussian weight and a position Gibbs weight. Reversing velocity leaves that density and volume unchanged. This symmetry permits the rejection flip to be included without changing the target measure.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.35.7.

03Use the Verlet energy change as acceptance §

Mathematical statement and proof

The acceptance probability is ah(z)=min⁡{1,exp⁡[−(H(Vhz)−H(z))]}a_h(z)=\min\{1,\exp[-(H(V_hz)-H(z))]\}ah​(z)=min{1,exp[−(H(Vh​z)−H(z))]}. Its measurability follows by expanding the explicit kick-drift-kick stages and composing the measurable potential and gradient. Positivity of the exponential gives ah≥0a_h\ge0ah​≥0, and the minimum gives ah≤1a_h\le1ah​≤1. These scalar bounds later justify finite weighted measures and nonnegative rejection estimates.

The acceptance ratio must use the difference of the true Hamiltonian evaluated at the proposed and current states. Replacing it by a truncated local approximation would define a different transition. The exact algebraic defect from the Verlet map therefore enters the formula unchanged.

04Derive exact corrected Verlet invariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

The proved Metropolis result uses the involution Jh=R∘VhJ_h=R\circ V_hJh​=R∘Vh​. Since H∘R=HH\circ R=HH∘R=H, its ratio is exactly the acceptance aha_hah​ just defined. Compute its accepted and rejected graph marginals, then push the resulting equality through RRR. The accepted state becomes R(Jhz)=VhzR(J_hz)=V_hzR(Jh​z)=Vh​z, whereas the rejected state becomes RzRzRz. Gibbs velocity symmetry fixes the right-hand side, giving (Vh)#(ahπ)+R#((1−ah)π)=π.(V_h)_\#(a_h\pi)+R_\#((1-a_h)\pi)=\pi.(Vh​)#​(ah​π)+R#​((1−ah​)π)=π. Composition of the graph and coordinate maps, together with R2=idR^2=\mathrm{id}R2=id, gives the stated equality. This theorem does not identify rejection with a small spatial jump; the rejected velocity flip is retained explicitly.

Integrating any bounded measurable test against the corrected transition gives its original target integral. This is an equality of probability measures after normalization. It will be used as a comparator when bounding weak error of the uncorrected Hamiltonian step.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.35.3.
