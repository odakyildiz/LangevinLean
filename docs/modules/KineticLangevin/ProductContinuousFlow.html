Actual continuous kinetic laws and the true Wasserstein infimum

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The initial position is independent of a common source carrying the initial velocity and continuous forcing. This construction makes the output law independent of any chosen coupling. We then couple two initial position laws, pull their genuine kinetic solutions onto the same source, and apply the proved arbitrary-shift kinetic energy contraction. Taking the infimum over all initial couplings gives the actual Wasserstein estimate.

Statement and proof

01Construct the actual independent-source diffusion §

Mathematical statement and proof

The probability space is the product of the initial position law and the common source. The first L² coordinate is the pulled-back velocity; the second is the original position coordinate. Pulling back the continuous additive forcing gives the genuine kinetic input. The selected Picard solution is then pushed forward at the terminal time to define the position law. The solution specification proves continuity, the exact initial condition and both integral equations. Measurability makes the output a probability measure, and its L² construction proves a finite second moment.

02Put any initial coupling on the same source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Adjoin the same independent source to an arbitrary coupling of the two initial position distributions. The two projections recover the original independent-source spaces exactly, so they preserve measure. Composition of L² pullbacks shows that both projected solutions receive exactly the same velocity and forcing. The representative formula identifies their initial positions with the two original coupling coordinates.

03Prove the actual bound before taking an infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source-pullback theorem transforms each genuine solution equation to the coupling extension. Their common velocity and noise cancel, so the arbitrary-shift L² contraction theorem applies. The initial position L² norm equals the actual root-mean-square transport cost of the supplied coupling: the independent extra source contributes a factor one. At the endpoint, measure-preserving projection recovers each independently defined output law. The L² transport inequality therefore gives the stated bound for this particular initial coupling.

04Pass to the genuine Wasserstein distance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Both initial laws and both output laws have finite second moments. The prefactor is nonnegative because the shifted damping denominator is positive. The proved affine bound over every finite-cost initial coupling therefore passes to the actual infimum defining Wasserstein distance. The result has the paper’s full arbitrary-shift exponential factor and holds for any common square-integrable velocity and any genuine continuous common input, so Brownian forcing is a direct specialization.
