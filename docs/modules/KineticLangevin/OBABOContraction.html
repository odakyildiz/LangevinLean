Contraction of the nonlinear OBABO map and its iterates

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

For a differentiable, m-strongly convex potential with M-Lipschitz gradient, the OBABO step driven by common thermostat vectors contracts the modified energy whenever γ2≥4M\gamma^2\ge4Mγ2≥4M and 33γ3h≤m33\gamma^3h\le m33γ3h≤m. The thermostat amplitude is arbitrary: it disappears through synchronous cancellation. This proves a property of the defined map and its iterates, rather than postulating a contractive transition.

Synchronous thermostat noises cancel from the discrepancy, but the two nonlinear force evaluations remain. Their full weighted remainder estimate proves contraction for the defined map and all its iterates.

03Iterate and project to the physical position §

Mathematical statement and proof

The small-step restrictions ensure the factor q=1−mh/(3γ)q=1-mh/(3\gamma)q=1−mh/(3γ) is nonnegative. Induction then applies the one-step theorem and multiplies the preceding energy inequality by q, giving E(Zn−Wn)≤qnE(Z0−W0)E(Z_n-W_n)\le q^nE(Z_0-W_0)E(Zn​−Wn​)≤qnE(Z0​−W0​) for any two recursions with the same two input vectors at every step. When the initial velocities agree, the initial mixed energy is exactly the squared initial position distance. The lower metric equivalence gives ∥Xn−Yn∥2≤2qn∥X0−Y0∥2\|X_n-Y_n\|^2\le2q^n\|X_0-Y_0\|^2∥Xn​−Yn​∥2≤2qn∥X0​−Y0​∥2. Both sides are nonnegative, so the square comparison applies the square comparison and the nonnegative square-root identity to obtain ∥Xn−Yn∥≤2qn∥X0−Y0∥\|X_n-Y_n\|\le\sqrt{2q^n}\|X_0-Y_0\|∥Xn​−Yn​∥≤2qn​∥X0​−Y0​∥. This is a direct statement about numerical iterates; identifying an invariant law and quantifying discretization bias are separate analytic steps.

Apply the same one-step inequality to the discrepancy at each iteration, using common noises at both thermostat stages. Induction gives the geometric phase decay. The position projection is then applied at the final step, keeping its geometric constant outside the iteration.
