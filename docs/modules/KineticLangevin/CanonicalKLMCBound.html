The KLMC law bound with the initial Wasserstein distance

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The numerical law is the pushforward of the defined KLMC chain on the independent Gaussian-velocity/Brownian product source. Every initial transport coupling yields this same output law. The complete constructed coupling proof gives the paper's first-order bound, and taking the transport infimum replaces the supplied coupling cost by the true initial Wasserstein distance. This intermediate module makes the sole stationary global-reference law property explicit; the final Gibbs theorem discharges it from the Brownian source.

The stationary global reference supplies all local marginals, and the numerical output is independent of the initial plan. The final bound is obtained by estimating every coupling and then taking its infimum.

Statement and proof

01Fix the numerical output law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The definition uses the original pointwise KLMC method, the standard Gaussian initial velocity and the exact canonical correlated Brownian innovations. The numerical L² chain is identified with the generic product-law construction by equality of the innovation variables. The established output-law theorem then proves that its pushforward is independent of which initial position coupling was chosen.

The numerical law is fixed by the initial position law, the independent standard Gaussian velocity, and the canonical innovation stream. It is defined without choosing a reference coupling. Every later coupling estimate therefore concerns one and the same sampler output.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2.

02Transfer one global stationary phase law to the reference segments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The named stationary property concerns the full constructed kinetic flow: at every time its phase law is N(0,Id)⊗π\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\piN(0,Id​)⊗π. It contains no numerical convergence conclusion. Exact chain-to-global identification transfers its position marginal to every reference grid point. Exact segment-to-global identification transfers its Gaussian velocity marginal to every intermediate time in a reference segment. The argument obtains these marginals by composition with the product-measure projections.

Exact uniqueness identifies the stitched reference segments with the global kinetic solution. Its phase stationarity supplies the Gibbs position at grid endpoints and the standard Gaussian velocity at every local time. These are the distributional facts used in the local-error integral.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.6.

03Apply the proved coupling estimate to one initial plan §

Mathematical statement and proof

The initial position L² discrepancy equals the true cost of the supplied transport coupling; this follows from the concrete extension's representative and norm identities. The segment specification furnishes all analytic local-error premises internally. Together with the derived stationary position and Gaussian velocity marginals, the quantitative L² KLMC theorem gives W2(νn,π)≤2(1−3mh/(4γ))ncost⁡(c)+Mh2d/mW_2(\nu_n,\pi)\le\sqrt2(1-3mh/(4\gamma))^n\operatorname{cost}(c)+Mh\sqrt{2d}/mW2​(νn​,π)≤2​(1−3mh/(4γ))ncost(c)+Mh2d​/m. Output-law equality and initial-cost equality rewrite this into a bound for the numerical law.

For one finite-cost initial plan, the numerical and reference variables form an output coupling. The analytic recurrence bounds its cost, and endpoint projection gives a position-law transport estimate. The plan’s own root-mean-square cost remains visible at this stage.

04Prove output finiteness and take the transport infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

Finite initial Wasserstein distance supplies an finite-cost initial coupling. Its numerical and reference states are L² variables, so their distance gives a finite transport bound on the output law. This proves output finiteness before real-valued transport arithmetic is used. The scalar recurrence estimate proves the contraction factor is nonnegative under the paper's step restrictions. The argument then applies the proved infimum theorem over all finite-cost couplings, obtaining the same estimate with W2(μ,π)W_2(\mu,\pi)W2​(μ,π) in place of the cost of a chosen plan. No existence of an optimal coupling is assumed.

Finite initial transport provides at least one finite-cost plan, proving output finiteness. Since the same affine estimate holds for every such plan and its coefficient is nonnegative, infimizing replaces the plan cost by the initial Wasserstein distance.
