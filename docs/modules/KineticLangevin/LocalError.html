Derive the actual frozen-force local defect

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The velocity defect is an explicit exponentially weighted integral of the changing force; the position defect is its primitive. Bounds follow from a Lipschitz force and a kinematic identity, not from a built-in defect hypothesis. The ambient norm may represent a deterministic Banach path or an L² random-vector norm. A kinetic stationarity theorem supplying the required velocity-moment bound remains separate.

Mathematical statement and proof

Lipschitzness gives ∥g(xs)−g(x0)∥≤MCs\|g(x_s)-g(x_0)\|\le MCs∥g(xs​)−g(x0​)∥≤MCs. 04Integrate once more to obtain the cubic position defect §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the proved velocity-defect bound at each intermediate time sss: ∥Dv(s)∥≤MCs2/2\|D_v(s)\|\le MCs^2/2∥Dv​(s)∥≤MCs2/2. Its continuous scalar majorant is integrable, so the norm of the position-defect integral is bounded by ∫0tMCs2/2 ds=MCt3/6\int_0^tMCs^2/2\,ds=MCt^3/6∫0t​MCs2/2ds=MCt3/6. Lean first verifies that every intermediate time stays in the original horizon, rewrites the scalar coefficient into the majorant's form, applies norm-integral comparison, and uses integral_pow. The integration variable inside the bound is sss, preserving the correct cubic constant. No stationarity or Gaussian moment is assumed implicitly by this deterministic estimate.
