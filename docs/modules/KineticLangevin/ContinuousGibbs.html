Gibbs invariance and exponential convergence of the kinetic position law

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The specified Brownian diffusion starts on an independent position–velocity–noise product. This section proves its exact Gibbs stationarity on that same source, rather than assuming a reference law. Combining it with the arbitrary-shift continuous coupling theorem gives unconditional transport to the Gibbs target. The simple friction regime yields the explicit factor 2e−mt/γ\sqrt{2}e^{-mt/\gamma}2​e−mt/γ.

The constructed Brownian kinetic flow preserves the normalized Gibbs phase law. Using its position marginal as a stationary comparator turns law-to-law contraction into explicit convergence to the sampling target.

01Reassociate the source and identify each thermal increment §

Mathematical statement and proof

The measurable source map sends (x,(v,B))(x,(v,B))(x,(v,B)) to ((v,x),B)((v,x),B)((v,x),B). Product associativity and exchange of independent position and velocity prove that this map preserves the exact phase-times-Brownian law. The continuous linear source pullbacks commute with the OU input integral, and the proved Brownian convolution formula identifies each filtered increment with the translated exponential kernel on the independent Brownian coordinate.

Reassociate the initial-phase and Brownian product factors without altering their joint law. Each filtered increment is a measurable function of the corresponding Brownian interval. The established stream law therefore supplies the precise thermal innovations of the stationarity argument.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02The complete initial/noise law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Disjoint Brownian intervals give an iid stream of thermal Gaussian innovations. On the phase-times-Brownian product this stream is independent of the initial phase. Pulling that law back along the source reassociation and using the L2L^{2}L2 representative identities proves the full initial-phase/entire-stream product law on the position–velocity–noise source.

The initial Gibbs phase is independent of the entire future source. Combining this product identity with the innovation-stream law proves the full initialization-and-noise input law. This supports repeated transition identities without a hidden independence assumption.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

03The exact stationary position law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The selected continuous solution satisfies the kinetic integral equation by construction. The initial/noise product law verifies every input-law hypothesis of the proved splitting-limit stationarity theorem. This first proves the full phase has exactly the independent Gaussian-times-Gibbs target law. Projecting that phase law then gives the Gibbs position distribution at the terminal time. Strong convexity supplies the finite target second moment used in the construction.

Phase stationarity of the kinetic solution implies position stationarity by projection. The projected target is exactly the normalized density e−fe^{-f}e−f. Thus the reference endpoint in the mixing theorem has the desired sampling law.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

phaseFlow_gibbs_stationary first installs the normalized Gibbs probability instance and its second-moment bound. The local objects Q, V, B and Y specify the independent Gaussian/Brownian source, initial velocity, forcing and integral solution. hs supplies the integral equation and continuity; hjoint supplies the complete initial-phase/OU-increment law. These are the inputs of kinetic_integral_stationary_of_jointNoise. Thus invariance is obtained for the constructed solution. positionLaw_gibbs composes the resulting HasLaw with the second-coordinate projection to recover the Gibbs position marginal.

04The full and simple explicit mixing bounds §

Mathematical statement and proof

In the arbitrary-shift two-law theorem, start the reference position from the Gibbs distribution and use its newly proved stationarity. This produces the full explicit shifted coefficient with the initial Wasserstein distance. For shift zero and m+M≤γ2m+M\le\gamma^2m+M≤γ2, the maximum in the exponent equals −m-m−m; the prefactor simplifies to 2\sqrt22​. Finite L2L^{2}L2 output and Gibbs moments also prove the transport distance is finite, ensuring the real-valued estimate never conceals an infinite distance.

Apply the arbitrary-shift law contraction with one initial law equal to the stationary target. Its output remains the target, so the result is an explicit convergence-to-equilibrium bound. The simple friction regime follows by setting the shift to zero.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.21.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

kinetic_integral_position_decay subtracts the two common-input integral equations, so the additive forcing disappears before differentiation and the transformed energy inequality is used. wasserstein2_gibbs_general applies the corresponding two-initial-law coupling theorem and rewrites the stationary comparator using positionLaw_gibbs. The initial coupling is arbitrary; the transport theorem passes its cost to the infimum defining Wasserstein distance.
