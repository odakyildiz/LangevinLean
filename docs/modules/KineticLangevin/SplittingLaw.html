The independent-noise Markov law of the splitting recursion

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A law identity must be established for the random variables used in the splitting recursion. The input here is the complete joint law of the initial phase and the entire innovation stream. Composing this law with the measurable defined recursion proves the endpoint law. The same identity is then established for the L2L^{2}L2 realization using its proved almost-everywhere representatives.

The lifted and pointwise descriptions are proved to have identical outputs under the complete input law. Consequently the splitting analyzed for convergence is the same independent-noise Markov chain used in stationarity transfer.

Statement and proof

01Push the full input law through the recursion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let Cn(z,w)C_n(z,w)Cn​(z,w) be the explicit recursion run for nnn steps. Its joint measurability has already been proved by induction. If (X,(Nk)k)(X,(N_k)_k)(X,(Nk​)k​) has the product law π⊗Q⊗N\pi\otimes Q^{\otimes\mathbb N}π⊗Q⊗N, the pushforward composition rule says its output has law (π⊗Q⊗N)∘Cn−1(\pi\otimes Q^{\otimes\mathbb N})\circ C_n^{-1}(π⊗Q⊗N)∘Cn−1​. The existing finite-prefix proof identifies this with the iterated Markov law πPn\pi P^nπPn. On an explicit initial-law/source product, the initial phase and a source stream are independent by the product pushforward identity. One proves both statements directly using composition of distributional identities and the pushforward formula for product measures.

The complete input consists of the initial phase and the full innovation stream. The recursion is a measurable function of that input. Pushing the product input law through the recursion determines its endpoint law uniquely.

02Match the two forms of the recursion step for step §

Mathematical statement and proof

The splitting sequence uses Verlet followed by the exact OU update. Induction shows it is precisely the additive-noise sequence for that step. Continuity of the force makes every kick, drift and thermostat continuous, so their composition is measurable. The law theorem can therefore be applied to the splitting map, with no additional transition-measurability premise.

The lifted and pointwise maps agree at initialization and at every successor step under the proved representative identities. Induction gives equality of their numerical endpoints almost everywhere. Their pushforward laws are consequently equal.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.20.3.

03Eliminate the choice of L2L^{2}L2 representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted chain has already been proved equal almost everywhere to the pointwise vector chain with the same initial state and innovations. Replace its pushforward using this equality, then apply the full-input-law theorem. This proves the L2L^{2}L2 endpoint law is the independent-noise Markov iterate. Quotient representatives cannot create an extra source of randomness or change the chain law.

Changing a representative on a null set changes neither its law nor the law of a measurable finite-step recursion. The construction therefore defines a sampler law intrinsically. This is needed when the analytic convergence proof is carried out in equivalence classes of random variables.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6.
