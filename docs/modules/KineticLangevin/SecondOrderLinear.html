Contraction of the frozen-linear second-order comparison

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The frozen-linear KLMC2 map must be controlled when friction is much larger than the square root of smoothness. A remainder estimate proportional to γ2\gamma^2γ2 would not retain the stated step range. Instead, the argument uses cocoercivity of the force difference together with the exact exponential response coefficients.

These identities give a second-order remainder proportional to MMM. Under 5γMt≤m5\gamma Mt\le m5γMt≤m and the stated curvature and friction hypotheses, the transformed norm contracts by 1−mt/(2γ)1-mt/(2\gamma)1−mt/(2γ). The negative curvature contribution is retained before the positive remainder terms are estimated.

01Separate fast damping from the Euler remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

For the kinetic difference drift A=(−γv−F,v)A=(-\gamma v-F,v)A=(−γv−F,v), write EEE for the transformed squared norm and E′E'E′ for its derivative in direction AAA. Expansion of the two transformed coordinates gives

E(A)=−γ2E′+2∥F∥2−γ2⟨x,F⟩,E((v,x)+sA)=E+sE′+s2E(A).E(A)=-\frac\gamma2E'+2\|F\|^2-\gamma^2\langle x,F\rangle,\qquad E((v,x)+sA)=E+sE'+s^2E(A).E(A)=−2γ​E′+2∥F∥2−γ2⟨x,F⟩,E((v,x)+sA)=E+sE′+s2E(A).

Both are quadratic-form identities obtained by the real inner-product rules. They separate the negative contribution of velocity damping from the remaining force terms.

The exact exponential response already accounts for the damping. The additional increment can therefore be estimated as an Euler correction in the transformed coordinates without replacing that damping by a positive quadratic upper bound.

02Use cocoercivity before bounding the remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

Cocoercivity and smoothness give ∥F∥2≤M⟨x,F⟩\|F\|^2\le M\langle x,F\rangle∥F∥2≤M⟨x,F⟩ and ⟨x,F⟩≤M∥x∥2\langle x,F\rangle\le M\|x\|^2⟨x,F⟩≤M∥x∥2. Since γ2≥m+M\gamma^2\ge m+Mγ2≥m+M, one has 2M−γ2≤M−m2M-\gamma^2\le M-m2M−γ2≤M−m. The position projection also gives γ2∥x∥2≤2E\gamma^2\|x\|^2\le2Eγ2∥x∥2≤2E. Substituting these inequalities in the drift-energy identity gives

E(A)≤−γ2E′+2M(M−m)γ2E.E(A)\le-\frac\gamma2E'+\frac{2M(M-m)}{\gamma^2}E.E(A)≤−2γ​E′+γ22M(M−m)​E.

Therefore E′≤−2mE/γE'\le-2mE/\gammaE′≤−2mE/γ can be inserted with its sign preserved. Comparing nonnegative squares then bounds the transformed norm by its initial value multiplied by

05Identify and contract the frozen-linear map §

Mathematical statement and proof

The frozen-linear corrected map has velocity and position
