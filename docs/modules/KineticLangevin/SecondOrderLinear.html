The frozen-linear contraction in Proposition 3

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The friction can be much larger than the square root of the smoothness constant. A crude Euler remainder proportional to γ² would lose the paper’s step-size regime. The identities below instead use the force’s cocoercivity to retain a second-order budget proportional to M, and prove the frozen-linear map contracts by 1−mt/(2γ) under 5γMt≤m.

The frozen-linear correction is contracted using strong-smooth interpolation and exact exponential coefficients. Its scalar budget retains the dissipative information that a crude force Lipschitz estimate would lose.

01Separate fast damping from the Euler remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

For the kinetic difference drift A=(−γv−F,v), its transformed squared norm satisfies the exact identity E(A)=−γE′/2+2‖F‖²−γ²⟨x,F⟩. The Euler phase (v,x)+sA(v,x)+sA(v,x)+sA has energy E+sE′+s2E(A)E+sE^{\prime}+s^2E(A)E+sE′+s2E(A). The proof expands the two transformed coordinates, uses the real inner-product identities, and checks both equalities by scalar polynomial expansion.

The exact exponential response retains the strongest velocity damping. The remaining increment is treated as an Euler-type perturbation in the transformed coordinates. This decomposition avoids paying a loose quadratic remainder for a damping effect already integrated exactly.

02Use cocoercivity before bounding the remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

The force satisfies ‖F‖²≤M⟨x,F⟩ and ⟨x,F⟩≤M‖x‖². Since γ²≥m+M, 2M−γ²≤M−m. Also γ²‖x‖²≤2E. Combining these facts gives E(A)≤−γE′/2+2M(M−m)E/γ². In the Euler expansion, the derivative coefficient becomes s(1−γs/2), which is nonnegative for γs≤1. Actual force dissipativity E′≤−2mE/γ then yields transformed norm at most [1−ms/γ+(m/2+M(M−m)/γ²)s²] times the initial norm. The final square comparison retains an additional nonnegative square instead of estimating it away.

Cocoercivity couples the gradient-difference square to its pairing with displacement. Using it before a separate norm bound keeps the negative curvature contribution available. Bounding the gradient difference only by smoothness would lose the dissipative balance needed for contraction.

03Bound the true frozen-Hessian correction §

Mathematical statement and proof

The remaining phase correction is (−φ₂Hv,−ψ₂F−φ₃Hv). The exact coefficient identity φ₂+γφ₃=ψ₂ transforms it into (−ψ₂(Hv+γF),φ₂Hv). The bounds ψ₂,φ₂≤t²/2, ‖H‖≤M, ‖v‖≤N, and γ‖x‖≤3N/2 give component envelopes 5Mt²N/4 and Mt²N/2. Their squared sum is below (3Mt²N/2)². Thus the correction costs at most 3Mt²N/2, with no symmetry or common Hessian eigenbasis assumed.

The frozen Hessian response is a term in the corrected map. Its operator bound and the exact response coefficients control its transformed contribution. Both its velocity and position components must enter the scalar coefficient budget.

04Check the scalar coefficient budget with exact ψ₁ §

Mathematical statement and proof

The friction balance γψ₂=t−ψ₁ and ψ₂≤t²/2 imply ψ₁≥t−γt²/2, while 0≤ψ₁≤t. Moreover m+M(M−m)/γ²≤M. These inequalities bound the Euler coefficient plus the frozen correction by 1−mt/γ+5Mt²/2. The source step restriction 5γMt≤m makes the last term at most mt/(2γ), leaving 1−mt/(2γ). All divisions are justified by positive friction.

The first response is (1−e−γh)/γ(1-e^{-\gamma h})/\gamma(1−e−γh)/γ for positive friction. Retaining this exact quantity in the contraction budget gives a sharper inequality than replacing it everywhere by hhh. The admissible step restriction then controls the remaining positive terms.

05Identify and contract the frozen-linear map §

Mathematical statement and proof

The frozen-linear corrected update is the explicit phase with ψ₀v−ψ₁F−φ₂Hv in velocity and x+ψ₁v−ψ₂F−φ₃Hv in position. The identity ψ₀=1−γψ₁ writes this map as the Euler phase plus the correction above. Strong-smooth interpolation supplies both cocoercivity and force dissipativity. The transformed triangle inequality and the scalar budget therefore prove the source contraction factor for the frozen-linear part of KLMC2.

The final map is the force-difference and frozen-Hessian comparison extracted from the defined correction. Its norm bound is therefore usable inside the stochastic coupled-step identity. It is not an assumed linear contraction detached from the nonlinear potential.
