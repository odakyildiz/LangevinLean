The KLMC2 one-step error recurrence on the Brownian coupling space

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The generic second-order one-step theorem is instantiated with the constructed numerical chain and the stationary reference segment driven by the same Brownian interval. Every source, Gaussian, stationarity, mild-equation and kinematic premise is verified by earlier proved constructions. The resulting recurrence is about the original defined sampler.

The specified probability source, reference paths, and numerical recursion verify all hypotheses of the repaired one-step theorem. Its resulting affine inequality can therefore be iterated for the KLMC2 chain.

Statement and proof

01The exact four-noise input §

Mathematical statement and proof

The constructed innovation is the Brownian source lift of each of the four deterministic convolution kernels. Unfolding this definition and the analytic lifted-noise definition proves exact equality, since converting the nonnegative numerical step to a nonnegative-real duration does not change it.

The four-response input has the packed correlated Gaussian law and the correct source representatives. Substitution in the numerical update identifies the recurrence’s innovation variables with those of the constructed sampler, including the Hessian-multiplied responses.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1.

02Verification of the hypotheses using reference and numerical paths §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference segment is continuous, starts at the current reference state and ends at its next state. Its Gaussian-times-Gibbs phase law, free-OU mild equation and position primitive are proved internally. Past/future independence identifies the free-OU velocity law as standard Gaussian throughout the same fresh interval. Applying the generic KLMC2 one-step theorem therefore gives its exact source coefficient, Gaussian cutoff tail and cubic stationary local defect. Equality of the four noises and the numerical successor formula identify its left side with the next numerical/reference error. Symmetry of the transformed norm reverses the current reference-minus-numerical discrepancy to the chosen numerical-minus-reference convention.

The constructed reference and auxiliary paths supply continuity, exact integral equations, stationarity, and past/future independence. The repaired one-step theorem therefore applies at every iteration. Its conclusion is the constructed error recurrence, rather than an abstract conditional recurrence.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
