The OBABO energy and the average of two force differences

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The OBABO source metric is E(v,x)=∥x∥2+2⟨x,v⟩/γ+∥v∥2/LE(v,x)=\|x\|^2+2\langle x,v\rangle/\gamma+\|v\|^2/LE(v,x)=∥x∥2+2⟨x,v⟩/γ+∥v∥2/L. Its continuous drift is contractive when γ2≥4L\gamma^2\ge4Lγ2≥4L. OBABO evaluates the force at two different position pairs; the proof below controls their average by an explicit centered-sector error. This avoids selecting a single Hessian point in a vector-valued mean-value theorem. The higher-order discrete remainder still has to be bounded separately.

The weighted metric separates the dissipative part of the nonlinear two-kick update. The force average is derived from the endpoint evaluations, with its sector error retained explicitly.

The error is explicit and vanishes when the average force satisfies the exact sector at the original position difference.

The ideal drift contribution is dissipative, but the two-kick method evaluates forces at different locations. The energy estimate isolates the resulting sector error rather than treating the whole force as arbitrary. This allows strong convexity to survive the numerical expansion.

03Control the average of the two nonlinear force evaluations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let F,F′F,F'F,F′ satisfy the strong-smooth centered bounds at differences x,x′x,x'x,x′, and set S=(F+F′)/2S=(F+F')/2S=(F+F′)/2. With μ=(m+L)/2\mu=(m+L)/2μ=(m+L)/2 and R=(L−m)/2R=(L-m)/2R=(L−m)/2, decompose S−μxS-\mu xS−μx into half of each centered force residual plus μ(x′−x)/2\mu(x'-x)/2μ(x′−x)/2. The triangle inequality and ∥x′∥≤∥x∥+∥x′−x∥\|x'\|\le\|x\|+\|x'-x\|∥x′∥≤∥x∥+∥x′−x∥ yield ∥S−μx∥≤R∥x∥+(L/2)∥x′−x∥\|S-\mu x\|\le R\|x\|+(L/2)\|x'-x\|∥S−μx∥≤R∥x∥+(L/2)∥x′−x∥. Thus the sector error is precisely e=(L/2)∥x′−x∥e=(L/2)\|x'-x\|e=(L/2)∥x′−x∥. A centered residual controls monotonicity by Cauchy–Schwarz. Moving its center from μx\mu xμx to Lx/2Lx/2Lx/2 and squaring yields the approximate cocoercivity bound needed above. One proves both consequences from the explicit norm inequality.

The averaged force is formed from the endpoint evaluations of the nonlinear gradient. Its difference from a centered sector model is bounded using smoothness and the step displacement. This is necessary because an average of evaluations is not itself automatically a gradient at one intermediate point.

04Apply the sector proof to the original potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

For two arbitrary position pairs, use the strong-smooth interpolation inequality and its centered-ball consequence to obtain the two force residual bounds directly from differentiability, strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient. Their average is exactly the average of the two gradient differences used by the two OBABO kicks. The preceding average-sector lemma supplies the explicit position-increment error, and the perturbed dissipativity theorem supplies the source drift bound. Only the first-order potential hypotheses are required for this nonlinear estimate. The remaining discrete proof must bound the displayed error and the integrator remainder using its step-size restrictions.

The strong-smooth sector inequality holds for each gradient difference. Combining those inequalities and the displacement bound supplies the average-force premise. Consequently the contraction theorem applies to the nonlinear OBABO update, with no assumed averaged-Hessian representation.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.10.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.10.3.
