Kinetic Gibbs invariance from independent initialization and Brownian increments

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The stationarity argument starts with a continuous L² kinetic integral solution whose joint input law is the product of the target initial phase law and the complete constructed Ornstein–Uhlenbeck innovation stream. The independent product construction supplies this joint law. Convergence of splitting approximations, vanishing stationary weak defects and equality of the limiting endpoint laws then prove invariance.

The splitting approximants have a vanishing target weak discrepancy and converge to the constructed kinetic solution. Their common limit identifies the solution’s full Gibbs phase law at every time.

Mathematical statement and proof

Let π\piπ be the normalized Gaussian-times-Gibbs target, and let YYY solve the kinetic integral equation driven by the continuous L² input BBB. On a mesh hn=T/(n+1)h_n=T/(n+1)hn​=T/(n+1), form the Verlet–OU approximation SnS_nSn​ using the filtered increments of this same BBB. The proved Banach splitting theorem gives Sn→YTS_n\to Y_TSn​→YT​ in the product of L² spaces.

The joint law of the initial phase and the entire innovation sequence is the explicit product π⊗Qhn⊗N\pi\otimes Q_{h_n}^{\otimes\mathbb N}π⊗Qhn​⊗N​. The source-law transfer theorem, together with the almost-everywhere vector-chain representation, identifies the law of SnS_nSn​ with the defined Markov iterate. Its bounded-test error is at most 2(n+1)hn2KA=2T2KAn+1,K=M(2M+11)8,A=∫(∥v∥2+∥∇f(x)∥2) dπ.2(n+1)h_n^2 K A=\frac{2T^2KA}{n+1},\qquad K=\frac{M(2M+11)}8,\quad A=\int(\|v\|^2+\|\nabla f(x)\|^2)\,d\pi.2(n+1)hn2​KA=n+12T2KA​,K=8M(2M+11)​,A=∫(∥v∥2+∥∇f(x)∥2)dπ. The moment is finite by the proved target tail theorem. The mesh is eventually at most one, so every quadratic rejection estimate applies, and the displayed scalar error tends to zero.

The phase-law continuity theorem now identifies the L² limiting phase law with π\piπ. The argument defines the splitting approximation sequence, applies the proved convergence theorem, rewrites its chain law using the explicit initial/noise product distribution, rewrites the bounded test expectation through the pushforward, invokes the proved Markov weak-error bound, and checks the scalar T2/(n+1)T^2/(n+1)T2/(n+1) decay. It then applies dual-characteristic uniqueness for finite measures. No stationary solution, local-error conclusion, assumed independence of the output law from the coupling, or numerical-limit conclusion is assumed. The joint probability-law hypothesis on the input is supplied by the independent product construction.

The constructed splitting chain has the exact independent thermal-noise law and its endpoint converges in second mean to the kinetic solution. Its bounded-test target discrepancy vanishes along the same refinement. Characteristic functions therefore identify the limiting phase law with the Gibbs target, proving stationarity of the continuous solution.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6.
