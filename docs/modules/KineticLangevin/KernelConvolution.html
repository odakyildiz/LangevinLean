Convolution and time-primitive identities for Wiener responses

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The φ corrections in KLMC2 are proved to be time convolutions of the same Brownian ψ responses. The proof establishes an ordinary integrable Fubini identity in the deterministic time-kernel Hilbert space, then applies the constructed isonormal map and spatial lift. Both velocity and position correction noises retain their common source.

The deterministic causal-convolution identities are established before applying the Gaussian source. Bounded linearity then transfers their exact primitive and damping relations to the stochastic responses.

01Name and control the ordinary causal exponential convolution §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a continuous scalar response kkk, define (Eβk)(t)=∫0te−β(t−s)k(s) ds(\mathcal E_\beta k)(t)=\int_0^t e^{-\beta(t-s)}k(s)\,ds(Eβ​k)(t)=∫0t​e−β(t−s)k(s)ds. Factoring the exponential gives (Eβk)(t)=e−βt∫0teβsk(s) ds(\mathcal E_\beta k)(t)=e^{-\beta t}\int_0^t e^{\beta s}k(s)\,ds(Eβ​k)(t)=e−βt∫0t​eβsk(s)ds. One proves this by the exponential addition law inside the exact interval integral. The factored form proves continuity using continuity of an ordinary primitive of a continuous function. Allowing a damping β\betaβ distinct from the response friction γ\gammaγ makes both the φ convolution and an ordinary ψ primitive instances of the same theorem.

The causal exponential kernel is nonnegative for nonnegative friction and has finite integral on every bounded interval. This permits continuity and norm bounds for its convolution with a continuous Hilbert-valued path. It is the deterministic response operator underlying the corrected equations.

03The deterministic Hilbert-space Fubini identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let Kk(t)(r)=1(0,t](r)ψk(γ,t−r)K_k(t)(r)=1_{(0,t]}(r)\psi_k(\gamma,t-r)Kk​(t)(r)=1(0,t]​(r)ψk​(γ,t−r) be the element of L² time. The key identity is KEβψk(t)=∫0te−β(t−s)Kk(s) ds.K_{\mathcal E_\beta\psi_k}(t)=\int_0^t e^{-\beta(t-s)}K_k(s)\,ds.KEβ​ψk​​(t)=∫0t​e−β(t−s)Kk​(s)ds. One proves equality by pairing with an arbitrary element fff of this Hilbert space. On the finite time rectangle, fff is integrable. The continuous scalar response has a finite bound on [0,t][0,t][0,t], and the damping is at most one on the triangle because β≥0\beta\ge0β≥0. Thus the double integrand is integrable, which justifies ordinary Bochner Fubini. The two triangular-domain identities identify the two integration orders with the two sides of the claimed Hilbert identity. Commuting the continuous linear inner-product functional with the time integral completes the equality. No stochastic Fubini conclusion is assumed.

Continuity on the compact triangular region gives integrable norm bounds. Bochner Fubini therefore applies to the Hilbert-valued integrand. This justifies the convolution identity used later under the Gaussian source map.

04Recover φ responses and every ψ primitive §

Mathematical statement and proof

With β=γ\beta=\gammaβ=γ, the causal convolution is exactly φk+1\varphi_{k+1}φk+1​, so the Hilbert kernel for φk+1\varphi_{k+1}φk+1​ is the damped time integral of the kernel for ψk\psi_kψk​. With β=0\beta=0β=0, the causal convolution is the defining ordinary primitive ψk+1\psi_{k+1}ψk+1​. This gives the exact time-primitive identity for every ψ index, including the second primitive needed by the position correction. The proof specializes the already proved general Hilbert identity and rewrites the defining scalar integrals. The ordinary primitive result works for every friction, since its outer damping is zero.

Time integration of a response raises its primitive index, while damping convolves it with the exponential kernel. Combining these two operations yields the precise φ\varphiφ responses. Their identities preserve all coefficients needed for the cancellation in the Hessian correction.

05Pass the identities through the Gaussian source and spatial lift §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scalar response on a Brownian interval is the constructed isonormal map applied to the translated deterministic time kernel. Translation and the isonormal map are continuous linear maps, so they commute with the Bochner time integrals. The proved Hilbert identities therefore yield Nφk+1(a,t)=∫0te−γ(t−s)Nψk(a,s) ds,Nψk+1(a,t)=∫0tNψk(a,s) ds.N_{\varphi_{k+1}}(a,t)=\int_0^t e^{-\gamma(t-s)}N_{\psi_k}(a,s)\,ds,\qquad N_{\psi_{k+1}}(a,t)=\int_0^tN_{\psi_k}(a,s)\,ds.Nφk+1​​(a,t)=∫0t​e−γ(t−s)Nψk​​(a,s)ds,Nψk+1​​(a,t)=∫0t​Nψk​​(a,s)ds. One proves continuity of each integrand, applies the continuous linear integral-commutation theorem, and rewrites scalar multiplication. It then applies the continuous linear spatial lift to obtain the same identities for arbitrary finite Euclidean coordinate sets. All responses use the very same translated Brownian source. In particular, the φ₂ and φ₃ noises in KLMC2 are exactly the damped integrals of the first and second primitives of the fresh OU noise, which is the identity required to retain the omitted stochastic Hessian-difference terms in the repaired comparison proof.

The isonormal map and the finite spatial assembly are bounded linear maps. They commute with the verified integrals, transferring the deterministic kernel identities to the stochastic response variables. This gives equalities on one source rather than merely covariance comparisons.
