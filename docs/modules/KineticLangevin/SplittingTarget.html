Quadratic stationary weak error for the kinetic splitting

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This section applies the exact corrected Verlet identity and the exact Gaussian thermostat identity to the normalized Gaussian-times-Gibbs target. Averaging a bounded test over thermostat preserves its bound. The uncorrected splitting therefore has a proved quadratic one-step stationary weak defect, and its Markov iterate accumulates at most a linear number of these defects.

Corrected Hamiltonian invariance and exact thermostat invariance yield a vanishing weak stationary error for the splitting. Its normalized moment envelope keeps every local error integrable.

01Use the thermal Gaussian innovation measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

The additive innovation measure is the pushforward of the Euclidean standard Gaussian by the coefficient σh=1−e−2γh\sigma_h=\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma h}}σh​=1−e−2γh​. It is a probability measure because it is a measurable pushforward of a probability measure. The preceding OU marginal theorem identifies this exact law with the canonical convolution innovation; here it is the concrete measure used by the Markov transition.

The innovation law is the exact Gaussian with thermostat’s thermal variance. It is independent of the initial phase. This independence and the fluctuation–dissipation relation are what preserve the Gaussian velocity factor, rather than a covariance calculation alone.

02Normalize the exact corrected Verlet identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The canonical target is an explicitly proved finite scalar normalization of the Hamiltonian weighted measure. A density-weighted measure, its measurable pushforward, and their sum all commute with this scalar multiplication. Scaling the earlier exact identity therefore gives (Vh)#(ahπ)+R#((1−ah)π)=π(V_h)_\#(a_h\pi)+R_\#((1-a_h)\pi)=\pi(Vh​)#​(ah​π)+R#​((1−ah​)π)=π for the normalized target. The argument rewrites the explicit normalization, both density scalings, both pushforward scalings, and the corrected Hamiltonian Gibbs identity.

The corrected Verlet identity initially concerns an unnormalized Hamiltonian density. Its finite positive partition mass permits division to obtain an identity for the probability target. Every subsequent weak-test comparison therefore uses normalized measures.

03Derive the normalized target phase moment §

Mathematical statement and proof

The unnormalized Hamiltonian measure already has integrable ∥v∥2+∥∇f(x)∥2\|v\|^2+\|\nabla f(x)\|^2∥v∥2+∥∇f(x)∥2 from first-order strong convexity and Lipschitz force growth. The explicit normalizing scalar is finite: its Gaussian factor is an ordinary finite real number and its inverse potential partition is finite because the partition is strictly positive. The argument checks these extended-real facts and applies integrability under finite scalar multiplication. Thus the rejection envelope remains an finite target moment.

The normalized quadratic moment is computed from the independent Gaussian velocity and Gibbs position factors. Smoothness transfers the position moment to a force moment. This yields a finite constant for integrating the numerical rejection envelope.

04Preserve the target with the additive OU law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The damping δh=e−γh\delta_h=e^{-\gamma h}δh​=e−γh and innovation scale satisfy δh2+σh2=1\delta_h^2+\sigma_h^2=1δh2​+σh2​=1. The already proved thermostat result preserves the target when driven by a fresh standard Gaussian. Mapping that Gaussian through σh\sigma_hσh​ changes its law to the explicit additive innovation measure. One proves the coefficient identity, maps the independent product source through this noise scaling, composes the two pushforwards, and obtains exact preservation by the additive OU transition.

The thermostat acts only on velocity and adds the independent filtered Gaussian response. The exact variance identity preserves the velocity Gaussian, while position is unchanged. Hence the concrete product target is preserved by this transition.

06Accumulate only a linear number of local defects §

Mathematical statement and proof

The generic Markov test theorem applies the one-step estimate to each iterated dual test, whose bound remains CCC, and telescopes their expectations under the fixed target. Consequently the nnn-step splitting law has weak error at most nnn times the displayed quadratic defect. The argument verifies nonnegativity of the target moment and all scalar coefficients, supplies the concrete measurable transition, and instantiates the proved generic theorem. With h=T/nh=T/nh=T/n, this error tends to zero.

Over a fixed horizon the number of steps is proportional to the inverse step length. A local weak discrepancy with a higher step power therefore has a vanishing accumulated total. The telescoping argument keeps this accumulation linear and avoids a spurious growth of the test bound.
