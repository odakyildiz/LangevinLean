Actual phase laws and their L² limits

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The phase variable is a pair of actual L² classes, and its law is the pushforward of their measurable representatives. Dual characteristic functions depend continuously on this pair. This identifies a limiting stationary phase law from bounded-test estimates without incorrectly treating the usual maximum product norm as a Hilbert norm.

01Verify measurability of actual phase representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each L² class has a fixed strongly measurable representative. Taking the pair of its velocity and position representatives gives a measurable phase map. Lean uses the two strong-measurability results and the measurable product constructor, making the subsequent pushforward an actual measure.

02Reduce phase characteristic functions to one real L² variable §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a continuous real linear functional LLL on phase space, let Lv(v)=L(v,0)L_v(v)=L(v,0)Lv​(v)=L(v,0) and Lx(x)=L(0,x)L_x(x)=L(0,x)Lx​(x)=L(0,x). The real L² variable R=Lv(V)+Lx(X)R=L_v(V)+L_x(X)R=Lv​(V)+Lx​(X) represents L(V(ω),X(ω))L(V(\omega),X(\omega))L(V(ω),X(ω)) almost everywhere. Its law therefore has scalar characteristic function at frequency one equal to the dual characteristic function of the phase law. The map (V,X)↦R(V,X)\mapsto R(V,X)↦R is continuous, since the two continuous linear maps lift continuously to L² and addition is continuous. The existing scalar characteristic-function continuity theorem proves the desired phase continuity. Lean explicitly intersects the representative events for both lifted linear maps and their sum, proves the coordinate decomposition by linearity, rewrites the actual pushforward through LLL, and composes the two continuous maps.

03Identify the law of an actual L² limit from vanishing weak errors §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose actual phase pairs SnS_nSn​ converge in the product of L² spaces to SSS, and their bounded complex-test errors relative to a fixed finite measure π\piπ are eventually bounded by δn→0\delta_n\to0δn​→0. For each dual functional use the norm-one test F(z)=eiLzF(z)=e^{iLz}F(z)=eiLz. The error squeeze gives convergence of the approximating characteristic functions to that of π\piπ; the continuity theorem gives convergence of the same sequence to the characteristic function of the actual limiting phase law. Uniqueness of limits equates them, and uniqueness of finite measures from their dual characteristic functions gives P∘S−1=πP\circ S^{-1}=\piP∘S−1=π. Lean rewrites the actual phase expectation by the measurable pushforward formula and invokes the checked scalar squeeze and measure-uniqueness theorems. The weak estimates must be proved by the concrete numerical and probability layers; stationary output is not assumed.
