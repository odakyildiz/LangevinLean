KLMC output laws on independent extensions of initial couplings

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

An estimate for one coupling must eventually be optimized over all initial couplings. Its numerical output law must therefore be independent of that choice. This section proves that independence for the KLMC chain. For every coupling ccc of the initial position law μ\muμ and reference law π\piπ, the sample measure is explicitly c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q, where QQQ supplies the common initial velocity and all phase innovations. The velocity and position components of each innovation stay together, so their correlation is preserved. No convergence or stationary-reference assumption enters these law identities.

The numerical law is fixed independently of an initial transport plan. Exact source projections and representative identities then show that every coupling construction used in analysis has that same output marginal.

Statement and proof

01Build velocity and innovations on each product extension §

Mathematical statement and proof

The initial coupling has a probability first marginal, hence its joint measure is a probability measure. Its product with the fixed probability source QQQ is therefore a probability measure; its total mass is the product of the two unit masses. Pull the source velocity VVV back through the second projection and take its constructed L² class. Do the same separately for the two components of each supplied phase innovation WnW_nWn​. Both use the same source point. This gives the pair of L² classes needed by the kinetic analysis without assuming that velocity and position noise are independent. The two innovation components retain their joint source distribution.

The initial position coupling and the specified Gaussian/Brownian source are independent product factors. Pulling source variables onto this extension preserves their moments and joint laws. Both numerical and reference paths can therefore use the same source while retaining their prescribed initial marginals.

02The chosen L² representatives almost everywhere §

Mathematical statement and proof

The constructed Lp representative equals the original pulled-back random variable almost everywhere. For velocity this is the existing product-noise representative theorem. For a phase innovation, intersect the two full-measure sets for its components and combine the velocity and position equalities. Thus the L² pair represents precisely Wn(s)W_n(s)Wn​(s), including all its correlation. This is an almost-everywhere equality of random variables, not a comparison of moments or covariances.

A square-integrable equivalence class has many pointwise representatives, but any two agree outside a null set. The coordinate and innovation identities are therefore stated almost everywhere. Their finite intersections suffice at each fixed iteration to identify the random update.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.23.5.

03Define one numerical output law from initialization and fixed source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Given a position xxx and source point sss, start the original pointwise KLMC recursion at (V(s),x)(V(s),x)(V(s),x), use its phase innovations Wn(s)W_n(s)Wn​(s), and project the final phase state to position. This is the numerical endpoint map. Its law is the pushforward of μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q, recorded by the numerical position law. The definition contains no reference coupling. In particular, it is the same candidate output law for every coupling used to estimate its transport error.

Define the numerical endpoint first on the product of its own initial position distribution and the fixed independent source. This gives one output law without referring to a transport plan. Couplings are introduced afterward solely to estimate that law.

04The L² numerical chain is exactly the pointwise chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

On c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q, the L² numerical start consists of the pulled-back common velocity and first initial-position coordinate. The reference position's finite second moment together with finite coupling cost supplies a square-integrable first coordinate. Apply the shared L²-to-pointwise KLMC chain theorem. A countable intersection supplies agreement of every phase innovation on one full-measure set; intersect it with the initial velocity and position agreements. Equality of both phase coordinates identifies the initial state. Equality at every time index identifies the innovation sequence. Projecting the resulting phase-chain equality onto position gives the stated result. Equal initial velocities arise by using the same constructed L² variable; no separate velocity coupling cost is inserted.

The initial phases agree almost everywhere with the pointwise construction. If the iterates agree at step nnn, applying the same measurable update and the same innovation proves agreement at step n+1n+1n+1. Induction identifies the entire analyzed recursion at every fixed time.

05Derive law independence with almost-everywhere measurable data §

Mathematical statement and proof

The projection ((x,y),s)↦(x,s)((x,y),s)\mapsto(x,s)((x,y),s)↦(x,s) sends c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q exactly to μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q. Consequently every almost-everywhere measurable function of (x,s)(x,s)(x,s) has the same law on both spaces. One proves this using the projection marginal and the almost-everywhere version of the composition rule for pushforwards. This avoids an unnecessary assumption that arbitrarily selected Lp representatives are everywhere measurable.

The output map is indeed almost-everywhere measurable: the source L² assumptions give that property for velocity and both innovation components. The force is continuous by Lipschitz regularity, so the KLMC update is measurable. Induction propagates measurability through every phase iterate, and projection gives the position output. The final output-law theorem rewrites the L² chain with its proved representative equality, then applies the projection-law identity. Both its initial marginal and its numerical law are derived rather than assumed as extra identities.

Projecting an extended coupling onto its first position coordinate and source gives the original numerical input law. Composing with the pointwise endpoint map proves that its output marginal is unchanged. This is the precise reason the output does not depend on which transport coupling was selected.

06The common output law has total mass one §

Mathematical statement and proof

The independent product of the initial probability law and source law is a probability measure. The preceding almost-everywhere measurability theorem ensures its pushforward remains a probability measure. The argument applies the probability property of a measurable pushforward. This establishes a numerical output law before any Wasserstein estimate is attached to it. A specified Gaussian source and a stationary continuous reference are additional hypotheses needed for the final unconditional sampler theorem.

The input product is a probability measure, and the endpoint map is almost everywhere measurable. Its pushforward therefore has total mass one. This probability verification is needed before transport comparisons are made with the target law.
