Iteration of the L2L^{2}L2 error inequality and position transport

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The numerical phase knots, auxiliary exact segments, reference segments, common raw inputs and convolution noises are explicit. The local coupling inequality follows from their integral equations. The remaining law inputs state the reference Gaussian velocity and stationary position marginals. They do not assume a convergence conclusion.

The numerical and reference states are constructed together throughout the iteration. The scalar recurrence bounds their root-mean-square discrepancy, which then bounds the transport distance of their endpoint laws.

Mathematical statement and proof

For each step, the auxiliary path begins at the numerical knot, while the reference segment joins consecutive reference knots. The initial term is the cost of the supplied coupling. Obtaining the infimum over all initial couplings, and proving reference stationarity from the Brownian kinetic process, remain separate probabilistic requirements.

More generally, suppose the reference has an explicit reference velocity radius C≥0C\ge0C≥0. A uniform bound ∥Vtref∥L2≤C\|V_t^{\rm ref}\|_{L^2}\le C∥Vtref​∥L2​≤C supplies C+ekC+e_kC+ek​ in the local defect and the terminal bias Mh2C/mMh\sqrt2C/mMh2​C/m. The standard-Gaussian statement follows by taking with the radius p\sqrt pp​ proved from its marginal law. This same shared theorem applies to particle temperature 1/N1/N1/N, using the separately proved thermal radius (dθ+Ndx)/N\sqrt{(d_\theta+Nd_x)/N}(dθ​+Ndx​)/N​; the same recurrence applies.

The recurrence is applied on a single common probability space throughout the iteration. Its initial norm is the cost of the prescribed initial position coupling times the transformation coefficient. Position projection and the coupling-cost upper bound then convert the accumulated root-mean-square error into an output Wasserstein bound.
