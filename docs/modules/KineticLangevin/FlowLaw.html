Transport contraction for independent-input kinetic laws

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The position law is an explicit pushforward of an independent product of the initial position law and a fixed source law for common initial velocity and continuous forcing. Every initial coupling is extended by that same source. The proof computes the two output marginals, preserves the initial cost, derives a coupling estimate and takes the infimum. The kinetic pointwise contraction is separately proved from the integral equation and the potential hypotheses; it follows from the solution equation.

The proof works over every initial transport plan extended by an independent common source. Exact output marginals and cost control allow the plan cost to be replaced by the Wasserstein infimum.

01The independent random-map law and its two output marginals §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a measurable endpoint F(x,s)F(x,s)F(x,s), set TQμ=F#(μ⊗Q)\mathcal T_Q\mu=F_\#(\mu\otimes Q)TQ​μ=F#​(μ⊗Q). Given any coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, extend it to c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q. Projecting to (y,s)(y,s)(y,s) produces ν⊗Q\nu\otimes Qν⊗Q, because the second marginal of ccc is ν\nuν. Pushing further by FFF therefore gives TQν\mathcal T_Q\nuTQ​ν. One proves this with the product-map identity, the coupling's marginal equality, and pushforward composition. The corresponding first marginal follows from the first marginal of the initial coupling. Both output-law identities are derived from the independent product construction and are independent of which coupling was chosen.

Given an initial position coupling, add one independent common source containing velocity and forcing. Evaluating the same endpoint map at the two coupled positions gives a coupling of the two desired output laws. Product integration verifies each marginal without assuming the initial coupling is optimal.

02Lift pathwise contraction through L² and take all initial couplings §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the endpoint obeys ∥F(x,s)−F(y,s)∥≤q∥x−y∥\|F(x,s)-F(y,s)\|\le q\|x-y\|∥F(x,s)−F(y,s)∥≤q∥x−y∥ for each common source. On c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q, the two outputs form a measurable coupling of TQμ,TQν\mathcal T_Q\mu,\mathcal T_Q\nuTQ​μ,TQ​ν. Pointwise contraction passes to the extended L² norm. The initial difference depends only on ccc, so its norm on the product is exactly the original coupling cost. Thus W2(TQμ,TQν)≤q cost⁡(c)W_2(\mathcal T_Q\mu,\mathcal T_Q\nu)\le q\,\operatorname{cost}(c)W2​(TQ​μ,TQ​ν)≤qcost(c) for every ccc. The argument then applies the infimum theorem, using the independent product μ⊗ν\mu\otimes\nuμ⊗ν to show that the family of couplings is nonempty. No optimal-coupling attainment and no supplied output-law independence are required.

Square the pathwise Lipschitz inequality and integrate over the extended coupling. Taking square roots bounds the output transport cost by the same coefficient times the initial cost. Since the construction works for every initial coupling, taking the infimum gives the Wasserstein inequality.

03Retain probability and justify the real-valued distance §

Mathematical statement and proof

A measurable pushforward of μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q has total mass one when both inputs are probability measures; this follows from preservation of total mass under a measurable pushforward. The primary distance bound lives in the extended nonnegative reals, so infinite distances are preserved. If the initial Wasserstein distance is finite, the finite coefficient qqq makes its upper bound finite, and monotonicity of the finite real value gives the real-valued estimate. One proves that finiteness before converting the inequality, then rewrites the real value of the product. This avoids accidentally converting an infinite output distance to zero.

A measurable map sends a probability measure to a probability measure. Finiteness of the second-moment transport cost is established before interpreting the extended transport distance as a real number. This prevents real-valued inequalities from concealing an infinite-distance case.

04Measurability of the kinetic endpoint map §

Mathematical statement and proof

The compact forcing space receives its Borel measurable structure. The source s=(v,B)s=(v,B)s=(v,B) contains initial velocity and a continuous forcing path; the separate input is initial position xxx. The terminal position map evaluates the selected flow at phase (v,x)(v,x)(v,x) and takes its position coordinate. The coordinate rearrangement (x,(v,B))↦((v,x),B)(x,(v,B))\mapsto((v,x),B)(x,(v,B))↦((v,x),B) is continuous. Composing it with the proved joint endpoint continuity and projecting position gives continuity, hence measurability, of the map. Independence of source and position is defined later by the explicit product measure rather than encoded as an unspecified output-law premise.

Continuity of the solution with respect to initialization and forcing implies measurability of its endpoint. Thus the law being compared is the pushforward of an explicitly defined random map. Its probability property follows from the input probability measure, not from a formal density calculation.

05Derive pointwise contraction from integral solutions §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take two values of the selected kinetic flow with phases (v,x)(v,x)(v,x) and (v,y)(v,y)(v,y) and the same forcing path. Their specification gives the integral equations and equal initial velocities. The proved integral-synchronous theorem cancels their common forcing, derives the right-derivative difference equation, and applies the nonlinear potential energy estimate. Under differentiability, mmm-strong convexity, MMM-Lipschitz gradient and γ2≥m+M\gamma^2\ge m+Mγ2≥m+M, this yields ∥Ft(x,s)−Ft(y,s)∥≤2e−mt/γ∥x−y∥\|F_t(x,s)-F_t(y,s)\|\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}\|x-y\|∥Ft​(x,s)−Ft​(y,s)∥≤2​e−mt/γ∥x−y∥. The argument obtains the integral premises by rewriting the flow's initial-value specification, then simplifies the initial position and velocity projections. No differential equation or contraction conclusion is assumed of the chosen flow.

Both endpoints are evaluated with a common initial velocity and common additive path. Their difference satisfies the already proved deterministic kinetic equation. This ties the pointwise Lipschitz coefficient to curvature of the potential, rather than postulating an abstract contractive transition.

06Conclude the law-to-law kinetic theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

The kinetic position law is the explicit independent-product pushforward for this endpoint map. Its probability theorem follows from endpoint measurability. Substituting the derived coefficient q=2e−mt/γq=\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}q=2​e−mt/γ in the general random-map transport theorem gives

W2(μt,νt)≤2e−mt/γW2(μ0,ν0).W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le\sqrt2e^{-mt/\gamma}W_2(\mu_0,\nu_0).W2​(μt​,νt​)≤2​e−mt/γW2​(μ0​,ν0​).

The source law may be any fixed probability law of initial velocity and continuous forcing, independent of initial position. The statement therefore applies to any such stochastic realization; a stationary Gibbs law is unnecessary for comparing two evolving laws. The extended-valued theorem retains infinite initial costs, and the real-valued theorem explicitly requires finite initial Wasserstein distance. The full all-friction spectral prefactor and a specified Brownian-source identification are separate refinements beyond this simple-friction law theorem.

The conclusion concerns the independently defined output laws for each initial distribution. Dependence on the auxiliary initial coupling has disappeared through the marginal identities. The coupling argument therefore proves a property of the kinetic transition family.

07The full shifted prefactor and exponent pass to output laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

The general contraction theorem for integral solutions uses the centered gradient sector estimate, so it requires differentiability, strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient without adding Hessian continuity. For d=γ−2a>0d=\gamma-2a>0d=γ−2a>0, its coefficient is

qa(t)=2((γ−a)2+a2)γ−2aexp⁡ ⁣(max⁡{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}γ−2at).q_a(t)=\frac{\sqrt{2((\gamma-a)^2+a^2)}}{\gamma-2a} \exp\!\left(\frac{\max\{a^2-m,M-(\gamma-a)^2\}}{\gamma-2a}t\right).qa​(t)=γ−2a2((γ−a)2+a2)​​exp(γ−2amax{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}​t).

The proof instantiates that theorem for integral solutions with the same two selected flows and their proved specifications. The coefficient is nonnegative, so it is an R≥0\mathbb R_{\ge0}R≥0​ scalar in the already proved random-map transport theorem. This yields W2(μt,νt)≤qa(t)W2(μ0,ν0)W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le q_a(t)W_2(\mu_0,\nu_0)W2​(μt​,νt​)≤qa​(t)W2​(μ0​,ν0​), with the infimum taken over the initial couplings and the output marginals identified as above. The real-valued version uses the same finite-initial-cost conversion. The theorem holds for every independent continuous-forcing source law; identifying the forcing with Brownian motion and verifying its independence remain separate assertions, and no Gibbs invariant law is assumed.

The positivity of the transformed-coordinate denominator makes the full prefactor nonnegative. That coefficient can therefore be pulled through the infimum over coupling costs. This yields the entire shifted bound, including its conditioning factor, rather than only the simple zero-shift specialization.
