Stitched exact segments are the actual global kinetic solution

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A reference defined one interval at a time must be identified with the actual continuous kinetic process whose stationarity is proved separately. The bridge here uses the true shifted-input equation and uniqueness. It does not assume a Markov transition formula or independence of arbitrarily chosen reference segments.

01Identify a selected local solution with a shifted global path §

Mathematical statement and proof

Start with an actual continuous global solution YYY, and suppose the local input is the actual increment Wt=Ba+t−BaW_t=B_{a+t}-B_aWt​=Ba+t​−Ba​. The previously proved time-shift lemma shows Ya+tY_{a+t}Ya+t​ satisfies the same kinetic integral equation on the local horizon. The selected local solution starts at YaY_aYa​. Lean checks the two initial conditions, the shared additive input, and continuity, then invokes the proved uniqueness theorem for the globally Lipschitz kinetic drift. Their equality follows at every time in the local interval.

02Identify every stitched grid point with the global solution §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference chain starts at Y0Y_0Y0​. Inductively assume its state at step nnn equals YnhY_{nh}Ynh​. The next selected segment begins at that same point, so the local-global uniqueness result identifies its endpoint with Ynh+hY_{nh+h}Ynh+h​. Lean performs induction over the actual chain recursion, checks the finite-horizon inequalities using nonnegative step size, and simplifies the real cast of n+1n+1n+1. The result is the exact identity Rn=YnhR_n=Y_{nh}Rn​=Ynh​, including zero step size.

03Identify all intermediate times within a reference segment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Once the starting grid point is identified, the same uniqueness result proves Zn(t)=Ynh+tZ_n(t)=Y_{nh+t}Zn​(t)=Ynh+t​ throughout the whole segment. This lets a stationary global phase law supply both the stationary reference position at grid points and the Gaussian reference velocity at intermediate times required by the KLMC local-error proof. No stationary law is assumed for a newly invented process: the stitched reference is proved to be the actual global solution.
