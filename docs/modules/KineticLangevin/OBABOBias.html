The global OBABO error bound under independent Gaussian noises

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This is the numerical error theorem for OBABO under the original strong-convexity and smoothness assumptions. The Markov laws converge toward the specified Gaussian-times-Gibbs target up to an explicit O(hd)O(h\sqrt d)O(hd​) bias. All stochastic reference laws, Hamiltonian invariance, moment bounds, common-noise local errors, weighted-coordinate law identities, and the initial Wasserstein infimum are proved internally.

The numerical transition is compared with an exact invariant OOO–Hamiltonian–OOO transition in the same weighted coordinates. Local cost and contraction combine into the complete global transport-bias theorem.

01The exact reference in the same contracting coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference takes the first thermostat output, evolves it by the Hamiltonian integral flow, and applies the same final thermostat input. Conjugate this exact map by the explicit invertible weighted transform. Its continuity follows from the verified inverse, the exact Hamiltonian endpoint continuity, the two thermostat formulas, and the final transform. The local Borel structure is made explicit for the Euclidean weighted phase space. The numerical and reference maps now share both the same innovations and the same contracting geometry.

The exact OOO–Hamiltonian–OOO transition is conjugated by the same weighted transformation as the numerical OBABO transition. Its invariant law is the transformed Gibbs target. This places contraction and local kernel discrepancy in one common transport geometry.

02The specified probability source cost and exact reference stationarity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source is precisely the constructed phase target times a fresh pair of independent standard Gaussian vectors. The specified probability source theorem supplies L2L^{2}L2 coordinate representatives and proves that the first thermostat output has the target law. The constructed Hamiltonian L2L^{2}L2 path therefore has the derived Gaussian velocity and Gibbs score moments. Its almost-everywhere identification with the vector flow keeps the whole local coupling intact. The integrated local-cost theorem yields the integrated cost bhb_hbh​, with no analytic error premise.

Exact OOO–Hamiltonian–OOO invariance on the original source is already proved. The general Markov conjugacy identity transports this invariance to the weighted target. For the weighted local cost, transforming the initial state commutes with adjoining the independent Gaussian pair. The L2L^{2}L2 pushforward formula, inverse coordinate identity, and invariance of the norm under reversing a difference identify its cost with the previously bounded constructed cost. Thus the exact target invariance and the local discrepancy needed by the global transport theorem are both established for the explicit maps.

The source consists of the stationary phase and two independent thermostat noises. The verified exact transition preserves its target law. Coupling it with the numerical map using those same noises gives the finite local root-mean-square cost used by the perturbation theorem.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.33.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6.

03The Markov bound and the accumulated bias §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let r=1−mh/(3γ)r=\sqrt{1-mh/(3\gamma)}r=1−mh/(3γ)​. Strong convexity, gradient Lipschitzness, γ2≥4M\gamma^2\ge4Mγ2≥4M, and 33γ3h≤m33\gamma^3h\le m33γ3h≤m give the numerical-map contraction. The preceding proofs give exact reference invariance and local cost bhb_hbh​. The all-couplings Markov perturbation theorem therefore applies to the independent-Gaussian laws. The coordinate-law identity proves these are exactly the original defined sampler laws pushed into the weighted geometry. The contraction-gap and local-error calculations yield

W2,E(μn,π)≤rnW2,E(μ0,π)+6γmhd(4M+Mh2).W_{2,E}(\mu_n,\pi)\le r^nW_{2,E}(\mu_0,\pi)+\frac{6\gamma}{m}h\sqrt d\left(4\sqrt M+\frac{Mh}{2}\right).W2,E​(μn​,π)≤rnW2,E​(μ0​,π)+m6γ​hd​(4M​+2Mh​).

The only initial transport premise is finite weighted Wasserstein distance, as required to use a real-valued distance. The thermostat parameters satisfy their variance identity δ2+σ2=1\delta^2+\sigma^2=1δ2+σ2=1, with δ=e−γh/2\delta=e^{-\gamma h/2}δ=e−γh/2; choosing σ=1−δ2\sigma=\sqrt{1-\delta^2}σ=1−δ2​ verifies it directly. The source step-size upper bound makes the displayed O(hd)O(h\sqrt d)O(hd​) coefficient uniform in hhh. There is no externally supplied stationary process, Gaussian reference marginal, local coupling inequality, or convergence theorem among the hypotheses.

The one-step transport inequality has the form Dn+1≤rDn+δhD_{n+1}\le rD_n+\delta_hDn+1​≤rDn​+δh​. Summing its geometric series gives Dn≤rnD0+δh/(1−r)D_n\le r^nD_0+\delta_h/(1-r)Dn​≤rnD0​+δh​/(1−r). The scalar gap estimate converts this into the explicit dimension-dependent global bias.

04Expose finite output distance for projection and mean-square use §

Mathematical statement and proof

The same fully constructed exact comparator and integrated local cost establish that every weighted OBABO output has finite Wasserstein distance to the target whenever its initial weighted distance is finite. This theorem states the extended-real induction before conversion to the real-valued distance. It allows subsequent coordinate projections and RMS bounds to use the result without any possibility of infinity being hidden by a real-valued distance.

A finite initial discrepancy and the finite local cost imply finite distance at every numerical step. This is proved before the distance is used as a real-valued bound. Subsequent coordinate projection and root-mean-square consequences therefore apply to finite transport quantities.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3.
