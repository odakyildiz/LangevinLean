Gibbs invariance of the exact split transition

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

To measure OBABO discretization bias, keep both thermostat noises exactly the same and replace its Verlet middle portion by the proved exact Hamiltonian flow. This comparison step preserves the Gibbs target. Its invariance and its common-source L² random variables are constructed here, so the numerical error bound can compare random variables on the same probability space.

Replacing only the central Verlet stage by exact Hamiltonian evolution creates a target-preserving comparison transition. Retaining the same two thermostat noises yields the natural coupling for its local numerical discrepancy.

01The exact comparison map with the same two noises §

Mathematical statement and proof

Starting from z=(v,x)z=(v,x)z=(v,x), apply Oδ,σ,ξO_{\delta,\sigma,\xi}Oδ,σ,ξ​, evolve the resulting phase under the selected Hamiltonian flow for time hhh, and then apply Oδ,σ,ξ′O_{\delta,\sigma,\xi\prime}Oδ,σ,ξ′​. The map uses the same first and second innovations as the OBABO update. Only the deterministic Hamiltonian evolution changes. Continuity of the selected Hamiltonian endpoint and of the two affine thermostat maps proves joint continuity in the input phase and the paired noise.

The comparison replaces the central Verlet stage by exact Hamiltonian evolution while retaining the same thermostat half steps. Since all three exact stages preserve the Gibbs target, their composition is a natural target-preserving comparator for the numerical OBABO map.

02Preserve the first updated phase and its independence of fresh noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source law is explicitly π⊗(N(0,I)⊗N(0,I))\pi\otimes(\mathcal N(0,I)\otimes\mathcal N(0,I))π⊗(N(0,I)⊗N(0,I)), where π\piπ is the normalized Gaussian-times-Gibbs phase target. Reassociate it as (π⊗N(0,I))⊗N(0,I)(\pi\otimes\mathcal N(0,I))\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)(π⊗N(0,I))⊗N(0,I). The first thermostat preserves π\piπ because δ2+σ2=1\delta^2+\sigma^2=1δ2+σ2=1, while the untouched second Gaussian remains independent. This follows using the measure-preserving product associator and the product pushforward identity. The conclusion is the joint law of the updated phase and the second fresh noise, not just a marginal invariance statement.

After the first thermostat update the phase still has the Gibbs law. The second thermostat innovation remains independent because it was chosen independently of the initial phase and the first innovation. This justifies applying its invariance property after the Hamiltonian stage.

03Compose the three proved invariant transitions §

Mathematical statement and proof

After the first O step the joint law is π⊗N(0,I)\pi\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)π⊗N(0,I). The exact Hamiltonian map preserves π\piπ under the original strong-convexity and Lipschitz-gradient assumptions; applying it only to the phase therefore leaves the same joint law with the second noise. The last O step preserves π\piπ again. Three explicit pushforward composition identities prove that the complete O–Hamiltonian–O random map preserves the normalized target. No invariance hypothesis is supplied to this theorem.

Push the target successively through the first thermostat, Hamiltonian flow, and final thermostat. Each pushforward equals the target, so their composition does as well. This proves invariance for the comparison transition, including both noises.

04The L² variables used by the local comparison §

Mathematical statement and proof

Pull the initial velocity and position through the first product projection, and the two Gaussian innovations through their separate projections. The target projection moment theorems and Gaussian finite moments prove all four coordinates belong to L². Their L² representatives agree almost everywhere with the original coordinates. The proved pointwise representation of a lifted thermostat identifies the first updated L² phase with the first O map. Projecting the established phase/fresh-noise joint law now gives its exact target marginal. These random variables retain the common noise coupling and have the stationary laws needed by the local Hamiltonian–Verlet error theorem.

The initial phase and both innovations are represented jointly on their product space. The exact Hamiltonian comparator is square integrable under its stationary law. These variables provide the common-noise coupling needed to integrate the Verlet local state error.
