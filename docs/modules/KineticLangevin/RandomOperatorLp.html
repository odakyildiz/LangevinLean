The action of bounded random operators on L2L^2L2

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A Hessian evaluated at a random position is a random linear operator. To use such an operator inside time integrals of square-integrable random vectors, this section constructs its action on L2L^2L2. Let (Ω,P)(\Omega,\mathbb P)(Ω,P) be a measure space, EEE a real normed space, and A:Ω→L(E)A:\Omega\to\mathcal L(E)A:Ω→L(E) a strongly measurable (up to null sets) random operator with ∥A(ω)∥≤M\|A(\omega)\|\le M∥A(ω)∥≤M for almost every ω\omegaω. Then

A:L2(P;E)→L2(P;E),(AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω),\mathcal A:L^2(\mathbb P;E)\to L^2(\mathbb P;E),\qquad(\mathcal AX)(\omega)=A(\omega)X(\omega),A:L2(P;E)→L2(P;E),(AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω),

is a bounded linear operator with ∥AX∥L2≤M∥X∥L2\|\mathcal AX\|_{L^2}\le M\|X\|_{L^2}∥AX∥L2​≤M∥X∥L2​ and, if M≥0M\ge0M≥0, ∥A∥≤M\|\mathcal A\|\le M∥A∥≤M. The operator AAA may depend on XXX in an arbitrary way: no independence is involved, because the construction acts sample by sample. In the second-order analysis A=∇2f(L0)A=\nabla^2f(L_0)A=∇2f(L0​) or a difference of two such Hessians.

01Construction by Hölder multiplication §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let A:Ω→L(E)A:\Omega\to\mathcal L(E)A:Ω→L(E) be almost everywhere strongly measurable with ∥A(ω)∥≤M\|A(\omega)\|\le M∥A(ω)∥≤M for almost every ω\omegaω. There is a bounded linear operator A\mathcal AA on L2(P;E)L^2(\mathbb P;E)L2(P;E) such that AX\mathcal AXAX is the class of ω↦A(ω)X(ω)\omega\mapsto A(\omega)X(\omega)ω↦A(ω)X(ω) (the representative identity is the next step).

Proof. Recall that L∞(P;F)L^\infty(\mathbb P;F)L∞(P;F) consists of the classes of almost everywhere strongly measurable functions that are essentially bounded. By hypothesis AAA is such a function with values in F=L(E)F=\mathcal L(E)F=L(E), with essential bound MMM, so A∈L∞(P;L(E))A\in L^\infty(\mathbb P;\mathcal L(E))A∈L∞(P;L(E)). The evaluation map L(E)×E→E\mathcal L(E)\times E\to EL(E)×E→E, (T,v)↦Tv(T,v)\mapsto Tv(T,v)↦Tv, is bilinear with ∥Tv∥≤∥T∥ ∥v∥\|Tv\|\le\|T\|\,\|v\|∥Tv∥≤∥T∥∥v∥. Hölder's inequality for such a bilinear map, with exponents 12=1∞+12\frac12=\frac1\infty+\frac1221​=∞1​+21​, states that for A∈L∞A\in L^\inftyA∈L∞ and X∈L2X\in L^2X∈L2 the function ω↦A(ω)X(ω)\omega\mapsto A(\omega)X(\omega)ω↦A(ω)X(ω) is in L2L^2L2, with

∥AX∥L2≤∥A∥L∞ ∥X∥L2,\|AX\|_{L^2}\le\|A\|_{L^\infty}\,\|X\|_{L^2},∥AX∥L2​≤∥A∥L∞​∥X∥L2​,

and that this product is bilinear and jointly continuous on L∞×L2L^\infty\times L^2L∞×L2. Fixing the first argument AAA gives a bounded linear map A\mathcal AA on L2(P;E)L^2(\mathbb P;E)L2(P;E). □\square□

02Identification of representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For every X∈L2(P;E)X\in L^2(\mathbb P;E)X∈L2(P;E), (AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω)(\mathcal AX)(\omega)=A(\omega)X(\omega)(AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω) for P\mathbb PP-almost every ω\omegaω.

Proof. The Hölder product of two classes is represented almost everywhere by the pointwise product of representatives, and the L∞L^\inftyL∞ class of AAA is represented by AAA itself almost everywhere. On the intersection of these two sets of full measure, (AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω)(\mathcal AX)(\omega)=A(\omega)X(\omega)(AX)(ω)=A(ω)X(ω). □\square□

The identity keeps the original probability space: A(ω)A(\omega)A(ω) and X(ω)X(\omega)X(ω) are evaluated at the same sample point, so pointwise inequalities such as the Taylor remainder bound or the Gaussian truncation can be applied to AX\mathcal AXAX.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The proof intersects, with filter_upwards, the representative identity coeFn_holder of the Hölder product and the representative identity coeFn_toLp of the L∞L^\inftyL∞ class of AAA; simpa with ContinuousLinearMap.id_apply then closes the goal.

03The deterministic operator bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. (a) For every X∈L2(P;E)X\in L^2(\mathbb P;E)X∈L2(P;E), ∥AX∥L2≤M∥X∥L2\|\mathcal AX\|_{L^2}\le M\|X\|_{L^2}∥AX∥L2​≤M∥X∥L2​. (b) If moreover M≥0M\ge0M≥0, the operator norm satisfies ∥A∥≤M\|\mathcal A\|\le M∥A∥≤M.

Proof. (a) By the previous step and the definition of the operator norm, for almost every ω\omegaω,

∥(AX)(ω)∥=∥A(ω)X(ω)∥≤∥A(ω)∥ ∥X(ω)∥≤M ∥X(ω)∥.\|(\mathcal AX)(\omega)\|=\|A(\omega)X(\omega)\|\le\|A(\omega)\|\,\|X(\omega)\|\le M\,\|X(\omega)\|.∥(AX)(ω)∥=∥A(ω)X(ω)∥≤∥A(ω)∥∥X(ω)∥≤M∥X(ω)∥.

If M≥0M\ge0M≥0, squaring and integrating gives ∥AX∥L22≤M2∥X∥L22\|\mathcal AX\|_{L^2}^2\le M^2\|X\|_{L^2}^2∥AX∥L22​≤M2∥X∥L22​, and taking square roots gives (a). If M<0M<0M<0, the hypothesis ∥A(ω)∥≤M\|A(\omega)\|\le M∥A(ω)∥≤M can only hold almost everywhere when P\mathbb PP is the zero measure; then L2(P;E)={0}L^2(\mathbb P;E)=\{0\}L2(P;E)={0} and (a) reads 0≤00\le00≤0.

(b) The operator norm of a bounded linear map is the smallest C≥0C\ge0C≥0 with ∥AX∥≤C∥X∥\|\mathcal AX\|\le C\|X\|∥AX∥≤C∥X∥ for every XXX; by (a), C=MC=MC=M is such a constant. □\square□

The bound passes from the pointwise operator norm to the L2L^2L2 operator norm without any assumption on the joint law of AAA and XXX.
