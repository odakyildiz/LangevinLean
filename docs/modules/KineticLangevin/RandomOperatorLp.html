The action of bounded random Hessians on L2L^{2}L2

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A Hessian evaluated at a random initial position is itself random. To use it inside Bochner time integrals, we construct its continuous linear action on square-integrable random vectors. The operator may depend on every coupled variable; independence is unnecessary.

The Hessian frozen at a random position defines a bounded action on square-integrable vectors. Its pointwise representative and unchanged deterministic norm bound support the corrected-step identities.

Statement and proof

01The action by Hölder multiplication §

Mathematical statement and proof

A measurable operator field A with a deterministic bound ∥A(ω)∥≤M\|A(\omega )\|\le M∥A(ω)∥≤M belongs to L∞L^\inftyL∞. Evaluation is a continuous bilinear map from operators and vectors to vectors. Hölder’s inequality therefore sends the L∞L^\inftyL∞ operator and an L2L^{2}L2 vector to an L2L^{2}L2 vector, with 1/∞+1/2=1/21/\infty+1/2=1/21/∞+1/2=1/2. Fixing the operator gives a bounded linear operator on the Hilbert space of square-integrable vectors.

A bounded measurable random operator applied to a square-integrable random vector is square integrable. Pointwise operator norm control gives the integrable bound. The construction therefore defines a bounded linear action on the Hilbert space of random vectors.

02Identify its representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Hölder construction supplies an almost-everywhere representative identity. The representative of the L∞L^\inftyL∞ equivalence class is A(ω)A(\omega )A(ω) almost everywhere, so the constructed L2L^{2}L2 output is exactly A(ω)X(ω)A(\omega )X(\omega )A(ω)X(ω). This identity preserves the original probability-space coupling and permits pointwise Taylor and truncation inequalities to be applied to the output.

The action’s representative is the pointwise operator applied to the vector representative almost everywhere. This identity is needed to insert a Hessian frozen at a random position into the numerical formula. The construction does not replace that Hessian by its expectation.

03The deterministic operator bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Pointwise, ∥A(ω)X(ω)∥≤∥A(ω)∥∥X(ω)∥≤M∥X(ω)∥\|A(\omega )X(\omega )\|\le \|A(\omega )\|\|X(\omega )\|\le M\|X(\omega )\|∥A(ω)X(ω)∥≤∥A(ω)∥∥X(ω)∥≤M∥X(ω)∥. Integrating this norm inequality gives ∥AX∥2≤M∥X∥2\|AX\|_{2}\le M\|X\|_{2}∥AX∥2​≤M∥X∥2​. Taking the supremum over vectors proves the operator norm of the constructed map is at most MMM. These estimates justify commuting the random Hessian through time integrals and bounding the integrated force correction.

If the operator norm is bounded by MMM almost surely, squaring and integrating gives ∥AX∥L2≤M∥X∥L2\|AX\|_{L^2}\le M\|X\|_{L^2}∥AX∥L2​≤M∥X∥L2​. The deterministic bound therefore passes unchanged to second mean, even when the operator and vector are dependent.
