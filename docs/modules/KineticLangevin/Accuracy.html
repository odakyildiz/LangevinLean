A step length and iteration count for the KLMC error tolerance

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The convergence theorem becomes an explicit choice of parameters: choose a positive step, an integer iteration count, and obtain a error at most ε\varepsilonε. The result concerns the constructed sampler. Neither a recurrence nor a local-error estimate is left as a premise.

The first-order sampling theorem is converted into a positive-step, integer-iteration prescription. Explicit bias allocation and a rounded logarithmic transient bound handle finite initialization and requested accuracy.

01Verify that the geometric factor is admissible §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set α=3mh/(4γ)\alpha=3mh/(4\gamma)α=3mh/(4γ). The curvature, friction and step assumptions imply 0<α≤10<\alpha\le10<α≤1. The proof reuses the exponential-coefficient estimate: e−mh/γ+(3/4)Mh2≤1−αe^{-mh/\gamma}+(3/4)Mh^2\le1-\alphae−mh/γ+(3/4)Mh2≤1−α. Its left side is positive, so the right side is nonnegative. This justifies every later geometric and logarithmic estimate.

A positive contraction rate ensures the logarithmic threshold is meaningful. The upper bound on the rate ensures the geometric base is nonnegative. Both properties are consequences of the friction and step restrictions, and are needed before solving the error inequality for the iteration number.

02Apply the scalar error allocation to the law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The KLMC theorem gives W2(νn,π)≤2(1−α)nD+bW_2(\nu_n,\pi)\le\sqrt2(1-\alpha)^nD+bW2​(νn​,π)≤2​(1−α)nD+b. The scalar error bound allocates b≤0.94εb\le0.94\varepsilonb≤0.94ε and (1−α)nD≤ε/24(1-\alpha)^nD\le\varepsilon/24(1−α)nD≤ε/24. The sum is at most ε\varepsilonε. The threshold is the natural ceiling of max⁡{0,log⁡(24D/ε)/α}\max\{0,\log(24D/\varepsilon)/\alpha\}max{0,log(24D/ε)/α}; if D=0D=0D=0, no initial-error iterations are needed. The finite-transport hypothesis prevents the real-valued Wasserstein convention from hiding an infinite distance.

When the initial discrepancy is already below its transient allocation, the maximum-logarithm prescription selects zero iterations. Otherwise the exponential upper bound for the geometric factor is below the allocated error after the selected ceiling count. The bias allocation then completes the total-error bound.

03Choose the paper step and round the count §

Mathematical statement and proof

For positive dimension choose h=min⁡{m/(4γM), 0.94εm/(M2p)}h=\min\{m/(4\gamma M),\,0.94\varepsilon m/(M\sqrt{2p})\}h=min{m/(4γM),0.94εm/(M2p​)}. Both terms are positive. The first enforces stability and the second enforces the discretization-error bound by multiplication through positive denominators. Take the corresponding nonnegative step length, use the rounded logarithmic count, and apply the accuracy theorem. At γ=m+M\gamma=\sqrt{m+M}γ=m+M​, this is precisely the manuscript’s minimum-step prescription. The explicit reciprocal dependence displays the p/ε\sqrt p/\varepsilonp​/ε iteration scaling up to the logarithm.

The chosen step is the minimum of a stability limit and an accuracy limit. Taking that minimum guarantees both requirements simultaneously. Its reciprocal makes explicit the tradeoff between small requested error, dimension-dependent stationary velocity fluctuations, and the curvature-dependent contraction rate.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
