The genuine quadratic Taylor remainder

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The second-order integrator linearizes the force at its initial position. Its error requires a quadratic estimate for this actual linearization, obtained from the explicitly integrated Hessian along a segment. The derivative and Hessian Lipschitz assumptions are ordinary analytic hypotheses on the force.

01Integrate Hessian variation along the segment §

Mathematical statement and proof

The averaged Hessian is the Bochner integral of H(y+t(x−y)), with t between zero and one. Subtracting H(y) inside the integral gives an integrand with norm at most M₂t‖x−y‖. The norm of an integral is at most the integral of this nonnegative envelope, whose value is M₂‖x−y‖/2. Lean checks continuity of the integrand, moves subtraction through the integral, applies the Lipschitz inequality on the segment, and evaluates the elementary integral of t.

02Apply the averaged operator to the displacement §

Mathematical statement and proof

The fundamental theorem of calculus identifies g(x)−g(y) with the averaged Hessian applied to x−y. Thus the true Taylor remainder equals (H̄−H(y))(x−y). The operator norm inequality bounds this by ‖H̄−H(y)‖‖x−y‖, yielding M₂‖x−y‖²/2. This is an actual remainder estimate; the Hessian is never replaced by an assumed scalar or a fictitious common mean-value point.
