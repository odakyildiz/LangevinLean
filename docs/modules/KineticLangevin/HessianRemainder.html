The quadratic force remainder from a Lipschitz Hessian

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

In the application H=∇2fH=\nabla^2fH=∇2f is the Hessian of the potential and g=∇fg=\nabla fg=∇f the force. For x,y∈Ex,y\in Ex,y∈E, the averaged Hessian on the segment from yyy to xxx is the operator

Hˉ(x,y)=∫01H(y+t(x−y)) dt,\bar H(x,y)=\int_0^1H\bigl(y+t(x-y)\bigr)\,dt,Hˉ(x,y)=∫01​H(y+t(x−y))dt,

a Bochner integral in the Banach space L(E)\mathcal L(E)L(E). This section proves ∥Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)∥≤M22∥x−y∥\|\bar H(x,y)-H(y)\|\le\frac{M_2}{2}\|x-y\|∥Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)∥≤2M2​​∥x−y∥ and, when H(x)H(x)H(x) is the derivative of ggg at every xxx, the Taylor remainder bound

01Variation of the Hessian along the segment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let H:E→L(E)H:E\to\mathcal L(E)H:E→L(E) be M2M_2M2​-Lipschitz, ∥H(u)−H(w)∥≤M2∥u−w∥\|H(u)-H(w)\|\le M_2\|u-w\|∥H(u)−H(w)∥≤M2​∥u−w∥ for all u,wu,wu,w, with M2≥0M_2\ge0M2​≥0. Then for all x,y∈Ex,y\in Ex,y∈E,

∥Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)∥≤M22 ∥x−y∥.\bigl\|\bar H(x,y)-H(y)\bigr\|\le\frac{M_2}{2}\,\|x-y\|.​Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)​≤2M2​​∥x−y∥.

Proof. The map t↦H(y+t(x−y))t\mapsto H(y+t(x-y))t↦H(y+t(x−y)) is continuous, as the composition of the Lipschitz map HHH with a continuous path; hence it is Bochner integrable on [0,1][0,1][0,1]. The integral of the constant H(y)H(y)H(y) over [0,1][0,1][0,1] is H(y)H(y)H(y), and the integral is linear, so

Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)=∫01(H(y+t(x−y))−H(y)) dt.\bar H(x,y)-H(y)=\int_0^1\Bigl(H\bigl(y+t(x-y)\bigr)-H(y)\Bigr)\,dt.Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)=∫01​(H(y+t(x−y))−H(y))dt.

For t∈(0,1]t\in(0,1]t∈(0,1] the Lipschitz condition gives

∥H(y+t(x−y))−H(y)∥≤M2 ∥t(x−y)∥=M2 t ∥x−y∥,\bigl\|H(y+t(x-y))-H(y)\bigr\|\le M_2\,\|t(x-y)\|=M_2\,t\,\|x-y\|,​H(y+t(x−y))−H(y)​≤M2​∥t(x−y)∥=M2​t∥x−y∥,

using t≥0t\ge0t≥0. The norm of a Bochner integral is at most the integral of any integrable bound on the norm of the integrand, so

∥Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)∥≤∫01M2 t ∥x−y∥ dt=M2∥x−y∥∫01t dt=M22∥x−y∥.□\bigl\|\bar H(x,y)-H(y)\bigr\|\le\int_0^1M_2\,t\,\|x-y\|\,dt=M_2\|x-y\|\int_0^1t\,dt=\frac{M_2}{2}\|x-y\|.\qquad\square​Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)​≤∫01​M2​t∥x−y∥dt=M2​∥x−y∥∫01​tdt=2M2​​∥x−y∥.□

02The first-order Taylor remainder of the force §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let g:E→Eg:E\to Eg:E→E be Fréchet differentiable at every point with derivative Dg(u)=H(u)Dg(u)=H(u)Dg(u)=H(u), and let HHH be M2M_2M2​-Lipschitz. Then for all x,y∈Ex,y\in Ex,y∈E,

∥g(x)−g(y)−H(y)(x−y)∥≤M22 ∥x−y∥2.\bigl\|g(x)-g(y)-H(y)(x-y)\bigr\|\le\frac{M_2}{2}\,\|x-y\|^2.​g(x)−g(y)−H(y)(x−y)​≤2M2​​∥x−y∥2.

Proof. Integral form of the increment. The path c(t)=y+t(x−y)c(t)=y+t(x-y)c(t)=y+t(x−y) has derivative x−yx-yx−y, so by the chain rule ddtg(c(t))=H(c(t))(x−y)\frac{d}{dt}g(c(t))=H(c(t))(x-y)dtd​g(c(t))=H(c(t))(x−y), which is continuous in ttt because HHH is continuous. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

g(x)−g(y)=∫01H(c(t))(x−y) dt=Hˉ(x,y)(x−y).g(x)-g(y)=\int_0^1H\bigl(c(t)\bigr)(x-y)\,dt=\bar H(x,y)(x-y).g(x)−g(y)=∫01​H(c(t))(x−y)dt=Hˉ(x,y)(x−y).

The second equality holds because evaluation at the fixed vector x−yx-yx−y is a bounded linear map on L(E)\mathcal L(E)L(E), and bounded linear maps commute with Bochner integrals. This identity was proved in the earlier section on the averaged Hessian.

Estimate. Consequently g(x)−g(y)−H(y)(x−y)=(Hˉ(x,y)−H(y))(x−y)g(x)-g(y)-H(y)(x-y)=\bigl(\bar H(x,y)-H(y)\bigr)(x-y)g(x)−g(y)−H(y)(x−y)=(Hˉ(x,y)−H(y))(x−y). By the definition of the operator norm and the previous step,

∥(Hˉ(x,y)−H(y))(x−y)∥≤∥Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)∥ ∥x−y∥≤M22∥x−y∥⋅∥x−y∥.□\bigl\|(\bar H(x,y)-H(y))(x-y)\bigr\|\le\bigl\|\bar H(x,y)-H(y)\bigr\|\,\|x-y\|\le\frac{M_2}{2}\|x-y\|\cdot\|x-y\|.\qquad\square​(Hˉ(x,y)−H(y))(x−y)​≤​Hˉ(x,y)−H(y)​∥x−y∥≤2M2​​∥x−y∥⋅∥x−y∥.□

The operator Hˉ(x,y)\bar H(x,y)Hˉ(x,y) is an average of Hessians along the whole segment; it is not the Hessian at a single intermediate point, which would not exist in general for vector-valued maps.
