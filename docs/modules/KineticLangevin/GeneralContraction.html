Arbitrary-friction contraction directly from the potential

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The paper's full shifted contraction can be derived from differentiability, strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient alone. This proof centers velocity at v+γx/2v+\gamma x/2v+γx/2, uses the strong-smooth sector inequality, and completes a scalar square. It removes any need to assume that the Hessian is continuous when constructing a mean Hessian, while recovering the exact maximum of the two endpoint curvature exponents.

Centering the force around a scalar curvature isolates a residual controlled by the strong-smooth sector bound. Completing a square recovers the full shifted exponent without imposing continuous Hessians.

01Center the phase energy and its derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

The concrete force-difference derivative is 2⟨v+(γ−a)x,−av−F⟩+2⟨−v−ax,(γ−a)v+F⟩2\langle v+(\gamma-a)x,-av-F\rangle+2\langle-v-ax,(\gamma-a)v+F\rangle2⟨v+(γ−a)x,−av−F⟩+2⟨−v−ax,(γ−a)v+F⟩. Put y=v+γx/2y=v+\gamma x/2y=v+γx/2, d=γ−2ad=\gamma-2ad=γ−2a, and c=((γ−a)2+a2)/2c=((\gamma-a)^2+a^2)/2c=((γ−a)2+a2)/2. Expanding the inner products gives the two exact identities

E=2∥y∥2+12d2∥x∥2,E′=−γE+4⟨y,cx−F⟩.\mathcal E=2\|y\|^2+\tfrac12d^2\|x\|^2,\qquad \mathcal E'=-\gamma\mathcal E+4\langle y,cx-F\rangle.E=2∥y∥2+21​d2∥x∥2,E′=−γE+4⟨y,cx−F⟩.

The proof rewrites squared norms as self-inner-products, distributes inner products over sums, differences and scalar multiplication, uses symmetry, and finishes with expansion of the scalar polynomials. These identities expose a scalar residual rather than a matrix diagonalization.

Centering the curvature interval at a scalar value separates a uniformly controlled residual force from the linear central drift. The transformed energy can then be estimated by completing a square in this residual. This replaces the need to select an averaged Hessian explicitly.

02Bound the residual around an arbitrary scalar curvature §

Mathematical statement and proof

Strong-smooth interpolation places FFF in the ball centered at μx\mu xμx, where μ=(m+M)/2\mu=(m+M)/2μ=(m+M)/2, with radius R∥x∥R\|x\|R∥x∥, where R=(M−m)/2R=(M-m)/2R=(M−m)/2. Decompose cx−F=(c−μ)x−(F−μx)cx-F=(c-\mu)x-(F-\mu x)cx−F=(c−μ)x−(F−μx). The triangle inequality gives ∥cx−F∥≤(∣c−μ∣+R)∥x∥\|cx-F\|\le(|c-\mu|+R)\|x\|∥cx−F∥≤(∣c−μ∣+R)∥x∥. The coefficient is exactly max⁡{c−m,M−c}\max\{c-m,M-c\}max{c−m,M−c}: The proof splits on c≥μc\ge\muc≥μ, simplifies the absolute value and the corresponding maximum, and checks the remaining arithmetic. No gradient or Hessian operator is invented in this argument.

The strong-smooth sector inequality bounds the residual by the half-width of the curvature interval. Its center and radius have different roles: the center determines the chosen linear comparison, whereas the radius controls the nonlinear deviation from it.

03Complete the centered square to obtain the exact exponent §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let K=max⁡{c−m,M−c}≥0K=\max\{c-m,M-c\}\ge0K=max{c−m,M−c}≥0. Cauchy–Schwarz and the residual bound give ⟨y,cx−F⟩≤K∥y∥∥x∥\langle y,cx-F\rangle\le K\|y\|\|x\|⟨y,cx−F⟩≤K∥y∥∥x∥. The nonnegative square (2∥y∥−d∥x∥)2(2\|y\|-d\|x\|)^2(2∥y∥−d∥x∥)2 implies 4d∥y∥∥x∥≤2E4d\|y\|\|x\|\le2\mathcal E4d∥y∥∥x∥≤2E. Multiplying by KKK and inserting the exact energy-derivative identity yields dE′≤(−γd+2K)Ed\mathcal E'\le(-\gamma d+2K)\mathcal EdE′≤(−γd+2K)E. Finally K=max⁡{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}+γd/2K=\max\{a^2-m,M-(\gamma-a)^2\}+\gamma d/2K=max{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}+γd/2, so the γd\gamma dγd terms cancel. The proof records the multiplied identities explicitly before elementary real inequalities, then divides by the positive ddd. This proves the precise spectral differential estimate even in a friction regime with a positive growth exponent.

After completing the square, the two endpoint curvature contributions lead to a maximum. Dividing by the positive coordinate gap gives the same exponent as the symmetric-Hessian calculation. Thus both routes recover the paper’s shifted geometry with the same constants.

04Supply the sector inequality from the potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

For x−yx-yx−y and F=∇f(x)−∇f(y)F=\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)F=∇f(x)−∇f(y), the project's proved the strong-smooth gradient interpolation inequality gives the strong-smooth interpolation inequality from differentiability, strong convexity and global Lipschitz continuity. Its centered-ball consequence is exactly the residual theorem's premise. The proof composes these already proved results, yielding arbitrary-shift dissipativity for the nonlinear gradient. It assumes neither a Hessian representation nor the desired contraction conclusion.

The gradient of a differentiable strongly convex smooth potential obeys this sector inequality. Substituting it closes every deterministic premise of the general contraction estimate. No auxiliary vector field with an assumed sector bound is introduced in the final application.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.10.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.10.3.

05Integrate the right-derivative inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

The two kinetic difference equations imply the transformed-coordinate right derivatives −av−F-av-F−av−F and (γ−a)v+F(\gamma-a)v+F(γ−a)v+F. Their squared-norm derivative rules add to the shifted energy derivative. For paths on [0,T][0,T][0,T], define their transformed energy E(t)E(t)E(t). Continuity follows from continuity of the paths, while the preceding nonlinear result gives E′(t)≤2kE(t)E'(t)\le2kE(t)E′(t)≤2kE(t), with k=max⁡{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}/(γ−2a)k=\max\{a^2-m,M-(\gamma-a)^2\}/(\gamma-2a)k=max{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}/(γ−2a). The right-slope form of Grönwall then proves E(t)≤E(0)e2ktE(t)\le E(0)e^{2kt}E(t)≤E(0)e2kt, including the initial endpoint. The hypotheses are the force-difference differential equations, not an exponential bound supplied by the caller.

The difference of common-input integral solutions has the required right derivatives. Apply the scalar integrating-factor argument to its transformed energy and then use the inverse coordinate identities. This proves the general position estimate on every finite horizon.
