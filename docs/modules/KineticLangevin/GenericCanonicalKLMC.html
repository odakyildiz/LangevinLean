References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. 01The probability space and Brownian projection §

Mathematical statement and proof

The phase variable is velocity first. The sample space contains the two coupled initial positions, one shared Gaussian velocity and one vector Brownian source. The measure is the initial-coupling product extension; probability follows from its first marginal and from the Gaussian/Brownian probability factors. The Brownian-source projection preserves its measure. Pullback along that projection gives a continuous linear map on L2L^{2}L2, and its representative theorem identifies it almost everywhere with evaluation on the product sample.

Let the finite coordinate set be III, so the physical dimension is ∣I∣|I|∣I∣. Extending the initial plan by one independent common source preserves both initial position marginals. The same Gaussian velocity and Brownian source can then drive the two coupled phase paths.

02The common Brownian input and filtered innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scaled source lift includes the physical amplitude 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. At iteration nnn, the input is the vector Brownian increment on (nh,nh+t](nh,nh+t](nh,nh+t], and the filtered input is the exponential response to that same increment. The unscaled innovation pair consists of the exact velocity and position Wiener responses at the step endpoint. Both input paths are continuous in L2L^{2}L2, and the additive input starts at zero. These follow from the source lift's continuity and linearity and the proved vector-response properties.

The physical amplitude multiplies both the raw Brownian input and its filtered responses. Keeping it outside the linear response construction preserves all covariance identities. The velocity and position innovations therefore have exactly thermal normalization of the prescribed KLMC method.

04Connect Brownian L2L^{2}L2 innovations with the generic output-law construction §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^{2}L2 innovation pair has the same representatives as the pointwise specified Brownian innovation. The proof pulls back the previously established vector-response representative theorem to the coupling product and combines the two component identities. Comparing with the representatives on the independent product space gives equality of the L2L^{2}L2 pairs. Thus the numerical chain analyzed here has the coupling-independent output law already proved for the defined KLMC chain.

Equality almost everywhere of response representatives implies equality of their pushforward laws. This transfers the fixed-source output identification to the coupling extension. In particular, the analytic random variables do not define a new sampler depending on the chosen initial plan.

06Construct all three chains and segment families §

Mathematical statement and proof

The numerical chain applies the lifted original gradient and the constructed correlated innovations. The reference chain stitches exact selected kinetic segments, each driven by the specified Brownian increment for that iteration. The corresponding reference segments start from these reference states. The auxiliary exact segment starts instead from the numerical state and receives the identical additive input. The drift is the pointwise gradient lifted to L2L^{2}L2; no substitute potential on the L2L^{2}L2 space is assumed.

The reference exact path is restarted from the reference phase, while the auxiliary exact path restarts from the numerical phase. Both use the same interval input. This arrangement separates contraction of the old error from the force-freezing defect of the new numerical step.
