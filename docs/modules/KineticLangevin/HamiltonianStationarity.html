Gibbs invariance of the Hamiltonian integral flow

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Hamiltonian stationarity follows by taking a limit of the corrected Verlet approximations. Explicit corrected Verlet proposals preserve the Gibbs measure exactly. Their rejection probability is quadratically small, while the uncorrected Verlet iterates converge to the selected Hamiltonian integral solution. Bounded tests transfer invariance through this limit. The finite-dimensional strong-convexity theorem derives every required tail premise, so its statement assumes no invariant flow, numerical convergence, or error bound.

Exact corrected invariance, a vanishing rejection discrepancy, and Verlet convergence are combined in one limit. This proves invariance for the constructed Hamiltonian solution rather than assuming a stationary comparator.

01Measurability of the final state as a function of its initial state §

Mathematical statement and proof

The constructed kinetic endpoint is jointly continuous in initialization and continuous forcing. Fix zero forcing, friction zero, inverse mass one, and endpoint time TTT. Composing this joint map with z↦(z,0)z\mapsto(z,0)z↦(z,0) proves continuity of the selected Hamiltonian endpoint. The argument supplies the compact forcing-space zero explicitly and simplifies the composed definition. The endpoint pushforward is therefore a well-defined measurable pushforward.

Continuous dependence on the initial phase makes the terminal Hamiltonian map measurable. Its pushforward under the normalized Gibbs phase law is therefore well defined. The selected solution’s uniqueness ensures this map does not depend on a choice in the existence construction.

03Verify all tail hypotheses from the potential assumptions §

Mathematical statement and proof

In finite dimensions, positive strong convexity and differentiability make the Hamiltonian Gibbs measure finite by the factorization and Gaussian domination proved in the moments module. The globally Lipschitz gradient supplies the finite force second moment, and thermal velocity factor supplies the velocity second moment. Substituting these results into the preceding limit theorem gives exact Hamiltonian Gibbs invariance from the original first-order potential assumptions. The theorem applies to every nonnegative finite horizon. It proves the Hamiltonian subflow; the full friction-and-noise kinetic diffusion additionally needs its thermostat splitting limit.

The necessary integrable upper bound follows from strong convexity and smoothness through the phase moment estimates. These hypotheses also supply finite normalization. Consequently the stationarity theorem has its tail and moment requirements verified rather than leaving an assumed invariant reference measure.
