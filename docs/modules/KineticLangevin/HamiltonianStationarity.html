Gibbs invariance of the Hamiltonian integral flow

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Hamiltonian flow ΦT\Phi_TΦT​ maps an initial state z=(v,x)z=(v,x)z=(v,x) to the value at time TTT of the continuous solution of the integral equations Vt=v−∫0t∇f(Xs) dsV_t=v-\int_0^t\nabla f(X_s)\,dsVt​=v−∫0t​∇f(Xs​)ds, Xt=x+∫0tVs dsX_t=x+\int_0^tV_s\,dsXt​=x+∫0t​Vs​ds, 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T0≤t≤T; this is the kinetic integral equation with zero friction, unit inverse mass and zero forcing, whose solution exists by the Picard argument and is unique by the Gronwall stability estimate. The module proves that ΦT\Phi_TΦT​ is continuous and that it preserves the Gibbs measure, π∘ΦT−1=π\pi\circ\Phi_T^{-1}=\piπ∘ΦT−1​=π.

The invariance is not obtained by differentiating the density. Instead ΦT\Phi_TΦT​ is approximated by NNN velocity Verlet steps of size T/NT/NT/N. A Verlet step followed by a Metropolis accept/reject decision with velocity reversal preserves π\piπ exactly; the rejection probability is of order h2h^2h2; hence the law of NNN uncorrected Verlet steps differs from π\piπ, on bounded test functions, by at most a multiple of T2/NT^2/NT2/N, and this error vanishes in the limit N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞, in which the Verlet iterates converge to ΦT\Phi_TΦT​. In finite dimension with Lebesgue reference measure, strong convexity of fff supplies the finiteness of π\piπ and the moment needed for the rejection bound. The invariance is used for the normalised Gibbs target and in the analysis of the exact OBABO scheme and its phase laws.

03Invariance under strong convexity in finite dimension §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with its Lebesgue measure λ\lambdaλ (the Haar measure giving volume one to the cube spanned by an orthonormal basis). Let f:E→Rf:E\to\mathbb Rf:E→R be differentiable and mmm-strongly convex for some m>0m>0m>0, that is,

f(ax+by)≤af(x)+bf(y)−ab m2 ∥x−y∥2for all x,y∈E, a,b≥0, a+b=1,f(ax+by)\le af(x)+bf(y)-ab\,\frac m2\,\|x-y\|^2\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in E,\ a,b\ge0,\ a+b=1,f(ax+by)≤af(x)+bf(y)−ab2m​∥x−y∥2for all x,y∈E, a,b≥0, a+b=1,

and let ∇f\nabla f∇f be MMM-Lipschitz. Then for every T≥0T\ge0T≥0 the Gibbs measure π\piπ with density e−f(x)−∥v∥2/2e^{-f(x)-\|v\|^2/2}e−f(x)−∥v∥2/2 relative to λ⊗λ\lambda\otimes\lambdaλ⊗λ satisfies π∘ΦT−1=π\pi\circ\Phi_T^{-1}=\piπ∘ΦT−1​=π.

Proof. Lebesgue measure on EEE is σ\sigmaσ-finite (hence s-finite), invariant under translations and invariant under x↦−xx\mapsto-xx↦−x, and EEE, being finite-dimensional, is complete and separable. It therefore suffices to check the two additional assumptions of the previous step.

Finiteness of π\piπ. The density factorises, e−H(v,x)=e−∥v∥2/2e−f(x)e^{-H(v,x)}=e^{-\|v\|^2/2}e^{-f(x)}e−H(v,x)=e−∥v∥2/2e−f(x), so by Tonelli's theorem π\piπ is the product of the measures e−∥v∥2/2λ(dv)e^{-\|v\|^2/2}\lambda(dv)e−∥v∥2/2λ(dv) and e−f(x)λ(dx)e^{-f(x)}\lambda(dx)e−f(x)λ(dx). The first is finite (a Gaussian integral). For the second, strong convexity and differentiability give the quadratic lower bound f(x)≥f(x0)+⟨∇f(x0),x−x0⟩+m2∥x−x0∥2f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle\nabla f(x_0),x-x_0\rangle+\frac m2\|x-x_0\|^2f(x)≥f(x0​)+⟨∇f(x0​),x−x0​⟩+2m​∥x−x0​∥2, so e−fe^{-f}e−f is dominated by a constant multiple of a Gaussian density and is integrable. Both factors being finite, π\piπ is finite. These facts were proved in the module on Hamiltonian moments.

Second moment. In the same module it is shown that ∫∥v∥2e−∥v∥2/2λ(dv)<∞\int\|v\|^2e^{-\|v\|^2/2}\lambda(dv)<\infty∫∥v∥2e−∥v∥2/2λ(dv)<∞ (a Gaussian second moment) and that ∫∥∇f(x)∥2e−f(x)λ(dx)<∞\int\|\nabla f(x)\|^2e^{-f(x)}\lambda(dx)<\infty∫∥∇f(x)∥2e−f(x)λ(dx)<∞. For the latter, the Lipschitz bound and the triangle inequality give ∥∇f(x)∥≤M∥x∥+∥∇f(0)∥\|\nabla f(x)\|\le M\|x\|+\|\nabla f(0)\|∥∇f(x)∥≤M∥x∥+∥∇f(0)∥, hence, by (a+b)2≤2a2+2b2(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2(a+b)2≤2a2+2b2, ∥∇f(x)∥2≤2M2∥x∥2+2∥∇f(0)∥2;\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\le2M^2\|x\|^2+2\|\nabla f(0)\|^2 ;∥∇f(x)∥2≤2M2∥x∥2+2∥∇f(0)∥2; the right-hand side is integrable against e−f(x)λ(dx)e^{-f(x)}\lambda(dx)e−f(x)λ(dx) because this measure is finite and has a finite second moment ∫∥x∥2e−f(x)λ(dx)\int\|x\|^2e^{-f(x)}\lambda(dx)∫∥x∥2e−f(x)λ(dx), again by the Gaussian domination of e−fe^{-f}e−f. Since each of the two summands ∥v∥2\|v\|^2∥v∥2 and ∥∇f(x)∥2\|\nabla f(x)\|^2∥∇f(x)∥2 depends on one coordinate only and the other factor of the product measure is finite, both summands are π\piπ-integrable, and so is their sum.

The previous step now gives π∘ΦT−1=π\pi\circ\Phi_T^{-1}=\piπ∘ΦT−1​=π. □\square□

The hypotheses of this claim concern the potential fff only; finiteness of π\piπ and the second moment are derived, not assumed.
