Construct independent correlated innovations on Brownian time intervals

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The one-step Gaussian covariance is only part of the algorithm: innovations at different iterations must be independent and must come from increments of the same Brownian source used by the exact reference. This module translates deterministic time kernels onto successive intervals of that source. Translation preserves the within-step Gram covariance. Disjoint time supports give zero cross covariances, and joint Gaussianity turns this orthogonality into independence of the entire innovation blocks. Correlations among coordinates within a block are retained.

01Translate a time kernel without changing its L² geometry §

Mathematical statement and proof

For an interval beginning at aaa, translate the original response kernel by (SaK)(r)=K(r−a)(S_aK)(r)=K(r-a)(Sa​K)(r)=K(r−a). Lebesgue measure is invariant under translation, so SaS_aSa​ is a real linear isometry of the deterministic time Hilbert space. The translated response is

Kka,t(r)=1(a,a+t](r)k(a+t−r).K^{a,t}_k(r)=1_{(a,a+t]}(r)k(a+t-r).Kka,t​(r)=1(a,a+t]​(r)k(a+t−r).

Its inner product with another response on the same interval remains ∫0tk(s)ℓ(s) ds\int_0^t k(s)\ell(s)\,ds∫0t​k(s)ℓ(s)ds.

Lean constructs the isometry with Lp.compMeasurePreservingₗᵢ and measurePreserving_sub_right. The representative theorem combines the almost-everywhere composition formula with the previously proved kernel representative. Elementary inequalities identify r−a∈(0,t]r-a\in(0,t]r−a∈(0,t] with r∈(a,a+t]r\in(a,a+t]r∈(a,a+t]; scalar algebra identifies the two elapsed-time expressions. Inner-product preservation of the linear isometry then reduces the covariance geometry to the unshifted kernel identity.

02Prove that disjoint time segments are orthogonal §

Mathematical statement and proof

If a+t≤ba+t\le ba+t≤b, the intervals (a,a+t](a,a+t](a,a+t] and (b,b+u](b,b+u](b,b+u] are disjoint. At every time coordinate, at least one of their indicators is zero. Their L² inner product is therefore zero, regardless of the two continuous response functions.

The Lean proof unfolds the L² inner product as an integral, substitutes the explicit almost-everywhere segment representatives, and splits on membership in the first interval. Membership implies r≤a+t≤br\le a+t\le br≤a+t≤b, which excludes the strict lower bound b<rb<rb<r of the second interval. Thus the integrand vanishes almost everywhere, and integral_eq_zero_of_ae concludes. The half-open convention makes adjacent segments disjoint exactly, including their common endpoint.

03Apply the same isonormal map to every time segment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define Zka,t=I(Kka,t)Z_k^{a,t}=I(K_k^{a,t})Zka,t​=I(Kka,t​) using the same isonormal map and Gaussian product sample space as the original Brownian motion. Each response is centered, and every finite family of responses on an interval is jointly Gaussian. The covariance on one interval is the same Gram integral as before. Responses on separated intervals have covariance zero by the deterministic support calculation.

Lean proves these assertions by composing the translation's linear map with the existing Gaussian isonormal map. The finite-family Gaussian theorem applies to that actual composite linear map. For covariances, isonormal_covariance reduces the probability calculation to the deterministic inner product, where the within-interval and disjoint-interval theorems apply. Independence is not assumed at this stage; zero covariance alone would not imply independence without the jointly Gaussian property proved next.

04Identify the full Gaussian block law on every interval §

Mathematical statement and proof

For finitely many response functions (ki)(k_i)(ki​), the Euclidean vector (Zkia,t)i(Z_{k_i}^{a,t})_i(Zki​a,t​)i​ has exactly the centered Gaussian law with covariance Cij=∫0tki(s)kj(s) dsC_{ij}=\int_0^t k_i(s)k_j(s)\,dsCij​=∫0t​ki​(s)kj​(s)ds. The starting time aaa does not appear in this law, because deterministic translation preserves all inner products.

Lean assembles the actual L² representatives into a Euclidean random vector and proves its Gaussianity via the continuous linear equivalence from finite function space. Coordinate projections commute with the Bochner integral, giving the zero vector mean. The covariance bilinear form expands into a finite double sum; each entry is rewritten by the proved segment covariance formula. Equality with the Gram Gaussian's bilinear form, together with equality of means, lets IsGaussian.ext identify the two probability measures. No distributional equality is supplied as a hypothesis.

05Derive independence of all successive Brownian innovation blocks §

Mathematical statement and proof

On the uniform grid, the nnn-th block uses the interval (nh,(n+1)h](nh,(n+1)h](nh,(n+1)h]. gridConvolutionLp places every response function on that interval. The family indexed by both iteration and response coordinate is a Gaussian process because every member is obtained by applying one Gaussian linear map to a deterministic Hilbert vector.

For n<mn<mn<m, the elementary inequality (n+1)h≤mh(n+1)h\le mh(n+1)h≤mh proves disjoint support and therefore zero cross covariance between every coordinate of the two blocks. The Gaussian-process independence theorem converts these coordinate covariance equalities into independence of the entire block-valued random variables, over all iteration numbers simultaneously. Packing a finite coordinate family into Euclidean space is a measurable map, so time independence survives that packing.

Lean constructs the jointly Gaussian process with isGaussianProcess_of_linearMap_Lp. It uses iIndepFun_of_covariance_eq_zero, proves the disjoint-segment inequality from Nat.succ_le_of_lt, and handles the reversed ordering through symmetry of covariance. Finally, iIndepFun.comp transports independence through WithLp.toLp. The per-block law is the segment joint-law theorem, so the actual Brownian-derived Euclidean blocks are identically distributed with the required correlated Gram-Gaussian law.
