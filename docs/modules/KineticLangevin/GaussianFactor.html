A Cholesky factor for the Gaussian response covariance

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A Gaussian law specified through its covariance must agree with the specified Gaussian construction to multiply independent standard normals by a Cholesky factor. This section proves that exact identity, constructs the positive-diagonal lower-triangular 2×2 factor, and applies it to the strictly positive exponential Gram covariance.

An explicit lower-triangular factor of the response Gram covariance maps independent scalar standard normals to the exact sampler innovation law. The proof includes positive pivots and Gaussian measure equality.

Statement and proof

01Identify every covariance factor by mean and covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

If AAT=SAA^\mathsf T=SAAT=S, then applying AAA to a standard Gaussian gives a centered Gaussian with covariance SSS. View the matrix as a linear operator on Euclidean space. Its transpose is the adjoint, and matrix multiplication is composition. The mean passes through the continuous linear map and remains zero. The covariance pushforward is ⟨ATx,ATy⟩=⟨x,AATy⟩\langle A^\mathsf Tx,A^\mathsf Ty\rangle=\langle x,AA^\mathsf Ty\rangle⟨ATx,ATy⟩=⟨x,AATy⟩, which is the target covariance. Uniqueness of Gaussian measures with equal mean and covariance proves equality of measures, not only equality of moments.

A deterministic linear image of independent standard normals is jointly Gaussian. Its covariance is the factor times its transpose. Equality of centered Gaussian covariances determines equality of measures, which is why any verified covariance factor gives the exact response law.

02Construct and verify the positive-diagonal Cholesky factor §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a positive definite symmetric 2×2 matrix with entries a=S00a=S_{00}a=S00​, b=S10b=S_{10}b=S10​, d=S11d=S_{11}d=S11​, define L=(a0b/ad−b2/a)L=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt a&0\\b/\sqrt a&\sqrt{d-b^2/a}\end{pmatrix}L=(a​b/a​​0d−b2/a​​). Positive definiteness gives a>0a>0a>0 and ad−b2>0ad-b^2>0ad−b2>0, hence a strictly positive Schur complement. The factor is lower triangular with positive diagonal. Expanding its four matrix entries, using the two square-root-square identities and symmetry, gives LLT=SLL^\mathsf T=SLLT=S. The general Gaussian-factor theorem therefore proves that this triangular factor generates exactly the target Gaussian measure.

The first pivot is positive by positive definiteness. The determinant condition makes the Schur complement positive, so both diagonal square roots are real and nonzero. Expanding the lower-triangular product verifies each matrix entry without an implicit numerical factorization assumption.

03Generate the exponential innovation from independent normals §

Mathematical statement and proof

The two response kernels ψ0,ψ1\psi_0,\psi_1ψ0​,ψ1​ have a strictly positive Gram covariance on every positive-length interval. Apply the explicit Cholesky construction to that matrix. The resulting output law is precisely the two-response Gaussian law, already identified with the Brownian convolution pair used by the sampler. Finally, the product of two real standard Gaussian measures pushes forward to the Euclidean standard Gaussian under the coordinate identification. Composing the two exact laws proves the paper’s realization starting from two independent scalar normal draws. This proves the covariance-factor identity for the shared exponential integrator and its particle specialization.

The response Gram matrix is strictly positive for positive step length. Its explicit triangular factor therefore transforms two independent scalar standard normals into the velocity–position innovation pair. This gives an exact realization of the same law used by the Brownian coupling proof.
