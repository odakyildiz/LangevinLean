The actual KLMC2 chain: a bound for every initial transport plan

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The reference is the constructed kinetic diffusion started from the normalized Gibbs target, with an independent Gaussian velocity and canonical Brownian source. The numerical chain is the implemented four-kernel KLMC2 update on the same source. All local equations, Gaussian marginals and stationary reference laws have been proved before the scalar recurrence is applied.

01Keep the accumulated contraction factor nonnegative §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source restrictions imply gamma h at most one fifth and m at most gamma squared. Hence mh/(4 gamma) is at most one twentieth, so q=1-mh/(4 gamma) is nonnegative. This elementary check is needed when comparing the actual initial error and projecting the iterated recurrence.

02Iterate the actual canonical coupling error §

Mathematical statement and proof

For every finite-cost initial position coupling, construct the actual numerical and reference chains with shared initial Gaussian velocity and shared canonical Brownian intervals. Let e(k) be the actual transformed L2 phase discrepancy and d(k) the actual L2 position discrepancy. The native canonical one-step theorem supplies the repaired local recurrence, including the fresh stochastic Hessian terms. The cutoff condition M2 sqrt(alpha)h at most m/4 absorbs its position-dependent correction into q. The checked affine recurrence and phase projection then give d(n) at most sqrt(2)q^n times the initial position cost, plus sqrt(2)h^2 C/m and 8(M-m)h exp(-(alpha-p)/8)/m. Here C=M2 sqrt(p^2+2p)+M sqrt(Mp). Equal initial velocities identify the initial transformed norm exactly, and the constructed initial-extension variables identify the initial L2 position norm with the supplied transport plan cost. There is no stationary-law, local-error, Brownian-independence or numerical-law premise in this final per-coupling theorem. Taking the infimum over actual initial couplings is handled by the subsequent Wasserstein endpoint.
