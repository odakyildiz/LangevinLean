The first-order KLMC Wasserstein estimate

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This final theorem concerns the defined KLMC chain on the explicit independent Gaussian-velocity and specified Brownian source. It proves the first-order Wasserstein bound of Dalalyan and Riou-Durand with the initial Wasserstein distance. All continuous reference laws, common-noise identities, local-error estimates, output-law identities and transport-infimum steps are established from the stated potential hypotheses.

All reference, source-law, and local-error premises have been constructed internally. Combining them proves the explicit first-order position theorem for the independent correlated-Gaussian sampler.

01Gibbs stationarity of the global coupling reference §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference begins with standard Gaussian velocity independent of the target Gibbs position. Its full Brownian forcing and its entire filtered innovation stream are constructed objects on the initial-coupling extension. Their exact initial/noise product law is already proved. The kinetic stationarity transfer theorem therefore applies directly to the integral solution. At a time ttt inside a larger horizon, the argument restricts the integral equation to [0,t][0,t][0,t], supplies the continuous input and its zero starting value, and applies the proved splitting-limit stationarity result. This yields the full phase law N(0,Id)⊗π\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\piN(0,Id​)⊗π at every reference time, without a remaining stationarity or Gaussian-marginal hypothesis.

The reference initialization and its entire Brownian innovation stream have the required independent product law. The splitting-limit stationarity theorem applies to the integral solution. Uniqueness then identifies its stationary samples with all reference segments used by the coupling argument.

More precisely, let the state dimension be a nonnegative integer, let fff be differentiable and mmm-strongly convex with m>0m>0m>0, and let its gradient be MMM-Lipschitz with M≥0M\ge0M≥0. For every γ≥0\gamma\ge0γ≥0, every initial position coupling with the Gibbs law, and every terminal time T≥0T\ge0T≥0, the constructed reference has phase law N(0,Id)⊗π\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\piN(0,Id​)⊗π at every t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T]. Indeed its initial/noise joint law is a product; the stationarity theorem applies to the restriction of the integral equation to [0,t][0,t][0,t]. This result needs no contraction or small-step assumption: those assumptions enter the numerical comparison below.

The initial position distribution may be arbitrary with finite initial Wasserstein distance; its velocity is the explicit independent standard Gaussian. First, the argument derives the finite Gibbs second moment from strong convexity. It then supplies the just-proved global-reference stationarity for every initial transport coupling to the complete constructed coupling/infimum theorem. The conclusion has no conditional local-error, reference-process, Gaussian-law, stationary-law or optimal-coupling premise. The smoothness assumptions are expressed by the gradient and strong convexity, so the theorem also covers the paper's Hessian-bounded setting through the existing deterministic geometry results.

Combine the verified local comparison, scalar recurrence, position projection, and all-couplings infimum. The result concerns the explicit independent-Gaussian KLMC sampler. Its linear step-size bias is the accumulated effect of force freezing, while the geometric term transports the initial position discrepancy.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

firstOrder_coupling_recurrence_bound combines one-step contraction and local error, iterates the scalar affine recurrence and projects its transformed phase norm to position. klmc_wasserstein_bound obtains the Gibbs second moment as hπ, then applies klmc_wasserstein_bound_of_globalStationarity with the stationarity function proved for every initial coupling. The helper theorem identifies both output marginals and takes the initial-coupling infimum. The displayed transient and bias are therefore conclusions for the actual sampler law.
