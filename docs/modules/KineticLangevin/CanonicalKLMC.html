References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This final theorem concerns the implemented KLMC chain on the explicit independent Gaussian-velocity and canonical Brownian source. It proves the first-order Wasserstein bound of Dalalyan and Riou-Durand with the true initial Wasserstein distance. All continuous reference laws, common-noise identities, local-error estimates, output-law identities and transport-infimum steps are discharged inside the proof chain.

01Prove Gibbs stationarity of the actual global coupling reference §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference begins with standard Gaussian velocity independent of the target Gibbs position. Its full Brownian forcing and its entire filtered innovation stream are actual constructed objects on the initial-coupling extension. Their exact initial/noise product law is already proved. The kinetic stationarity transfer theorem therefore applies directly to the actual integral solution. For a time ttt inside a larger horizon, Lean restricts the actual integral equation to [0,t][0,t][0,t], supplies the continuous input and its zero starting value, and applies the proved splitting-limit stationarity result. This yields the full phase law N(0,Id)⊗π\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes\piN(0,Id​)⊗π at every reference time, without a remaining stationarity or Gaussian-marginal hypothesis.
