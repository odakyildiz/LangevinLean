The exponential integral formula for velocity

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Let X\mathcal XX be a real Banach space, let γ∈R\gamma\in\mathbb Rγ∈R and T≥0T\ge0T≥0. The velocity of the kinetic diffusion satisfies an integral equation of the form Vt−V0=−∫0t(γVs+Fs) ds+WtV_t-V_0=-\int_0^t(\gamma V_s+F_s)\,ds+W_tVt​−V0​=−∫0t​(γVs​+Fs​)ds+Wt​, where FFF is the force term along the path and WWW is the noise. The first-order KLMC update instead uses the variation-of-constants (mild) form

VT=e−γTV0−∫0Te−γ(T−s)Fs ds+BT,V_T=e^{-\gamma T}V_0-\int_0^Te^{-\gamma(T-s)}F_s\,ds+B_T,VT​=e−γTV0​−∫0T​e−γ(T−s)Fs​ds+BT​,

in which the noise enters through the filtered process BBB solving Bt=Wt−γ∫0tBs dsB_t=W_t-\gamma\int_0^tB_s\,dsBt​=Wt​−γ∫0t​Bs​ds (in the stochastic application BT=∫0Te−γ(T−s) dWsB_T=\int_0^Te^{-\gamma(T-s)}\,dW_sBT​=∫0T​e−γ(T−s)dWs​). This module proves that every continuous solution of the integral equation satisfies the mild form. The noise WWW is not differentiated: only VVV, FFF and BBB are assumed continuous, and the identity is derived from the two integral equations.

The result is used with X=L2\mathcal X=L^2X=L2 of random vectors, to identify the velocity of an exact kinetic segment with the expression whose force term is frozen by the KLMC step, when both are driven by the same Brownian increments.

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let X\mathcal XX be a real Banach space, γ∈R\gamma\in\mathbb Rγ∈R, T≥0T\ge0T≥0, and let V,F,W,B:R→XV,F,W,B:\mathbb R\to\mathcal XV,F,W,B:R→X with VVV, FFF, BBB continuous (no regularity of WWW is assumed). Suppose that for every t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T]

Vt−V0=−∫0t(γVs+Fs) ds+Wt(1)V_t-V_0=-\int_0^t(\gamma V_s+F_s)\,ds+W_t\qquad(1)Vt​−V0​=−∫0t​(γVs​+Fs​)ds+Wt​(1) Bt=Wt−γ∫0tBs ds.(2)B_t=W_t-\gamma\int_0^tB_s\,ds.\qquad(2)Bt​=Wt​−γ∫0t​Bs​ds.(2)

Then

VT=e−γTV0−∫0Te−γ(T−s)Fs ds+BT.V_T=e^{-\gamma T}V_0-\int_0^Te^{-\gamma(T-s)}F_s\,ds+B_T .VT​=e−γTV0​−∫0T​e−γ(T−s)Fs​ds+BT​.

The integrals are Bochner integrals of continuous X\mathcal XX-valued functions on bounded intervals; they are linear in the integrand, commute with multiplication by real scalars, and satisfy the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Proof. Step 1: B0=0B_0=0B0​=0. At t=0t=0t=0 both integrals vanish, so (1) gives 0=W00=W_00=W0​, and then (2) gives B0=W0=0B_0=W_0=0B0​=W0​=0.

Step 2: the equation for D=V−BD=V-BD=V−B. The function DDD is continuous. For t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T], linearity of the integral (all integrands are continuous, hence integrable on [0,t][0,t][0,t]) gives

Step 3: derivative of DDD. Fix s∈(0,T)s\in(0,T)s∈(0,T). The integrand u↦γDu+Fuu\mapsto\gamma D_u+F_uu↦γDu​+Fu​ is continuous, so by the fundamental theorem of calculus the function t↦V0−∫0t(γDu+Fu) dut\mapsto V_0-\int_0^t(\gamma D_u+F_u)\,dut↦V0​−∫0t​(γDu​+Fu​)du is differentiable at sss with derivative −(γDs+Fs)-(\gamma D_s+F_s)−(γDs​+Fs​). By (3) this function coincides with DDD on the open interval (0,T)(0,T)(0,T), which is a neighbourhood of sss; derivatives depend only on the values near the point, so D′(s)=−(γDs+Fs)D'(s)=-(\gamma D_s+F_s)D′(s)=−(γDs​+Fs​).

Step 4: integrating factor. For s∈(0,T)s\in(0,T)s∈(0,T), the product rule for a real function times a vector function gives

dds(eγsDs)=γeγsDs+eγsD′(s)=γeγsDs−eγs(γDs+Fs)=−eγsFs.\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(e^{\gamma s}D_s\bigr)=\gamma e^{\gamma s}D_s+e^{\gamma s}D'(s)=\gamma e^{\gamma s}D_s-e^{\gamma s}(\gamma D_s+F_s)=-e^{\gamma s}F_s .dsd​(eγsDs​)=γeγsDs​+eγsD′(s)=γeγsDs​−eγs(γDs​+Fs​)=−eγsFs​.

The function s↦eγsDss\mapsto e^{\gamma s}D_ss↦eγsDs​ is continuous on [0,T][0,T][0,T], differentiable on (0,T)(0,T)(0,T), and its derivative s↦−eγsFss\mapsto-e^{\gamma s}F_ss↦−eγsFs​ is continuous, hence integrable on [0,T][0,T][0,T]. The fundamental theorem of calculus therefore gives, using D0=V0−B0=V0D_0=V_0-B_0=V_0D0​=V0​−B0​=V0​ from Step 1,

eγTDT−V0=−∫0TeγsFs ds.(4)e^{\gamma T}D_T-V_0=-\int_0^Te^{\gamma s}F_s\,ds .\qquad(4)eγTDT​−V0​=−∫0T​eγsFs​ds.(4)

Step 5: conclusion. Multiply (4) by e−γTe^{-\gamma T}e−γT. Since e−γTeγT=1e^{-\gamma T}e^{\gamma T}=1e−γTeγT=1 and scalar multiplication commutes with the integral, e−γTeγs=e−γ(T−s)e^{-\gamma T}e^{\gamma s}=e^{-\gamma(T-s)}e−γTeγs=e−γ(T−s) gives

Adding BTB_TBT​ to both sides, VT=DT+BTV_T=D_T+B_TVT​=DT​+BT​ is the claimed formula. □\square□

No sign condition on γ\gammaγ is needed, and T=0T=0T=0 is allowed (then the claim reads V0=V0+B0V_0=V_0+B_0V0​=V0​+B0​, consistent with Step 1). Continuity of BBB is used in Step 2 to integrate BBB and in Step 4 to integrate the derivative; without it the difference DDD need not be differentiable.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

damped_integral_eq_mild is stated for a complete real normed space E (NormedSpace ℝ E, CompleteSpace E). The hypotheses hV, hF, hB are the continuity of V,F,BV,F,BV,F,B; heq is (1) and hnoise is (2), both for t ∈ Icc 0 T (the closed interval [0,T][0,T][0,T]). Integrals are interval integrals ∫ s in 0..t, …, and • is multiplication of a vector by a real scalar.

  1. D is V−BV-BV−B and hD its continuity. hzero is Step 1: it evaluates heq and hnoise at 000, where intervalIntegral.integral_same makes the integrals vanish.
  2. hDeq is identity (3).   3. hder is Step 3: intervalIntegral.integral_hasDerivAt_right differentiates the integral at sss, .const_sub (V 0) accounts for the constant, and HasDerivAt.congr_of_eventuallyEq transfers the derivative to DDD, which agrees with the integral expression on Ioo_mem_nhds hs.1 hs.2 (the open interval is a neighbourhood of sss).
  4. hd is the derivative computation of Step 4, obtained from the product rule .smul applied to the derivative of s↦eγss\mapsto e^{\gamma s}s↦eγs and hder; the resulting expression is simplified by module.
  5. hi := intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt_of_le … is the fundamental theorem of calculus on [0,T][0,T][0,T] for a function continuous on the closed interval and differentiable in its interior, i.e. (4); hD0 replaces D0D_0D0​ by V0V_0V0​.
  6. hx multiplies (4) by e−γTe^{-\gamma T}e−γT; he is e−γTeγT=1e^{-\gamma T}e^{\gamma T}=1e−γTeγT=1 (via Real.exp_add); hi' combines the exponentials inside the integral. The last two lines solve for VTV_TVT​ with sub_eq_iff_eq_add and rearrange by abel.
