The exponential integral formula for velocity

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The KLMC update freezes the force inside an exponentially weighted integral. To compare it with an exact path, that weighted formula must follow from the integral equation driven by the same Brownian input. The theorem below derives this formula for any complete real normed space, including the L² space of random Euclidean vectors. No time derivative of Brownian motion is used.

The integrating-factor derivation removes the filtered forcing before differentiating. It gives an exact exponential response to the moving force while preserving the stochastic convolution.

Continuous integrands allow the fundamental theorem of calculus on the interval interior. The argument derives D ⁣′=−γD−FD\!\prime=-\gamma D-FD′=−γD−F, differentiates eγtDte^{\gamma t}D_teγtDt​, and obtains derivative −eγtFt-e^{\gamma t}F_t−eγtFt​. A second application of the fundamental theorem integrates this identity over the closed interval; the proof handles the endpoints by continuity. Finally it multiplies by e−γTe^{-\gamma T}e−γT, commutes scalar multiplication with the Bochner integral, and simplifies the exponent. The hypotheses are the integral equations, and the resulting mild equation is proved rather than postulated. The argument works for arbitrary real friction and includes T=0T=0T=0.

After subtracting the filtered noise, the remaining velocity solves a linear inhomogeneous equation with the moving force as forcing. Multiplication by eγte^{\gamma t}eγt gives its integral formula. Adding the filtered noise back yields the exponential mild identity needed to compare the exact path with a frozen-force step.
