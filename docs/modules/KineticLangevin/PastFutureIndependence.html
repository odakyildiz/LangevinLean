Independence of past and future responses and preservation under L² limits

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The future convolution has support after the terminal time of every past kernel. Their isonormal Hilbert inner products vanish. Joint Gaussianity therefore proves independence of the whole past block and the future response. Independent spatial copies retain this property. The final argument proves independence survives L² limits, which lets numerical approximants establish independence for the nonlinear reference solution.

Disjoint deterministic time supports give independence of Gaussian response families. This property survives coordinate assembly, independent initialization, source changes, and second-mean limits of past-dependent approximants.

01Regroup independent arrays without changing their laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

The exact independent array law is the corresponding infinite product. The measurable currying equivalence transports this law to the product of row laws. Pulling the independent product coordinate projections back along that law proves row independence. This handles the change from individual spatial coordinates to entire past and future spatial blocks.

Regrouping a product family into finite prefixes and later blocks preserves its joint product law. The operation changes indexing only. It is useful because the numerical or splitting state at a grid time depends on a prefix, while the next response uses a later interval.

02Use disjoint deterministic time supports §

Mathematical statement and proof

Place all past kernels in one Gaussian block and the future kernel in a second block. The joint family is Gaussian because every coordinate is the same isonormal linear map applied to its deterministic time kernel. A past interval ends before the future begins, so every cross covariance is exactly zero. Gaussian block independence follows. Independent coordinate source projections then assemble the full rectangular spatial array, and currying groups it into independent past and future vector blocks.

Past and future deterministic kernels have disjoint time supports and hence zero inner product. Their isonormal images are jointly Gaussian with zero cross covariance. This proves independence of the corresponding response families.

03The Euclidean convolution variables §

Mathematical statement and proof

Pack each spatial row with the Euclidean coordinate map and read the future response from any nonempty block coordinate. The spatial-lift representative theorem identifies these packed functions with the vector L² segment convolutions. A countable almost-everywhere intersection handles the entire past family. Independence is unchanged by these almost-everywhere representative identities.

Assembling scalar coordinates and response blocks is a separate measurable transformation on each independent family. Independence therefore survives the assembly into Euclidean convolution variables. This yields the vector law needed in the free-OU argument.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04Adjoin initial data and change probability sources §

Mathematical statement and proof

Measure-preserving source composition transports independence by exact pushforward composition. For a separate independent initial coordinate, product mapping first gives the joint initial/past/future law. Product associativity then places initial data and past together opposite the future coordinate. The two product projections are independent, proving that arbitrary measurable functions of the initial data and past remain independent of the future.

Independent initial data can be adjoined as another product factor. A measure-preserving change of source transfers the entire joint law. Thus independence holds on the coupling space where the reference process and numerical discrepancy are compared.

05Pass independence through L² limits §

Mathematical statement and proof

A continuous linear functional sends an L² vector to an L² scalar. Continuity of the scalar characteristic function therefore gives continuity of every dual characteristic function of the vector law. Independence is characterized by factorization of the joint dual characteristic function. For a convergent sequence of past L² variables and a fixed future variable, both the joint characteristic function and the product of marginal characteristic functions converge. Their equality at every approximation passes to the limit, proving independence of the limiting past variable.

If past approximants converge in second mean to a limit, bounded characteristic functions of the pairs converge as well. Factorization of the approximating joint characteristic functions passes to the limit. This proves independence of the limiting reference state from the future interval.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.5.7.
