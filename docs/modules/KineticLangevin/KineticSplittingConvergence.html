The actual Verlet–OU chain converges to the kinetic integral solution

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This module proves the missing numerical limit directly in a Banach space, including the intended space of square-integrable random variables. The actual scheme uses explicit Verlet stages, exact exponential damping, and the filtered increments of the same continuous additive input as the actual kinetic equation. Compact continuity supplies every local modulus used in the proof. No stationary solution, local-error contract, or numerical-convergence hypothesis is assumed.

03Derive all moduli and prove convergence §

Mathematical statement and proof

The continuous exact path is bounded on [0,T][0,T][0,T]. Its drift path is continuous by global Lipschitz continuity of ggg, and the additive input BBB is continuous. Compactness makes both uniformly continuous. Given any target error ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, choose a common oscillation tolerance η=ε/(D+1)\eta=\varepsilon/(D+1)η=ε/(D+1), where the explicit finite horizon constant DDD contains the state bound, the splitting defect coefficient, friction, and the stability exponential. Since hn=T/(n+1)→0h_n=T/(n+1)\to0hn​=T/(n+1)→0, the mesh is eventually at most one, at most η\etaη, and below both continuity radii. The finite error estimate is then at most ηD<ε\eta D<\varepsilonηD<ε. Lean obtains the two compact uniform-continuity radii, combines the four eventual mesh bounds, instantiates the actual finite estimate, and checks this final scalar comparison. Thus the concrete scheme converges to every actual continuous kinetic integral solution driven by the same input.

04Identify the limit with the constructed kinetic flow §

Mathematical statement and proof

The earlier Picard construction supplies a continuous solution for every initial state and every compact continuous forcing path, with its actual kinetic integral identity already proved. Its normalized forcing is globally continuous and vanishes at zero. Substituting these checked facts into the generic Banach convergence theorem proves that the splitting limit is exactly kineticFlow at the requested horizon. This ties the approximation to the existing jointly continuous solution map, not to an unspecified solution chosen by a new contract.
