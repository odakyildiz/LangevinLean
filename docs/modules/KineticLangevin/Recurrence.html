Accumulation of first-order coupling errors

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The numerical accumulation part of the KLMC proof begins with an explicit one-step coupling inequality. This section derives the stable contraction coefficient and sums its affine recurrence, retaining the coupling inequality and reference velocity norm as visible hypotheses. It proves the implication from the stated coupling recurrence to the numerical bound. Gibbs stationarity and identification of the Gaussian-chain law are proved separately when this implication is applied to the sampler.

The scalar proof explains the transition from a local quadratic error to a global linear bias. Its hypotheses include the part of the local error proportional to the current discrepancy; that coefficient is combined with the contraction factor.

01The one-sided exponential quadratic estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

The argument constructs the derivative through the polynomial and exponential derivative rules, proves its sign with the inequality 1+s≤es1+s\le e^s1+s≤es, applies the derivative monotonicity criterion, and evaluates the zero endpoint. The estimate is proved analytically rather than introduced as an unchecked Taylor remainder.

The scalar estimate is applied only for a nonnegative step parameter. Its quadratic remainder quantifies how much the exact exponential contraction differs from its linear approximation. That remainder is later paid for using the small-step restriction, rather than silently discarded.
