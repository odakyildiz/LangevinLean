Brownian response innovations on an arbitrary finite coordinate set

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Particle states are naturally indexed by a finite disjoint union of parameter and particle coordinates. These constructions work directly for any finite index type, while retaining the same specified Brownian source, correlated Gaussian innovations and independent Gaussian velocity.

All constructions are indexed by an arbitrary finite coordinate set. This permits direct reuse on particle spaces while preserving Gaussian covariance, response identities, and complete time-stream independence.

01The independent velocity/Brownian source §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source is the explicit product of a standard Euclidean Gaussian velocity and the existing independent-coordinate Brownian source. Product projection laws prove the velocity distribution and its second moment. The phase innovation packs the two exponential-response coordinates from every spatial Brownian interval. Measurability, temporal independence and L2L^{2}L2 bounds follow from the generic spatial-block construction. Pullback along the source product projection gives the corresponding L2L^{2}L2 innovation components on the enlarged source.

A finite coordinate set specifies a Euclidean space without requiring a particular indexing convention. The source is the product of its standard Gaussian velocity and coordinate Brownian family. The construction works unchanged under any relabeling of the coordinates.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02The correlated Gaussian phase law and its complete stream §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scalar block on each coordinate has the two-dimensional Gram-Gaussian law. The already proved spatial/time array law identifies each complete spatial block as the product of these scalar laws. Packing its first and second response coordinates gives exactly the correlated phase innovation used by the algorithm. Mutual independence across time identifies the entire defined innovation stream with the infinite product of that same phase law. The proof retains the correlation between velocity and position in each coordinate.

Each interval response is a centered Gaussian phase pair with the prescribed Gram covariance. Distinct spatial coordinates and time intervals are independent. The complete stream law follows from its finite cylinder laws, retaining within-pair correlations exactly.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

03Connect L2L^{2}L2 endpoints and the exact thermal OU stream §

Mathematical statement and proof

The two vector-response representative equalities prove that the L2L^{2}L2 endpoint pair is the pointwise constructed innovation. Taking and scaling its velocity component preserves temporal independence. The exact generic vector OU marginal theorem identifies its scale as 1−ψ0(h)2\sqrt{1-\psi_0(h)^2}1−ψ0​(h)2​. The independent-family law theorem then identifies the entire OU stream with the iid thermal Gaussian product required by the kinetic Gibbs stationarity proof.

Almost-everywhere identities for the terminal responses identify the Hilbert-space source variables to the pointwise innovations. The filtered velocity endpoints also have the exact thermal stream law. This single generic source therefore supports both the KLMC coupling and the splitting-based stationarity proof.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.
