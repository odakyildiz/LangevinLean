Wasserstein estimates under Lipschitz maps and random initialization

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

These lemmas prove the Wasserstein effect of any measurable Lipschitz map between two normed spaces. They support coordinate projections and metric changes without assuming an optimal coupling.

Projection and randomized initialization are treated by pushing couplings through Lipschitz maps. The proof takes the full coupling infimum and therefore applies without assuming an optimal plan exists.

Statement and proof

01Push each coupling through the map and then take the infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

For any coupling c of the two input laws, apply the same measurable map T to both coordinates. The two marginal identities are proved by composing pushforwards with the original coupling marginals. The pointwise Lipschitz inequality and monotonicity of L² norms bound the output coupling cost by q times the input cost, even though the input and output spaces may differ. Taking the infimum over every original coupling gives W2(T#μ,T#ν)≤qW2(μ,ν)W_2(T_\#\mu,T_\#\nu)\le qW_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(T#​μ,T#​ν)≤qW2​(μ,ν). A product coupling establishes that the set of input couplings is nonempty. For the real-valued statement, the extended upper bound first implies finite output cost from finite initial cost, then the nonnegative-real coefficient and the finite real value identities give the claimed real inequality. This prevents an infinite transport cost from disappearing through conversion to a real number.

A Lipschitz map sends any coupling to a coupling of the pushforward laws. Its pointwise distance bound controls the squared coupling cost. Taking square roots and then the infimum yields the Wasserstein Lipschitz bound, including for projections onto a coordinate block.

02Adjoin an independent common source before changing the output space §

Mathematical statement and proof

The same argument applies to a measurable map from an input state and a common independent innovation into a different normed output space. Extend every original coupling by the source law, prove both output marginals, and retain the unchanged input coupling cost. The pointwise contraction and L² monotonicity bound each output cost. Taking the infimum over input couplings yields the cross-space random-map transport bound. In particular, adjoining one shared Gaussian velocity to two position laws has no cost of its own.

Adjoining a common independent source does not change an initial coupling’s transport cost when the relevant discrepancy depends only on the original coordinates. This permits randomized initialization and output maps to be treated by the same coupling-pushforward argument.
