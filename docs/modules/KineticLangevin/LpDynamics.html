Almost-everywhere correspondence between L2L^{2}L2 and pointwise updates

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The L2L^{2}L2 update can be evaluated using representatives of its random-vector equivalence classes. The lemmas here prove that the lifted exponential update, KLMC noise scaling and every recursively defined iterate agree almost everywhere with the original pointwise vector formulas. No Gaussian or independence assumptions are required for this algebraic correspondence.

The functional-analytic formulation is tied to pointwise random vectors by representative identities. Thus the sequence analyzed in second mean is exactly the original exponential recursion with its prescribed innovations.

Statement and proof

01Representatives of the exponential update §

Mathematical statement and proof

The representative of a phase consists of the velocity and position representatives of a pair of L2L^{2}L2 classes at the same sample. Scalar multiplication, subtraction, and addition of equivalence classes agree almost everywhere with the corresponding pointwise operations. Apply it to the velocity formula ψ0v−ψ1G(x)+ηv\psi_0v-\psi_1G(x)+\eta_vψ0​v−ψ1​G(x)+ηv​. For position, rewrite its affine expression as ψ1v−ψ2G(x)+(x+ηx)\psi_1v-\psi_2G(x)+(x+\eta_x)ψ1​v−ψ2​G(x)+(x+ηx​), then use the same lemma. Finally the pointwise representative of the lifted drift identifies G(x)(ω)G(x)(\omega)G(x)(ω) with ∇f(x(ω))\nabla f(x(\omega))∇f(x(ω)). The two component equalities give equality of the phase update and the pointwise exponential update.

A square-integrable phase is represented almost everywhere by a pair of ordinary random vectors. Evaluating the exponential update on these representatives gives the original pointwise algorithm. The lifted force acts by applying the original gradient separately at each sample point.

02Affine operations on equivalence-class representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

The representative of a sum or scalar multiple of LpL^pLp classes agrees with the corresponding operation almost everywhere. Intersecting the full-measure sets for the finitely many operations in aX−bY+ZaX-bY+ZaX−bY+Z gives one common full-measure set. On this set the representative agrees with the representative of the outer sum, the inner difference, and the two scalar multiples. The resulting equality is precisely the pointwise affine formula. This step accounts for the fact that LpL^pLp elements are equivalence classes; pointwise equalities of their arbitrarily selected representatives cannot be assumed everywhere.

Addition, scalar multiplication, and the bounded coordinate projections commute with passage to square-integrable equivalence classes. Consequently the affine update identities hold almost everywhere simultaneously for the finitely many operations in each step. No alternative numerical map is introduced by the functional-analytic formulation.

03Noise scaling and equality of the recursively defined iterates §

Mathematical statement and proof

The two phase components of a scaled L2L^{2}L2 innovation agree almost everywhere with the same scalar times their representatives. Combining this with the exponential-step correspondence proves the KLMC correspondence, including its 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​ fluctuation-dissipation factor. Induction then proves the entire chain identity: at zero both chains use the supplied initial point; at a successor, intersect the almost-everywhere previous-chain identity with the current-step identity, and rewrite the recursive definitions. This shows the L2L^{2}L2 error estimates concern the original numerical sampler for the supplied innovation stream, rather than a different abstract sequence.

Induct on the iteration number, using the same representative innovation at each successor step. The physical amplitude is retained in the induction identity. Thus the sequence whose error is analyzed in second mean has exactly the same random outputs as the defined KLMC recursion.
