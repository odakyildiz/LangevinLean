The second-order local error from Gaussian and Gibbs moments

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This theorem combines the random-Hessian Taylor estimate with the cubic/quartic defect decomposition. Its reference is the coupled L² solution: exact mild and kinematic equations, with each time marginal equal to the Gaussian-times-Gibbs target. The moment and local-error inequalities are derived inside the proof.

Stationary Gaussian and Gibbs marginals verify every moment premise of the second-order local-error theorem. The resulting bound applies directly to the corrected numerical endpoint.

Statement and proof

01Derive all local constants from target marginals §

Mathematical statement and proof

Project the phase pushforward law to obtain the standard Gaussian velocity marginal and the Gibbs position marginal. The Gaussian fourth moment gives the displacement Taylor forcing bound M₂√(p²+2p)t²/2. The Gibbs score theorem gives ‖∇f(X(t))‖₂≤√(Mp), while the frozen Hessian operator has norm at most M. The generic local decomposition therefore has constant at most M₂√(p²+2p)+M√(Mp). It yields velocity error Ct³/6 and position error Ct⁴/24. If γt≤1/5, the transformed phase error is at most Ct³/4. The conclusions refer to the corrected paths retaining the entire free-OU response; separate kernel identities identify those paths with the defined KLMC2 formula.

The stationary Gaussian velocity provides its exact fourth moment, while the Gibbs position controls the force moment. The Taylor remainder and corrected local equations then supply the cubic transformed defect. Each coefficient is derived from these marginals, establishing the stochastic local-error estimate used in the second-order recurrence.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.28.2.
