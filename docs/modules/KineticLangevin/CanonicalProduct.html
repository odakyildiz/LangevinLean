A concrete independent initial phase and canonical Brownian source

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The stationarity proof takes place on one explicit probability space: the product of an initial phase law and the constructed vector Brownian source. This section builds the initial L² variables, the continuous Brownian forcing and its OU-filtered innovations, then proves the complete initial/noise joint law. The resulting splitting law identity has no assumed independence or Gaussian marginal fields.

Independent initialization and the canonical Brownian source give the exact input product law. This identifies the additive-input and explicit thermal-noise splitting endpoints without changing their probability distributions.

01Represent the original initial phase on the product source §

Mathematical statement and proof

the response Hilbert space is the finite-dimensional Euclidean state, and the product sample space pairs a phase with a vector Brownian sample. The probability measure is exactly π⊗PB\pi\otimes P_Bπ⊗PB​. Pull the velocity and position projections through the first product projection. Their square integrability follows from that of the original phase law. The two the square-integrable representative representative identities prove that the resulting phase agrees almost everywhere with the original initial coordinate.

The initial phase is realized as the first coordinate of an independent product with the canonical Brownian source. Its law is exactly the prescribed initial law. The second coordinate supplies the full source independently of every initial phase component.

02Lift the continuous Brownian input §

Mathematical statement and proof

The second product projection preserves the Brownian source law. Its pullback is a linear isometry on L², hence a continuous linear map. Apply it to 2γBt\sqrt{2\gamma}B_t2γ​Bt​, where the constructed Brownian input is continuous in L² and zero at time zero. Linearity and continuity give the same properties after adjoining the independent initial phase. Thus the forcing used by the deterministic integral solver is an Brownian forcing on this very source.

Pullback of a square-integrable variable along a measure-preserving projection is an isometry. It preserves the Brownian response identities and second-mean continuity. Thus adjoining initial data does not alter the source equations or moments.

03Use the exact OU-filtered Brownian intervals §

Mathematical statement and proof

The innovation in interval [nh,(n+1)h][nh,(n+1)h][nh,(n+1)h] is the pulled-back isonormal convolution 2γ∫nh(n+1)he−γ((n+1)h−s)dBs\sqrt{2\gamma}\int_{nh}^{(n+1)h}e^{-\gamma((n+1)h-s)}dB_s2γ​∫nh(n+1)h​e−γ((n+1)h−s)dBs​. Its representative is exactly the original source variable composed with the Brownian projection. Continuous linear maps commute with the deterministic OU filter. Combined with the proved canonical integration-by-parts identity, this shows the continuous-input filter is exactly this innovation, not merely a random variable with a matching covariance.

The splitting uses the exponentially filtered increment on each numerical interval. The source calculation identifies that increment exactly with the corresponding canonical Gaussian response. The physical amplitude is retained, so its law has the correct thermal variance.

04Derive the complete independent Gaussian stream law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The canonical OU marginal theorem gives covariance (1−e−2γh)I(1-e^{-2\gamma h})I(1−e−2γh)I; the disjoint-interval theorem gives independence of the full vector innovations across time. Their whole stream therefore has the infinite product of thermal Gaussian law. The explicit initial-law/source product makes the initial phase independent of that entire stream. Finally use the almost-everywhere initial and innovation identities to obtain the complete joint law of the L² variables.

Independence of disjoint Brownian intervals gives independence of the filtered vector responses. Their common covariance identifies their marginal law. Together with the independent initialization this proves the full input product law required by the splitting Markov chain.

05Identify both splitting endpoint laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the proved full-input-law theorem to the lifted Verlet–OU chain. Its phase pushforward is exactly the Markov iterate with fresh thermal Gaussian OU innovations and initial law π\piπ. The exact OU-filter identity also identifies the continuous-input chain with the same noise-driven chain, so its endpoint has that law as well. These identities provide the missing connection between the pathwise convergence theorem and the measure-level weak stationarity calculation.

The deterministic additive-input splitting and the explicit independent-noise splitting have equal endpoints under these identities. Their laws therefore coincide. This lets the pathwise convergence theorem and the stationary weak-test theorem refer to the same numerical process.
