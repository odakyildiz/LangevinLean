References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

These transport lemmas connect actual measurable random maps to their iterated independent-noise laws. They preserve every marginal and take the actual infimum over initial couplings. Their inputs are a pathwise contraction and the measured L² discrepancy between two explicit maps; these analytic inputs are proved separately for OBABO and its Hamiltonian comparator.

01Extend every coupling by a common independent innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Given any input coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, append one independent innovation with law Q. Let the numerical output be F(x,s)F(x,s)F(x,s) and the comparator output be G(y,s)G(y,s)G(y,s). Their difference decomposes exactly as

F(x,s)−G(y,s)=[F(x,s)−F(y,s)]−[G(y,s)−F(y,s)].F(x,s)-G(y,s)=[F(x,s)-F(y,s)]-[G(y,s)-F(y,s)].F(x,s)−G(y,s)=[F(x,s)−F(y,s)]−[G(y,s)−F(y,s)].

Minkowski and the pathwise contraction bound the first term by q times the original coupling cost. The law of (y,s)(y,s)(y,s) is proved to be exactly ν⊗Q\nu\otimes Qν⊗Q; the pushforward formula for L² therefore identifies the second term with the actual local discrepancy under that fixed product law. Both output marginals are the actual random-map laws. The resulting bound holds for every c, so the established affine-infimum theorem gives W2(F#(μ⊗Q),G#(ν⊗Q))≤qW2(μ,ν)+bW_2(F_\#(\mu\otimes Q),G_\#(\nu\otimes Q))\le qW_2(\mu,\nu)+bW2​(F#​(μ⊗Q),G#​(ν⊗Q))≤qW2​(μ,ν)+b. No optimal-coupling existence is assumed.

02Iterate the actual Markov laws against an invariant comparator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the explicit comparator map preserves π\piπ under the independent source Q and its actual local L² error is at most b. Applying the preceding transport theorem at every numerical iteration gives en+1≤qen+be_{n+1}\le qe_n+ben+1​≤qen​+b. The proof first establishes that each iterated law is a probability and that every transport distance is finite, using the extended-real recurrence and finite initial distance. Only then does it convert to real distances. The proved geometric recurrence yields

W2(μn,π)≤qnW2(μ0,π)+b1−q,0≤q<1.W_2(\mu_n,\pi)\le q^nW_2(\mu_0,\pi)+\frac{b}{1-q},\qquad0\le q<1.W2​(μn​,π)≤qnW2​(μ0​,π)+1−qb​,0≤q<1.

This general lemma keeps the invariant-map and integrated-local-error obligations explicit. The Hamiltonian and local-state modules supply them for the actual OBABO comparison; the convergence theorem itself is not used as an input.

03Expose finite output transport before taking real values §

Mathematical statement and proof

The extended affine one-step bound also proves finite output distance at every actual Markov iteration. Start with finite initial transport. At each step the finite nonnegative coefficient times the preceding finite distance, plus the finite local budget, is finite. The actual distance lies below that finite quantity. This induction is exposed separately so coordinate projections and mean-square consequences can use a real Wasserstein estimate without silently converting infinity to zero.
