Choice of the Gaussian cutoff and accumulation of the second-order error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The repaired local comparison has a random-Hessian term proportional to M2αh2M_2\sqrt\alpha h^2M2​α​h2. Choosing the cutoff α=(m/(4M2h))2\alpha=(m/(4M_2h))^2α=(m/(4M2​h))2 absorbs it into the contraction gap. This section proves the choice, both Gaussian tails, the affine accumulation, the position projection, and the exact dimension range for the manuscript’s final simplification.

The cutoff is selected to absorb a random-Hessian displacement contribution into contraction. Geometric accumulation then yields a quadratic Taylor bias and an explicit Gaussian tail, with low-dimensional qualifications preserved.

01Choose the cutoff and retain both tail estimates §

Mathematical statement and proof

For m,M2,h>0m,M_2,h>0m,M2​,h>0, the positive square root of α=(m/(4M2h))2\alpha=(m/(4M_2h))^2α=(m/(4M2​h))2 is m/(4M2h)m/(4M_2h)m/(4M2​h). Thus M2αh=m/4M_2\sqrt\alpha h=m/4M2​α​h=m/4. The source dimension restriction 45pM2h≤m4\sqrt{5p}M_2h\le m45p​M2​h≤m implies α≥5p\alpha\ge5pα≥5p. It follows that (α−p)/8≥p/2(\alpha-p)/8\ge p/2(α−p)/8≥p/2, giving the printed tail. Also p≤α/5p\le\alpha/5p≤α/5 implies (α−p)/8≥α/10=m2/(160M22h2)(\alpha-p)/8\ge\alpha/10=m^2/(160M_2^2h^2)(α−p)/8≥α/10=m2/(160M22​h2), giving the stronger source footnote. One proves the square-root identity, multiplies through positive denominators, compares nonnegative squares, and applies monotonicity of the exponential.

The cutoff is chosen so that its displacement-dependent coefficient equals a fixed fraction of the contraction coefficient. The dimension restriction simultaneously places it in the valid Gaussian-tail range. Both conditions are necessary to close the recurrence with a geometric factor below one.

02Sum the one-step comparison and project to position §

Mathematical statement and proof

The local bound has coefficient 1−mh/(2γ)+M2αh2/γ1-mh/(2\gamma)+M_2\sqrt\alpha h^2/\gamma1−mh/(2γ)+M2​α​h2/γ. The cutoff makes this at most q=1−mh/(4γ)q=1-mh/(4\gamma)q=1−mh/(4γ). The additive local terms are 2h2Be−(α−p)/8+h3C/4\sqrt2h^2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}+h^3C/42​h2Be−(α−p)/8+h3C/4, where B=M−mB=M-mB=M−m and C=M2p2+2p+MMpC=M_2\sqrt{p^2+2p}+M\sqrt{Mp}C=M2​p2+2p​+MMp​. The affine recurrence divides this additive bound by 1−q=mh/(4γ)1-q=mh/(4\gamma)1−q=mh/(4γ). Position projection multiplies by 2/γ\sqrt2/\gamma2​/γ, and equal initial velocities give e0≤γd0e_0\le\gamma d_0e0​≤γd0​. The exact resulting bias is 2h2C/m+(8B/m)he−(α−p)/8\sqrt2h^2C/m+(8B/m)h e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}2​h2C/m+(8B/m)he−(α−p)/8, with the desired transient 2qnd0\sqrt2q^nd_02​qnd0​. The recurrence is a reusable scalar lemma; the chain error theorem supplies its one-step inequality separately.

The additive error contains a cubic local Taylor term and a quadratic local Gaussian-tail term. Dividing their geometric sum by the first-order contraction gap gives a quadratic global Taylor term and a linear-step exponential tail. Position projection cancels the remaining friction normalization.

03State the dimension restriction explicitly §

Mathematical statement and proof

Extra assumption for the printed simplification: p≥2p\ge2p≥2.

For p≥2p\ge2p≥2, p2+2p≤2p2p^2+2p\le2p^2p2+2p≤2p2, hence 2p2+2p≤2p\sqrt2\sqrt{p^2+2p}\le2p2​p2+2p​≤2p. Distributing this through CCC gives the printed Hessian term 2M2p2M_2p2M2​p, while 2Mp=2Mp\sqrt2\sqrt{Mp}=\sqrt{2Mp}2​Mp​=2Mp​ gives the score term. The supplied proof’s simplification does not justify the same expression at p=1p=1p=1. The general theorem therefore retains the exact fourth-moment expression there; the printed simplification is an explicit p≥2p\ge2p≥2 corollary. The proof compares nonnegative squares and never uses the invalid inequality in dimension one.

The exact Gaussian fourth moment is valid in every dimension, whereas the printed simplification uses an additional inequality requiring p≥2p\ge2p≥2. Keeping the exact-moment theorem as the general result preserves the valid one-dimensional statement while still recovering the printed higher-dimensional corollary.
