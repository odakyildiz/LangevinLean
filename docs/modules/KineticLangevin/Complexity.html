Step restrictions and integer iteration counts for a prescribed accuracy

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The paper's step-size and iteration formulas are scalar consequences of its numerical error estimate. This section verifies that implication, retains a stability-compatible bound for the discretization term, and rounds the logarithmic threshold to a natural number. It handles zero initial error separately and clips negative logarithmic thresholds at zero. These accuracy corollaries have an explicit error inequality as input; they do not replace the outstanding unconditional stochastic theorem.

The error theorem becomes a choice of parameters only after selecting a positive step and an integer count. The logarithmic threshold handles initialization, while an explicit allocation controls the persistent bias.

01An exponential upper bound for geometric decay §

Mathematical statement and proof

For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the base 1−α1-\alpha1−α is nonnegative. The elementary exponential inequality gives 1−α≤e−α1-\alpha\le e^{-\alpha}1−α≤e−α. Raising this comparison to a natural power preserves order, so (1−α)n≤e−αn(1-\alpha)^n\le e^{-\alpha n}(1−α)n≤e−αn. The argument uses the inequality 1+s≤es1+s\le e^s1+s≤es, monotonicity of nonnegative powers, and the exponential power identity. The lemma itself needs no positive lower bound on α\alphaα; that requirement enters when dividing the logarithmic threshold.

For 0≤α≤10\le\alpha\le10≤α≤1, (1−α)n≤e−αn(1-\alpha)^n\le e^{-\alpha n}(1−α)n≤e−αn. The exponential form can be solved explicitly for the required iteration number. Its use must retain the admissible range of α\alphaα, which comes from the sampler’s step restrictions.

02Round the logarithmic threshold and include small initial errors §

Mathematical statement and proof

For initial coupling radius DDD, define

n0=⌈max⁡{0,log⁡(24D/ε)α}⌉.n_0=\left\lceil\max\left\{0,\frac{\log(24D/\varepsilon)}\alpha\right\}\right\rceil.n0​=⌈max{0,αlog(24D/ε)​}⌉.

This is a natural number even when the logarithm is negative. If α>0\alpha>0α>0 and n≥n0n\ge n_0n≥n0​, then αn≥log⁡(24D/ε)\alpha n\ge\log(24D/\varepsilon)αn≥log(24D/ε). The proof combines the ceiling's lower bound, the right-hand inequality for a maximum, and the cast of the natural comparison, before multiplying by the positive α\alphaα. The explicit maximum and ceiling handle the initialization-dependent small-error regime and integer rounding.

The count is a natural-number ceiling of a nonnegative threshold. Taking the maximum with zero ensures that an already small initial error requires no negative number of steps. The ceiling adds fewer than one extra iteration beyond the real-valued requirement.

03Verify the transient-error bound only when logarithmic cancellation is valid §

Mathematical statement and proof

For D>0D>0D>0 and ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, exponentiating the iteration inequality gives e−αn≤e−log⁡(24D/ε)=ε/(24D)e^{-\alpha n}\le e^{-\log(24D/\varepsilon)}=\varepsilon/(24D)e−αn≤e−log(24D/ε)=ε/(24D). The exponential upper bound for the geometric factor and multiplication by DDD imply (1−α)nD≤ε/24(1-\alpha)^nD\le\varepsilon/24(1−α)nD≤ε/24. One proves positivity of the logarithm argument before using the exponential–logarithm identity, rewrites the negative exponential as an inverse, and cancels the nonzero factors. Zero initial error is handled by the next theorem without a logarithmic cancellation.

When the logarithm is positive, multiplying the chosen threshold by the contraction rate cancels its denominator. Exponentiating then reduces the initial error to the allocated fraction of accuracy. When the logarithm is nonpositive, the initial error already satisfies that allocation.

04Combine the manuscript's 0.94 bias allocation with the transient §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose an explicit estimate gives En≤2(1−α)nD+bE_n\le\sqrt2(1-\alpha)^nD+bEn​≤2​(1−α)nD+b, with 0<α≤10<\alpha\le10<α≤1, D≥0D\ge0D≥0, and b≤0.94εb\le0.94\varepsilonb≤0.94ε. When D=0D=0D=0, the bias already gives the conclusion and there is no iteration requirement. Otherwise use the rounded threshold. The exact rational inequality 2≤36/25\sqrt2\le36/252​≤36/25 bounds the remaining contribution by 0.06ε0.06\varepsilon0.06ε, so the total error is at most ε\varepsilonε.

One proves the square-root comparison by squaring, splits on zero versus positive initial radius, derives the logarithmic condition in the positive case, and combines the inequalities with nonnegative scalar multiplication. For KLMC, substitute α=3mh/(4γ)\alpha=3mh/(4\gamma)α=3mh/(4γ) and bias Mh2p/mMh\sqrt{2p}/mMh2p​/m, with a step size that is the minimum of the stability threshold and discretization-error allowance. The same scalar argument applies to KIPLMC1 with its thermal radius and a separately bounded concentration term.

The persistent bias and the decaying transient are bounded by separate fractions of the requested error tolerance. The specific numerical allocation leaves enough a strict allowance for the 2\sqrt22​ positional prefactor and the rounded count. Adding the two verified contributions gives the final epsilon guarantee.
