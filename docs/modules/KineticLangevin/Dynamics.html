Existence for the kinetic integral equation

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Let X\mathcal XX be a real normed space, g:X→Xg:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Xg:X→X a Lipschitz map (in the application g=∇fg=\nabla fg=∇f), γ,u≥0\gamma,u\ge0γ,u≥0 the friction and the inverse mass, and B:R→XB:\mathbb R\to\mathcal XB:R→X a continuous path with B(0)=0B(0)=0B(0)=0. This section proves that the kinetic integral equation

vt=v0−∫0t(γvs+u g(xs)) ds+Bt,xt=x0+∫0tvs ds,0≤t≤T,v_t=v_0-\int_0^t\bigl(\gamma v_s+u\,g(x_s)\bigr)\,ds+B_t,\qquad x_t=x_0+\int_0^tv_s\,ds,\qquad 0\le t\le T,vt​=v0​−∫0t​(γvs​+ug(xs​))ds+Bt​,xt​=x0​+∫0t​vs​ds,0≤t≤T,

has a continuous solution on every finite horizon [0,T][0,T][0,T] when X\mathcal XX is complete. With B=2γu WB=\sqrt{2\gamma u}\,WB=2γu​W for a Brownian path WWW this is the integral form of the kinetic Langevin equation (kinetic) of Dalalyan and Riou-Durand, dVt=−(γVt+u∇f(Lt)) dt+2γu dWtdV_t=-(\gamma V_t+u\nabla f(L_t))\,dt+\sqrt{2\gamma u}\,dW_tdVt​=−(γVt​+u∇f(Lt​))dt+2γu​dWt​, dLt=Vt dtdL_t=V_t\,dtdLt​=Vt​dt; the noise enters the velocity only. The statement is pathwise: BBB is an arbitrary continuous path, so it applies to every continuous Brownian trajectory, and also to continuous paths with values in a space of square-integrable random vectors.

The phase space X×X\mathcal X\times\mathcal XX×X is given here the maximum norm ∥(v,x)∥∞=max⁡{∥v∥,∥x∥}\|(v,x)\|_\infty=\max\{\|v\|,\|x\|\}∥(v,x)∥∞​=max{∥v∥,∥x∥}, which is equivalent to the Euclidean product norm and is used only for existence. The drift is shown to be Lipschitz for this norm, and existence follows from the Picard theorem for integral equations with Lipschitz drift and continuous additive forcing, proved for the overdamped Langevin equation. Uniqueness, probability laws and contraction estimates are not part of this section.

A map FFF between normed spaces is KKK-Lipschitz, K≥0K\ge0K≥0, if ∥F(s)−F(t)∥≤K∥s−t∥\|F(s)-F(t)\|\le K\|s-t\|∥F(s)−F(t)∥≤K∥s−t∥ for all s,ts,ts,t.
