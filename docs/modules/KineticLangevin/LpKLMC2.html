The random-Hessian L² analysis represents the KLMC2 sampler

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Hessian in the analysis is the bounded random operator obtained by evaluating the original Hessian at the random position. This section proves that each L² update and every L² iterate agree almost everywhere with the paper’s defined four-noise vector updates. Thus later stochastic estimates concern the sampler, including both Hessian-times-velocity and Hessian-times-noise corrections.

The lifted gradient and random Hessian have their original pointwise representatives. This identifies the full second-order Hilbert-space recursion with the defined four-response vector algorithm.

Statement and proof

01Read each affine Hessian correction pointwise §

Mathematical statement and proof

Scalar multiplication, subtraction and addition of L² classes agree with the corresponding operations on their representatives almost everywhere. Intersecting these finitely many identities proves the exact affine expression used by both KLMC2 coordinates has its original pointwise value.

The affine parts of the corrected formula are evaluated using one common set of representatives. Their finite almost-everywhere identities can be intersected. This ensures the full vector equality, including signs and scalar coefficients, holds at each sample point outside one null set.

02Identify the step and full chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted gradient has the original gradient as its representative. The proved random-operator construction similarly represents the Hessian evaluated at the random position, applied to velocity or to either Hessian correction noise. Substitution in the two affine coordinates identifies the complete L² step with the vector KLMC2 formula. Induction then proves every numerical iterate agrees with the original chain driven by exactly the same four Brownian responses.

The random Hessian acts pointwise on both velocity and correction-noise representatives. Substituting its representative formula identifies every term of the four-response update. Induction then identifies the numerical sequence at every fixed iteration.

03Connect to the exact Gaussian Markov chain law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Continuity of the original force and Hessian makes the full state-and-four-noise update measurable. Its deterministic recursion equals the general random recursion with the same update. Given a proved initial/entire-noise joint law, the generic Markov law theorem identifies every L² phase output with the corresponding defined Markov iterate. No replacement covariance or per-step independence assumption is introduced by the representative correspondence.

Measurability of the gradient, Hessian, and affine response operations makes the complete update measurable. Pushing the independent initialization-and-stream law through its recursion therefore gives the defined Markov law. The analytic Hilbert-space chain has exactly this law.
