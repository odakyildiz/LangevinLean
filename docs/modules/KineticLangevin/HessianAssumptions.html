Strong convexity and gradient smoothness from Hessian bounds

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The paper states its first two main results with Hessians bounded between mImImI and MIMIMI. The kinetic error theorems use strong convexity and a globally Lipschitz gradient. This section proves the exact implication from symmetric Hessian derivatives, without an additional Hessian-continuity premise.

The source Hessian inequalities are connected to the first and second derivatives along every segment. They imply precisely the strong-convexity and gradient-smoothness assumptions used by the kinetic sampling theorem.

01Derive both derivatives along every affine line §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let H(x)H(x)H(x) be the Fréchet derivative of ∇f\nabla f∇f. Along x+tux+tux+tu, the first derivative of fff is ⟨∇f(x+tu),u⟩\langle\nabla f(x+tu),u\rangle⟨∇f(x+tu),u⟩, and its derivative is ⟨H(x+tu)u,u⟩\langle H(x+tu)u,u\rangle⟨H(x+tu)u,u⟩. The argument constructs the affine-line derivative, composes it with the gradient representation of the derivative of fff, and applies the chain rule again to ∇f\nabla f∇f. The inner-product derivative and the Riesz representation reduce to precisely these scalar formulas.

On an affine line, the first derivative of the potential is the gradient paired with the line direction. Differentiating again gives the Hessian quadratic form in that direction. These are chain-rule derivatives, tying the matrix assumptions to one-dimensional convexity.

02Strong convexity from the lower Hessian bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

The definition of strong convexity is equivalent to convexity of g(x)=f(x)−m∥x∥2/2g(x)=f(x)-m\|x\|^2/2g(x)=f(x)−m∥x∥2/2. Restrict ggg to the line from xxx to yyy. Its second derivative is ⟨H(x+t(y−x))(y−x),y−x⟩−m∥y−x∥2≥0\langle H(x+t(y-x))(y-x),y-x\rangle-m\|y-x\|^2\ge0⟨H(x+t(y−x))(y−x),y−x⟩−m∥y−x∥2≥0. The argument derives both line derivatives using the previous result and the true squared-norm derivative, applies the one-dimensional convexity theorem, and evaluates the resulting Jensen inequality at line times 0 and 1. The affine identity x+b(y−x)=ax+byx+b(y-x)=ax+byx+b(y−x)=ax+by, when a+b=1a+b=1a+b=1, yields exactly strong convexity of f with constant m\text{strong convexity of }f\text{ with constant }mstrong convexity of f with constant m.

Subtracting m∥x∥2/2m\|x\|^2/2m∥x∥2/2 from the potential makes every line second derivative nonnegative. One-dimensional convexity on each segment therefore yields convexity of the shifted potential. Rearranging its Jensen inequality gives precisely mmm-strong convexity.

03An operator norm bound from the upper symmetric quadratic bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a symmetric positive semidefinite operator TTT, its operator norm is the supremum of the absolute Rayleigh quotient ∣⟨Tu,u⟩∣/∥u∥2|\langle Tu,u\rangle|/\|u\|^2∣⟨Tu,u⟩∣/∥u∥2. Positivity removes the absolute value; the upper Loewner bound makes every nonzero quotient at most MMM. The zero vector is treated explicitly. The argument applies the symmetric Rayleigh norm theorem and proves the quotient bound directly, obtaining ∥T∥≤M\|T\|\le M∥T∥≤M without diagonalization assumptions.

For a symmetric positive semidefinite Hessian, the operator norm equals its largest Rayleigh quotient. The upper quadratic-form bound controls every nonzero quotient by MMM. This converts a spectral assumption into the operator estimate required for the gradient mean-value bound.

04Apply the mean value theorem to the gradient derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each Hessian has operator norm at most MMM, by the preceding symmetric argument and the nonnegative lower bound. The Banach-space mean value theorem therefore gives ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤M∥x−y∥\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\le M\|x-y\|∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤M∥x−y∥. The argument rewrites the Fréchet derivative using the supplied Hessian derivative identity and converts the real norm bound to its nonnegative-real formulation. Together these conclusions give this global gradient Lipschitz bound with the strong-convexity conclusion. Thus the first-order premises in the sampling theorems follow from the source Hessian inequalities.

Integrating the gradient derivative along a segment, or applying the Banach-space mean-value inequality, gives gradient Lipschitz constant MMM. Together with the lower-Hessian argument, this verifies the first-order sampler hypotheses from the manuscript’s Hessian hypotheses.
