Kinetic integral solutions under measure-preserving changes of source

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Changing a probability source by a measure-preserving projection preserves the random variables and their output laws. This section proves that the lifted nonlinear drift and the selected kinetic integral solution commute with that change of source. It supplies coupling-independent continuous-flow laws without assuming samplewise continuity of an arbitrary L² representative.

Measure-preserving source changes preserve the kinetic integral equation because pullback is bounded linear and commutes with the pointwise drift. Uniqueness then identifies solutions constructed on different coupling spaces.

Statement and proof

01Define continuous linear source and phase pullbacks §

Mathematical statement and proof

Measure-preserving composition is a linear isometry on L². The source pullback is this isometry viewed as a continuous linear map, and the phase pullback applies it separately to velocity and position. Their representative theorems identify the resulting random variables with composition. Pushforward composition proves preservation of every output law. A further representative argument proves that two successive pullbacks equal pullback along the composed projection.

Changing probability source by a measure-preserving map defines an isometry on square-integrable variables. Apply it separately to velocity and position to obtain the phase pullback. Bounded linearity preserves continuity and commutes with the time integrals in the kinetic equation.

02Commute the nonlinear drift with pullback §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted vector field is pointwise application of the original Lipschitz field. The proof compares its representatives before and after source projection, obtaining exact equality in L². Combining this with linearity proves the kinetic drift commutes with the phase pullback, including both friction and force terms and the position equation.

The lifted drift is defined pointwise from the original gradient. Composing a representative with the source map therefore commutes with evaluating that gradient. Together with the affine phase terms, this proves that pullback preserves the nonlinear kinetic drift.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.3.

03The selected integral solutions §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the phase pullback to the original kinetic integral equation. Continuity and linearity commute it with the Bochner time integral, and the drift commutation identity produces the target-space kinetic equation. The transformed initial condition and additive input are exact. The proved integral-solution uniqueness theorem therefore identifies the selected target-space solution with the pulled original solution at every time in the horizon. This equality transfers laws between an independent initial-state/noise product and any larger initial-coupling extension.

The integral-equation identity proves this transformation for any continuous kinetic solution before specializing to selected flows.

Pulling back the solution equation gives a solution with the pulled-back initial phase and forcing. Uniqueness identifies it with the independently selected solution on the new source. This validates the coupling-space comparison of flows defined on different product spaces.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.
