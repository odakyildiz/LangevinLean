Bounds for bounded tests from the corrected invariant measure

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The rejected move can reverse a velocity of order one. A weak approximation therefore uses bounded complex-valued tests rather than assuming the rejected jump is proportional to the step. The exact corrected measure identity implies that one uncorrected proposal has test bias at most twice the test bound times its mean rejection probability.

Bounded tests permit rejection errors to be controlled by probability mass alone. This yields an argument from an exact corrected invariant measure to vanishing weak error under numerical refinement.

01Keep all acceptance and rejection mass §

Mathematical statement and proof

For measurable 0≤a≤10\le a\le10≤a≤1, the two weighted measures aπa\piaπ and (1−a)π(1-a)\pi(1−a)π sum exactly to π\piπ. The two nonnegative densities sum pointwise to one, so additivity of integration gives equality of the measures. In particular both weighted measures are bounded above by the original finite measure.

For each state, the accepted and rejected coefficients sum to one. Keeping both terms makes the corrected transition a probability kernel and its target identity exact. Dropping the rejected term would produce a subprobability expression and an incorrect invariance statement.

02The integral identity for the explicit invariant measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the already constructed corrected measure satisfies V#(aπ)+R#((1−a)π)=πV_\#(a\pi)+R_\#((1-a)\pi)=\piV#​(aπ)+R#​((1−a)π)=π. This identity is proved for the Verlet and reversal maps in the preceding module. For bounded measurable complex FFF, integration gives ∫F dπ=∫a(z)F(Vz) dπ+∫(1−a(z))F(Rz) dπ.\int F\,d\pi=\int a(z)F(Vz)\,d\pi+\int(1-a(z))F(Rz)\,d\pi.∫Fdπ=∫a(z)F(Vz)dπ+∫(1−a(z))F(Rz)dπ. First, the argument proves finiteness of each weighted measure from the exact mass split. The bound on FFF supplies integrability for both pushforward measures. It then applies the Bochner integral formula for a pushforward and the formula for a density-weighted integral; the acceptance and rejection factors are finite nonnegative real numbers.

An invariant measure equality implies equality of integrals for every bounded measurable test. This formulation is convenient for passing to numerical limits, because the error between corrected and uncorrected tests can be controlled pointwise by the rejection probability.

03Bound the entire rejected move through the test bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Subtract the invariant-test identity from the ordinary proposal expectation. The exact difference is ∫F(Vz) dπ−∫F dπ=∫(1−a(z)) [F(Vz)−F(Rz)] dπ.\int F(Vz)\,d\pi-\int F\,d\pi=\int(1-a(z))\,[F(Vz)-F(Rz)]\,d\pi.∫F(Vz)dπ−∫Fdπ=∫(1−a(z))[F(Vz)−F(Rz)]dπ. If ∥F∥≤C\|F\|\le C∥F∥≤C, the norm of the bracket is at most 2C2C2C, irrespective of the size of the rejected physical jump. Since 1−a≥01-a\ge01−a≥0, the norm of the integral is at most 2C∫(1−a) dπ2C\int(1-a)\,d\pi2C∫(1−a)dπ. The argument explicitly checks bounded integrability of the proposal test, accepted test, rejected test, and rejection factor, rewrites the subtraction under the integral, applies the norm-of-integral inequality, and integrates the pointwise triangle bound. Thus the velocity flip is fully accounted for.

If a test is bounded in absolute value by BBB, changing its argument changes its value by at most 2B2B2B. Multiplying this bound by the rejection probability controls the entire rejected move. No Lipschitz regularity of the test is required for this step.
