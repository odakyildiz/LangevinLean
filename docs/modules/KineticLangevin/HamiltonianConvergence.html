Prove Verlet converges to the actual Hamiltonian flow

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Exact reversibility and volume preservation of the Verlet map do not by themselves identify the continuous Hamiltonian flow. This module supplies that analytic limit. For a globally Lipschitz force, the actual Verlet iterates on a mesh of size T/(n+1)T/(n+1)T/(n+1) converge to every continuous Hamiltonian integral solution at time TTT. Specializing to the gradient force identifies the limit with the Picard-selected hamiltonianFlow. The proof uses a genuine local state-error calculation, uniform growth of an Euler comparison chain, a discrete Grönwall estimate, and the existing Euler convergence theorem. It needs no Hessian assumption.

01Control the elementary shears and their symmetric composition §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the standard maximum norm on phase pairs (v,x)(v,x)(v,x). A kick of duration h≥0h\ge0h≥0 is (1+Mh)(1+Mh)(1+Mh)-Lipschitz when the force is MMM-Lipschitz, and a drift is (1+h)(1+h)(1+h)-Lipschitz. ∥Verlet⁡h(z)−Verlet⁡h(w)∥≤(1+(M+1)2h)∥z−w∥.\|\operatorname{Verlet}_h(z)-\operatorname{Verlet}_h(w)\|\le\bigl(1+(M+1)^2h\bigr)\|z-w\|.∥Verleth​(z)−Verleth​(w)∥≤(1+(M+1)2h)∥z−w∥.

Lean first bounds the velocity and position projections by the phase norm. It rewrites each difference so the shared force difference is explicit, applies the triangle inequality and the force Lipschitz estimate, and handles the product norm using max_le. The Verlet estimate composes the three bounds; a scalar polynomial inequality controls the resulting product. This is a stability estimate for the actual symmetric map, not an assumed numerical property.
