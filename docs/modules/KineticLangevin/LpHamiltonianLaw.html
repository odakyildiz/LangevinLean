The Gibbs law and moments of the L2L^{2}L2 Hamiltonian reference

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This section verifies the reference-law and moment hypotheses for the Hamiltonian part of the OBABO bias argument. It constructs the L2L^{2}L2 Picard flow, identifies its finite-dimensional output law, derives exact Gibbs stationarity and velocity/score moments, and applies the proved local OOO–Verlet–OOO comparison. No stationary reference-law or local-error bound is postulated.

The second-mean Hamiltonian comparator is identified with the pointwise deterministic flow. Its exact target law then supplies the finite stationary moments needed to integrate the OBABO local state defect.

01Identify an L2L^{2}L2 limit with a pointwise numerical limit §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose L2L^{2}L2 phase variables SnS_nSn​ converge to SSS, and their representatives agree almost everywhere with measurable vector approximants fnf_nfn​ converging almost everywhere to YYY. For any continuous linear functional ℓ\ellℓ, the characteristic test eiℓ(z)e^{i\ell(z)}eiℓ(z) has norm one. Bounded dominated convergence identifies the characteristic-function limit of the pointwise approximants, while the proved L2L^{2}L2 characteristic continuity identifies the limit of the L2L^{2}L2 variables. Their equality yields L(S)=L(Y)\mathcal L(S)=\mathcal L(Y)L(S)=L(Y) through characteristic uniqueness. The next lemma proves the required almost-everywhere correspondence for every Verlet iterate by induction from the lifted kick and drift identities. Thus the two convergence arguments refer to precisely the same defined approximants.

A second-mean convergent sequence has a subsequence converging almost everywhere. If the same numerical maps converge pointwise to the deterministic Hamiltonian endpoint, the two limits coincide almost everywhere. This identifies the Hilbert-space limit with the intended pointwise flow.

02The coupled representatives almost everywhere §

Mathematical statement and proof

Marginal equality alone is insufficient when the local numerical error must be coupled with the same initial state and thermostat inputs. This stronger result identifies the representatives almost everywhere. Each component of an L2L^{2}L2-convergent phase sequence converges in measure. The known almost-everywhere equality transfers this convergence to the same pointwise Verlet approximants. Their almost-everywhere convergence also implies convergence in measure, because the source is a probability measure. Uniqueness of the limit in measure therefore gives equality almost everywhere of each component, hence of the phase pair.

Apply this argument to the L2L^{2}L2 Hamiltonian flow and its Verlet approximants. The result states that its representative at time ttt is almost everywhere the finite-dimensional Hamiltonian flow evaluated at the same representative of the initial state. Thus adjoining any initial state or thermostat variable preserves the coupling used in the local comparison; no equality of marginal laws is substituted for a joint-law argument.

The initial and numerical representatives are chosen on the same probability source. The verified stage identities identify them with the original vector algorithm. The resulting law statement concerns random variables, independently of any representative chosen for their equivalence classes.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.

03The L2L^{2}L2 Hamiltonian integral flow and its law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^{2}L2 force is pointwise application of the Lipschitz gradient; its global Lipschitz bound permits the existing Picard construction with zero friction and zero noise. Its specification gives a continuous global path, the prescribed initial value, and the Hamiltonian integral equation on the finite horizon. Verlet convergence holds in this Banach space. The same vector Verlet maps converge pointwise to the selected finite-dimensional Hamiltonian flow. The proved almost-everywhere pointwise identification and the composition rule for pushforwards give the exact law identity L(At)=(Ht)#L(A0)\mathcal L(A_t)=(H_t)_\#\mathcal L(A_0)L(At​)=(Ht​)#​L(A0​). This does not infer a pathwise identification from an unproved interchange of representatives and time integrals.

Solve the Hamiltonian integral equation in second mean and identify its representative with the pointwise selected flow. Pushing forward the known initial law then gives its exact endpoint law. For Gibbs initialization, the previously proved Hamiltonian invariance makes that law stationary.

04Stationarity and the velocity and force moment bounds §

Mathematical statement and proof

If the initial phase law is the constructed independent Gaussian-times-Gibbs target, the exact Hamiltonian invariance theorem and the derived output-law identity prove that the L2L^{2}L2 flow has that target law at every time. Projecting the integral equation with the continuous linear coordinate maps gives the velocity and kinematic component equations; commuting those maps with Bochner integrals is justified by continuity and interval integrability. Projecting the target law gives standard Gaussian velocity and normalized Gibbs position. The exact Gaussian coordinate calculation therefore yields ∥Vt∥L2=d\|V_t\|_{L^2}=\sqrt d∥Vt​∥L2​=d​, and the already proved Gibbs score estimate gives ∥∇f(Xt)∥L2≤Md\|\nabla f(X_t)\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Md}∥∇f(Xt​)∥L2​≤Md​. These are conclusions from the law, not supplied moment hypotheses.

The target factorization supplies the Gaussian velocity marginal and the Gibbs position marginal. Their finite moments control the velocity and force bounds along the stationary Hamiltonian reference. These are the quantities entering the Verlet local-error estimate.

05Apply the local OBABO comparison to this constructed stationary reference §

Mathematical statement and proof

Start the L2L^{2}L2 Hamiltonian path at the first thermostat half-step and assume its phase law is the normalized target, as supplied by thermostat invariance. Its continuity, initial value, velocity equation, kinematic equation, and uniform moments have all been proved above. The local OOO–Verlet–OOO versus OOO–Hamiltonian–OOO theorem therefore applies with C=dC=\sqrt dC=d​ and G=MdG=\sqrt{Md}G=Md​. It yields the weighted error bound

2h2(Md+Md+h4MMd).2h^2\left(\sqrt{Md}+\sqrt M\sqrt d+\frac{h}{4}\sqrt M\sqrt{Md}\right).2h2(Md​+M​d​+4h​M​Md​).

The two thermostat noises are retained, their common final contribution cancels, and the damping needs only ∣δ∣≤1|\delta|\le1∣δ∣≤1. This provides the local numerical error bound for the global sampling-bias estimate.

Apply the deterministic common-noise OBABO comparison to the constructed stationary reference variables. Integrating its phase bound gives the stated local root-mean-square cost. Thus the local kernel discrepancy uses Hamiltonian and thermostat laws.
