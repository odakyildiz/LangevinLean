The KLMC2 Wasserstein estimate with its exact dimension and tail terms

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This is the second-order sampling theorem for the defined four-kernel Gaussian chain. The continuous reference, its Gibbs law and Gaussian moments, fresh-noise independence, local Taylor and memory error, stochastic Hessian comparison, and output-law identities are proved internally. Taking the infimum over initial couplings yields the desired initial Wasserstein term.

The complete second-order sampler bound includes the exact fourth-moment dimension term and the repaired stochastic correction tail. Sharper-tail and printed-form corollaries retain their explicit cutoff and dimension restrictions.

Mathematical statement and proof

For every finite-cost initial plan, the constructed chain theorem bounds its L2L^{2}L2 position discrepancy from a stationary reference. The output law is independent of that plan. The reference endpoint has the Gibbs law. The L2L^{2}L2 transport-cost inequality therefore gives the same bound for the output Wasserstein distance. The preceding output-finiteness result proves that this Wasserstein distance is a finite real number. The all-couplings affine-infimum lemma then replaces each plan’s cost by the initial W2W_2W2​. 02The source footnote’s sharper tail §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source dimension restriction implies α≥5p\alpha\ge5pα≥5p. Hence (α−p)/8≥α/10=m2/(160M22h2)(\alpha-p)/8\ge\alpha/10=m^2/(160M_2^2h^2)(α−p)/8≥α/10=m2/(160M22​h2). Multiply this exponential comparison by the nonnegative tail coefficient and apply the exact sampler bound. This gives the chain theorem with the sharper inverse-step-square Gaussian tail, not merely a scalar observation detached from the algorithm.

The cutoff restriction implies p≤α/5p\le\alpha/5p≤α/5, which turns the exponent into an inverse-square-step bound. The coefficient multiplying the exponential is nonnegative, so replacing the exponential by this larger explicit upper bound preserves the sampling inequality.
