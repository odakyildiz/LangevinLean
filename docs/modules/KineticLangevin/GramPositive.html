The literal exponential-integrator covariance is positive definite

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The two-kernel covariance is strictly positive definite on every positive-duration step. This supplies the genuine Cholesky input required by the first particle algorithm, rather than merely positive semidefiniteness of an abstract Gaussian covariance.

01Use strict positivity of the integrated exponential §

Mathematical statement and proof

The first integrated response is ψ1(h)=∫0he−γt dt\psi_1(h)=\int_0^he^{-\gamma t}\,dtψ1​(h)=∫0h​e−γtdt. For h>0h>0h>0, the continuous integrand is positive everywhere, hence this integral is strictly positive for every real γ\gammaγ. Lean invokes strict positivity of a continuous interval integral, using its positive value at zero. This avoids dividing by friction and includes γ=0\gamma=0γ=0.

03Integrate the square to prove strict positive definiteness §

Mathematical statement and proof

The already proved Gram identity is z⊤Chz=∫0h(z0ψ0(t)+z1ψ1(t))2 dt.z^\top C_hz=\int_0^h\bigl(z_0\psi_0(t)+z_1\psi_1(t)\bigr)^2\,dt.z⊤Ch​z=∫0h​(z0​ψ0​(t)+z1​ψ1​(t))2dt. The integrand is continuous and nonnegative, and the previous result supplies a point where it is positive. Its integral is therefore strictly positive for every nonzero zzz. Lean rewrites the matrix quadratic form as the exact scalar integral, proves its continuity from the finite kernel sum, and combines strict integral positivity with the proved covariance symmetry.
