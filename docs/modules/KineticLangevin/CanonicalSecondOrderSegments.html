Integral identities and stationary laws of the second-order reference segments

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Each local second-order comparison uses a selected kinetic solution on a specified Brownian interval. This section exposes its continuous integral equation, exact endpoint stitching, stationary phase law, force-mild formula, and kinematic identity.

The global stationary reference supplies every local path equation, endpoint identity, and moment marginal needed by the corrected-step analysis. No separate reference-law premise remains at each numerical step.

03Transfer global stationarity to every local segment point §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference segment is already proved equal to the single global reference solution at time nh+tnh+tnh+t. Choose horizon T=(n+1)hT=(n+1)hT=(n+1)h; the segment time lies in [0,T][0,T][0,T]. The argument rewrites the local solution using this stitching identity and applies the proved unconditional Gibbs stationarity of the global constructed solution. Thus the full phase law at every segment point, including both endpoints, is N(0,I)⊗πN(0,I)\otimes\piN(0,I)⊗π. This supplies the stationary moment hypotheses for the local second-order Taylor estimate.

Global phase stationarity gives the target position and Gaussian velocity marginals at every local time. Exact segment identification transfers those laws without a new stationarity assumption at each restart. The required moment bounds therefore hold throughout the local integration interval.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
