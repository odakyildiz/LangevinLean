Verlet energy-error and rejection bounds

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The exact Hamiltonian defect proved earlier becomes quantitative under a globally Lipschitz gradient. The proof uses only first derivatives: two-sided Taylor control bounds the trapezoidal force work, while a norm factorization bounds the force-square correction. These estimates concern the algebraic Verlet map and can control the rejection mass of the invariant corrected approximation.

Symmetric Taylor expansions match the two half kicks of Verlet and cancel the leading energy terms. The remaining bound is expressed through velocity and force moments suitable for Gibbs integration.

01The two-sided Taylor remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

The smooth Taylor upper bound gives f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩≤M∥y−x∥2/2f(y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle\le M\|y-x\|^2/2f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩≤M∥y−x∥2/2. Apply the same theorem to −f-f−f. Its gradient is −∇f-\nabla f−∇f, proved by rewriting the Fréchet derivative and the linear Riesz inverse, and its Lipschitz constant remains MMM. The second application gives the reverse inequality, so the absolute remainder has the same bound. The argument explicitly rewrites the negative gradient and combines the upper and lower scalar inequalities with the two-sided absolute-value bound.

The fundamental theorem along the segment gives f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩f(y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\ranglef(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩. Gradient Lipschitzness bounds its absolute value by M∥y−x∥2/2M\|y-x\|^2/2M∥y−x∥2/2. The two-sided form is needed because numerical energy can increase or decrease.

02Average Taylor errors from both endpoints §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write d=y−xd=y-xd=y−x, rx=f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),d⟩r_x=f(y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),d\ranglerx​=f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),d⟩, and ry=f(x)−f(y)+⟨∇f(y),d⟩r_y=f(x)-f(y)+\langle\nabla f(y),d\ranglery​=f(x)−f(y)+⟨∇f(y),d⟩. The trapezoidal force-work error is exactly (rx−ry)/2(r_x-r_y)/2(rx​−ry​)/2. Both remainders have absolute value at most M∥d∥2/2M\|d\|^2/2M∥d∥2/2. The triangle inequality therefore gives ∣f(y)−f(x)−12⟨∇f(x)+∇f(y),d⟩∣≤M2∥d∥2.\left|f(y)-f(x)-\tfrac12\langle\nabla f(x)+\nabla f(y),d\rangle\right|\le\tfrac M2\|d\|^2.​f(y)−f(x)−21​⟨∇f(x)+∇f(y),d⟩​≤2M​∥d∥2. One proves the algebraic average identity after expanding the inner product, reverses the displacement in the second Taylor bound, and applies the absolute-value triangle inequality.

Writing the potential difference once from each endpoint and averaging produces a trapezoidal force term. Its remainder is controlled by the same segment displacement. This symmetric arrangement matches the two force evaluations of velocity Verlet.

03Control the squared-force correction §

Mathematical statement and proof

Factor ∥g(y)∥2−∥g(x)∥2=(∥g(y)∥−∥g(x)∥)(∥g(y)∥+∥g(x)∥)\|g(y)\|^2-\|g(x)\|^2=(\|g(y)\|-\|g(x)\|)(\|g(y)\|+\|g(x)\|)∥g(y)∥2−∥g(x)∥2=(∥g(y)∥−∥g(x)∥)(∥g(y)∥+∥g(x)∥). The reverse triangle inequality and Lipschitz continuity bound the first factor by M∥y−x∥M\|y-x\|M∥y−x∥. The ordinary triangle inequality bounds the second by 2∥g(x)∥+M∥y−x∥2\|g(x)\|+M\|y-x\|2∥g(x)∥+M∥y−x∥. Multiplying these nonnegative estimates proves the displayed force-square bound. The argument derives the endpoint-force growth estimate before applying multiplication of nonnegative inequalities, so every sign condition is checked.

The kinetic-square expansion contains differences of squared force norms. Factor each difference as an inner product of the force difference and force sum. Lipschitz continuity controls the first factor; the second is bounded by the endpoint force magnitudes.

04Bound the complete Verlet energy defect §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set w=v−h∇f(x)/2w=v-h\nabla f(x)/2w=v−h∇f(x)/2, y=x+hwy=x+hwy=x+hw. The exact identity from the Hamiltonian module writes the defect as a trapezoidal work error plus h2(∥∇f(y)∥2−∥∇f(x)∥2)/8h^2(\|\nabla f(y)\|^2-\|\nabla f(x)\|^2)/8h2(∥∇f(y)∥2−∥∇f(x)∥2)/8. Since ∥y−x∥=∣h∣∥w∥\|y-x\|=|h|\|w\|∥y−x∥=∣h∣∥w∥, the preceding two lemmas give ∣H(Vhz)−H(z)∣≤M2h2∥w∥2+h28M∣h∣∥w∥(2∥∇f(x)∥+M∣h∣∥w∥).|H(V_hz)-H(z)|\le\frac M2h^2\|w\|^2+\frac{h^2}{8}M|h|\|w\|\bigl(2\|\nabla f(x)\|+M|h|\|w\|\bigr).∣H(Vh​z)−H(z)∣≤2M​h2∥w∥2+8h2​M∣h∣∥w∥(2∥∇f(x)∥+M∣h∣∥w∥). The proof substitutes the intermediate stage, changes the inner product to the displacement form, applies the triangle inequality to the two exact defect terms, and simplifies ∣h∣2=h2|h|^2=h^2∣h∣2=h2. The leading term is quadratic in the step under only gradient Lipschitz regularity.

Combine the potential and kinetic expansions before estimating their absolute values. Their leading terms cancel because the Verlet stages use matching half kicks. The remaining estimate is expressed in terms of velocity and force magnitudes so it can later be integrated under a Gibbs moment bound.
