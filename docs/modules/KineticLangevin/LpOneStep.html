Gaussian velocity moments and the L² local-error inequality

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This section proves the exact p\sqrt pp​ reference velocity norm from a standard Gaussian marginal, then combines the L² nonlinear contraction with the exponential-integrator error. Its hypotheses display the Gaussian reference marginal and exact integral solutions; stationarity proving that marginal is a separate theorem.

The reference Gaussian marginal supplies the dimension-dependent velocity radius. Combining it with the auxiliary-flow comparison gives the first-order one-step estimate on the coupling space.

Statement and proof

01The exact velocity moment from its Gaussian law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The standard Gaussian vector is the pushforward of independent scalar N(0,1)N(0,1)N(0,1) coordinates by the Euclidean identification. Compose that pushforward with coordinate evaluation; the finite-product marginal formula reduces its law to N(0,1)N(0,1)N(0,1), since every other coordinate measure has mass one. If an L² velocity random variable has this standard Gaussian vector law, composition of laws gives the scalar coordinate laws. Summation of the scalar Gaussian second moments then yields ∥V∥L2=#ι\|V\|_{L^2}=\sqrt{\#\iota}∥V∥L2​=#ι​. Thus the reference moment is derived from the explicit distribution rather than supplied as a numerical norm assumption.

A standard Gaussian vector in dimension ppp has squared Euclidean norm equal to a sum of ppp unit-variance coordinate squares. Hence its second moment is ppp and its root-mean-square radius is p\sqrt pp​. Stationarity supplies this same radius at every reference time.

02The stochastic one-step inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let AAA be the auxiliary exact L² path starting at the numerical phase point, and RRR a synchronously driven exact reference. Their transformed L² distance is at most its initial value eee, because e−ms/γ≤1e^{-ms/\gamma}\le1e−ms/γ≤1 for nonnegative time. Velocity projection and the exact Gaussian reference moment therefore give ∥Asv∥L2≤p+e\|A_s^v\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt p+e∥Asv​∥L2​≤p​+e. Apply the mild-equation local-error theorem with this velocity bound: the numerical exponential update differs from AhA_hAh​ by at most 3Mh2(p+e)/43Mh^2(\sqrt p+e)/43Mh2(p​+e)/4. Insert AhA_hAh​ with the transformed Hilbert triangle inequality and contract its distance to RhR_hRh​. The result is enext≤e−mh/γe+34Mh2(p+e)e_{\mathrm{next}}\le e^{-mh/\gamma}e+\frac34Mh^2(\sqrt p+e)enext​≤e−mh/γe+43​Mh2(p​+e). Because the ambient space is L², the Bochner-integral and Hilbert triangle estimates are precisely the stochastic Minkowski estimates required by the paper.

More generally, suppose the reference has an explicit reference velocity radius C≥0C\ge0C≥0. A uniform bound ∥Vtref∥L2≤C\|V_t^{\rm ref}\|_{L^2}\le C∥Vtref​∥L2​≤C supplies C+ekC+e_kC+ek​ in the local defect and the terminal bias Mh2C/mMh\sqrt2C/mMh2​C/m. The standard-Gaussian statement follows by taking with the radius p\sqrt pp​ proved from its marginal law. This same shared theorem applies to particle temperature 1/N1/N1/N, using the separately proved thermal radius (dθ+Ndx)/N\sqrt{(d_\theta+Nd_x)/N}(dθ​+Ndx​)/N​; the same local-error calculation applies.

At a given step, contraction controls the auxiliary-reference gap and the freezing estimate controls the numerical-auxiliary gap. The auxiliary velocity radius is at most p\sqrt pp​ plus the current transformed discrepancy. Combining the two inequalities gives the affine recurrence used in the global theorem.
