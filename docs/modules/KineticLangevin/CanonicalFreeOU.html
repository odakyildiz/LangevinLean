The Gaussian marginal of the free response from a stationary reference

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Gaussian cutoff in the repaired second-order proof applies to a free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path on each reference interval. Its marginal law follows from proved stationarity and proved independence of future Brownian increments from the past solution.

Proved stationarity and past/future independence give the Gaussian marginal of the free-OU response starting from the reference. This fact holds at grid points and arbitrary continuous restart times.

02Apply the same result at arbitrary continuous reference times §

Mathematical statement and proof

The global reference solution has the same proven past/future joint law at every a∈[0,T]a\in[0,T]a∈[0,T]. The identical Gaussian calculation therefore proves the free-OU response beginning at an arbitrary continuous reference time is standard Gaussian at every later duration. This supports local cutoff estimates independently of a chosen numerical grid. Neither statement assumes the desired marginal or an assumed adaptedness property; both invoke the already proved constructed independence and stationarity results.

The proof uses only stationarity at the restart time and independence of the following interval. It therefore does not depend on the restart being a grid point. This flexibility supports local estimates at any reference time in the finite horizon.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
