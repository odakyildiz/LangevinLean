The second-order one-step coupling inequality

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

These results connect the defined four-kernel KLMC2 update to the stationary kinetic segment and combine the local and repaired comparison estimates. Every moment and error constant has already been derived from path equations and Gaussian/Gibbs marginals.

The exact local Taylor defect and repaired stochastic comparison are combined through an auxiliary exact path. Their compatible step restrictions give the second-order coupling recurrence.

01The local friction restriction from the source step size §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume 5γMh≤m5\gamma Mh\le m5γMh≤m, m≤Mm\le Mm≤M, and M>0M>0M>0. Combining the first two inequalities gives 5γMh≤M5\gamma Mh\le M5γMh≤M. Division by the positive MMM yields

γh≤15.\gamma h\le\frac15.γh≤51​.

This is the small-time condition used to combine the cubic velocity and quartic position defects into the transformed local bound with coefficient one quarter. Thus the source step restriction implies the local restriction needed alongside the synchronous-comparison estimate. Every division uses a positive denominator.

02Bound the local error of the defined step §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the identity between the defined four-response update and the corrected free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck endpoint. The initial Gaussian velocity and the fresh filtered Brownian interval form the complete free response. The stationary reference satisfies the mild velocity and kinematic integral equations and has Gaussian-times-Gibbs marginals.

The local Taylor and force-memory estimates therefore give, for the stated small-time range,

∥Tγ,0(Z^t−Zt)∥L2≤t34[M2p2+2p+MMp].\left\|T_{\gamma,0}\bigl(\widehat Z_t-Z_t\bigr)\right\|_{L^2}\le\frac{t^3}{4}\left[M_2\sqrt{p^2+2p}+M\sqrt{Mp}\right].​Tγ,0​(Zt​−Zt​)​L2​≤4t3​[M2​p2+2p​+MMp​].

The exact coefficient p2+2pp^2+2pp2+2p is the stationary Gaussian fourth moment and remains valid in every dimension. The term MMpM\sqrt{Mp}MMp​ uses the stationary Gibbs score second moment. The four-response identity proves that this estimate applies to the numerical update, with both deterministic and stochastic Hessian corrections included.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

03Combine local defect and repaired synchronous comparison §

Mathematical statement and proof

Insert the numerical update started at the stationary reference state between the numerical output and the exact reference endpoint. The triangle inequality for the transformed norm separates the comparison of the two numerical updates from the local defect at the reference state.

The synchronous comparison includes both fresh stochastic Hessian-difference terms. Adding the local bound gives the one-step inequality with coefficient

1−mt2γ+M2α t2γ,1-\frac{mt}{2\gamma}+\frac{M_2\sqrt\alpha\,t^2}{\gamma},1−2γmt​+γM2​α​t2​,

tail contribution 2t2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8\sqrt2t^2(M-m)e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}2​t2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8, and local contribution

t34[M2p2+2p+MMp].\frac{t^3}{4}\left[M_2\sqrt{p^2+2p}+M\sqrt{Mp}\right].4t3​[M2​p2+2p​+MMp​].

The segment integral equations and marginal-law hypotheses in this comparison are proved for the constructed Brownian reference before the final sampler theorem is applied. This insertion combines two estimates for the same innovations and reference segment.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.
