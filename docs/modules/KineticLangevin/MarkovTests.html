Bounded tests and the independent-noise transition operator

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

To prove that a continuous evolution preserves its Gibbs distribution, approximate it by the specified random steps. A small one-step weak error at the Gibbs measure must be propagated through those steps. The dual transition integrates the next test value against the independent fresh noise. Its measurability, uniform bound and exact relation to the forward law are proved here.

Fresh-noise independence identifies explicit random recursions with iterated transition operators. Probability averaging preserves test bounds, making one-step weak errors accumulate linearly through the chain.

Statement and proof

01Integrate the next test against the fresh noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a measurable step map S(x,ξ)S(x,\xi)S(x,ξ) and noise probability QQQ, define PF(x)=∫F(S(x,ξ))Q(dξ)PF(x)=\int F(S(x,\xi))Q(d\xi)PF(x)=∫F(S(x,ξ))Q(dξ). The measurable parameter-integral theorem proves that PFPFPF is measurable. If ∥F∥≤C\lVert F\rVert\le C∥F∥≤C everywhere, the norm-of-integral inequality gives ∥PF(x)∥≤∫C dQ=C\lVert PF(x)\rVert\le\int C\,dQ=C∥PF(x)∥≤∫CdQ=C. Independence gives the product measure used by the Markov update.

Conditioning on the present state, integrate a test of the next updated state against the independent fresh-noise law. The resulting function is the transition operator applied to that test. Its uniform bound is no larger than the original test bound.

02The duality identity for the defined recursion §

Mathematical statement and proof

The law update is the pushforward of μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q by SSS. The pushforward integration formula and Fubini give ∫F d(μP)=∫PF dμ\int F\,d(\mu P)=\int PF\,d\mu∫Fd(μP)=∫PFdμ. Boundedness on a probability space supplies integrability. Induction proves that every PnFP^nFPnF remains measurable and bounded by the original CCC, and that ∫F d(μPn)=∫PnF dμ\int F\,d(\mu P^n)=\int P^nF\,d\mu∫Fd(μPn)=∫PnFdμ. Thus the dual operator describes the law recursion rather than an assumed sequence of measures.

Independence of the next innovation from the previous history gives the one-step conditional expectation identity. Iterating it identifies expectations of the explicit recursion with iterated transition operators. This is the dual relation between the pathwise chain and its Markov laws.

03Accumulate one-step weak errors without losing the test bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose every measurable test bounded by CCC has one-step defect at π\piπ at most δ\deltaδ. Apply this estimate to PnFP^nFPnF, whose bound is still CCC. Insert and subtract ∫PnF dπ\int P^nF\,d\pi∫PnFdπ, apply the triangle inequality, and use the induction hypothesis. The resulting estimate is ∣∫F d(πPn)−∫F dπ∣≤nδ\left|\int F\,d(\pi P^n)-\int F\,d\pi\right|\le n\delta​∫Fd(πPn)−∫Fdπ​≤nδ. No exact invariance is assumed. In the splitting application the rejection bound is quadratic in the time step, so nδ→0n\delta\to0nδ→0 on a fixed time interval.

Telescope the difference between numerical and reference test expectations over the number of steps. Each propagated test retains its uniform bound because transition operators average probability measures. Consequently a uniform one-step weak error accumulates at most linearly in the step count.
