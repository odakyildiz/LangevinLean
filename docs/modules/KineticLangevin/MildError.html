The integral expression for the exponential-step error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The local errors are explicit integrals of changes in the force. This section proves that these integrals really are the errors of the defined exponential step when compared with a mild exact velocity and its integrated position. The variation-of-constants equation is an explicit input here; constructing its Brownian convolution and deriving it from the original integral equation remain distinct steps.

Identifying the exact mild response with the defined update is essential before estimating a numerical defect. The two paths use the same filtered input, so their discrepancy is an ordinary nonlinear-force integral.

02Integrating the frozen velocity gives the position formula §

Mathematical statement and proof

The frozen position is x+ψ1(t)v−ψ2(t)gx+\psi_1(t)v-\psi_2(t)gx+ψ1​(t)v−ψ2​(t)g. Its derivative is the frozen velocity ψ0(t)v−ψ1(t)g\psi_0(t)v-\psi_1(t)gψ0​(t)v−ψ1​(t)g, and its value at zero is xxx. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives ∫0tVfrozen(s)ds=Xfrozen(t)−x\int_0^t V_{\rm frozen}(s)ds=X_{\rm frozen}(t)-x∫0t​Vfrozen​(s)ds=Xfrozen​(t)−x. The argument invokes the already proved derivative theorem and proves integrability from continuity of the coefficients. The identity holds also at zero friction because the coefficients were defined by integrals.

The position equation is obtained by integrating the velocity formula. Interchanging the two deterministic integrals produces the first primitive response kernel. This explains why the position defect has an additional time factor and why the position noise is the primitive of the velocity response.

03Velocity and position use the same noise convolution §

Mathematical statement and proof

The position innovation is ∫0hBsds\int_0^h B_sds∫0h​Bs​ds, the time integral of the same velocity convolution. If xh−x0=∫0hVsdsx_h-x_0=\int_0^hV_sdsxh​−x0​=∫0h​Vs​ds, integrate the velocity error identity to obtain the position defect Dx(h)=∫0hDv(s)dsD_x(h)=\int_0^hD_v(s)dsDx​(h)=∫0h​Dv​(s)ds. Thus

Kh(V0,x0;Bh,∫0hBsds)−(Vh,xh)=(Dv(h),Dx(h)).\mathcal K_h(V_0,x_0;B_h,\int_0^hB_sds)-(V_h,x_h)=(D_v(h),D_x(h)).Kh​(V0​,x0​;Bh​,∫0h​Bs​ds)−(Vh​,xh​)=(Dv​(h),Dx​(h)).

One proves the velocity identity at every point in the interval, changes the integrand using that pointwise equality, distributes the integral over sums and differences, and uses the frozen-velocity integral and the kinematic identity. The two coordinate equalities together give the phase-space equality. No stochastic error estimate is hidden inside the definitions.

The same convolution appears on the exact and numerical sides of the coupling. Thus the comparison involves no independent Gaussian variance penalty. Correctly retaining their common source is essential; merely matching separate marginal covariances would not justify pathwise cancellation.

04Bound the defined step in the transformed norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the exact step-error identity to replace the numerical-minus-exact endpoint discrepancy by the explicit pair (Dv,Dx)(D_v,D_x)(Dv​,Dx​). The local-error module has already integrated the kinematic velocity bound to prove E(Dv,Dx)≤(3MCh2/4)2\mathcal E(D_v,D_x)\le(3MC h^2/4)^2E(Dv​,Dx​)≤(3MCh2/4)2 under γh≤1/4\gamma h\le1/4γh≤1/4. The norm-square identity and nonnegativity allow comparison of the nonnegative transformed norm and its energy square to take square roots, giving ∥step−exact∥T≤3MCh2/4\|\text{step}-\text{exact}\|_T\le3MC h^2/4∥step−exact∥T​≤3MCh2/4. Every input is visible: the mild velocity equation, continuous common convolution noise, the kinematic identity, and the uniform velocity norm bound. No stochastic moment or stationary law is asserted here.

Combine the explicit velocity and position remainder bounds before taking the transformed norm. The triangle inequality in that norm separates the two coordinates with their friction coefficient. The bound applies to the exponential update because that map equals the frozen-force response.
