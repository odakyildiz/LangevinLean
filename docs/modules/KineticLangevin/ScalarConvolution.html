Scalar convolution and primitive identities

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The two general coefficient identities are proved for every pair of natural indices. The primitive identity starts at φ1\varphi _{1}φ1​, as required by the definition φ0=0\varphi _{0}=0φ0​=0; the convolution identity includes both zero indices. The proofs hold for every real friction and time.

Integration by parts transfers primitive indices between convolution factors. The resulting friction and derivative recurrences give the exact correction relations, with the base-index exception stated explicitly.

01Move a primitive between the two convolution factors §

Mathematical statement and proof

Differentiate ψk+1(s)ψj+1(t−s)\psi_{k+1}(s)\psi_{j+1}(t-s)ψk+1​(s)ψj+1​(t−s). Its derivative is ψk(s)ψj+1(t−s)−ψk+1(s)ψj(t−s)\psi_k(s)\psi_{j+1}(t-s)-\psi_{k+1}(s)\psi_j(t-s)ψk​(s)ψj+1​(t−s)−ψk+1​(s)ψj​(t−s). Both boundary products vanish because every positive-index response vanishes at zero. One proves the derivative by the product and chain rules, proves both continuous terms integrable, and applies the fundamental theorem. Therefore ∫0tψk(s)ψj+1(t−s) ds=∫0tψk+1(s)ψj(t−s) ds.\int_0^t\psi_k(s)\psi_{j+1}(t-s)\,ds=\int_0^t\psi_{k+1}(s)\psi_j(t-s)\,ds.∫0t​ψk​(s)ψj+1​(t−s)ds=∫0t​ψk+1​(s)ψj​(t−s)ds. Induction moves the entire second index to the first. At second index zero the defining exponential convolution gives exactly φk+1\varphi_{k+1}φk+1​, proving φk+j+1=ψk∗ψj\varphi_{k+j+1}=\psi_k*\psi_jφk+j+1​=ψk​∗ψj​ for all k,j≥0k,j\ge0k,j≥0.

The boundary terms vanish because positive-index response primitives are zero at the origin. Integration by parts can therefore transfer one primitive from one convolution factor to the other without a remainder. Repeating this operation yields the full index-addition convolution identity.

02The full friction recurrence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the just-proved identity with j=1j=1j=1, multiply the integral by γ\gammaγ, and substitute γψ1(t−s)=1−ψ0(t−s)\gamma\psi_1(t-s)=1-\psi_0(t-s)γψ1​(t−s)=1−ψ0​(t−s). Splitting the scalar integral yields φk+1(t)+γφk+2(t)=ψk+1(t).\varphi_{k+1}(t)+\gamma\varphi_{k+2}(t)=\psi_{k+1}(t).φk+1​(t)+γφk+2​(t)=ψk+1​(t). The proof establishes continuity and integrability before splitting; no division by friction is needed. The recurrence therefore includes γ=0\gamma=0γ=0.

The friction relation is derived by multiplying inside an integral and substituting the division-free balance for the first response. Since no friction denominator is introduced, the identity remains valid at zero friction. This matters for uniform scaling and correction formulas.

03Differentiate and integrate the correction coefficients §

Mathematical statement and proof

The factored definition φk+1(t)=e−γt∫0teγsψk(s) ds\varphi_{k+1}(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\int_0^te^{\gamma s}\psi_k(s)\,dsφk+1​(t)=e−γt∫0t​eγsψk​(s)ds has derivative ψk(t)−γφk+1(t)\psi_k(t)-\gamma\varphi_{k+1}(t)ψk​(t)−γφk+1​(t). The argument applies the fundamental theorem, product rule, and exact exponential cancellation. At the next index the friction recurrence reduces this derivative to φk+1(t)\varphi_{k+1}(t)φk+1​(t). Since the next correction vanishes at zero, integrating gives φk+2(t)=∫0tφk+1(s) ds.\varphi_{k+2}(t)=\int_0^t\varphi_{k+1}(s)\,ds.φk+2​(t)=∫0t​φk+1​(s)ds. This is the first source identity with its necessary index restriction: the printed assertion at k=0k=0k=0 would incorrectly equate positive φ1(t)=te−γt\varphi_1(t)=te^{-\gamma t}φ1​(t)=te−γt with the integral of φ0=0\varphi_0=0φ0​=0.

The derivative formula has a base-index exception. At positive indices the friction recurrence converts the forcing-minus-damping derivative into the preceding correction response. Checking that exception explicitly prevents an incorrect primitive identity at index zero.
