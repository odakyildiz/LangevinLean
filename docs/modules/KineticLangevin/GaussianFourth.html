Exact Gaussian fourth moments for the Hessian correction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Hessian-Lipschitz Taylor error is quadratic in the position increment. Its L² bound therefore needs a fourth moment. The exact standard Gaussian value is p2+2pp^2+2pp2+2p; retaining both terms is necessary in dimension one. These identities are proved for the actual Gaussian measures and transferred to any realization by its proved law.

01Differentiate the actual scalar moment-generating function four times §

Mathematical statement and proof

A centered Gaussian of variance vvv has moment-generating function evt2/2e^{vt^2/2}evt2/2. Successive derivatives are vtevt2/2vt e^{vt^2/2}vtevt2/2, (v+v2t2)evt2/2(v+v^2t^2)e^{vt^2/2}(v+v2t2)evt2/2, and (3v2t+v3t3)evt2/2(3v^2t+v^3t^3)e^{vt^2/2}(3v2t+v3t3)evt2/2. The fourth derivative at zero is 3v23v^23v2. Lean constructs each derivative from the power, multiplication and exponential rules, then invokes the proved theorem identifying the derivative of a Gaussian moment-generating function with its moment. This also includes variance zero.

02Separate diagonal fourth moments from independent cross moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

For G=(G1,…,Gp)G=(G_1,\ldots,G_p)G=(G1​,…,Gp​), expand ∥G∥4=∑i,jGi2Gj2\lVert G\rVert^4=\sum_{i,j}G_i^2G_j^2∥G∥4=∑i,j​Gi2​Gj2​. A diagonal term has expectation three by the scalar theorem. Distinct coordinates are independent under the constructed product Gaussian measure, so their squared product has expectation 1⋅1=11\cdot1=11⋅1=1. Finite integrability follows from fourth moments and Gi2Gj2≤Gi4+Gj4G_i^2G_j^2\le G_i^4+G_j^4Gi2​Gj2​≤Gi4​+Gj4​. The sum is 3p+p(p−1)=p2+2p3p+p(p-1)=p^2+2p3p+p(p−1)=p2+2p. Lean first works on the actual product of scalar Gaussians, then uses its proved Euclidean standard-Gaussian pushforward identity. Empty coordinate sets are included.

03Transfer the moment to the coupled stationary velocity §

Mathematical statement and proof

If an actual random vector has the standard Gaussian law, the pushforward integration identity gives the same norm fourth moment on its original probability space. Applying this to each stationary kinetic velocity supplies exactly the p2+2p\sqrt{p^2+2p}p2+2p​ factor in the second-order Taylor remainder. No independence between this velocity and the numerical position discrepancy is assumed.
