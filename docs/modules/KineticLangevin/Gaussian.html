Construct correlated Gaussian blocks and an independent noise stream

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A covariance matrix alone is insufficient to define a random algorithm. This module constructs actual centered finite-dimensional Gaussian probability measures with the Gram covariance and an infinite product giving one independent block per time and spatial coordinate. Correlation is retained within each block. The exact covariance theorem requires a nonnegative step and continuous kernels; the general Gaussian constructor remains a probability measure even outside that parameter regime.

01Construct the centered Gaussian probability measure and its vector moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a finite kernel index set, kernelGaussian is Mathlib's multivariate Gaussian with mean zero and the defined Gram matrix. The named instances establish Gaussianity and total probability mass using the distribution constructor. The mean theorem computes its vector expectation as zero, and kernelGaussian_memLp invokes the finite Gaussian second-moment theorem. These are derived properties of a specified probability measure, rather than moment fields of an abstract noise source. They hold even if the covariance argument is degenerate; identifying its entries with the requested matrix uses the positive-semidefinite theorem below.

02Evaluate coordinate means, moments and exact covariances §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each coordinate projection is a continuous linear functional. Commuting it with the vector integral proves zero coordinate mean, and composing the finite-second-moment identity with that functional proves coordinate L² integrability. For continuous kernels and h≥0h\ge0h≥0, Gram positivity permits the multivariate-Gaussian covariance formula:

Cov⁡(Xi,Xj)=∫0hki(t)kj(t)dt,Var⁡(Xi)=∫0hki(t)2dt.\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=\int_0^hk_i(t)k_j(t)dt,\quad \operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\int_0^hk_i(t)^2dt.Cov(Xi​,Xj​)=∫0h​ki​(t)kj​(t)dt,Var(Xi​)=∫0h​ki​(t)2dt.

The two distribution theorems receive the already proved positive-semidefinite matrix witness. The variance proof only rewrites a product as a square. This identifies the exact correlated coordinate law required by the integrators, while its representation as actual Brownian stochastic integrals is a separate identity.

03Instantiate the two-block and four-block kinetic laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

klmcGaussian uses the kernel vector (ψ0,ψ1)(\psi_0,\psi_1)(ψ0​,ψ1​); klmc2Gaussian uses (ψ0,ψ1,φ2,φ3)(\psi_0,\psi_1,\varphi_2,\varphi_3)(ψ0​,ψ1​,φ2​,φ3​). Separate spatial coordinates will use separate copies of these entire blocks, rather than separate copies of their individual components.

04Build one independent Gaussian block for each time and coordinate §

Mathematical statement and proof

The path space is Ω=(Rι)N×Fin(d)\Omega=(\mathbb R^{\iota})^{\mathbb N\times\mathrm{Fin}(d)}Ω=(Rι)N×Fin(d). Define its law as the infinite product of the kernel Gaussian. Evaluation at (n,i)(n,i)(n,i) is measurable and has exactly the original Gaussian law, by the infinite-product marginal theorem. The coordinate-evaluation family is jointly independent by iIndepFun_infinitePi. Thus a time step retains all within-block covariance while distinct iteration–coordinate pairs are independent. The product's probability instance establishes total mass one; the HasLaw proof records both measurability and the exact pushforward equality.

05Transfer block moments and assemble Euclidean innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Evaluation on the infinite product is measure preserving, so composing the finite-block L² identity with evaluation gives a finite second moment for each block. Its expectation is zero by the established HasLaw integral transformation. klmcInnovation collects component zero across spatial coordinates into velocity and component one into position. klmc2Innovation similarly collects each of the four components into a Euclidean vector. WithLp.toLp equips the coordinate functions with the Euclidean norm. Their measurable-map theorems unfold these finite coordinate constructions and apply compositional measurability. These maps supply concrete innovation streams for pathwise integrator recursions.

06Turn covariance into actual second and cross moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Gaussian coordinates have zero mean. Thus their variance equals ∫Xi2\int X_i^2∫Xi2​, and their covariance equals ∫XiXj\int X_iX_j∫Xi​Xj​. The second-moment proof starts from the exact variance formula, rewrites variance as the integral of the centered square, and substitutes the proved zero mean. The cross-moment proof unfolds covariance and substitutes both zero means. Each conclusion is an equality for the actual constructed Gaussian measure: EXi2=∫0hki2\mathbb E X_i^2=\int_0^hk_i^2EXi2​=∫0h​ki2​ and EXiXj=∫0hkikj\mathbb E X_iX_j=\int_0^hk_ik_jEXi​Xj​=∫0h​ki​kj​. These formulas can be used in numerical-error calculations without assuming moments of an unspecified noise source.

07The scalar KLMC transition is an actual Gaussian pushforward §

Mathematical statement and proof

klmcScalarTransition maps the constructed correlated two-dimensional Gaussian through the scalar KLMC step at a fixed phase state. The map is continuous in its innovation, so the pushforward remains a probability measure. Both output coordinates are a constant frozen-force response plus 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​ times a Gaussian coordinate. Their finite second moments follow by adding a constant L² function to a scalar multiple of the proved Gaussian L² coordinate. To compute the transition mean, change variables through the pushforward, integrate the two coordinates separately using their established integrability, and substitute zero Gaussian means. The result is exactly the frozen deterministic position–velocity pair. This is a genuine one-step law and moment result, with no assumption that an abstract transition has the desired mean.

08Correlated transition covariance and independent initialization §

Mathematical statement and proof

The actual scalar phase transition retains covariance 2γC012\gamma C_{01}2γC01​ between its velocity and position coordinates. Lean pulls covariance through the transition pushforward, removes the deterministic means, moves both factors 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​ outside covariance, and uses the Gaussian Gram-covariance theorem; their product is 2γ2\gamma2γ when friction is nonnegative. This checks the correlation in the update rather than replacing the two noises by independent draws.

To initialize independently, the canonical probability space is the product μ0⊗Q\mu_0\otimes Qμ0​⊗Q, where QQQ is the countable independent Gaussian-block path measure. The first projection has law μ0\mu_0μ0​, the second projection has law QQQ, and the two projections are independent. Lean constructs this product explicitly, obtains probability from the two probability instances, and applies the product-marginal and independent-projection theorems. No later-time independence or convergence inequality is assumed by this construction.
