The first-order contraction and local-error inequality

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This theorem inserts an auxiliary exact kinetic path between the exponential numerical step and an exact reference state. Its proof combines the defined update's local error with continuous-time contraction. The reference velocity norm bound is an explicit hypothesis; establishing it from a stationary kinetic Gibbs law is proved separately.

The auxiliary exact path makes local numerical error depend on the current coupling discrepancy. Its velocity is controlled by a stationary Gaussian reference plus exact-flow contraction.

Mathematical statement and proof

Let AAA be the exact path starting at the numerical phase point and RRR the reference path, driven by the same additive input. Put e=∥A0−R0∥Te=\|A_0-R_0\|_Te=∥A0​−R0​∥T​. The integral-flow theorem gives ∥As−Rs∥T≤e−ms/γe≤e\|A_s-R_s\|_T\le e^{-ms/\gamma}e\le e∥As​−Rs​∥T​≤e−ms/γe≤e, because m,s≥0m,s\ge0m,s≥0. Velocity projection and the triangle inequality therefore give ∥Asv∥≤∥Asv−Rsv∥+∥Rsv∥≤e+C\|A_s^v\|\le\|A_s^v-R_s^v\|+\|R_s^v\|\le e+C∥Asv​∥≤∥Asv​−Rsv​∥+∥Rsv​∥≤e+C. The argument derives the exponential comparison from monotonicity of the exponential, multiplies it by the nonnegative initial norm, invokes the velocity projection bound, and combines the explicit reference bound with the triangle inequality.

The mild equation identifies the defined update's error relative to AhA_hAh​. Applying its proved local-norm theorem with C+eC+eC+e yields ∥step⁡(A0)−Ah∥T≤3Mh2(C+e)/4\|\operatorname{step}(A_0)-A_h\|_T\le3Mh^2(C+e)/4∥step(A0​)−Ah​∥T​≤3Mh2(C+e)/4. Finally insert AhA_hAh​ with the transformed triangle inequality and use exact-flow contraction between AhA_hAh​ and RhR_hRh​. The resulting inequality is

∥step⁡(A0)−Rh∥T≤e−mh/γe+34Mh2(C+e).\|\operatorname{step}(A_0)-R_h\|_T\le e^{-mh/\gamma}e+\tfrac34Mh^2(C+e).∥step(A0​)−Rh​∥T​≤e−mh/γe+43​Mh2(C+e).

The update noise is exactly (B(h),∫0hB(s) ds)(B(h),\int_0^hB(s)\,ds)(B(h),∫0h​B(s)ds); the raw additive input and its velocity convolution are both specified. Thus the path equations and the numerical formula imply the one-step recurrence. Its application to random trajectories additionally requires the stated velocity moment bound and the specified Brownian input law.

Insert the auxiliary exact path between the numerical state and the stationary exact state. Exact-flow contraction controls its discrepancy from the reference. The triangle inequality bounds its velocity by the reference velocity radius plus that discrepancy, yielding a local error that depends linearly on the current coupling error.
