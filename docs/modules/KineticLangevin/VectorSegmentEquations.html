Vector Brownian response equations in L²

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The scalar Brownian equations must also hold for Euclidean random vectors so they can drive the L² kinetic diffusion used by the contraction proof. A continuous linear spatial assembly map achieves this directly: it preserves sums, scalar multiples, continuity, and Bochner time integrals.

Bounded linear spatial assembly transfers scalar response identities to vector-valued second-mean paths. The resulting equations identify raw inputs, filtered inputs, and numerical values on the same probability space.

01Assemble scalar L² variables through a continuous linear map §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a scalar random variable ZZZ, spatial assembly produces the vector (Z(ωi))i(Z(\omega_i))_i(Z(ωi​))i​, evaluating it on independent spatial copies of the specified probability source. The assembly is a finite sum. Each summand first pulls back along a measure-preserving coordinate evaluation, then inserts the scalar into one Euclidean basis coordinate. Both operations are continuous linear maps on L². The representative theorem is proved by the finite-sum almost-everywhere identity and the explicit representatives of these two maps; equality in every Euclidean coordinate verifies the vector identity. This construction avoids interchanging pointwise evaluation with an L² Bochner integral.

A finite family of scalar square-integrable variables can be assembled into one Euclidean square-integrable vector. The assembly map is bounded and linear. It therefore commutes with limits, time integrals, and the affine operations used by the kinetic equations.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02Define vector responses and prove continuity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The vector response is the spatial assembly of the scalar response on (a,a+t](a,a+t](a,a+t]. The vector Brownian input is the assembly of the scalar Brownian increment. Their definitions remain on the existing constructed product source. The response representative theorem gives exactly the expected independent-coordinate vector. Continuity in time follows by composing the continuous scalar response with the continuous linear assembly map.

Each spatial response is continuous in the scalar second-mean norm. Finite-dimensional assembly transfers that continuity to the vector response. Its squared norm is the sum of the coordinate second moments, so the required bounds remain explicit in dimension.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

These equalities follow from the already proved scalar equations by applying the spatial assembly map. The proof commutes that continuous linear map with each Bochner integral using commutation of a bounded linear map with integration. Thus the exact filtered Brownian input required by the generic mild-equation theorem is constructed for the vector L² diffusion.

Apply the assembly map to the scalar primitive and damped integral identities. Bounded linearity permits it to pass through the Bochner integrals. The vector equations follow componentwise with the exact same friction and response coefficients.

04The input with specified vector Brownian increments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The lifted input is proved almost everywhere equal to the vector whose coordinates are Bi(a+t)−Bi(a)B_i(a+t)-B_i(a)Bi​(a+t)−Bi​(a) for the existing constructed coordinate process. The scalar subtraction identity is pulled back through each measure-preserving spatial projection and combined with the spatial representative theorem. Continuity follows by identifying the increment with the unit-kernel vector response. This connects the L² forcing to the specified vector Brownian construction.

The zero-response kernel is the indicator of the local Brownian interval. Its assembled isonormal image is therefore the vector Brownian increment. This verifies that the local input used in the kinetic equation comes from the constructed vector process.

05The OU increment and numerical innovation endpoints §

Mathematical statement and proof

The vector velocity response is exactly Vta=Wta−γ∫0te−γ(t−s)WsadsV^a_t=W^a_t-\gamma\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}W^a_sdsVta​=Wta​−γ∫0t​e−γ(t−s)Wsa​ds. The argument applies spatial assembly to the established scalar integration-by-parts formula, uses its linearity, and commutes it with the continuous Bochner integrand. This is precisely the OU input increment used by the kinetic splitting limit. At each grid interval, the paired L² velocity and position response endpoints are proved almost everywhere equal to the specified pointwise first-order innovation already equipped with the exact Gaussian law. The endpoint proof combines the two representative equalities with equality of the two phase coordinates; it does not replace the constructed inputs by new independent Gaussian variables.

At the step endpoint the vector responses equal the numerical innovations. The filtered velocity response is also the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck increment used by the splitting. These identities keep the continuous and numerical couplings tied to one underlying source.
