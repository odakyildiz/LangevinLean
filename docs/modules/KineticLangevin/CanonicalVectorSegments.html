Vector Brownian responses and independent Gaussian initial velocity

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The paper draws correlated velocity and position innovations in each spatial coordinate, independently across coordinates and time steps. Here every such draw is realized from the existing product of scalar specified Brownian sources. The entire constructed array is proved to have exactly the previously defined Gaussian product law; equality of a few moments alone is not used as a substitute for equality of laws.

The scalar Brownian responses are assembled into finite-dimensional vector innovations with their complete joint stream law. Independent Gaussian initialization is adjoined once, independently of the entire future source.

01Transport independence along a prescribed law §

Mathematical statement and proof

A measurable map YYY with law QQQ can pull back any independent measurable family defined on QQQ. One proves this by the characterization of mutual independence through equality with the infinite product of marginal laws. It applies the pushforward composition theorem to the joint family and to each marginal. This elementary tool is used repeatedly to transfer established independence to the Brownian source.

If a random vector has a product law, its coordinate projections are independent with the stated marginals. Pushing this law through separate measurable coordinate maps preserves independence. This equality of joint laws allows independence to be transferred to the constructed response variables.

02Build correlated scalar and spatial blocks §

Mathematical statement and proof

A scalar block consists of all deterministic response kernels applied to the same Brownian interval. Packing their values with the Euclidean identification gives a finite Euclidean vector whose law is the Gram Gaussian already proved in the translated Brownian interval construction. A vector block evaluates that scalar block on each spatial coordinate source. Every map is measurable because each L² representative is strongly measurable, coordinate evaluation is measurable, and finite or infinite coordinatewise packing preserves measurability.

At each time interval and spatial coordinate, retain the entire correlated response block. Assembling like response coordinates into Euclidean vectors gives the velocity and position innovations. Separate spatial coordinates remain independent, while the within-coordinate covariance is unchanged.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16.

03The law of the entire array §

Mathematical statement and proof

For one spatial source, disjoint Brownian intervals give independent scalar blocks. Different spatial sources are independent by the explicit product construction. The theorem regrouping independent coordinates combines these two facts into independence over iteration-coordinate pairs; reindexing swaps their order. Each marginal has the established Gram-Gaussia prescribed law, so the law of the entire independent stream identifies the full array distribution. Currying the array is a measurable equivalence, and the product-measure curry theorem identifies the distribution as an independent stream of complete spatial blocks. Pullback along this path law proves independence across time steps.

Finite-dimensional cylinder distributions determine the law of the countable innovation array. Each cylinder is the product of its independent block laws. Consequently the whole array has the independent-product stream law required by Markov iteration.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04Equality with the KLMC Gaussian innovation sequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

For ddd finite spatial coordinates, the pair-indexed array has the product law of the filtered response sequence. Specializing the response family to (ψ0,ψ1)(\psi_0,\psi_1)(ψ0​,ψ1​) and packing each response into a vector produces the unscaled velocity-position innovation. Composition of the stream law with the first-order Gaussian innovation map proves its exact distribution. Independence of the complete vector response blocks survives this measurable packing. Each vector coordinate lies in L² by the Brownian isometry; pulling it back along spatial evaluation and using square integrability of a coordinate proves both phase components have finite second moments.

The Brownian response pair and the numerical Gaussian block have the same centered joint Gaussian covariance. Equality of their laws identifies them as two realizations of the same sampler innovation. The construction thereby connects the source process to the prescribed transition measure.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

05Adjoin the independent initial Gaussian velocity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The source for a sampler is Q=N(0,Id)⊗PBrownianQ=\mathcal N(0,I_d)\otimes P_{\mathrm{Brownian}}Q=N(0,Id​)⊗PBrownian​. Its first projection is the standard Gaussian initial velocity. Its second projection carries all constructed innovations. Product-measure projection laws prove the velocity law and preserve the innovation law. The product independence theorem proves that initial velocity is independent of the entire future innovation stream, while measure-preserving pullback gives all required L² bounds. An arbitrary initial-position coupling can subsequently be extended by this same fixed source, independently of that coupling.

Pullback along the second projection also proves mutual independence of complete innovations across all iterations on this enlarged source; the new Gaussian velocity does not alter the noise law.

An additional independent standard Gaussian vector supplies the initial velocity. Taking its product with the entire Brownian source proves independence from every future innovation simultaneously. This is stronger than checking independence from only the first numerical interval.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.
