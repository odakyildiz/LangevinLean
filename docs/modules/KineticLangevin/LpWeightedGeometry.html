Equality of weighted L² norms and Euclidean phase coupling costs

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A local estimate in a pair of L² spaces must refer to the same Euclidean phase geometry as the vector sampler. This section constructs the explicit L² Euclidean pair and proves its exact norm and representative identities. It then proves that the weighted representative cost is precisely the weighted phase norm used by the local analysis.

The weighted Hilbert-space norm is proved equal to the second-mean finite-dimensional phase cost. This equality converts analytic local-error bounds directly into transport costs of coupled outputs.

01Construct a Euclidean phase variable in L² §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coordinate injections u↦(u,0)u\mapsto(u,0)u↦(u,0), w↦(0,w)w\mapsto(0,w)w↦(0,w) are continuous linear maps into the Euclidean product Hilbert space. Lift each through the bounded pullback map and add their outputs. Almost-everywhere identities for lifted linear maps and L² addition show that the resulting variable represents exactly (A(ω),B(ω))(A(\omega),B(\omega))(A(ω),B(ω)). Expand its L² inner product as an integral, use the Euclidean product inner product pointwise, and split the two integrable self-inner-products. This proves ∥pair⁡(A,B)∥L22=∥A∥L22+∥B∥L22\|\operatorname{pair}(A,B)\|_{L^2}^2=\|A\|_{L^2}^2+\|B\|_{L^2}^2∥pair(A,B)∥L22​=∥A∥L22​+∥B∥L22​. Thus the construction uses the Euclidean geometry, not the default maximum norm of an ordinary product.

Combine velocity and position as one Euclidean phase-valued random variable. Its second-mean squared norm is the sum of the two component second moments. This identifies the functional-analytic phase cost with the finite-dimensional cost used in transport.

02Lift the weighted transform without changing its norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the Euclidean pair construction to X+bVX+bVX+bV and a−b2V\sqrt{a-b^2}Va−b2​V. The representative is almost everywhere the original vector weighted transform T(V(ω),X(ω))T(V(\omega),X(\omega))T(V(ω),X(ω)), with all addition and scalar-multiplication representatives justified explicitly. The squared norm identity for the pair is exactly the defining squared norm of the weighted phase transform on the L² Hilbert space. Both norms are nonnegative, so equality of their squares gives equality of the norms. Finally the standard L² norm definition and finiteness of an L² variable give

∥T(V(⋅),X(⋅))∥L2=∥(V,X)∥E.\|T(V(\cdot),X(\cdot))\|_{L^2}=\|(V,X)\|_E.∥T(V(⋅),X(⋅))∥L2​=∥(V,X)∥E​.

The identity retains extended-real L² norms on the left and converts the finite norm with the nonnegative extended-real embedding. Consequently the local Hilbert-space norm equals the transport cost of this particular coupling.

The weighted transformation is bounded linear, so applying it before or after passage to square-integrable classes gives the same element. Its norm identity therefore transfers exactly to random phase errors. No extra coordinatewise inequality is needed.

03Keep differences and the entire OBABO step tied to their representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

The first lemma records the almost-everywhere representative of a phase difference, using the two component subtraction identities. The second expands OBABO into first thermostat, Verlet, and final thermostat, then composes the already proved representative identities for all three. Consequently the lifted nonlinear force update represents exactly the original finite-dimensional O-B-A-B-O sampler with the same two supplied noise variables. These equalities preserve the joint coupling; a marginal-law equality is never substituted for the common-noise realization.

The representative identities hold for differences and for each affine stage of the OBABO update. Thus the weighted local-error norm is the root-mean-square distance between the coupled vector outputs. This connects the analytic bound with a transport coupling.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2.
