The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck innovation law and independence across time

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The constructed convolution is a random variable on the constructed Brownian source. This section proves that its fluctuation-dissipation scaling has exactly thermal Gaussian law, and that full spatial vectors on distinct grid intervals are independent. The complete stream therefore has the independent Gaussian product law required by the Markov transition.

The thermal response law is computed from its exponential kernel and assembled across independent spatial coordinates. Disjoint intervals yield the complete independent Gaussian stream required by the splitting chain.

01Identify independent spatial copies through their scalar law §

Mathematical statement and proof

A scalar L2L^{2}L2 variable ZZZ is evaluated on each independent coordinate Brownian source. Each copy has the same proved law ν\nuν, and the coordinate product construction proves their independence. The infinite-product law theorem, specialized to finitely many spatial coordinates, gives the finite product measure ν⊗ι\nu^{\otimes\iota}ν⊗ι. Transporting this law through the Euclidean coordinate map identifies the law of the coordinate assembly map; its representative theorem supplies the almost-everywhere equality. For ν\nuν equal to a scaled real standard Gaussian, the product pushforward identity and the definition-independent standard Gaussian theorem identify this law as the same scalar scaling of the Euclidean standard Gaussian.

The scalar response at each spatial index has the computed centered Gaussian law. Independence of the coordinate sources gives their product law. Assembling coordinates then yields a Euclidean Gaussian with a scalar covariance multiple of the identity.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

02Compute the scalar thermal variance §

Mathematical statement and proof

The scalar segment convolution is the constructed isonormal map applied to its time kernel KKK, so it has centered Gaussian variance ∥K∥2\|K\|^2∥K∥2. With c=2γc=\sqrt{2\gamma}c=2γ​, its scaled variance is 2γ∥K∥22\gamma\|K\|^22γ∥K∥2. The proved kernel inner-product identity gives ∥K∥2=∫0he−2γs ds\|K\|^2=\int_0^h e^{-2\gamma s}\,ds∥K∥2=∫0h​e−2γsds, and the exact fluctuation-decay balance gives 2γ∥K∥2=1−e−2γh2\gamma\|K\|^2=1-e^{-2\gamma h}2γ∥K∥2=1−e−2γh. This equals σh2\sigma_h^2σh2​ for σh=1−e−2γh\sigma_h=\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma h}}σh​=1−e−2γh​. The argument derives nonnegativity from the damping bound, uses the isonormal Gaussian law, checks the equality of nonnegative-real variances, and rewrites the scaled real Gaussian pushforward. Zero friction is included without division.

The filtered velocity response variance is the square integral of the exponential kernel. After multiplying by 2γ2\gamma2γ, it is 1−e−2γh1-e^{-2\gamma h}1−e−2γh. This is the missing variance of a velocity damped for time hhh.

03The entire spatial OU Gaussian law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the independent-spatial-copy result to the proved scalar thermal law. Linearity of the coordinate assembly map identifies its scaled scalar input with the scaled vector segment convolution. Thus 2γ Za,h\sqrt{2\gamma}\,Z_{a,h}2γ​Za,h​ has exactly the law of 1−e−2γh ξ\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma h}}\,\xi1−e−2γh​ξ, where ξ\xiξ is the Euclidean standard Gaussian. This proves the full vector distribution rather than only its covariance.

A centered Euclidean Gaussian is determined by its covariance bilinear form. The independent coordinates give covariance equal to the scalar thermal variance times the identity. Thus the assembled innovation is exactly the thermal Gaussian law required by the thermostat.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04Independence of full-vector innovations across time §

Mathematical statement and proof

The constructed KLMC innovation pairs are already proved independent over disjoint time-grid intervals by their Brownian kernel supports. Project each pair to its velocity component and apply any fixed scalar scaling. The L2L^{2}L2 vector-segment representatives agree almost everywhere with these projected constructed innovations. Independence is preserved under these measurable projections and almost-everywhere changes. The argument explicitly combines the representative scalar-multiplication event with the constructed terminal state identity.

Entire vector blocks from different intervals are measurable functions of disjoint collections of Gaussian response variables. Independence of those collections therefore gives independence of the vectors, rather than only pairwise independence of their individual coordinates.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

05The full independent Gaussian stream §

Mathematical statement and proof

Every scaled OU grid innovation has the exact thermal Gaussian law, and the preceding theorem proves independence of all grid vectors. The infinite-product law theorem therefore identifies the entire innovation stream with the product of this Gaussian law over all iterations. Measurability follows from the strongly measurable L2L^{2}L2 representative at each countable grid index. This stream identity is the law identity used to transfer the Brownian-driven splitting chain to its iterated Markov laws.

The finite-dimensional distributions of the full stream are products of thermal Gaussian vector law. This identifies the stream as independent and identically distributed. It supplies the exact fresh-noise law required when the splitting transition is iterated.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.
