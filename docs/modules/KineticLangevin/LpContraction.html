Contraction in the Hilbert space of square-integrable random vectors

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Random vectors form a Hilbert space under the L2L^{2}L2 norm. The gradient acts on these vectors through pointwise application of the gradient. This section lifts the centered strong-smooth inequality to that vector field, then proves contraction of kinetic integral paths in L2L^{2}L2. It does not introduce a hypothetical differentiable potential on the infinite-dimensional space of random variables.

Pointwise potential geometry lifts to the Hilbert space of square-integrable variables by integration. Consequently deterministic energy calculations become root-mean-square coupling bounds without changing the curvature constants.

Statement and proof

01The gradient sector inequality in L2L^{2}L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

For deterministic points, strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient imply ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)−m+M2(x−y)∥≤M−m2∥x−y∥\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)-\frac{m+M}{2}(x-y)\|\le\frac{M-m}{2}\|x-y\|∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)−2m+M​(x−y)∥≤2M−m​∥x−y∥. The lifted gradient agrees almost everywhere with pointwise application of the original gradient. The proof collects the almost-everywhere identities for subtraction, scalar multiplication and the pointwise representative of the lifted drift, rewrites the random-variable representatives, and applies the deterministic centered inequality at each sample. Monotonicity of the second-mean norm integrates this norm comparison, yielding the same constant in L2L^{2}L2. No independence or moment estimate beyond membership in L2L^{2}L2 is needed for this step.

Apply the finite-dimensional sector inequality at almost every sample point, then integrate. All pairings are integrable by Cauchy–Schwarz. The integrated inequality is precisely the Hilbert-space sector bound for square-integrable random vectors, with the original curvature constants unchanged.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.10.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.10.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.3.

02Contraction of kinetic integral paths in L2L^{2}L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

Two phase paths satisfy the kinetic integral equations with the lifted gradient and common additive input. The noise cancels in those equations, so their difference has a right derivative. Continuous linear projections extract the velocity and position equations. The general centered dissipativity theorem applies in the Hilbert space L2L^{2}L2 because the preceding pointwise lift supplies its sector premise. Under γ2≥m+M\gamma^2\ge m+Mγ2≥m+M, the maximum curvature coefficient equals −m-m−m, giving E′(t)≤−2mE(t)/γE'(t)\le-2mE(t)/\gammaE′(t)≤−2mE(t)/γ. Continuity and the right-slope Grönwall theorem prove E(t)≤E(0)e−2mt/γE(t)\le E(0)e^{-2mt/\gamma}E(t)≤E(0)e−2mt/γ. Taking nonnegative square roots, using the exponential-addition identity, gives the transformed L2L^{2}L2 norm contraction.

The shared-input random paths are viewed as paths in a Hilbert space of square-integrable variables. Their integral equations give the same right derivatives and energy identity as in the deterministic argument. The resulting norm contraction is a root-mean-square coupling estimate.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.
