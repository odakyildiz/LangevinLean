The previously proved splitting limit establishes its exact Gaussian-times-Gibbs law at every time; stationarity is a conclusion of this concrete construction.

The initial Gibbs phase and canonical Brownian source are realized on a concrete independent product. Splitting convergence and the verified joint source law establish stationarity of the selected kinetic integral solution.

01Derive the two target projection moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The target is the product π=N(0,I)⊗πf\pi=\mathcal N(0,I)\otimes\pi_fπ=N(0,I)⊗πf​, where πf(dx)=Zf−1e−f(x)dx\pi_f(dx)=Z_f^{-1}e^{-f(x)}dxπf​(dx)=Zf−1​e−f(x)dx. Its velocity projection lies in L² because a finite-dimensional Gaussian has a finite second moment. Its position projection lies in L² because strong convexity with m>0m>0m>0 gives Gaussian tail domination for the normalized Gibbs measure. First, use that πf\pi_fπf​ has total mass one. Factor the phase product and compose the existing Gaussian or Gibbs L² identity with the corresponding measure-preserving product projection. These lemmas prove the exact integrability required by the random initial state; they do not introduce moment hypotheses.

The target’s product structure gives the position second moment and the exact Gaussian velocity second moment. These ensure that the phase initialization belongs to the square-integrable Hilbert space. They are derived from the target rather than imposed as unexplained moment assumptions.

02Build the independent Gibbs and Brownian probability space §

Mathematical statement and proof

The state space is E=RdE=\mathbb R^dE=Rd, defined as Euclidean space on Fin d\mathrm{Fin}\,dFind. The probability space is the product of the normalized phase law with the already constructed canonical Brownian source. Its initial phase is the two L² coordinate projections, whose integrability was proved above. Thus the starting velocity is genuinely standard Gaussian, the starting position has the normalized Gibbs law, and the whole Brownian source is independent of both. The argument uses the derived projection square−integrabilitysquare-integrabilitysquare−integrability statements to construct the L² elements through the initial phase variable. The definitions name this concrete source and initial state so subsequent theorems cannot silently substitute a source with an assumed stationary output law.

Take the product of the normalized Gibbs phase measure and the canonical Brownian probability space. The coordinate projections have exactly those laws and are independent. This concrete probability space supports both the initial phase and all future filtered responses.

03Construct the fluctuation-dissipation input §

Mathematical statement and proof

On the independent product source, the forcing is Bt=2γ WtB_t=\sqrt{2\gamma}\,W_tBt​=2γ​Wt​, where the vector Brownian motion has the already covariance and independent increment laws. It is a continuous function of time taking values in the L² space, and B0=0B_0=0B0​=0. The proof pulls back the canonical Brownian coordinates along the product projection, carries over their proved L² continuity, and rewrites their value at zero. These are properties of the constructed input, rather than regularity assumptions on an unspecified process. The drift is the pointwise potential gradient lifted to this same L² space; its global Lipschitz constant is inherited from the stated gradient bound.

The additive velocity input is the vector Brownian process multiplied by 2γ\sqrt{2\gamma}2γ​. Its filtered increments have thermal Gaussian law required by the splitting. The amplitude is fixed by the fluctuation–dissipation relation, ensuring the target velocity temperature is one.
