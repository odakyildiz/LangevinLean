Synchronous comparison with the stochastic Hessian-difference terms

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The printed comparison omits the difference of the two Hessians multiplying the fresh phi2 and phi3 innovations. The comparison below retains these terms. They combine with the initial reference velocity into the complete free-OU response, whose Gaussian marginals admit the cutoff estimate without independence of the Hessian.

The full stochastic Hessian difference is retained when two corrected steps are subtracted. Frozen-linear contraction and the Gaussian cutoff bound yield the repaired synchronous comparison for the sampler.

01Use the transformed phase geometry §

Mathematical statement and proof

Negating both phase coordinates negates their transformed coordinates and preserves their Euclidean norm. The centered force sector, when squared and expanded, is exactly the ordinary strong-smooth interpolation inequality. This lets the already proved centered-gradient bound in L2 supply the interpolation hypothesis of the frozen-linear theorem.

The phase transformation is linear, so negating a discrepancy preserves its norm. This allows a consistent sign convention in the difference identity. The centered sector inequality is then used in exactly the geometry where the frozen-linear contraction was proved.

02Project the cutoff displacement with the exact coefficient §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let DDD be the L2L^2L2 position discrepancy and EEE its transformed phase norm. The position projection gives D≤(2/γ)ED\le(\sqrt2/\gamma)ED≤(2​/γ)E, with γ>0\gamma>0γ>0. The complete free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck correction has the bound

2t22[M2α D+2Be−(α−p)/8]≤M2α t2γE+2t2Be−(α−p)/8.\frac{\sqrt2t^2}{2}\left[M_2\sqrt\alpha\,D+2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}\right]\le\frac{M_2\sqrt\alpha\,t^2}{\gamma}E+\sqrt2t^2B e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}.22​t2​[M2​α​D+2Be−(α−p)/8]≤γM2​α​t2​E+2​t2Be−(α−p)/8.

The position projection coefficient is needed here because the cutoff bounds the position discrepancy, whereas contraction controls the transformed phase norm. This scalar calculation preserves both the friction factor and the tail coefficient.

03Keep all four innovations on the same source §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the continuous linear Brownian-source map to the four response kernels. The resulting variables give the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck velocity and its two Hessian correction responses. Including the thermal amplitude in this linear map and distributing the bounded Hessian over the sums gives exactly the four-response KLMC2 update. All these variables are defined on the same Brownian interval.

Every correction response in both numerical states is generated from the same four-response block. Shared additive terms cancel, while differences of the random Hessians multiplying those responses remain. These residual stochastic terms are part of the comparison.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04The corrected difference identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Subtract the two source steps at z and w with common innovations. The common psi noise cancels. Freeze the linear part at the Hessian of the second starting position; its data are the velocity, position and force differences. The remainder is minus the Hessian difference applied to the complete phi2 and phi3 free-OU corrections built from the first reference velocity. This exact identity includes both fresh stochastic Hessian terms absent from the printed algebra.

Subtract the two complete corrected updates before estimating. Separate the frozen-linear part from the Hessian-difference correction acting on the full free-OU path. This subtraction retains the stochastic terms that a deterministic-only subtraction would miss.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

05The frozen-linear estimate from the potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

The gradient acting on square-integrable random vectors has the same Lipschitz constant and centered strong-smooth inequality as the pointwise gradient. The random Hessian, acting by pointwise application, has operator norm at most MMM. These are the hypotheses of the frozen-linear contraction estimate in the Hilbert space of square-integrable vectors.

Under γ2≥m+M\gamma^2\ge m+Mγ2≥m+M and 5γMt≤m5\gamma Mt\le m5γMt≤m, with the stated positivity and nonnegative-time assumptions, that estimate gives contraction factor

1−mt2γ.1-\frac{mt}{2\gamma}.1−2γmt​.

The sector inequality follows from the original potential's strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient. The derivative defining its Hessian supplies the operator bound. Thus the factor is derived from the potential hypotheses for the same random variables used in the comparison.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.

06Combine the coupled terms by Minkowski §

Mathematical statement and proof

The generic triangle lemma applies the exact difference identity, invariance of transformed norm under negation, and the Euclidean phase triangle inequality. Its two inputs are the separately proved frozen-linear component and the complete Hessian correction. This elementary composition introduces no stochastic approximation or omitted noise.

Minkowski’s inequality separates the frozen-linear contraction, the cutoff-dependent displacement contribution, and the Gaussian tail contribution in second mean. The separation is made after exact cancellation, preserving the sharp common-source response identity.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

07The repaired source comparison inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let AAA be the difference of the two random Hessians, and let DDD and EEE denote the initial position discrepancy in L2L^2L2 and the initial transformed phase norm. The Hessian assumptions give ∥A∥≤M−m\|A\|\le M-m∥A∥≤M−m pointwise and a Lipschitz bound with constant M2M_2M2​. Every free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck velocity has standard Gaussian law. The cutoff estimate, valid for α≥5p\alpha\ge5pα≥5p, therefore bounds its Hessian response by

M2α D+2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8.M_2\sqrt\alpha\,D+2(M-m)e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}.M2​α​D+2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8.

This estimate uses the Gaussian velocity marginal and does not require independence between the velocity and random Hessian. Integrating the full response gives the quadratic time factor. Substituting D≤(2/γ)ED\le(\sqrt2/\gamma)ED≤(2​/γ)E and adding the frozen-linear contraction gives

Eout≤(1−mt2γ+M2α t2γ)E+2t2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8.E_{\mathrm{out}}\le\left(1-\frac{mt}{2\gamma}+\frac{M_2\sqrt\alpha\,t^2}{\gamma}\right)E+\sqrt2t^2(M-m)e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}.Eout​≤(1−2γmt​+γM2​α​t2​)E+2​t2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8.

The full stochastic Hessian-difference term is included in this inequality. Its Gaussian-law hypothesis is verified by the independent past/future construction before this comparison is applied to the sampler. The subsequent cutoff choice absorbs the position-dependent term into contraction and retains the displayed tail.
