The exponential integrator and its Hessian correction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The deterministic maps accept a force vector field and supplied noise vectors. The same map can be applied to a particle force or to a force multiplied by inverse mass. Throughout the section, the first phase coordinate is velocity and the second is position. The response-kernel construction specifies the covariance matrices required by the probabilistic interpretation; these maps accept arbitrary noise vectors, so the Gaussian law remains an additional assumption when the maps define a stochastic algorithm.

The update is an exact solution of the linear frozen-force problem. Local numerical error therefore comes only from the nonlinear force variation, while all friction and Gaussian response coefficients remain exact.

01Definition of the frozen-force phase update §

Mathematical statement and proof

The velocity–position space is E×EE\times EE×E. For supplied additive innovation (nv,nx)(n_v,n_x)(nv​,nx​), define

v+=ψ0v−ψ1g(x)+nv,x+=x+ψ1v−ψ2g(x)+nx.v^+=\psi_0v-\psi_1g(x)+n_v,\qquad x^+=x+\psi_1v-\psi_2g(x)+n_x.v+=ψ0​v−ψ1​g(x)+nv​,x+=x+ψ1​v−ψ2​g(x)+nx​.

For KLMC, take the innovation to be 2γ(ξ,ξ′)\sqrt{2\gamma}(\xi,\xi')2γ​(ξ,ξ′), matching the paper's noise normalization. The two coordinates of the update are the displayed velocity and position expressions. KIPLMC1 uses this same map with its particle force and thermal amplitude.

Conditioning on the starting phase freezes the force at one position. The resulting affine linear system can be solved by an integrating factor for velocity and then a time integral for position. The only nonlinear approximation in this step is keeping that force constant.

02Zero step length and subtraction with common noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

At zero time, ψ0=1\psi_0=1ψ0​=1, ψ1=ψ2=0\psi_1=\psi_2=0ψ1​=ψ2​=0; with zero innovation the step is the identity. Both coordinate formulas reduce to their initial values. For two starting phases driven by the same innovation, subtraction cancels each additive noise term before any estimate:

Δv+=ψ0Δv−ψ1Δg,Δx+=Δx+ψ1Δv−ψ2Δg.\Delta v^+=\psi_0\Delta v-\psi_1\Delta g,\quad \Delta x^+=\Delta x+\psi_1\Delta v-\psi_2\Delta g.Δv+=ψ0​Δv−ψ1​Δg,Δx+=Δx+ψ1​Δv−ψ2​Δg.

Subtracting the corresponding velocity formulas and then the position formulas cancels the common innovation and gives these identities. The KLMC cancellation theorem reuses this exact identity with the scaled noise argument. It does not need Gaussianity.

For two states using the same additive innovation, subtraction removes the innovation exactly. The remaining difference is a deterministic expression involving the two starting velocities, positions, and force values. This permits the numerical comparison to be analyzed pathwise before any expectation is taken.

03The Hessian-corrected phase update §

Mathematical statement and proof

With H=∇2f(x)H=\nabla^2f(x)H=∇2f(x), the second-order map subtracts φ2Hv\varphi_2Hvφ2​Hv from velocity and φ3Hv\varphi_3Hvφ3​Hv from position. Its innovations are 2γ(ξ0−Hξ2)\sqrt{2\gamma}(\xi_0-H\xi_2)2γ​(ξ0​−Hξ2​) and 2γ(ξ1−Hξ3)\sqrt{2\gamma}(\xi_1-H\xi_3)2γ​(ξ1​−Hξ3​). The Hessian-corrected update includes all four terms. With zero Hessian, the deterministic and random corrections vanish and the map becomes the original KLMC map using (ξ0,ξ1)(\xi_0,\xi_1)(ξ0​,ξ1​). This follows by setting each Hessian term in the two coordinates equal to zero. When comparing two nonzero Hessians, the noise terms differ too; this explicit map prevents silently discarding that contribution.

The four innovations are correlated because all are deterministic responses to the same Brownian interval. A Hessian multiplies the appropriate noise responses as well as the deterministic response. Omitting those stochastic correction terms changes the map and invalidates a second-order comparison.

04Constant-force motion at zero friction §

Mathematical statement and proof

At γ=0\gamma=0γ=0, the coefficient identities give ψ0=1\psi_0=1ψ0​=1, ψ1=h\psi_1=hψ1​=h, ψ2=h2/2\psi_2=h^2/2ψ2​=h2/2, while 2γ=0\sqrt{2\gamma}=02γ​=0. Therefore v+=v−hg(x)v^+=v-hg(x)v+=v−hg(x) and x+=x+hv−h2g(x)/2x^+=x+hv-h^2g(x)/2x+=x+hv−h2g(x)/2. The proof simplifies the map with the exact zero-friction coefficient lemmas. It asserts exact integration of a frozen force, not exact integration of a spatially varying Hamiltonian force along the whole interval.

When friction is zero, ψ0=1\psi_0=1ψ0​=1, ψ1=h\psi_1=hψ1​=h, and ψ2=h2/2\psi_2=h^2/2ψ2​=h2/2. Substitution gives the elementary constant-acceleration formulas. This is a useful consistency check on signs, on the order of the phase coordinates, and on the factor one half in position.

05Recursion for a prescribed innovation sequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

The KLMC sequence starts at the supplied phase and recursively applies the KLMC update with innovation number kkk. The initial value is the prescribed phase, and the successor value is the preceding phase updated with the next innovation. The Hessian-corrected sequence uses the same recursion pattern with the four-block innovation required by the Hessian-corrected update. A prescribed noise stream is a pathwise object: assigning independent Gaussian coordinate laws and proving finite moments of this recursion is the next probabilistic task, not a hidden assumption in the definitions.

The recursion is a deterministic measurable function of the initial phase and the full innovation sequence. Probabilistic assumptions are imposed on these inputs afterward. In particular, independence across time is a property of the prescribed stream and is not supplied by recursion alone.

06Differential equations for the frozen-force response §

Mathematical statement and proof

For constant force ggg, define vt=ψ0(t)v0−ψ1(t)gv_t=\psi_0(t)v_0-\psi_1(t)gvt​=ψ0​(t)v0​−ψ1​(t)g and xt=x0+ψ1(t)v0−ψ2(t)gx_t=x_0+\psi_1(t)v_0-\psi_2(t)gxt​=x0​+ψ1​(t)v0​−ψ2​(t)g. The coefficient derivative identities immediately give xt′=vtx'_t=v_txt′​=vt​. For velocity they give vt′=−γψ0(t)v0−ψ0(t)gv'_t=-\gamma\psi_0(t)v_0-\psi_0(t)gvt′​=−γψ0​(t)v0​−ψ0​(t)g. Substituting γψ1=1−ψ0\gamma\psi_1=1-\psi_0γψ1​=1−ψ0​ proves this equals −γvt−g-\gamma v_t-g−γvt​−g. The proof builds the vector derivatives from the scalar coefficient derivatives and rewrites the friction balance before normalizing scalar actions. the exact frozen-force response identity is then a definitional equality: the deterministic step evaluates these exact frozen responses at hhh and adds the supplied noise. Thus the term “exponential integrator” is supported by an ODE identity.

The deterministic response has initial velocity vvv and acceleration −γvt−g(x)-\gamma v_t-g(x)−γvt​−g(x). Its integrated position starts at xxx. Verifying these equations and the initial conditions identifies the response uniquely, providing a precise reference for the local force-freezing error.
