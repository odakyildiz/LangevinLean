Curvature-width and Gaussian truncation bounds for Hessian differences

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Hessians of one strongly convex smooth potential differ in operator norm by at most M−mM-mM−m. The proof derives this directly from the centered gradient sector inequality and derivatives. It then proves the identical bound for the random L2L^{2}L2 Hessian operators and establishes the precise Gaussian truncation term used in the repaired KLMC2 comparison.

The curvature interval bounds the Hessian difference by its width, while Hessian Lipschitzness gives a displacement bound. Their minimum supports the Gaussian truncation estimate without position–velocity independence.

Statement and proof

01Center the gradient and derive its sharp Lipschitz radius §

Mathematical statement and proof

For an mmm-strongly convex potential with MMM-Lipschitz gradient, the strong-smooth interpolation inequality implies ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)−m+M2(x−y)∥≤M−m2∥x−y∥.\left\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)-\frac{m+M}{2}(x-y)\right\|\le\frac{M-m}{2}\|x-y\|.​∇f(x)−∇f(y)−2m+M​(x−y)​≤2M−m​∥x−y∥. Thus gc(x)=∇f(x)−m+M2xg_c(x)=\nabla f(x)-\frac{m+M}{2}xgc​(x)=∇f(x)−2m+M​x is globally (M−m)/2(M-m)/2(M−m)/2-Lipschitz. The argument constructs the nonnegative Lipschitz constant, rewrites the difference of the centered gradients as the residual in this previously proved sector estimate, and applies that exact estimate. No Hessian or spectral conclusion is inserted as a premise.

Centering at (m+M)/2(m+M)/2(m+M)/2 treats the curvature endpoints symmetrically. The residual Lipschitz radius is half their width. This is sharper than using the full smoothness constant and is especially informative when the potential is nearly quadratic with constant curvature.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.10.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.10.3.

02The Hessian curvature-width bound from derivatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

If Hx=D∇f(x)H_x=D\nabla f(x)Hx​=D∇f(x), the derivative of the centered gradient is Hx−m+M2IH_x-\frac{m+M}{2}IHx​−2m+M​I. The derivative of a globally Lipschitz map has operator norm at most its Lipschitz constant, so ∥Hx−m+M2I∥≤M−m2.\left\|H_x-\frac{m+M}{2}I\right\|\le\frac{M-m}{2}.​Hx​−2m+M​I​≤2M−m​. The proof differentiates the centered gradient by subtracting the scalar identity derivative, then applies the derivative/Lipschitz operator bound. Subtracting the centered derivatives at two points yields ∥Hx−Hy∥≤M−m2+M−m2=M−m.\|H_x-H_y\|\le\frac{M-m}{2}+\frac{M-m}{2}=M-m.∥Hx​−Hy​∥≤2M−m​+2M−m​=M−m. This is the sharp Hessian interval-width estimate. It is derived from the original potential assumptions and gradient derivatives, without an additional continuous-Hessian, symmetry, or operator-range hypothesis.

The derivative of a globally Lipschitz map has operator norm bounded by the same constant wherever it exists. Applying this to the centered gradient bounds each centered Hessian. The triangle inequality between two centered Hessians gives the full interval width M−mM-mM−m.

03Identify and bound the random Hessian difference on L2L^{2}L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

The existing random-Hessian construction is a continuous linear map on L2L^{2}L2, with representative H(X(ω))Z(ω)H(X(\omega))Z(\omega)H(X(ω))Z(ω). One proves that subtracting the two such maps has the exact representative (H(X(ω))−H(Y(ω)))Z(ω).(H(X(\omega))-H(Y(\omega)))Z(\omega).(H(X(ω))−H(Y(ω)))Z(ω). It rewrites the almost-everywhere representatives of the two random Hessians and their L2L^{2}L2 difference. The pointwise sharp bound just proved gives ∥(H(X)−H(Y))Z∥≤(M−m)∥Z∥\|(H(X)-H(Y))Z\|\le(M-m)\|Z\|∥(H(X)−H(Y))Z∥≤(M−m)∥Z∥, so the L2L^{2}L2 operator norm of the difference is at most M−mM-mM−m. The argument applies the L2L^{2}L2 norm monotonicity theorem and the continuous linear operator-norm characterization; no operator bound is assumed on an unspecified random map.

The pointwise difference representation ensures that the operator bound concerns the Hessians frozen at the two random positions. Squaring and integrating the uniform bound transfers it to second mean without any independence assumption between positions and the vector.

04The exact Hessian-difference Gaussian truncation term §

Mathematical statement and proof

The original Hessian Lipschitz assumption also gives ∥H(X)−H(Y)∥≤M2∥X−Y∥\|H(X)-H(Y)\|\le M_2\|X-Y\|∥H(X)−H(Y)∥≤M2​∥X−Y∥ pointwise. Combining this with the uniform bound M−mM-mM−m, and retaining a standard Gaussian velocity VVV, gives ∥(H(X)−H(Y))V∥L2≤M2α ∥X−Y∥L2+2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8,α≥5p.\|(H(X)-H(Y))V\|_{L^2}\le M_2\sqrt\alpha\,\|X-Y\|_{L^2}+2(M-m)e^{-(\alpha-p)/8},\qquad\alpha\ge5p.∥(H(X)−H(Y))V∥L2​≤M2​α​∥X−Y∥L2​+2(M−m)e−(α−p)/8,α≥5p. The argument rewrites the exact random-Hessian difference representative and the position discrepancy representative, combines the two proved operator bounds by their minimum, and applies the Gaussian cutoff theorem. Only the marginal Gaussian law of VVV is required; the positions and Hessian difference may depend on VVV. This is the precise random coefficient bound needed at each free-OU time in the corrected KLMC2 argument.

The global width bound controls the large-velocity region, while Hessian Lipschitzness controls the moderate region through position discrepancy. The Gaussian positive-part tail bounds the excess. Choosing the cutoff later balances absorption of the displacement term against exponential decay of the tail.
