The arbitrary-shift kinetic contraction for L² random vectors

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The continuous mixing theorem permits an arbitrary shifted kinetic metric, not only the convenient zero-shift case used by KLMC. The pointwise strong-smooth gradient sector bound already lifts to L². Here it gives the complete shifted energy and position estimates for shared-input L² kinetic solutions.

The full arbitrary-shift transformed geometry extends to second-mean random paths. Its exact initialization and endpoint projection recover the same prefactor and exponent needed for general kinetic law contraction.

01Prove the full shifted L² energy decay §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let d=γ−2a>0d=\gamma-2a>0d=γ−2a>0. The exact common-noise integral equations make the difference differentiable, with the original gradient difference lifted pointwise. The proof differentiates the explicit shifted energy and applies the general dissipativity inequality using the lifted sector bound. The resulting rate is 2max⁡(a2−m,M−(γ−a)2)/d2\max(a^2-m,M-(\gamma-a)^2)/d2max(a2−m,M−(γ−a)2)/d. A right-derivative Grönwall argument integrates this differential inequality, including the starting endpoint. No potential on the L² space is introduced.

The strong-smooth sector bound lifts by integration to the square-integrable Hilbert space. Applying the shifted energy calculation there yields the same exponent as for deterministic paths. Thus no dimension-dependent norm conversion is introduced by taking root-mean-square errors.

02Project the shifted energy to the position error §

Mathematical statement and proof

Equal initial velocities make the initial energy equal to ((γ−a)2+a2)∥X0−Y0∥22((\gamma-a)^2+a^2)\|X_0-Y_0\|_2^2((γ−a)2+a2)∥X0​−Y0​∥22​. The invertible transformed coordinates bound the position error by the energy divided by (γ−2a)2(\gamma-2a)^2(γ−2a)2. The proof combines these identities with the energy decay, proves the exact squared-exponential identity, and takes the nonnegative square-root inequality. The conclusion is the full source-paper prefactor 2((γ−a)2+a2)/(γ−2a)\sqrt{2((\gamma-a)^2+a^2)}/(\gamma-2a)2((γ−a)2+a2)​/(γ−2a) multiplied by the explicit exponential rate and the initial L² position cost.

Equal initial velocities reduce the initial transformed energy to its position coefficient. The endpoint position projection then supplies the full shifted prefactor. The resulting estimate applies to common-input random solutions and can be pushed forward to their probability laws.
