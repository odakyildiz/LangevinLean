A quadratic persistent-error bound and an admissible accuracy step

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The stronger source Gaussian tail allows a completely explicit second-order accuracy prescription. The exponentially small term is bounded by a quadratic upper bound, making the entire persistent error term at most Bph2B_p h^2Bp​h2 for h≤1h\le1h≤1. A positive minimum step then satisfies stability, the Gaussian cutoff dimension restriction, and the epsilon error bound simultaneously.

The full Gaussian tail is included in a uniform quadratic discretization-error bound. A positive minimum step then simultaneously satisfies stability, cutoff admissibility, and the requested second-order accuracy allocation.

01Bound the cutoff tail by a reciprocal §

Mathematical statement and proof

02Collect the complete persistent error into a quadratic coefficient §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write Cp=M2p2+2p+MMpC_p=M_2\sqrt{p^2+2p}+M\sqrt{Mp}Cp​=M2​p2+2p​+MMp​. The exact persistent error is 2h2Cp/m+(8(M−m)/m)he−(α−p)/8\sqrt2h^2C_p/m+(8(M-m)/m)h e^{-(\alpha-p)/8}2​h2Cp​/m+(8(M−m)/m)he−(α−p)/8. Since M−m≤MM-m\le MM−m≤M, the tail upper bound bounds the second term by 1280MM22h3/m31280MM_2^2h^3/m^31280MM22​h3/m3. For 0<h≤10<h\le10<h≤1, h3≤h2h^3\le h^2h3≤h2. Thus the complete persistent error is at most Bph2B_ph^2Bp​h2, where Bp=2Cp/m+1280MM22/m3B_p=\sqrt2 C_p/m+1280MM_2^2/m^3Bp​=2​Cp​/m+1280MM22​/m3. Every coefficient is explicit, and the exact fourth moment remains valid in dimension one.

Combine the exact fourth-moment Taylor coefficient, the score-moment coefficient, and the controlled tail coefficient into one nonnegative constant. The persistent error term is then at most that constant times h2h^2h2. This is the quantity allocated to the requested accuracy.

03Choose a positive step satisfying every restriction §

Mathematical statement and proof

For positive dimension and accuracy choose the minimum of 111, m/(5γM)m/(5\gamma M)m/(5γM), m/(45pM2)m/(4\sqrt{5p}M_2)m/(45p​M2​), and 0.94ε/Bp\sqrt{0.94\varepsilon/B_p}0.94ε/Bp​​. All four terms are positive. The second and third enforce the two source step restrictions; the last gives Bph2≤0.94εB_ph^2\le0.94\varepsilonBp​h2≤0.94ε. One proves each minimum bound, compares nonnegative squares for the final term, and multiplies through positive denominators. For fixed curvature constants, BpB_pBp​ grows linearly with ppp, so the displayed step and the logarithmic count exhibit the source p/ε\sqrt{p/\varepsilon}p/ε​ iteration dependence.

Take the minimum of the stability limit, cutoff limit, any auxiliary small-step limit, and the square-root accuracy limit. Positivity of each entry gives a positive admissible step. Every analytic restriction and the bias allocation then hold simultaneously.
