Independence of the reference velocity and the following Brownian interval

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

This section proves the stochastic source condition needed in the repaired second-order KLMC argument. The reference trajectory is constructed from its initial phase and past Brownian input. Its terminal velocity is independent of the future OU convolution. Stationarity then identifies their joint law as standard Gaussian velocity times the exact thermal Gaussian innovation.

Finite-prefix splitting approximants depend only on past innovations. Their second-mean limit is the nonlinear reference state, so independence from the following Brownian interval passes to that state.

01Make dependence on a finite innovation prefix explicit §

Mathematical statement and proof

A recursion at index n reads only noise entries with index less than n. Induction proves this for the general random recursion and for the deterministic Verlet–OU splitting. These identities permit arbitrary continuation of a finite past noise grid without changing its terminal state.

After finitely many splitting steps, the state is a measurable function of initialization and a finite innovation prefix. This follows inductively from the explicit recursion. Hence future interval responses can be separated from all information used to construct that state.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.20.3.

02Identify source representatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coupling extension has independent Gaussian velocity and a specified Brownian coordinate. The square-root fluctuation-dissipation factor is already built into its continuous linear noise lift. The representative theorem proves that each lifted segment is the original Brownian random vector, evaluated at that coordinate and multiplied by the exact factor.

The finite-prefix recursion agrees almost everywhere with the Hilbert-space splitting representatives. This permits the independence proof to use pointwise measurable functions while retaining the same variables that appear in the analytic convergence theorem.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

03Independence for splitting approximants §

Mathematical statement and proof

Divide the past horizon into n+1 intervals. Continue its innovation stream by repeating the last past interval, so every deterministic kernel stays entirely before the future interval. The proved Brownian support theorem makes this whole stream independent of the future response. Adjoin the independent initial positions and velocity, and reassociate the product source. The finite pointwise splitting recursion is a measurable function of precisely those past inputs. Its L² representative is the original vector recursion. Prefix dependence identifies its endpoint with the continuous-input splitting chain, proving independence of each splitting approximant.

The prefix source and the following Brownian interval are independent. Applying the recursion to the prefix and the response map to the future block preserves independence. Thus every splitting approximant is independent of the next interval source.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.20.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.20.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

04Pass to the nonlinear kinetic reference §

Mathematical statement and proof

The matching-input splitting theorem converges in L² to the continuous kinetic integral solution on any finite horizon. Projecting to velocity preserves this convergence. Independence from the fixed future convolution is closed under L² convergence, so the limiting reference velocity is independent of that same future random vector. The future marginal itself is exactly the translated OU thermal Gaussian law, by the proved Brownian convolution distribution and measure-preserving source projection.

The splitting approximants converge to the nonlinear reference state in second mean. The closedness-of-independence argument passes the product joint law to that limit. This supplies the fresh-noise property for the constructed continuous reference, rather than assuming a stochastic filtration theorem.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

05The Gaussian product law of reference velocity and future response §

Mathematical statement and proof

The reference phase remains Gaussian-times-Gibbs distributed by the proved stationarity theorem. Its velocity marginal is therefore standard Gaussian. Combining this marginal, the exact future thermal Gaussian marginal and the newly proved independence gives their full product joint law. The stitched reference chain equals the same continuous solution at grid points, yielding the identical joint-law statement for every numerical interval.

The reference velocity marginal is standard Gaussian and is independent of the following Wiener responses. Combining these facts gives the exact Gaussian initial/future product laws used to establish free-OU stationarity inside the Hessian correction.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
