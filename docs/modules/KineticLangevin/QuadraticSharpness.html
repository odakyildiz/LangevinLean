Quadratic examples and the repeated-root boundary of the contraction rate

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Exact solutions of the quadratic kinetic equations justify the slow exponential rate and explain why the repeated-root boundary cannot have the singular prefactor substituted into it. Both examples start with unit position difference and equal initial velocities.

Explicit quadratic solutions test the optimized contraction exponent. The slow characteristic mode proves rate sharpness, while the repeated-root solution explains why the critical prefactor requires special treatment.

01The overdamped quadratic response §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let 0≤a<b0\le a<b0≤a<b, set friction γ=a+b\gamma=a+bγ=a+b, and curvature m=abm=abm=ab. The deterministic difference solution is x(t)=be−at−ae−btb−a,v(t)=ab(e−bt−e−at)b−a.x(t)=\frac{be^{-at}-ae^{-bt}}{b-a},\qquad v(t)=\frac{ab(e^{-bt}-e^{-at})}{b-a}.x(t)=b−abe−at−ae−bt​,v(t)=b−aab(e−bt−e−at)​. The argument checks x(0)=1x(0)=1x(0)=1, v(0)=0v(0)=0v(0)=0, then differentiates both exponentials and proves exactly x′=vx\prime=vx′=v, v′=−γv−mxv\prime=-\gamma v-mxv′=−γv−mx. These are the synchronous kinetic difference equations for the quadratic potential mx2/2mx^2/2mx2/2; shared Brownian forcing cancels. Thus the example respects the equal-initial-velocity premise of the source contraction statement.

For a quadratic potential the synchronous difference equation has constant coefficients. Its characteristic polynomial is r2−γr+mr^2-\gamma r+mr2−γr+m when decay is written as e−rte^{-rt}e−rt. The two roots give explicit exponential solutions, making the candidate slow rate directly testable.

02No faster exponential rate is possible §

Mathematical statement and proof

The position response satisfies x(t)≥e−atx(t)\ge e^{-at}x(t)≥e−at for all t≥0t\ge0t≥0, since x(t)−e−at=a(e−at−e−bt)/(b−a)≥0x(t)-e^{-at}=a(e^{-at}-e^{-bt})/(b-a)\ge0x(t)−e−at=a(e−at−e−bt)/(b−a)≥0. For any proposed faster exponent β>a\beta>aβ>a and any finite real constant CCC, choose t=log⁡(∣C∣+1)/(β−a)t=\log(|C|+1)/(\beta-a)t=log(∣C∣+1)/(β−a). Then e−at=(∣C∣+1)e−βt>Ce−βte^{-at}=(|C|+1)e^{-\beta t}>Ce^{-\beta t}e−at=(∣C∣+1)e−βt>Ce−βt. The argument constructs this nonnegative time explicitly and proves the strict violation, so the sharpness assertion involves an explicit time at which the faster bound fails.

Choose an initial condition with a nonzero component in the slow eigendirection. Multiplying its trajectory by any faster exponential yields an unbounded factor. Hence no finite uniform prefactor can make that faster exponential a valid bound for all later times.

03The repeated-root solution and its unavoidable prefactor §

Mathematical statement and proof

At critical damping, γ=2a\gamma=2aγ=2a and m=a2m=a^2m=a2, the exact equal-initial-velocity solution is x(t)=(1+at)e−atx(t)=(1+at)e^{-at}x(t)=(1+at)e−at, v(t)=−a2te−atv(t)=-a^2te^{-at}v(t)=−a2te−at. The argument verifies the same kinetic equations directly. When a>0a>0a>0, no finite constant can bound this position by Ce−atCe^{-at}Ce−at: choose t=(∣C∣+1)/at=(|C|+1)/at=(∣C∣+1)/a. Thus the critical exponent is a limiting rate whose polynomial prefactor cannot be suppressed. This explains the singular denominator at the equal-curvature boundary of the displayed contraction prefactor.

At a repeated characteristic root, the independent solutions include a polynomial time factor multiplying the exponential. That factor cannot be bounded by a fixed constant at the same exponent. The singular prefactor at the critical boundary therefore reflects real dynamics.

04The source overdamped rate with the quadratic roots §

Mathematical statement and proof

For m>0m>0m>0, γ>0\gamma>0γ>0, and γ2>4m\gamma^2>4mγ2>4m, put a=(γ−γ2−4m)/2a=(\gamma-\sqrt{\gamma^2-4m})/2a=(γ−γ2−4m​)/2, b=(γ+γ2−4m)/2b=(\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma^2-4m})/2b=(γ+γ2−4m​)/2. One proves 0<a<b0<a<b0<a<b, a+b=γa+b=\gammaa+b=γ, and ab=mab=mab=m, using the exact square-root identity. Therefore the lower-bound example has precisely the slow rate reported in the source overdamped regime; at γ=2M\gamma=2\sqrt Mγ=2M​ it gives a=M−M−ma=\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-m}a=M​−M−m​.

The optimized large-friction exponent equals the smaller positive root of the quadratic equation. This identifies the general bound with the exact slow decay of this quadratic potential. It establishes rate sharpness for that deterministic comparison, with the repeated-root qualification retained.
