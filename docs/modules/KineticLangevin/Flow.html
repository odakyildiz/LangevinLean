The actual kinetic solution depends continuously on its initial state and noise path

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Picard existence alone does not ensure a selected solution can be used as a measurable random process. This module proves stability when both the initial condition and continuous forcing vary, then constructs the actual kinetic flow by selection from the existing existence theorem. The stability estimate proves that the selected endpoint, and indeed the whole compact solution path, is jointly Lipschitz. No measurability, output-law identity or contraction conclusion is postulated.

02Use compact paths with a uniform norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

VelocityForcing T is the Banach space of continuous velocity-forcing paths on [0,T][0,T][0,T], equipped with the supremum norm. The extension B^t=B(clip⁡[0,T]t)−B(0)\widehat B_t=B(\operatorname{clip}_{[0,T]}t)-B(0)Bt​=B(clip[0,T]​t)−B(0) is continuous on the whole real line and starts at zero. This permits direct use of the proved finite-horizon Picard construction. Two normalized extensions satisfy ∥B^t−C^t∥≤2∥B−C∥∞\|\widehat B_t-\widehat C_t\|\le2\|B-C\|_\infty∥Bt​−Ct​∥≤2∥B−C∥∞​: each of the two evaluation differences is bounded by the uniform norm. Lean uses continuity of the interval projection, evaluates at zero, and applies the norm triangle inequality to the two differences. No derivative of a forcing path is taken.

03Select an actual solution from proved existence §

Mathematical statement and proof

kineticFlow uses classical choice on exists_kinetic_integral, supplying the normalized forcing (B^t,0)(\widehat B_t,0)(Bt​,0). Its specification theorem retains all conclusions of that construction: the path is globally continuous, its initial phase is exactly the supplied phase, and it satisfies the concrete kinetic integral equation at every time on the horizon. Classical selection is justified because existence was proved analytically. It does not add a solution axiom. Subsequent stability between any two solutions makes every selected representative agree on the horizon and provides continuous parameter dependence.

kineticFlow_eq_of_integral applies the proved Lipschitz integral-equation uniqueness theorem to identify the selection with any other continuous solution driven by the same normalized forcing and initial phase. Thus a concrete Brownian realization can be identified with this canonical flow without assuming equality of endpoint laws.

04Prove joint endpoint continuity quantitatively §

Mathematical statement and proof

The kinetic negative drift has Lipschitz constant K0=γ+uL+1K_0=\gamma+uL+1K0​=γ+uL+1 in the product maximum norm. Apply the two-input estimate with forcing difference D=2∥B−C∥∞D=2\|B-C\|_\inftyD=2∥B−C∥∞​. This gives

∥Ytz,B−Ytw,C∥≤(∥z−w∥+2∥B−C∥∞)e(K0+1)t.\|Y_t^{z,B}-Y_t^{w,C}\|\le(\|z-w\|+2\|B-C\|_\infty)e^{(K_0+1)t}.∥Ytz,B​−Ytw,C​∥≤(∥z−w∥+2∥B−C∥∞​)e(K0​+1)t.

In the product parameter norm both input differences are bounded by the same distance, so the endpoint is Lipschitz with constant 3e(K0+1)t3e^{(K_0+1)t}3e(K0​+1)t. Lean derives the estimate from the actual selected paths and their specifications, then bounds each coordinate by the product norm. kineticFlow_endpoint_continuous follows from the Lipschitz theorem. Thus the eventual random endpoint map is measurable for a proved reason, even though its original existence definition used classical choice.

05Control the entire solution as a compact continuous path §

Mathematical statement and proof

kineticFlowPath restricts the selected solution to [0,T][0,T][0,T], retaining its proved continuity. At each time its difference has the endpoint bound. Since K0+1≥0K_0+1\ge0K0​+1≥0, e(K0+1)t≤e(K0+1)Te^{(K_0+1)t}\le e^{(K_0+1)T}e(K0​+1)t≤e(K0​+1)T on the horizon. The supremum-norm characterization then bounds the whole path difference by 3e(K0+1)T3e^{(K_0+1)T}3e(K0​+1)T times the initial-state/forcing-path distance. Lean applies ContinuousMap.norm_le, proves the pointwise exponential comparison, and uses the endpoint Lipschitz estimate. The resulting path-valued Lipschitz and continuity theorems support pushforwards into a genuine space of continuous trajectories, not merely separate endpoint choices.
