Derive the chi-square positive-part tail from the Gaussian density

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Hessian comparison needs a Gaussian tail estimate even when the Gaussian velocity and position discrepancy are dependent. The relevant bound is E(∥G∥2−a)+≤4e−(a−p)/4\mathbb E(\lVert G\rVert^2-a)_+\le4e^{-(a-p)/4}E(∥G∥2−a)+​≤4e−(a−p)/4 for a≥5pa\ge5pa≥5p. The proof below derives its exponential moment and the tail estimate from Gaussian measures.

The excess of a Gaussian squared norm above a cutoff is controlled through an explicit exponential moment. This is the precise tail quantity needed when a bounded random Hessian difference multiplies a Gaussian velocity.

01Compute the scalar exponential square moment with its integrability §

Mathematical statement and proof

Multiply the standard Gaussian density by ex2/3e^{x^2/3}ex2/3. Its exponent becomes −x2/2+x2/3=−x2/6-x^2/2+x^2/3=-x^2/6−x2/2+x2/3=−x2/6. The real Gaussian integral therefore gives EeG2/3=3\mathbb Ee^{G^2/3}=\sqrt3EeG2/3=3​. The proof establishes integrability of this weighted density before computing its integral, so the Bochner integral cannot conceal divergence. The argument uses the Gaussian density, the with-density integrability equivalence, the real Gaussian integral and exact square-root algebra.

The exponential tilt changes the scalar Gaussian precision from one to one third. Its normalization ratio is therefore 3\sqrt33​. Establishing integrability before evaluation is essential because the later cutoff argument needs a finite majorant.

02Multiply the independent coordinate moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

Since e∥G∥2/3=∏ieGi2/3e^{\lVert G\rVert^2/3}=\prod_i e^{G_i^2/3}e∥G∥2/3=∏i​eGi2​/3, product integrability and finite-product Fubini give Ee∥G∥2/3=(3)p\mathbb Ee^{\lVert G\rVert^2/3}=(\sqrt3)^pEe∥G∥2/3=(3​)p. The proof works on the constructed product of scalar Gaussian measures and uses its established Euclidean standard-Gaussian pushforward. This handles dimension zero as well.

The product identity uses independence of the Gaussian coordinates. Each factor has the same finite exponential moment, so Fubini multiplies them. Dimension enters through the number of factors, which is why the later exponent contains a term proportional to ppp.

03Choose an elementary Chernoff tilt §

Mathematical statement and proof

The elementary bound eu≥1+ue^u\ge1+ueu≥1+u at u=1/3u=1/3u=1/3, raised to the fourth power, proves 3≤e2/3\sqrt3\le e^{2/3}3​≤e2/3. Also (y−a)+≤3e(y−a)/3(y-a)_+\le3e^{(y-a)/3}(y−a)+​≤3e(y−a)/3, since its positive branch is bounded using the same exponential inequality and its zero branch is nonnegative. These two inequalities give a convenient exponential moment estimate without importing an unproved chi-square concentration result.

For y>ay>ay>a, the inequality (y−a)≤3e(y−a)/3(y-a)\le3e^{(y-a)/3}(y−a)≤3e(y−a)/3 follows by scaling the elementary exponential bound. For y≤ay\le ay≤a, the positive part vanishes. This provides one pointwise estimate valid on both sides of the cutoff.

04Integrate the positive-part bound and use the threshold §

Mathematical statement and proof

Applying the pointwise bound to y=∥G∥2y=\lVert G\rVert^2y=∥G∥2 gives E(y−a)+≤3e−a/3(3)p≤3e−a/3+2p/3\mathbb E(y-a)_+\le3e^{-a/3}(\sqrt3)^p\le3e^{-a/3+2p/3}E(y−a)+​≤3e−a/3(3​)p≤3e−a/3+2p/3. If a≥5pa\ge5pa≥5p, the exponent is at most −(a−p)/4-(a-p)/4−(a−p)/4. Replacing the factor three by four yields exactly the source bound. First, the argument derives integrability of the positive part from its integrable exponential majorant, then applies integral monotonicity, the exact moment and scalar exponent comparison.

The threshold a≥5pa\ge5pa≥5p absorbs the exponential-moment dimension factor into a decaying tail. The resulting bound controls the excess above the cutoff, rather than merely the probability of exceeding it. That stronger quantity is what appears after squaring the truncated operator estimate.

05Use the bound on the original coupled probability space §

Mathematical statement and proof

The pushforward integration identity transfers the estimate to any standard-Gaussian velocity. Its joint distribution with a numerical position discrepancy is unrestricted. This is essential for a synchronous sampling proof, where past noise generally makes those two variables dependent.

Only the velocity marginal is used in the tail integral. The displacement and velocity may remain dependent on the common coupling space. This is crucial when the displacement was created by earlier steps of the same numerical process.
