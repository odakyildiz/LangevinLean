A positive-part tail bound for the squared Gaussian norm

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

Let GGG be a standard Gaussian vector in Rp\mathbb R^pRp, that is, a random vector whose ppp coordinates are independent with the standard normal law N(0,1)\mathcal N(0,1)N(0,1) of density ϕ(x)=(2π)−1/2e−x2/2\phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}ϕ(x)=(2π)−1/2e−x2/2. Write u+=max⁡(u,0)u_+=\max(u,0)u+​=max(u,0) for the positive part. This section proves the tail estimate

E(∥G∥2−a)+≤4 e−(a−p)/4for every a≥5p,\mathbb E\bigl(\|G\|^2-a\bigr)_+\le4\,e^{-(a-p)/4}\qquad\text{for every }a\ge5p,E(∥G∥2−a)+​≤4e−(a−p)/4for every a≥5p,

which is the χ2\chi^2χ2 bound used in the paper's comparison of two second-order updates. The proof is a Chernoff-type argument: the exponential moment E e∥G∥2/3=(3)p\mathbb E\,e^{\|G\|^2/3}=(\sqrt3)^pEe∥G∥2/3=(3​)p is computed exactly from the Gaussian density, the positive part is dominated pointwise by an exponential, and the threshold a≥5pa\ge5pa≥5p absorbs the dimension factor.

The bound concerns the expected excess of ∥G∥2\|G\|^2∥G∥2 above the level aaa, not merely the probability of exceeding aaa. In the final step it is transferred to any random vector whose law is N(0,Ip)\mathcal N(0,I_p)N(0,Ip​), on any probability space. No independence between this vector and other random variables is required, which is what the comparison of two coupled second-order chains needs: there the Gaussian velocity and the position discrepancy are built from the same past noise.

02The exponential moment of the squared Gaussian norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let G∼N(0,Ip)G\sim\mathcal N(0,I_p)G∼N(0,Ip​) on Rp\mathbb R^pRp, p≥0p\ge0p≥0. The function z↦e∥z∥2/3z\mapsto e^{\|z\|^2/3}z↦e∥z∥2/3 is N(0,Ip)\mathcal N(0,I_p)N(0,Ip​)-integrable and

Proof. Hence it suffices to integrate w↦e∥w∥2/3w\mapsto e^{\|w\|^2/3}w↦e∥w∥2/3 against the product measure. For w=(w1,…,wp)w=(w_1,\dots,w_p)w=(w1​,…,wp​), ∥w∥2=∑iwi2\|w\|^2=\sum_iw_i^2∥w∥2=∑i​wi2​, so

Each factor is integrable with respect to its coordinate measure N(0,1)\mathcal N(0,1)N(0,1) by the previous step. A product ∏iui(wi)\prod_iu_i(w_i)∏i​ui​(wi​) of functions each integrable in its own variable is integrable with respect to the product measure, and by Fubini's theorem its integral is the product of the integrals. Therefore the function (1) is integrable and

Mathematical statement and proof

Only the law of VVV enters. The probability space may carry other random variables, such as a position discrepancy produced by the same noise, with an arbitrary joint law with VVV.
