Wasserstein contraction of the independent-noise OBABO laws

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The preceding pathwise energy contraction now becomes a theorem about the Markov laws. An explicit invertible change of coordinates turns the modified energy into Euclidean squared distance. Measurability, the commuting law identity, independent source extension, and passage to the Wasserstein infimum are all proved. The result holds for every probability law of the two thermostat inputs, including the independent Gaussian law used by the sampler.

Changing to contracting coordinates transports the complete transition measure as well as its deterministic update. This makes the pathwise OBABO contraction a theorem about the iterated probability laws.

01Build and verify the weighted-coordinate inverse §

Mathematical statement and proof

The weighted Hilbert coordinates are T(v,x)=(x+bv,cv)T(v,x)=(x+bv,c v)T(v,x)=(x+bv,cv), where c=a−b2c=\sqrt{a-b^2}c=a−b2​. For b2<ab^2<ab2<a, the inverse is T−1(u,w)=(w/c,u−bw/c)T^{-1}(u,w)=(w/c,u-bw/c)T−1(u,w)=(w/c,u−bw/c). First, the argument proves c≠0c\ne0c=0, then uses the inverse cancellation identities for real scalars to check both compositions. The transform commutes with subtraction by direct vector-space algebra. Its continuity follows from continuity of scalar multiplication, coordinate projections, addition, and the Euclidean-product identification. The local measurable structure on the Euclidean product geometry is explicitly its Borel structure, so these continuous maps supply the measurable changes of variables required for the measure arguments.

The weighted coordinate map is linear and has an explicitly computed inverse. Applying both compositions verifies the identification of physical and contracting coordinates. This ensures that transformed laws can be returned to their original physical meaning.

02Conjugate the noisy step and prove its Lipschitz bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the transformed update by the conjugation P~ξ=T∘Pξ∘T−1\widetilde P_\xi=T\circ P_\xi\circ T^{-1}Pξ​=T∘Pξ​∘T−1, retaining the original two noise arguments and their damping and amplitude. Conjugating the noisy map means changing coordinates before and after its update. Under common noises its Lipschitz coefficient is the weighted pathwise contraction factor. The innovation still enters through its pushforward law.

03Markov iteration commutes with the coordinate change §

Mathematical statement and proof

The OBABO law sequence is the repeated independent-noise product-and-pushforward transition of the original defined step; the transformed OBABO laws use its transformed realization. A generic induction proves the commuting diagram for any measurable maps satisfying T(F(x,ξ))=G(Tx,ξ)T(F(x,\xi))=G(Tx,\xi)T(F(x,ξ))=G(Tx,ξ). At each step, the pushforward formula for product measures proves that transforming the current state commutes with adjoining an independent innovation. Two applications of composition of pushforwards reduce the law identity to the pointwise conjugacy. Instantiating with the verified inverse gives T#Ln(μ)=L~n(T#μ)T_\#\mathcal L_n(\mu)=\widetilde{\mathcal L}_n(T_\#\mu)T#​Ln​(μ)=Ln​(T#​μ), so the contracting measures are the transformed sampler laws.

A measurable coordinate change commutes with a single transition pushforward after conjugation. Induction extends this identity to every Markov iterate. Thus contraction in transformed coordinates describes the same probability chain as the physical OBABO recursion.

04Take the infimum over all couplings and iterate the laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let r=1−mh/(3γ)r=\sqrt{1-mh/(3\gamma)}r=1−mh/(3γ)​. For every coupling of two current laws, append one common innovation independently. Its two output marginals are the Markov outputs, and the pointwise bound gives output transport cost at most rrr times the input coupling cost. The previously proved random-map theorem takes the infimum over all input couplings; it assumes no optimal coupling exists. Induction, with the probability property of each iterate established by induction, yields W2(L~nμ,L~nν)≤rnW2(μ,ν)W_2(\widetilde{\mathcal L}_n\mu,\widetilde{\mathcal L}_n\nu)\le r^nW_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(Ln​μ,Ln​ν)≤rnW2​(μ,ν). The commuting law identity transfers this to the original laws measured in the modified geometry. The extended-real theorem is unconditional on transport finiteness. If the initial weighted transport distance is finite, the proved upper bound first establishes finiteness of the output and then permits conversion to the real-valued Wasserstein estimate. Stationarity and numerical target bias are separate claims, not additional hypotheses to this law contraction.

For every input coupling, use the same independent fresh noise in both updates and bound the output cost by the pathwise contraction. Taking the coupling infimum yields one-step Wasserstein contraction. Iterating applies this to the transformed Markov laws.
