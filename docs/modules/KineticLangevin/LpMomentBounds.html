Gaussian fourth moments control the Taylor error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The quadratic Hessian remainder requires a fourth displacement moment, even though the stochastic solution itself lives in L². These results connect L4 seminorms, exact Gaussian fourth moments, and the existing L² kinematic equation.

A quadratic pointwise Taylor remainder requires a fourth displacement moment to be bounded in second mean. Stationary Gaussian velocities and the time-integral estimate provide that moment with its exact dimension factor.

01Turn a pointwise quadratic remainder into an L² bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose random vectors D and X satisfy ‖D(ω)‖≤a‖X(ω)‖², with a≥0 and ‖X‖₄≤C. Monotonicity of the extended L² norm gives ‖D‖₂≤a‖‖X‖²‖₂. The power rule identifies the last factor with ‖X‖₄², yielding aC². The proof works first with extended nonnegative norms, where the power and monotonicity rules hold without hidden finiteness assumptions, and then converts the finite upper bound back to the L² norm.

If a remainder is bounded pointwise by C∥D∥2C\|D\|^2C∥D∥2, its second-mean norm is bounded by C(E∥D∥4)1/2C(\mathbb E\|D\|^4)^{1/2}C(E∥D∥4)1/2. This is why second-order analysis requires a fourth displacement moment even though the final metric is Wasserstein of order two.

02Compute the Gaussian L4 radius §

Mathematical statement and proof

A standard Gaussian vector in p dimensions has fourth norm moment p²+2p. Fernique integrability ensures its fourth seminorm is finite. The integral formula for the L4 seminorm therefore yields (p²+2p)^(1/4) on any realization of the Gaussian law. This proof transfers an integral identity through the given Gaussian distributional identity and keeps the 2p correction, including the one-dimensional case.

The exact Gaussian fourth moment is p2+2pp^2+2pp2+2p. Taking its fourth root gives the L4L^4L4 radius, and squaring that radius gives the coefficient needed for a quadratic remainder. Retaining this exact value avoids an invalid small-dimension simplification.

03Apply it to the existing kinetic displacement §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume X(t)−X(0)=∫₀ᵗV(s)ds in the L² space and every V(s) has the standard Gaussian marginal. Each velocity lies in the same L4 ball of radius (p²+2p)^(1/4). The closed-convex averaging theorem for the existing L² integral gives ‖X(t)−X(0)‖₄≤t(p²+2p)^(1/4). No independence across times is used or required. This supplies exactly the fourth moment needed by the Hessian Taylor remainder in Proposition 2.

The kinetic position displacement is an integral of the reference velocity. The moment-integral estimate bounds its fourth-moment radius by elapsed time times the stationary velocity radius. Substitution therefore gives a quadratic time envelope for the force Taylor remainder.
