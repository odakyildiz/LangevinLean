Fourth displacement moments and the quadratic force remainder

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The Taylor remainder of the force is bounded by M22∥Ls−L0∥2\frac{M_2}{2}\|L_s-L_0\|^22M2​​∥Ls​−L0​∥2, a quadratic function of the displacement. Its L2L^2L2 norm is therefore controlled by the fourth moment of the displacement: if ∥D∥≤a∥X∥2\|D\|\le a\|X\|^2∥D∥≤a∥X∥2 almost surely, then ∥D∥L2≤a∥X∥L42\|D\|_{L^2}\le a\|X\|_{L^4}^2∥D∥L2​≤a∥X∥L42​. This section proves that estimate, computes the L4L^4L4 norm of a standard Gaussian vector in Rp\mathbb R^pRp,

(E∥V∥4)1/4=(p2+2p)1/4,\bigl(\mathbb E\|V\|^4\bigr)^{1/4}=(p^2+2p)^{1/4},(E∥V∥4)1/4=(p2+2p)1/4,

and combines the two with the averaging bound of the previous section: if Xt−X0=∫0tVr drX_t-X_0=\int_0^tV_r\,drXt​−X0​=∫0t​Vr​dr in L2L^2L2 and every VrV_rVr​ is standard Gaussian, then ∥Xt−X0∥L4≤t (p2+2p)1/4\|X_t-X_0\|_{L^4}\le t\,(p^2+2p)^{1/4}∥Xt​−X0​∥L4​≤t(p2+2p)1/4. Squaring gives the factor t2p2+2pt^2\sqrt{p^2+2p}t2p2+2p​ of the second-order local error. The exact value p2+2pp^2+2pp2+2p is kept; no simplification valid only for p≥2p\ge2p≥2 is made here.

01A quadratic pointwise bound in second mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let (Ω,P)(\Omega,\mathbb P)(Ω,P) be a measure space, EEE a normed space, D,X∈L2(P;E)D,X\in L^2(\mathbb P;E)D,X∈L2(P;E), and a,C≥0a,C\ge0a,C≥0. Assume ∥X∥L4≤C\|X\|_{L^4}\le C∥X∥L4​≤C and ∥D(ω)∥≤a∥X(ω)∥2\|D(\omega)\|\le a\|X(\omega)\|^2∥D(ω)∥≤a∥X(ω)∥2 for almost every ω\omegaω. Then ∥D∥L2≤aC2\|D\|_{L^2}\le aC^2∥D∥L2​≤aC2.

Proof. Work with the extended norms ∥⋅∥Lq∈[0,∞]\|\cdot\|_{L^q}\in[0,\infty]∥⋅∥Lq​∈[0,∞]. The L2L^2L2 norm is monotone with respect to almost-everywhere pointwise bounds and absolutely homogeneous, so the hypothesis gives

∥D∥L2≤a ∥ ∥X∥2∥L2.\|D\|_{L^2}\le a\,\bigl\|\,\|X\|^2\bigr\|_{L^2}.∥D∥L2​≤a​∥X∥2​L2​.

Since ∫(∥X∥2)2 dP=∫∥X∥4 dP\int(\|X\|^2)^2\,d\mathbb P=\int\|X\|^4\,d\mathbb P∫(∥X∥2)2dP=∫∥X∥4dP,

∥ ∥X∥2∥L2=(∫∥X∥4 dP)1/2=∥X∥L42≤C2.\bigl\|\,\|X\|^2\bigr\|_{L^2}=\Bigl(\int\|X\|^4\,d\mathbb P\Bigr)^{1/2}=\|X\|_{L^4}^2\le C^2.​∥X∥2​L2​=(∫∥X∥4dP)1/2=∥X∥L42​≤C2.

Hence ∥D∥L2≤aC2\|D\|_{L^2}\le aC^2∥D∥L2​≤aC2, a finite number; for an element of L2L^2L2 the extended norm is the usual norm. □\square□

02The L4L^4L4 norm of a standard Gaussian vector §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let V:Ω→RpV:\Omega\to\mathbb R^pV:Ω→Rp have law N(0,Ip)\mathcal N(0,I_p)N(0,Ip​) under P\mathbb PP. Then

∥V∥L4=(p2+2p)1/4.\|V\|_{L^4}=\bigl(p^2+2p\bigr)^{1/4}.∥V∥L4​=(p2+2p)1/4.

Proof. A Gaussian measure on a finite-dimensional space has finite moments of every order (for instance by Fernique's theorem, or directly from the integrability of e∥z∥2/3e^{\|z\|^2/3}e∥z∥2/3 shown in the section on Gaussian tails); since VVV has a Gaussian law, E∥V∥4<∞\mathbb E\|V\|^4<\inftyE∥V∥4<∞. Hence ∥V∥L4=(E∥V∥4)1/4\|V\|_{L^4}=(\mathbb E\|V\|^4)^{1/4}∥V∥L4​=(E∥V∥4)1/4. The fourth moment was computed in the section on Gaussian fourth moments: with independent standard normal coordinates ViV_iVi​,

E∥V∥4=E(∑iVi2)2=∑iEVi4+∑i≠jEVi2 EVj2=3p+p(p−1)=p2+2p.□\mathbb E\|V\|^4=\mathbb E\Bigl(\sum_iV_i^2\Bigr)^2=\sum_i\mathbb EV_i^4+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb EV_i^2\,\mathbb EV_j^2=3p+p(p-1)=p^2+2p.\qquad\squareE∥V∥4=E(i∑​Vi2​)2=i∑​EVi4​+i=j∑​EVi2​EVj2​=3p+p(p−1)=p2+2p.□

Only the law of VVV is used, so the value holds on every probability space carrying such a vector. For p=1p=1p=1 it gives 31/43^{1/4}31/4, and p2+2p\sqrt{p^2+2p}p2+2p​ is 3\sqrt33​, larger than 2 p=2\sqrt2\,p=\sqrt22​p=2​.

03The displacement of a Gaussian-velocity path in L4L^4L4 §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let X,V:R→L2(P;Rp)X,V:\mathbb R\to L^2(\mathbb P;\mathbb R^p)X,V:R→L2(P;Rp) with VVV continuous, let T∈RT\in\mathbb RT∈R, and assume for every t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T]

Then for every t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T],

∥Xt−X0∥L4≤t (p2+2p)1/4.\|X_t-X_0\|_{L^4}\le t\,\bigl(p^2+2p\bigr)^{1/4}.∥Xt​−X0​∥L4​≤t(p2+2p)1/4.

Proof. Fix t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T] and put R=(p2+2p)1/4R=(p^2+2p)^{1/4}R=(p2+2p)1/4. For s∈[0,t]⊆[0,T]s\in[0,t]\subseteq[0,T]s∈[0,t]⊆[0,T], the previous step gives ∥Vs∥L4=R\|V_s\|_{L^4}=R∥Vs​∥L4​=R. ∥Xt−X0∥L4=∥∫0tVr dr∥L4≤t R.□\|X_t-X_0\|_{L^4}=\Bigl\|\int_0^tV_r\,dr\Bigr\|_{L^4}\le t\,R.\qquad\square∥Xt​−X0​∥L4​=​∫0t​Vr​dr​L4​≤tR.□

The velocities at different times are not assumed independent; only their individual Gaussian laws and the continuity of the path in L2L^2L2 are used.
