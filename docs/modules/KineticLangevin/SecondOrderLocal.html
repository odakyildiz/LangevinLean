The actual cubic and quartic errors of the Hessian correction

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The audit follows the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. Actual canonical Brownian integral-solution mixing, full Gibbs stationarity, both correlated-Gaussian KLMC/KLMC2 Wasserstein bounds, literal Cholesky sampling, and explicit accuracy guarantees are proved. The corrected proofs checked here are unpublished: the audit records the continuous prefactor correction, the stationary local reference, and the retained stochastic Hessian-difference terms. KLMC2 keeps the exact fourth-moment factor in every dimension and states p≥2 for the printed coarser simplification; its sharper inverse-step-square tail is proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite horizons; no separate almost-surely continuous sample-path modification theorem is claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The free-OU path U contains the exact initial velocity and the shared Brownian convolution. The exact velocity is U minus the damped force integral. Correcting the frozen-force approximation by the Hessian acting on the integrated free path leaves two explicit errors: the quadratic force Taylor remainder and a triple force-memory integral. Their orders and constants are proved from these definitions.

01Build genuine damped integrals and continuous paths §

Mathematical statement and proof

dampedIntegral(γ,F,t)=∫₀ᵗexp(−γ(t−s))F(s)ds. It is continuous for a continuous F: factor exp(−γt) outside the primitive of exp(γs)F(s), then apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to that primitive. The general primitive continuity lemma also supports all nested integrals used below.

02Define the corrected approximation and its two actual defects §

Mathematical statement and proof

The corrected velocity is U(t)−ψ₁(t)G(0)−H₀∫₀ᵗexp(−γ(t−s))∫₀ˢU(r)dr ds. The Taylor defect integrates G(s)−G(0)−H₀(X(s)−X(0)) against the damping kernel. The memory defect applies H₀ to the damped integral of the primitive of the damped force integral. These definitions retain the entire free-OU path, including its stochastic Hessian correction.

03Derive the local-error identity from the exact equations §

Mathematical statement and proof

From V=U−KγG and X(s)−X(0)=∫₀ˢV, obtain ∫₀ˢU=X(s)−X(0)+∫₀ˢKγG. Substitute this identity into the correction, commute the bounded linear Hessian through the Bochner integral, and separate the frozen constant force using ∫exp(−γ(t−s))ds=ψ₁(t). Algebra then proves correctedVelocity−V equals the Taylor defect minus the memory defect. The result assumes exact mild and kinematic equations, rather than an error inequality.

04Integrate polynomial envelopes with their exact constants §

Mathematical statement and proof

For forward time and γ≥0, the damping kernel lies in (0,1]. If ‖F(s)‖≤Csⁿ, the norm of its damped integral is at most ∫₀ᵗCsⁿds=Ctⁿ⁺¹/(n+1). The undamped case follows at γ=0. Applying this rule three times to a score bound G₀ gives the memory norm at most ‖H₀‖G₀t³/6.

05Obtain cubic velocity and quartic position errors §

Mathematical statement and proof

A Taylor forcing bound Rs² integrates to Rt³/3. Adding the memory estimate yields velocity error at most (2R+‖H₀‖G₀)t³/6. The corrected position is X(0) plus the integral of the corrected velocity. Its difference from the exact position is therefore the integral of the actual velocity error, giving (2R+‖H₀‖G₀)t⁴/24. Continuity of the corrected velocity justifies this subtraction of integrals.

06Check the paper’s quarter-constant in transformed coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write B=Ct³. The component estimates give ‖v‖≤B/6 and ‖x‖≤Bt/24. Under γt≤1/5, the first transformed coordinate satisfies ‖v+γx‖≤7B/40; the second has norm ‖v‖. Squaring and adding gives an energy below (B/4)², hence transformed norm at most B/4. Applying this exact component lemma with C=2R+‖H₀‖G₀ proves the actual local phase estimate. For stationary kinetic velocities and score moments, C becomes M₂√(p²+2p)+M√(Mp).
