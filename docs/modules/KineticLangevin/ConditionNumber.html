A uniform correction to the Hessian condition-number bound

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The supplied manuscript informally simplifies its tail-free KLMC2 step and iteration bound using κ2=(M22/3+Mp−1/3)/m\kappa_2=(M_2^{2/3}+Mp^{-1/3})/mκ2​=(M22/3​+Mp−1/3)/m. This section proves a valid refinement with max⁡(κ,κ2)\max(\kappa,\kappa_2)max(κ,κ2​), identifies the extra regime in which the printed shape follows, and constructs an exact natural-dimension family disproving a uniform removal of the ordinary condition number. Every scalar quantity is tied to the source definition. These are statements about the explicit step and count prescription, with its Gaussian tail intentionally excluded; the separate sampler accuracy theorems include that tail.

The ordinary curvature condition number and proposed Hessian refinement need separate control. A corrected maximum gives a uniform bound, and an explicit parameter family shows why the printed simplification requires an extra regime.

01Keep both condition numbers and normalize the source definition §

Mathematical statement and proof

The ordinary gradient condition number is κ=M/m\kappa=M/mκ=M/m. The supplied source proposes ρ=(M22/3+Mp−1/3)/m\rho=(M_2^{2/3}+Mp^{-1/3})/mρ=(M22/3​+Mp−1/3)/m. Introduce the dimensionless Hessian ratio η=M2/(mm)\eta=M_2/(m\sqrt m)η=M2​/(mm​). The first proof establishes the exact identity ρ=η2/3+κp−1/3\rho=\eta^{2/3}+\kappa p^{-1/3}ρ=η2/3+κp−1/3, rather than assuming a convenient relationship between the two quantities. Positivity of mmm permits the real-power product and quotient rules: (mm)2/3=m(m\sqrt m)^{2/3}=m(mm​)2/3=m. The argument rewrites the square root as a half power, combines exponents, distributes the quotient power, and proves the remaining fraction identity with expansion of the scalar polynomials.

The normalized Hessian ratio removes the strong-convexity unit before taking fractional powers. This makes the identity between the proposed refined condition number and its two summands exact. Comparing those summands is a separate question and cannot be inferred from their notation.

02The useful cubic inequalities from the definition §

Mathematical statement and proof

For κ>0\kappa>0κ>0, η≥0\eta\ge0η≥0, and p>0p>0p>0, the source quantity ρ\rhoρ is positive. Each nonnegative summand is at most their sum. Cubing η2/3≤ρ\eta^{2/3}\le\rhoη2/3≤ρ gives η2≤ρ3\eta^2\le\rho^3η2≤ρ3. 07Construct an exact family exposing the missing ordinary condition number §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take t>0t>0t>0, κ=t2\kappa=t^2κ=t2, η=1\eta=1η=1, p=t6p=t^6p=t6, and ε=1\varepsilon=1ε=1. The exact fractional-power rules give (t6)−1/3=t−2(t^6)^{-1/3}=t^{-2}(t6)−1/3=t−2, so the source condition number is identically ρ=2\rho=2ρ=2. Meanwhile the ordinary stability term alone implies W≥20κ2=20t4\mathcal W\ge20\kappa^2=20t^4W≥20κ2=20t4. The proof therefore compares exact finite expressions, without relying on informal asymptotic notation. This is a counterexample to a scalar step/count simplification. It does not establish a lower bound on the possible performance of the stochastic algorithm.

The counterexample keeps the refined quantity fixed while allowing the ordinary stiffness to grow. The stability branch then grows by an additional power of the family parameter. This isolates the algebraic obstruction to the proposed uniform simplification.
