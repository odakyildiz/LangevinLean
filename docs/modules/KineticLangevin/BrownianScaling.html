Brownian scaling under a change of kinetic time

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

Time change is a probability statement as well as a deterministic substitution in the drift. If c>0c>0c>0, the process B^t=cBt/c\widehat B_t=\sqrt c B_{t/c}Bt​=c​Bt/c​ has the same Brownian finite-dimensional laws, because its covariance is cmin⁡(s/c,t/c)=min⁡(s,t)c\min(s/c,t/c)=\min(s,t)cmin(s/c,t/c)=min(s,t). This section proves that assertion on the already constructed specified probability source, retains independence of entire coordinate paths, and calculates the physical noise amplitude.

The clock change is proved at the level of the Brownian coordinate law. Applying it together with velocity scaling gives the correct normalized thermal input for the kinetic equation.

Statement and proof

01Change the coordinate process and prove its Brownian law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The rescaled Brownian process applies the clock directly to each coordinate of the specified probability source. The Brownian scaling identity uses a−1/2Bata^{-1/2}B_{at}a−1/2Bat​; setting a=c−1a=c^{-1}a=c−1 gives the desired formula. The argument simplifies the inverse square root and the double inverse to identify this process exactly. The resulting Brownian law gives all finite-dimensional Gaussian distributions and independent increments, rather than a single matching variance.

For a positive clock factor, scale time and Brownian amplitude together so that increment variance remains equal to elapsed new time. Centering, covariance, Gaussianity, and independent increments then identify the rescaled coordinate process as standard Brownian motion.

02Preserve independence and recover the kinetic temperature §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each entire changed-clock coordinate path is a measurable function of its own original coordinate path. Independence is therefore preserved by measurable composition. Finally, velocity scaling by c−1c^{-1}c−1 changes the standard thermal input to c−12cγ Bt/c=2γ/c2 B^tc^{-1}\sqrt{2c\gamma}\,B_{t/c}=\sqrt{2\gamma/c^2}\,\widehat B_tc−12cγ​Bt/c​=2γ/c2​Bt​. The scalar proof uses the square-root product and quotient identities with their nonnegativity hypotheses. For c=Nc=\sqrt Nc=N​, this is exactly the physical particle temperature 1/N1/N1/N.

The rescaled Brownian path is a measurable function of the original source, so independence from initialization is preserved. Applying the same clock change to the kinetic noise amplitude restores the normalized thermal coefficient, provided velocity and friction are transformed consistently.
