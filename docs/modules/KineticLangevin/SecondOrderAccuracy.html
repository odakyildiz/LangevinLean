A KLMC2 step length and integer count for a prescribed error

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The KLMC2 law theorem and the proved quadratic upper bound on persistent error combine into explicit accuracy corollaries. The second result chooses a positive step satisfying every source restriction and a rounded natural iteration count, with no remaining local-error, stationarity, law or recurrence premise.

The full corrected sampler theorem yields a finite ε\varepsilonε prescription after bounding every persistent contribution quadratically. The selected positive step and rounded count bound the output error.

Mathematical statement and proof

The exact Gaussian-chain theorem gives W2(νn,π)≤2qnD+b(h)W_2(\nu_n,\pi)\le\sqrt2q^nD+b(h)W2​(νn​,π)≤2​qnD+b(h), where q=1−mh/(4γ)q=1-mh/(4\gamma)q=1−mh/(4γ). The proved source restrictions make 0≤q<10\le q<10≤q<1. For h≤1h\le1h≤1, the complete persistent error term satisfies b(h)≤Bph2b(h)\le B_ph^2b(h)≤Bp​h2. Allocate Bph2≤0.94εB_ph^2\le0.94\varepsilonBp​h2≤0.94ε and choose the natural ceiling of max⁡{0,log⁡(24D/ε)/(mh/(4γ))}\max\{0,\log(24D/\varepsilon)/(mh/(4\gamma))\}max{0,log(24D/ε)/(mh/(4γ))}. The shared scalar accuracy lemma makes the total at most ε\varepsilonε. A zero initial distance needs no transient iterations; finite transport is retained explicitly.

The exact sampler bound is decomposed into its decaying transient and complete persistent error term, including the Gaussian tail. Bounding that persistent error by a quadratic upper bound permits an explicit square-root step choice. The logarithmic ceiling count then controls the remaining initial discrepancy.

02Choose the positive step and integer count directly §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the minimum of 111, m/(5γM)m/(5\gamma M)m/(5γM), m/(45pM2)m/(4\sqrt{5p}M_2)m/(45p​M2​), and 0.94ε/Bp\sqrt{0.94\varepsilon/B_p}0.94ε/Bp​​. The step-specification theorem supplies positivity, both source restrictions and the quadratic bias allocation. Construct the nonnegative real step and the rounded iteration count, then apply the preceding sampler accuracy theorem. For fixed curvature constants, the explicit coefficient is of order ppp, so the formula displays the p/ε\sqrt{p/\varepsilon}p/ε​ step count up to its visible logarithm. Positive dimension is required for this particular step prescription; the general sampling bound retains all finite dimensions.

The prescribed minimum enforces all source restrictions as well as the accuracy allocation. For fixed curvature parameters its bias coefficient grows linearly with dimension, so the inverse step grows like p/ε\sqrt{p/\varepsilon}p/ε​. The additional logarithm comes from reducing the initial error geometrically.
