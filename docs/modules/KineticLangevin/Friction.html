Minimization of the shifted contraction coefficient

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L² on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

The general kinetic theorem permits every shift 0≤a<γ/20\le a<\gamma/20≤a<γ/2. Its exponential coefficient is k(a)=max⁡{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}/(γ−2a)k(a)=\max\{a^2-m,M-(\gamma-a)^2\}/(\gamma-2a)k(a)=max{a2−m,M−(γ−a)2}/(γ−2a); a negative coefficient is a contraction rate. This section proves the three scalar optimization regimes in Table 1 of the supplied manuscript. It establishes both the value at the proposed shift and a lower bound valid for every admissible competing shift. Singular square-root and denominator boundaries are explicit. These are algebraic corollaries of the kinetic estimate, separate from construction of the Brownian process and its invariant law.

The shift optimization is a scalar problem with a piecewise numerator and a positive coordinate-gap denominator. Different friction regimes correspond to different active curvature endpoints and require separate comparisons.

01Center the shift interval §

Mathematical statement and proof

Write d=γ−2a>0d=\gamma-2a>0d=γ−2a>0. The two branches of the coefficient become

k(a)=−γ2+max⁡{d2+γ2−4m4d,4M−γ2−d24d}.k(a)=-\frac\gamma2+\max\left\{\frac{d^2+\gamma^2-4m}{4d},\frac{4M-\gamma^2-d^2}{4d}\right\}.k(a)=−2γ​+max{4dd2+γ2−4m​,4d4M−γ2−d2​}.

For each branch, the centered expression follows by expressing its numerator as (−γ/2)d(-\gamma/2)d(−γ/2)d plus a quarter of the new numerator, distributing division, and cancelling the nonzero ddd. Positivity allows the maximum to commute with division. Translating both branches by the same scalar then extracts −γ/2-\gamma/2−γ/2. The denominator sign matters; a mere nonzero premise would not justify the maximum identity.

The shift changes both the numerator’s competing curvature terms and the denominator’s coordinate gap. Reparameterizing the admissible interval around its center makes those two dependencies explicit. The optimization must minimize their ratio, rather than optimizing either part in isolation.

02Optimality of zero shift at low friction §

Mathematical statement and proof

If γ2≤m+M\gamma^2\le m+Mγ2≤m+M and m≤Mm\le Mm≤M, the optimum is a=0a=0a=0, with coefficient (M−γ2)/γ(M-\gamma^2)/\gamma(M−γ2)/γ. For any admissible a≥0a\ge0a≥0, compare only the second branch to this value. After multiplying by the two positive denominators, their difference is a(2M−γa)a(2M-\gamma a)a(2M−γa), which is nonnegative under the regime assumptions. The full maximum is at least its second branch. Both denominators are positive, so these multiplied inequalities are equivalent to the desired quotient inequalities. At zero, M−γ2≥−mM-\gamma^2\ge-mM−γ2≥−m, so the maximum is exactly the second endpoint. In particular, γ2≤M\gamma^2\le Mγ2≤M gives a nonnegative best exponent, explaining the table's absence of a contraction rate in that range.

In the low-friction regime, moving away from zero cannot lower the controlling endpoint coefficient enough to compensate for the smaller denominator. The proof compares the resulting rational expressions after multiplying by their positive denominators. This avoids an unjustified derivative argument across a point where the two terms of the maximum agree.

03Balance the endpoints in the middle regime §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set D=2(m+M)−γ2D=\sqrt{2(m+M)-\gamma^2}D=2(m+M)−γ2​ and a=(γ−D)/2a=(\gamma-D)/2a=(γ−D)/2. If γ2≥m+M\gamma^2\ge m+Mγ2≥m+M and the radicand is strictly positive, then 0<D≤γ0<D\le\gamma0<D≤γ; hence the shift is admissible. Substituting D2=2(m+M)−γ2D^2=2(m+M)-\gamma^2D2=2(m+M)−γ2 shows that the two curvature branches agree. The resulting coefficient is

k(a)=−γ2+M−m2D.k(a)=-\frac\gamma2+\frac{M-m}{2D}.k(a)=−2γ​+2DM−m​.

One proves the root sign and square first, establishes the denominator identity γ−2a=D\gamma-2a=Dγ−2a=D, rewrites the equal branches, and clears the positive denominator. It never evaluates the formula with D=0D=0D=0.

The middle regime occurs when the two terms inside the maximum are equal. Solving that equality locates the point where the two numerator branches agree. Checking admissibility of the resulting shift is necessary before using its resulting exponent.

04Every competing shift at the middle-regime optimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume additionally γ2≤3m+M\gamma^2\le3m+Mγ2≤3m+M, and let d=γ−2a>0d=\gamma-2a>0d=γ−2a>0 describe any competing shift. If d≤Dd\le Dd≤D, compare the second branch with the balanced value. Clearing positive denominators factors its error as a positive multiple of (D−d)(D2+Dd+2(M−m))(D-d)(D^2+Dd+2(M-m))(D−d)(D2+Dd+2(M−m)). If d≥Dd\ge Dd≥D, compare the first branch instead; the corresponding factor is (d−D)(D(d−D)+2D2−2(M−m))(d-D)(D(d-D)+2D^2-2(M-m))(d−D)(D(d−D)+2D2−2(M−m)). The last coefficient is nonnegative because γ2≤3m+M\gamma^2\le3m+Mγ2≤3m+M. The proof splits precisely on d≤Dd\le Dd≤D, proves these products nonnegative, and uses the square identity for DDD, also multiplied by DDD, to close the polynomial comparisons. This proves global optimality, including the nondifferentiable corner of the maximum, without assuming a derivative there.

On each side of the point where the branches agree, a different curvature endpoint controls the maximum. Comparing every admissible shift with the corner therefore requires two inequalities. Together they establish global optimality for this regime, rather than merely a stationary-point condition.

05Select the smaller characteristic root at high friction §

Mathematical statement and proof

For γ2≥3m+M\gamma^2\ge3m+Mγ2≥3m+M, let D=γ2−4m>0D=\sqrt{\gamma^2-4m}>0D=γ2−4m​>0 and a=(γ−D)/2a=(\gamma-D)/2a=(γ−D)/2. Nonnegative mmm gives D≤γD\le\gammaD≤γ, so the shift is nonnegative and its coordinate denominator is D>0D>0D>0. The identity a(γ−a)=ma(\gamma-a)=ma(γ−a)=m makes the first branch equal −a-a−a, and the high-friction condition places the second branch below it. Therefore the rate is a=(γ−γ2−4m)/2a=(\gamma-\sqrt{\gamma^2-4m})/2a=(γ−γ2−4m​)/2.

For any competing shift, the first branch alone bounds the maximum from below. The inequality that it is at least −a-a−a is exactly the nonnegative square (γ−2a′−D)2≥0(\gamma-2a'-D)^2\ge0(γ−2a′−D)2≥0 after division by the positive competing denominator. The argument supplies that square to elementary real inequalities, then uses the maximum comparison. The strict discriminant excludes the equal-curvature critical point where the selected transformation becomes singular; a separate limiting argument would be required at that point.

For large friction, the relevant root solves r(γ−r)=mr(\gamma-r)=mr(γ−r)=m. Choosing the smaller root gives the slower positive decay rate of the quadratic dynamics. Its discriminant and the coordinate gap must be positive; the repeated-root boundary is treated separately in the sharpness discussion.
