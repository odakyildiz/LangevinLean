Gibbs invariance of the corrected Verlet proposal

References and publication status for this development

Published source; corrected checked proofs

The comparison uses the supplied mainDR_dec_18.tex manuscript. The results prove Wasserstein convergence of the kinetic diffusion, invariance of its Gibbs measure, KLMC and KLMC2 error estimates for correlated Gaussian increments, their Cholesky representations, and explicit iteration counts for a prescribed error. The corrected proofs are unpublished. The reference table records the correction to the continuous prefactor and the stochastic Hessian-difference terms retained in the second-order comparison. The KLMC2 estimate retains the exact fourth Gaussian moment in every dimension; the coarser displayed simplification assumes p≥2p\ge2p≥2. The stronger inverse-step-square exponential term is also proved. Continuous solutions are continuous in L2L^2L2 on finite time intervals. Almost-sure continuity of sample paths is not proved here.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Lionel Riou-Durand (2020). On sampling from a log-concave density using kinetic Langevin diffusions. Bernoulli 26(3), 1956–1988. arXiv

A volume-preserving involution has a symmetric old/new-state graph measure. Weighting that graph by a positive target density and applying the ordinary Metropolis ratio makes its accepted flow symmetric. The rejected mass stays at the old state. This section proves exact invariance for the explicit reversed-Verlet proposal. Passing from these corrected proposals to the unadjusted Hamiltonian flow and then to the full kinetic diffusion still requires quantitative limits.

Involutivity and volume preservation produce a symmetric accepted-flow measure. The rejected mass completes a probability transition, while the energy defect controls how closely it resembles the uncorrected Verlet step.

01The proposal graph and its symmetry under coordinate exchange §

Mathematical statement and proof

For J2=idJ^2=\mathrm{id}J2=id, the graph map is G(z)=(z,Jz)G(z)=(z,Jz)G(z)=(z,Jz). Swapping the old and new state gives swap⁡∘G=G∘J\operatorname{swap}\circ G=G\circ Jswap∘G=G∘J. If JJJ preserves a base measure λ\lambdaλ, pushforward composition yields swap⁡#G#λ=G#J#λ=G#λ\operatorname{swap}_\#G_\#\lambda=G_\#J_\#\lambda=G_\#\lambdaswap#​G#​λ=G#​J#​λ=G#​λ. One proves the pointwise graph identity using the involution equation, verifies graph measurability, and rewrites the two pushforward compositions. Thus symmetry of the graph measure is derived from the proposal rather than assumed as an additional hypothesis.

The proposal graph associates each state with its deterministic proposal. Swapping the two graph coordinates corresponds to applying the proposal again. Involutivity and volume preservation therefore identify the swapped graph measure with the original one after the appropriate density change.

02Apply the accepted-flow argument to an arbitrary positive density §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let the target be wλw\lambdawλ, with measurable w>0w>0w>0. Its proposal joint law is G#(wλ)G_\#(w\lambda)G#​(wλ). The pushforward-with-density theorem expresses this as the symmetric base graph weighted by www at its first coordinate. The Metropolis ratio is min⁡{1,w(y)/w(x)}\min\{1,w(y)/w(x)\}min{1,w(y)/w(x)}, so accepted joint density is min⁡{w(x),w(y)}\min\{w(x),w(y)\}min{w(x),w(y)}, symmetric under swapping. The Metropolis balance identity proves that accepted new-state mass plus rejected old-state mass has the initial marginal. The proof computes that marginal by projecting the graph back to its first coordinate, giving the exact identity with wλw\lambdawλ. No invariant measure or acceptance-balance conclusion is assumed.

The accepted mass is the smaller of the initial and proposed density weights. This minimum is symmetric under swapping the graph coordinates. Rejected mass remains at the initial state, so the accepted and rejected contributions together have the required invariant marginal.

03Bound rejection by an energy increase §

Mathematical statement and proof

For energy increment ddd, rejection is 1−min⁡{1,e−d}1-\min\{1,e^{-d}\}1−min{1,e−d}. If d≤0d\le0d≤0, the proposed energy decreases and rejection is zero. If d≥0d\ge0d≥0, the exponential tangent inequality e−d≥1−de^{-d}\ge1-de−d≥1−d gives rejection at most ddd. Thus rejection is at most max⁡{d,0}≤∣d∣\max\{d,0\}\le|d|max{d,0}≤∣d∣. The proof splits the sign cases, rewrites the minimum and maximum with the corresponding exponential bounds, and applies the tangent inequality. This is a scalar probability bound; converting it to an L2L^{2}L2 trajectory error also requires the size of the rejected jump and must not silently assume that jump is of order hhh.

For a nonnegative energy increase uuu, the rejection probability is 1−e−u≤u1-e^{-u}\le u1−e−u≤u; for a nonpositive increase it is zero. Thus rejection is bounded by the positive part of the energy defect. The inequality turns an analytic energy estimate into a probabilistic rejection bound.

04Instantiate the exact corrected Verlet Gibbs theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take λ\lambdaλ to be product Haar phase measure, Jh=R VhJ_h=R\,V_hJh​=RVh​ to be the constructed reversed Verlet proposal, and w(z)=e−H(z)w(z)=e^{-H(z)}w(z)=e−H(z). The Hamiltonian module proves that JhJ_hJh​ is both an involution and measure preserving. Measurability of the potential and gradient proves measurability of the weight and proposal, and the exponential weight is strictly positive. The generic theorem therefore proves exact preservation of the Gibbs weighted measure by this Metropolis step. On acceptance the state is velocity-reversed Verlet, and on rejection it remains at the initial state. Applying a further velocity reversal changes those to ordinary Verlet on acceptance and a velocity flip on rejection; any limiting kinetic argument must account for this distinction explicitly.

For the Gibbs density the ratio of proposal and initial weights is the exponential of the negative energy defect. Substitution in the general balance calculation gives the corrected Verlet invariant measure. This exact statement does not imply invariance of uncorrected Verlet.
