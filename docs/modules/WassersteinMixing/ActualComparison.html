A diagonal Gibbs initialization and an independent canonical Brownian path provide one probability space for the stationary Langevin diffusion and its Gaussian Euler chain. Both random processes, their common initial state and their actual laws are constructed.

Statement and proof

01The concrete probability space and initial moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construction. Couple a Gibbs variable with itself on the diagonal, then take its independent product with the coordinate Brownian source. The diagonal coupling has zero transport cost. Strong convexity gives a finite, positive Gibbs partition function and a finite second moment, so both the target and extended law are probability measures and the initialization belongs to L2L^2L2.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.16; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

diagonalGibbsCoupling uses the exact diagonal pushforward, whose cost is computed as zero. actualProbability is its independent Brownian extension. The probability and moment lemmas instantiate the shared Gibbs theorems from the actual potential assumptions.

02The genuine stationary diffusion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construction. Normalize each Brownian increment to obtain independent standard Gaussian Euler innovations. Solve the Langevin integral equation with the Gibbs initialization and these Brownian paths. The reference segments have the Gibbs law at every interior time, and adjacent segment endpoints agree. In particular the grid reference Yn=YnhY_n=Y_{nh}Yn​=Ynh​ is stationary.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.19.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.20; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.22; ULA-SC, Section 3.23.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.25.6; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.29.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.29.7; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.12; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.17; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.42.7; ULA-SC, Section 3.44.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.19; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.20; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.22.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

actualReferenceData instantiates the proved Brownian reference construction. actualSegment and actualReference select its actual segments and grid knots. Their stationarity follows from the constructed reference, rather than a new stationarity hypothesis.

03The actual Gaussian Euler chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. On the same probability space, define Xn+1=Xn−h∇V(Xn)+2h ξnX_{n+1}=X_n-h\nabla V(X_n)+\sqrt{2h}\,\xi_nXn+1​=Xn​−h∇V(Xn​)+2h​ξn​, with the same initialization and Brownian innovations. Then X0=Y0X_0=Y_0X0​=Y0​ and

Law⁡(Xn)=Khnπ,π(dx)∝e−V(x) dx.\operatorname{Law}(X_n)=K_h^n\pi,\qquad \pi(dx)\propto e^{-V(x)}\,dx.Law(Xn​)=Khn​π,π(dx)∝e−V(x)dx.

Proof. The common diagonal initialization has zero L2L^2L2 difference. The actual independent-Gaussian recursion has the Markov law obtained by applying the Gaussian ULA law step at each iteration; induction identifies its pushforward with the displayed iterate.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.19.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.19; ULA-SC, Section 3.22.23; ULA-SC, Section 3.23.9; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.32.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.17; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.19; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.20; ULA-SC, Section 3.8.22.
