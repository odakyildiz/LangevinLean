Continuity of actual stationary observable evaluations

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

If stationary random states with common law μ\muμ converge in measure, their evaluation maps converge strongly on every L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ) observable. The proof first treats bounded continuous functions and then uses their density and the common evaluation isometry.

01The actual evaluation and its square norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. A state XXX with law μ\muμ gives an evaluation isometry f↦f(X)f\mapsto f(X)f↦f(X). For bounded continuous ϕ\phiϕ,

∥ϕ(X)−ϕ(Y)∥22=∫∥ϕ(X(ω))−ϕ(Y(ω))∥2 dQ.\|\phi(X)-\phi(Y)\|_2^2=\int\|\phi(X(\omega))-\phi(Y(\omega))\|^2\,dQ.∥ϕ(X)−ϕ(Y)∥22​=∫∥ϕ(X(ω))−ϕ(Y(ω))∥2dQ.

Proof. Evaluation is pullback by a measure-preserving state map. Compare the exact almost-everywhere representatives and use the integral square-norm formula. The auxiliary implication from vanishing mean norm to convergence in measure follows from the integral Markov bound.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

evaluation is actual measure-preserving composition. lp_norm_sq_eq_integral takes the real part of the integrable inner-product formula. The bounded-continuous representative lemmas identify the norm of the actual evaluation difference with the displayed integral.

02Extending convergence from bounded continuous functions to all observables §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If Xi→XX_i\to XXi​→X in measure and all states have law μ\muμ, then ϕ(Xi)→ϕ(X)\phi(X_i)\to\phi(X)ϕ(Xi​)→ϕ(X) in L2L^2L2 for bounded continuous ϕ\phiϕ, and the same is true for every square-integrable observable.

Proof. Composition with a continuous bounded function preserves convergence in measure. The squared difference has a uniform constant bound, so dominated convergence in measure gives its integral tending to zero. For arbitrary fff, approximate it in L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ) by a bounded continuous function. All evaluation maps have norm one, so both approximation errors have the same uniform bound; the middle bounded-continuous evaluation difference tends to zero. Norm convergence of states implies convergence in measure, giving the final specializations.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The bounded-continuous theorem uses its exact square-norm integral and bounded convergence in measure. evaluation_tendsto_inMeasure uses density and the common isometry bound. The last two theorems convert norm convergence of states to convergence in measure before applying these results.
