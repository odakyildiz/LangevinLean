Coordinate independence of Gibbs targets, Gaussian ULA and transport

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

Let e:H→Fe:H\to Fe:H→F be a linear isometric equivalence of finite-dimensional real inner product spaces with their Borel structures. The normalized Gibbs law for VVV pushes forward to the Gibbs law for V∘e−1V\circ e^{-1}V∘e−1; standard Gaussian ULA and all its iterates commute with the same coordinate change; and the exact quadratic transport distance is unchanged. These proved identities identify the IPLA coordinates with the Euclidean coordinates of the sharp theorem.

01The true gradient in the new coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For differentiable VVV,

∇(V∘e−1)(y)=e ∇V(e−1y).\nabla(V\circ e^{-1})(y)=e\,\nabla V(e^{-1}y).∇(V∘e−1)(y)=e∇V(e−1y).

Proof. The chain rule gives D(V∘e−1)(y)[w]=DV(e−1y)[e−1w]D(V\circ e^{-1})(y)[w]=DV(e^{-1}y)[e^{-1}w]D(V∘e−1)(y)[w]=DV(e−1y)[e−1w]. Identifying derivatives with gradients and using the inner-product identity for the linear isometry gives the inner product of www with the asserted right side. Equality of all such pairings proves equality of the gradients.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

gradient_comp_isometry combines the Fréchet derivative chain rule with inner-product extensionality and inner_map_map. Its differentiability hypothesis is exactly what the chain rule needs; no regularity of a separate coordinate gradient is postulated.

02Volume preservation gives the exact normalized Gibbs law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Write ZV=∫e−VZ_V=\int e^{-V}ZV​=∫e−V and πV=ZV−1e−V dx\pi_V=Z_V^{-1}e^{-V}\,dxπV​=ZV−1​e−Vdx, using the library's extended-valued normalization convention. Then

ZV∘e−1=ZV,e#πV=πV∘e−1.Z_{V\circ e^{-1}}=Z_V,\qquad e_\#\pi_V=\pi_{V\circ e^{-1}}.ZV∘e−1​=ZV​,e#​πV​=πV∘e−1​.

Proof. A linear isometric equivalence preserves finite-dimensional volume. Change variables through eee in the nonnegative integral defining the partition function. For a measurable set, the same change of variables identifies the weighted-volume measures before normalization. The equality of partition functions then identifies their scalar normalizations. These identities hold at the defining measure level; they do not assume that the Gibbs law is invariant or even separately assume a positive finite partition function.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

gibbsPartition_comp_isometry uses measurePreserving and the nonnegative change-of-variables formula. gibbsMeasure_map_isometry proves equality on measurable sets after pushing the normalized density forward, applying the same measure-preserving embedding identity. Later potential assumptions establish the probability and moment properties independently.

03Transporting actual couplings in both directions §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Every coupling ccc of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν pushes forward through (x,y)↦(ex,ey)(x,y)\mapsto(e x,e y)(x,y)↦(ex,ey) to a coupling of e#μ,e#νe_\#\mu,e_\#\nue#​μ,e#​ν with the same root-mean-square cost. Consequently, including possibly infinite distances,

W2(e#μ,e#ν)=W2(μ,ν).W_2(e_\#\mu,e_\#\nu)=W_2(\mu,\nu).W2​(e#​μ,e#​ν)=W2​(μ,ν).

Proof. Composing each coordinate projection with the pair map proves the two marginal identities. Since e(x−y)=ex−eye(x-y)=e x-e ye(x−y)=ex−ey and eee preserves norms, the nonnegative quadratic displacement integral and its square root are unchanged. Taking the infimum over original couplings gives one inequality. Apply the same construction using e−1e^{-1}e−1 to the pushed-forward laws to get the reverse inequality. Converting the resulting equality of extended distances to the library's real-distance convention preserves equality; no order argument through an infinite-to-real conversion is used.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.5.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

isometryCoupling constructs the pushed joint measure and proves both marginals by composition of measure maps. isometryCoupling_cost uses the exact norm equality in the eLpNorm change-of-variables formula. wasserstein2_map_isometry_le passes this cost through the coupling infimum; wasserstein2_map_isometry uses the inverse map for equality. wasserstein2Real_map_isometry applies the real-value operation only to this established equality.

04The actual independent-Gaussian transition commutes with reindexing §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If VVV is differentiable with a globally Lipschitz gradient and μ\muμ is an s-finite measure, the actual Gaussian ULA transition satisfies

e#KV,hμ=KV∘e−1,h(e#μ).e_\#K_{V,h}\mu=K_{V\circ e^{-1},h}(e_\#\mu).e#​KV,h​μ=KV∘e−1,h​(e#​μ).

Proof. Its deterministic proposal is x−h∇V(x)+2h ξx-h\nabla V(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,\xix−h∇V(x)+2h​ξ. The gradient identity and linearity show that applying eee to this proposal equals the proposal in the new coordinates using ex,eξe x,e\xiex,eξ. A standard isotropic Gaussian law is preserved by the linear isometry. Therefore the image of the product of the initial law and the independent Gaussian law is exactly the product measure used by the transformed transition. Pushing this identity through the measurable proposal proves the equality of the actual update laws.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.34.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

ulaLawStep_map_isometry first proves the pointwise proposal identity, then rewrites the standard Gaussian pushforward with stdGaussian_map. Measure.map_prod_map and composition of measure maps turn it into the desired transition identity. The Lipschitz assumption establishes measurability of the true gradient and proposal.

05Finite second moments and every Gaussian iterate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. A finite second moment is preserved by eee. Under the potential contract, for every probability initialization and every nonnegative integer kkk,

e#KV,h kμ=KV∘e−1,h k(e#μ).e_\#K_{V,h}^{\,k}\mu=K_{V\circ e^{-1},h}^{\,k}(e_\#\mu).e#​KV,hk​μ=KV∘e−1,hk​(e#​μ).

Proof. The coordinate map preserves the pointwise norm, so its image has the same square-integrability as the original random vector. For the iterate identity, the case k=0k=0k=0 is the initial law. If the equality holds at kkk, apply the proved one-step covariance to that iterate and then substitute the induction hypothesis. The already constructed Gaussian transition preserves probability, supplying the measure-finiteness hypothesis needed at every induction step.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

memLp_id_map_isometry uses the mapped-measure characterization of MemLp and composition with the continuous linear map of the isometry. ula_iterate_map_isometry inducts on the actual update count, supplies the proved probability instance for each iterate, and invokes ulaLawStep_map_isometry. These are distributional identities, so the application does not replace the IPLA recursion with a different sampler.
