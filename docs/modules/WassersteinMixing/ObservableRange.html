Doob–Dynkin factorization identifies every square-integrable function of a state with an observable in the L2L^2L2 space of its actual law. On this range, evaluation followed by its adjoint is the identity, identifying state-measurable conditional means with actual adjoint compression.

01Actual square-integrable Doob–Dynkin factorization §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If X∗Q=μX_*Q=\muX∗​Q=μ and Z∈L2(Q)Z\in L^2(Q)Z∈L2(Q) is measurable from XXX alone, then Z=f(X)Z=f(X)Z=f(X) for some actual f∈L2(μ)f\in L^2(\mu)f∈L2(μ).

Proof. Choose a state-measurable representative of ZZZ. Measurable factorization writes it as g∘Xg\circ Xg∘X. Since this is almost surely the square-integrable original variable, its square norm is integrable. The exact pushforward identity transfers that integrability to ggg under μ\muμ. Its L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ) equivalence class evaluates back to ZZZ.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

observable_range_of_state_measurable uses measurable composition factorization on the chosen strongly measurable representative. It transfers MemLp through the actual measure-preserving state map before constructing the observable class and comparing pullback representatives.

02Identifying the true conditional mean with compression §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For an evaluation isometry JJJ, JJ∗Z=ZJJ^*Z=ZJJ∗Z=Z on its range. If all evaluations are past-measurable and E[Y∣F]\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]E[Y∣F] belongs to that range, then

E[Y∣F]=J(J∗Y).\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]=J(J^*Y).E[Y∣F]=J(J∗Y).

Proof. The isometry identity J∗J=IJ^*J=IJ∗J=I proves the first claim. Pair the conditional expectation against arbitrary evaluated observables. Adaptation lets the conditional projection be removed from that pairing, yielding J∗E[Y∣F]=J∗YJ^*\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]=J^*YJ∗E[Y∣F]=J∗Y. Apply JJJ and use the first claim for the known range member.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

evaluation_projection_eq_of_mem_range cancels the isometry adjoint on an explicit range witness. conditional_eq_evaluation_adjoint proves equality of extracted adjoint vectors by testing against every observable and using the conditional-expectation pairing identity.
