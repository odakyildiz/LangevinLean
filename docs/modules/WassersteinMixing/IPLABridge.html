The sharp actual-law Wasserstein bound in the scaled IPLA coordinates

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

The scaled IPLA state is a finite Euclidean space indexed by a parameter block and a particle/latent block. A proved linear isometry identifies it with the coordinate convention of the sharp Wasserstein theorem. Pulling the constructed invariant law back through that isometry gives a genuine invariant probability measure with a finite second moment. The actual Gaussian transition, Gibbs target and transport distance all respect this change of coordinates, so the sharp bias and convergence estimates apply to the actual lifted particle law.

01Constructing the invariant law in the original coordinate type §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construction. Let VVV satisfy the potential contract with parameters α,β,h,d\alpha,\beta,h,dα,β,h,d, and let e:H→Rde:H\to\mathbb R^de:H→Rd be a linear isometric equivalence. For W=V∘e−1W=V\circ e^{-1}W=V∘e−1, let π^W\widehat\pi_WπW​ be the invariant law already constructed from the actual Gaussian ULA transition. Define

π^V=(e−1)#π^W.\widehat\pi_V=(e^{-1})_\#\widehat\pi_W.πV​=(e−1)#​πW​.

Claim. It is a probability law with finite second moment, e#π^V=π^We_\#\widehat\pi_V=\widehat\pi_We#​πV​=πW​, and KV,hπ^V=π^VK_{V,h}\widehat\pi_V=\widehat\pi_VKV,h​πV​=πV​.

Proof. The measurable inverse coordinate map preserves total mass, and its norm preservation transfers the constructed finite second moment. Composing its pushforward with the forward map gives the exact recovery identity. Gaussian-transition covariance then gives e#KV,hπ^V=KW,hπ^W=π^W=e#π^Ve_\#K_{V,h}\widehat\pi_V=K_{W,h}\widehat\pi_W=\widehat\pi_W=e_\#\widehat\pi_Ve#​KV,h​πV​=KW,h​πW​=πW​=e#​πV​. Injectivity of pushforward through the measurable equivalence proves invariance in the original coordinates. Thus invariance is derived for the same transition used by the particle algorithm.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

02Transferring the sharp bias and finite-time comparison §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Set πV=ZV−1e−V\pi_V=Z_V^{-1}e^{-V}πV​=ZV−1​e−V, q=1−αhq=1-\alpha hq=1−αh and B=6h(β/α)βdB=6h\sqrt{(\beta/\alpha)\beta d}B=6h(β/α)βd​. Then

W2(πV,π^V)≤B,W2(KV,h kμ,πV)≤qkW2(μ,π^V)+B,W_2(\pi_V,\widehat\pi_V)\le B,\qquad W_2(K_{V,h}^{\,k}\mu,\pi_V)\le q^kW_2(\mu,\widehat\pi_V)+B,W2​(πV​,πV​)≤B,W2​(KV,hk​μ,πV​)≤qkW2​(μ,πV​)+B,

for probability initializations with finite second moment.

Proof. Push both measures through eee. The exact Gibbs pushforward, invariant-law recovery and transport-isometry identities reduce the first inequality to the already constructed sharp bias theorem for WWW. For the second inequality, the Gaussian-iterate covariance also identifies the pushed-forward iterate with the actual ULA iterate for WWW. Finite second moments pass to the mapped initialization. The constructed comparison theorem in the Euclidean coordinates then gives precisely the displayed bound, with all constants unchanged.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

The actual comparison is constructed from the potential contract rather than passed in as an extra theorem hypothesis.

03Establishing finite output transport before taking real values §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The extended distance W2(KV,h kμ,πV)W_2(K_{V,h}^{\,k}\mu,\pi_V)W2​(KV,hk​μ,πV​) is finite for every finite update count and every finite-second-moment probability initialization under the potential contract.

Proof. The mapped initialization has finite second moment. Positive strong convexity gives the Gibbs probability law and its finite second moment, so their initial transport distance is finite. The established Gaussian ULA output-finiteness theorem gives finite distance from every Euclidean-coordinate iterate to that target. Rewrite its iterate and Gibbs target through the isometry; the exact equality of extended transport distances transfers finiteness back to the original coordinate space. This supplies a genuine finite extended distance before later parameter-projection statements use its ordinary real value.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.21.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7; ULA-SC, Section 3.45.6.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

coordinate_convergence_finite first applies ULA.wasserstein2_ne_top_of_memLp and ULA.Hilbert.dalalyan_output_finite in the standard coordinates. It then rewrites by the Gaussian iterate, Gibbs pushforward and extended-distance isometry lemmas. This use of the earlier ULA theorem only establishes finiteness; the improved numerical rate still comes from the new sharp comparison.

04Keeping the sharp bias with exact Gibbs-based initialization §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The actual Gaussian chain started from the exact Gibbs target has finite transport distance from that target at every update count, and

W2(πV,KV,h kπV)≤B.W_2(\pi_V,K_{V,h}^{\,k}\pi_V)\le B.W2​(πV​,KV,hk​πV​)≤B.

For any finite-second-moment initialization this gives the sharp target-based estimate

W2(KV,h kμ,πV)≤qkW2(μ,πV)+B.W_2(K_{V,h}^{\,k}\mu,\pi_V)\le q^kW_2(\mu,\pi_V)+B.W2​(KV,hk​μ,πV​)≤qkW2​(μ,πV​)+B.

Proof. The constructed Poisson comparison already couples a stationary Langevin process to the actual ULA chain started with the same Gibbs state. Its uniform shifted-energy estimate bounds this stationary-start displacement by BBB, with finite extended transport proved separately. The coordinate isometry transfers both facts exactly to the original space. The contraction theorem for the same Gaussian transition compares its two actual iterates:

W2(KV,h kμ,KV,h kπV)≤qkW2(μ,πV).W_2(K_{V,h}^{\,k}\mu,K_{V,h}^{\,k}\pi_V)\le q^kW_2(\mu,\pi_V).W2​(KV,hk​μ,KV,hk​πV​)≤qkW2​(μ,πV​).

Apply the proved finite real transport triangle inequality through the intermediate law KV,h kπVK_{V,h}^{\,k}\pi_VKV,hk​πV​. The displayed contraction bounds the first term and the stationary-start comparison bounds the second by BBB. This retains the same exact-Gibbs initialization distance as Project 03 with a single copy of the sharp bias. The weaker bound with (1+qk)B(1+q^k)B(1+qk)B also follows by adding the nonnegative term qkBq^kBqkB; it remains available as a supporting corollary.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.21.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.13; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.15; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

coordinate_stationary_start_bound and coordinate_stationary_start_finite transport the corresponding consequences of actualULAComparison through the exact coordinate identities. coordinate_convergence_target_bound_sharp proves initial transport finiteness from moments, supplies probability for both iterates, and combines ula_iterate_wasserstein2Real_le with wasserstein2Real_triangle and the stationary-start bound. coordinate_convergence_target_bound is an algebraic weakening of that sharp theorem using nonnegativity of the contraction power and the bias. No invariant-triangle loss is needed by the primary target-based result.

Claim. For N>0N>0N>0, let U∈C2U\in C^2U∈C2 be mmm-strongly convex with an LLL-Lipschitz gradient, 0<m≤L0<m\le L0<m≤L, and 0<γ0<\gamma0<γ with γL≤1\gamma L\le1γL≤1.
