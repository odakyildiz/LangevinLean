Conditional expectation of a measurable function of a current state and an independent future input depends only on the state. Expressed inside the true L2L^2L2 space, this property is closed under strong limits and can therefore pass from actual Euler approximants to the diffusion.

Statement and proof

01Independent-input averaging and strong-limit closure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If Z=F(X,W)Z=F(X,W)Z=F(X,W) is square integrable, XXX is past-measurable and WWW is independent of the whole past, then E[Z∣F]\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F]E[Z∣F] is measurable from XXX alone. If Zi→ZZ_i\to ZZi​→Z strongly and all such conditional means are state-measurable, the limit conditional mean is state-measurable too.

Proof. Square integrability implies integrability on the probability space. The independent-input averaging theorem identifies ordinary conditional expectation as an average over the independent input; the actual L2L^2L2 projection has the same almost-everywhere representative. For the limit, conditional expectation is a bounded linear projection, hence continuous. The square-integrable random variables measurable from the state form a closed subspace, so the strong limit remains in it.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The first theorem identifies condExpL2 with ordinary conditional expectation and applies the actual averaging theorem. The second uses continuity of that projection and the closed measurable subspace isClosed_aestronglyMeasurable.
