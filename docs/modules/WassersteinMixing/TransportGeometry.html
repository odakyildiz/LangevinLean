  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

The quadratic Wasserstein distance is the infimum of root-mean-square displacement over actual couplings. Symmetry follows by swapping coordinates. The triangle inequality is proved by disintegrating one coupling over its shared marginal, constructing a triple law, and applying the L2L^2L2 triangle inequality. No optimal coupling or assumed metric triangle inequality is required.

01Swapping the endpoints of a coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Swapping the two coordinates of a coupling of μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν produces a coupling of ν,μ\nu,\muν,μ with the same cost. Consequently W2(μ,ν)=W2(ν,μ)W_2(\mu,\nu)=W_2(\nu,\mu)W2​(μ,ν)=W2​(ν,μ), both as an extended distance and under the ordinary real convention.

Proof. The coordinate-swap map interchanges the two marginal projections. The displacement changes from x−yx-yx−y to y−xy-xy−x, preserving its norm and its quadratic cost. Every coupling therefore supplies an equal-cost coupling in the reverse direction. Taking the infimum in each direction gives the two inequalities required for equality. Applying the real-value operation preserves that equality.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.5.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

couplingSwap pushes the actual joint measure through Prod.swap and proves its marginals. couplingCost_swap uses invariance of the norm of a reversed difference. wasserstein2_symm passes these coupling-cost bounds to the two infima, and wasserstein2Real_symm rewrites the equal extended values.

02Constructing the glued triple and checking both pair marginals §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construction. In a standard Borel space, let γXY\gamma_{XY}γXY​ couple μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν, and let γYZ\gamma_{YZ}γYZ​ couple ν,ξ\nu,\xiν,ξ. Disintegrate the second coupling as γYZ(dy,dz)=ν(dy)K(y,dz)\gamma_{YZ}(dy,dz)=\nu(dy)K(y,dz)γYZ​(dy,dz)=ν(dy)K(y,dz). Define

R(dx,dy,dz)=γXY(dx,dy)K(y,dz).R(dx,dy,dz)=\gamma_{XY}(dx,dy)K(y,dz).R(dx,dy,dz)=γXY​(dx,dy)K(y,dz).

Claim. This is a probability law with (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y)-marginal γXY\gamma_{XY}γXY​ and (Y,Z)(Y,Z)(Y,Z)-marginal γYZ\gamma_{YZ}γYZ​.

Proof. Integrating out zzz leaves γXY\gamma_{XY}γXY​, since the conditional kernel has mass one. Integrating a measurable function of (y,z)(y,z)(y,z) first in K(y,dz)K(y,dz)K(y,dz) and then in γXY\gamma_{XY}γXY​ is the same as integrating it against ν(dy)K(y,dz)\nu(dy)K(y,dz)ν(dy)K(y,dz), because the second marginal of γXY\gamma_{XY}γXY​ is ν\nuν. The disintegration identity makes the latter measure γYZ\gamma_{YZ}γYZ​. Applying the first marginal equality to the entire space proves total mass one.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

glueCouplings uses the actual conditional kernel of the second joint measure and pulls it back along the shared coordinate. glueCouplings_left applies the composition-product marginal identity. glueCouplings_right proves the projection by disintegration and the shared marginal, using equality of the relevant nonnegative integrals. glueCouplings_isProbability checks the total mass.

03Minkowski and the two coupling infima §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For probability laws on the stated measurable normed space,

W2(μ,ξ)≤W2(μ,ν)+W2(ν,ξ),W_2(\mu,\xi)\le W_2(\mu,\nu)+W_2(\nu,\xi),W2​(μ,ξ)≤W2​(μ,ν)+W2​(ν,ξ),

including infinite values.

Proof. On the constructed triple law, X−Z=(X−Y)+(Y−Z)X-Z=(X-Y)+(Y-Z)X−Z=(X−Y)+(Y−Z). The extended L2L^2L2 triangle inequality bounds its cost by the sum of the two pair costs, which equal the costs of the supplied couplings by the marginal identities. The (X,Z)(X,Z)(X,Z)-projection is an actual coupling of μ,ξ\mu,\xiμ,ξ, hence

W2(μ,ξ)≤cost⁡(γXY)+cost⁡(γYZ).W_2(\mu,\xi)\le\operatorname{cost}(\gamma_{XY})+\operatorname{cost}(\gamma_{YZ}).W2​(μ,ξ)≤cost(γXY​)+cost(γYZ​).

This holds for every pair of couplings. Taking the infimum over each one gives the desired inequality. The argument neither selects an optimal coupling nor replaces an infinite cost by a real number.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.9.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

wasserstein2_le_couplingCost_add verifies both endpoint laws and both pair costs of the glued triple, then applies eLpNorm_add_le. wasserstein2_triangle distributes the extended infima over addition and passes the arbitrary-coupling inequality through both infima.

04Finiteness and the ordinary real triangle inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If W2(μ,ν)W_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(μ,ν) and W2(ν,ξ)W_2(\nu,\xi)W2​(ν,ξ) are finite, then W2(μ,ξ)W_2(\mu,\xi)W2​(μ,ξ) is finite and the same triangle inequality holds for their ordinary real values.

Proof. The two finite distances have finite sum. The proved extended triangle inequality bounds the endpoint distance by that sum, proving its finiteness. Conversion of an extended inequality with finite upper side preserves order; the real value of the finite sum is the sum of the real values. These facts give the ordinary real triangle inequality with every required finiteness premise explicit.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

wasserstein2_triangle_finite combines the extended bound with finiteness of the sum. wasserstein2Real_triangle applies ENNReal.toReal_mono to that finite upper bound and uses ENNReal.toReal_add with both finite premises.
