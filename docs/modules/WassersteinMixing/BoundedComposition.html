Strong convergence of bounded observables without stationary approximants

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

Euler approximation states need not have the target law. A bounded continuous observable nevertheless gives a genuine square-integrable random variable at each state, and convergence in measure of those states implies strong convergence of the bounded observable compositions.

Statement and proof

01Actual composition, subsequences and dominated convergence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For a bounded continuous ϕ\phiϕ, if Xi→XX_i\to XXi​→X in measure on a probability space, then ϕ(Xi)→ϕ(X)\phi(X_i)\to\phi(X)ϕ(Xi​)→ϕ(X) in L2L^2L2, even when their marginal laws differ.

Proof. Boundedness gives square integrability and an exact representative for every composition. Every sequence approaching the filter has a subsequence along which state convergence holds almost surely. Continuity gives almost-sure convergence of the compositions. Their squared difference is bounded by the integrable constant 4∥ϕ∥∞24\|\phi\|_\infty^24∥ϕ∥∞2​, so dominated convergence forces its integral to zero along that subsequence. The subsequence criterion gives the full strong limit. Strong state convergence is a special case because it implies convergence in measure.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

boundedComposition constructs the actual L2L^2L2 class from its uniform bound. The private norm identity uses its exact representative. boundedComposition_tendsto_inMeasure applies subsequence extraction and dominated convergence; the final theorem converts strong state convergence to convergence in measure.
