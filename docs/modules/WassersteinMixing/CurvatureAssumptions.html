The original Hessian hypothesis implies the potential contract

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

Statement and proof

01The sharp derivative and gradient bounds §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The quadratic-form bounds imply ∥∇2V(x)∥≤β\|\nabla^2V(x)\|\le\beta∥∇2V(x)∥≤β, hence ∥∇V(x)−∇V(y)∥≤β∥x−y∥\|\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y)\|\le\beta\|x-y\|∥∇V(x)−∇V(y)∥≤β∥x−y∥.

Proof. Scale the symmetric Hessian by β−1\beta^{-1}β−1. It is a positive contraction in quadratic form, so its squared norm on a vector is bounded by that quadratic form and then by the vector norm squared. Rescaling yields the Hessian norm estimate. The mean-value bound for a differentiable map with uniformly bounded derivative gives the gradient estimate.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

HessianAssumptions records the actual second-derivative inequalities. hessian_norm_le_of_forms applies the proved positive-contraction inequality to the scaled Hessian. gradient_lipschitz_of_hessian applies the derivative mean-value theorem with that sharp bound.

02Integrating the lower Hessian bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For all x,yx,yx,y,

V(y)≥V(x)+⟨∇V(x),y−x⟩+α2∥y−x∥2.V(y)\ge V(x)+\langle\nabla V(x),y-x\rangle+\frac\alpha2\|y-x\|^2.V(y)≥V(x)+⟨∇V(x),y−x⟩+2α​∥y−x∥2.

Proof. Along x+t(y−x)x+t(y-x)x+t(y−x), the derivative of ⟨∇V(x+t(y−x)),y−x⟩\langle\nabla V(x+t(y-x)),y-x\rangle⟨∇V(x+t(y−x)),y−x⟩ is at least α∥y−x∥2\alpha\|y-x\|^2α∥y−x∥2. Integrating once bounds that scalar gradient; integrating again gives the displayed supporting inequality. Subtracting α∥x∥2/2\alpha\|x\|^2/2α∥x∥2/2 yields supporting planes for a convex function. To verify convexity directly, combine its two supporting inequalities at a convex combination; the linear terms cancel. Thus the original Hessian hypothesis supplies every part of the potential contract.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

strong_support_of_hessian uses the actual gradient derivative and interval fundamental theorem. convexOn_of_supporting_planes proves the convex-combination inequality algebraically. strongConvexity_of_hessian and HessianAssumptions.toPotentialAssumptions assemble the consequences without additional hypotheses.
