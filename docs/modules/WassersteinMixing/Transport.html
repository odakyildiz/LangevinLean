Actual Gaussian ULA contraction and transport to an invariant law

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

Let VVV satisfy the stated differentiability, α\alphaα-strong convexity and β\betaβ-Lipschitz gradient conditions, with 0<h≤1/β0<h\le1/\beta0<h≤1/β. The actual ULA update is x↦x−h∇V(x)+2h ξx\mapsto x-h\nabla V(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,\xix↦x−h∇V(x)+2h​ξ, with independent Gaussian innovations. This section proves its transport contraction from the potential conditions. The separate Gaussian-past fixed-point construction proves existence, finite second moment and exact invariance of the actual invariant law, discharging the invariant-law hypothesis of the general contraction result.

Statement and proof

01Common noise gives the exact pathwise factor §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For all x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z, the two updates using the same innovation satisfy

∥(x−h∇V(x)+2hz)−(y−h∇V(y)+2hz)∥≤(1−αh)∥x−y∥.\|(x-h\nabla V(x)+\sqrt{2h}z)-(y-h\nabla V(y)+\sqrt{2h}z)\| \le(1-\alpha h)\|x-y\|.∥(x−h∇V(x)+2h​z)−(y−h∇V(y)+2h​z)∥≤(1−αh)∥x−y∥.

Proof. The common noise cancels. The strong-convexity and smoothness gradient-step estimate applies for h≤2/(α+β)h\le2/(\alpha+\beta)h≤2/(α+β). This restriction follows from hβ≤1h\beta\le1hβ≤1 and α≤β\alpha\le\betaα≤β, since h(α+β)≤2hβ≤2h(\alpha+\beta)\le2h\beta\le2h(α+β)≤2hβ≤2. Its exact contraction factor is 1−αh≥01-\alpha h\ge01−αh≥0, giving the claim.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.10.7; ULA-SC, Section 3.34.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

ula_shared_noise_contraction verifies the admissible shared ULA step range and invokes ULA.strong_smooth_gradient_step_contract. Unfolding ULA.malaProposal cancels the identical noise terms. The contraction is derived from the potential contract rather than assumed as a coupling field.

02Transport contraction of the actual Markov laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let TTT be the independent-innovation Markov transition and let μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν be probability laws. Then, in extended nonnegative transport distance,

W2(Tμ,Tν)≤qW2(μ,ν),W2(Tkμ,Tkν)≤qkW2(μ,ν),q=1−αh.W_2(T\mu,T\nu)\le qW_2(\mu,\nu),\qquad W_2(T^k\mu,T^k\nu)\le q^kW_2(\mu,\nu),\quad q=1-\alpha h.W2​(Tμ,Tν)≤qW2​(μ,ν),W2​(Tkμ,Tkν)≤qkW2​(μ,ν),q=1−αh.

Proof. Extend any initial coupling by an independent innovation and use that same innovation for both updates. The proved pathwise bound multiplies the coupling's root-mean-square cost by at most qqq. Passing to the infimum over couplings proves the one-step estimate. Induction gives the iterated estimate because q≥0q\ge0q≥0. For standard Gaussian innovations, the independent-product transition is exactly the existing Gaussian ULA law transition; induction identifies their iterated laws.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.11; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.33.6; ULA-SC, Section 3.34.1; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7; Kinetic, Section 3.9.2.

03Finite transport and convergence to the constructed invariant law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If W2(μ,ν)<∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<∞, then the iterated transport distance is finite and its ordinary real value obeys the same geometric estimate. If additionally Tν=νT\nu=\nuTν=ν, then

W2(Tkμ,ν)≤qkW2(μ,ν).W_2(T^k\mu,\nu)\le q^kW_2(\mu,\nu).W2​(Tkμ,ν)≤qkW2​(μ,ν).

Proof. The extended upper bound is a finite product, so the distance it bounds is finite. Converting a finite extended inequality to real values preserves it. The stationarity equality implies Tkν=νT^k\nu=\nuTkν=ν by induction; substitute this into the two-law estimate. The independent Gaussian-past fixed-point argument constructs this invariant probability law and proves its finite second moment and exact stationarity, so the final bounds apply to that actual law.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.17.4; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

ula_iterate_transport_finite bounds the output by a finite extended product. ula_iterate_wasserstein2Real_le applies ENNReal.toReal_mono only after finiteness is proved. ula_to_invariant_wasserstein2Real_le uses the supplied invariance equality through Function.iterate_fixed.

The separate invariant-law construction proves the invariance equality and finite second moment required here, and the final comparison theorems apply this generic result to that constructed law.
