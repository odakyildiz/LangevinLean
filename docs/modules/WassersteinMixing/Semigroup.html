Actual stationary-pair transition operators

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

For a stationary pair law JJJ with both marginals μ\muμ, pullback by the first and second coordinates gives actual isometries U0,U1:L2(μ;F)→L2(J;F)U_0,U_1:L^2(\mu;F)\to L^2(J;F)U0​,U1​:L2(μ;F)→L2(J;F). The adjoint compression P=U0∗U1P=U_0^*U_1P=U0∗​U1​ is a genuine contraction. Reversal symmetry of the pair law makes it self-adjoint.

Statement and proof

01Constructing the contraction from the two actual marginals §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The coordinate evaluations preserve norms and

⟨Pf,g⟩=⟨U1f,U0g⟩,∥Pf∥≤∥f∥.\langle Pf,g\rangle=\langle U_1f,U_0g\rangle,\qquad\|Pf\|\le\|f\|.⟨Pf,g⟩=⟨U1​f,U0​g⟩,∥Pf∥≤∥f∥.

Proof. Each marginal equality makes the corresponding coordinate map measure-preserving, hence its pullback is an isometry. Define the transition by adjoint compression. The adjoint identity proves the pairing formula. The first evaluation and its adjoint have norm at most one, while the second evaluation preserves norms, giving the pointwise and operator contraction bounds.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

evalFst and evalSnd are actual measure-preserving pullbacks. transition is their continuous adjoint composition. The correlation and norm estimates use the defining adjoint identity and the evaluation isometries.

02Reversal symmetry proves self-adjointness §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. If swapping the two coordinates preserves JJJ, then ⟨Pf,g⟩=⟨f,Pg⟩\langle Pf,g\rangle=\langle f,Pg\rangle⟨Pf,g⟩=⟨f,Pg⟩, so PPP is self-adjoint.

Proof. Pullback by the actual swap map is an isometry on the joint space and exchanges the two evaluation maps. Apply preservation of the inner product by this isometry to the correlation identity, then use the adjoint identity in the other direction. This is the symmetric pairing characterization of a bounded self-adjoint operator.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

reverse_evalFst and reverse_evalSnd compare almost-everywhere pullback representatives. transition_symmetric uses the actual reversal isometry and transition_isSelfAdjoint applies the symmetric-operator characterization.

03Compression on any common observation space §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The same construction works for any two isometries into a common Hilbert space: P=U0∗U1P=U_0^*U_1P=U0∗​U1​ has the same pairing formula and norm bound. For equal observations, U0∗U0=IU_0^*U_0=IU0∗​U0​=I.

Proof. The contraction argument uses only the two isometry properties. At equal observations, the adjoint composition of an isometry with itself is exactly the identity.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

compression abstracts only the isometric evaluation algebra. Its correlation and contraction lemmas are used by the actual Brownian observations; compression_self supplies their exact time-zero identity.
