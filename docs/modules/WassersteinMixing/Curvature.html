01The actual second derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The gradient is continuously differentiable, ⟨A(x)u,v⟩=D2V(x)[u,v]\langle A(x)u,v\rangle=D^2V(x)[u,v]⟨A(x)u,v⟩=D2V(x)[u,v], and A(x)A(x)A(x) is symmetric and continuous in xxx.

Proof. Identify the gradient with the inverse Riesz image of the first derivative. Differentiation through this continuous linear identification gives the displayed bilinear form. Equality of mixed second derivatives gives symmetry. Continuous second differentiability gives continuity of the derivative of the gradient.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

contDiff_gradient_of_two differentiates the Riesz representation. hessianOperator is the actual Fréchet derivative of the gradient. inner_hessianOperator identifies its form with the second derivative; symmetry follows from the second-derivative symmetry theorem.

02Pointwise curvature bounds §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Under the potential contract, ∥A(x)∥≤β\|A(x)\|\le\beta∥A(x)∥≤β and

α∥v∥2≤⟨v,A(x)v⟩≤β∥v∥2.\alpha\|v\|^2\le\langle v,A(x)v\rangle\le\beta\|v\|^2.α∥v∥2≤⟨v,A(x)v⟩≤β∥v∥2.

Proof. A derivative of a β\betaβ-Lipschitz map has norm at most β\betaβ, giving the upper estimate by Cauchy–Schwarz. Strong convexity makes the gradient α\alphaα-strongly monotone. Apply that inequality to x+tvx+tvx+tv and xxx, divide by the positive displacement parameter, and pass to the directional derivative as t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0 to obtain the lower bound.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.2.3.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

hessianOperator_norm_le uses the derivative bound of a Lipschitz map. hessianOperator_lower takes a one-sided slope limit of the strong-gradient-monotonicity inequality. hessianOperator_upper combines the norm estimate with the real inner-product bound.

03The exact averaged secant operator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The actual operator integral exists and satisfies

A‾(x,y)(x−y)=∇V(x)−∇V(y),αI⪯A‾(x,y)⪯βI.\overline A(x,y)(x-y)=\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y),\qquad \alpha I\preceq\overline A(x,y)\preceq\beta I.A(x,y)(x−y)=∇V(x)−∇V(y),αI⪯A(x,y)⪯βI.

Proof. Its integrand is continuous on a compact interval. Evaluation and inner-product functionals commute with the Bochner integral. Integrating the pointwise symmetry and form bounds therefore preserves them, and the norm bound is preserved because the interval has length one. The derivative of the gradient along the segment is A(y+t(x−y))(x−y)A(y+t(x-y))(x-y)A(y+t(x−y))(x−y); the fundamental theorem gives the secant identity. Joint continuity in the endpoints follows by dominated convergence, with the uniform bound β\betaβ.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

averagedHessian is an operator-valued interval integral. The application and pairing lemmas justify moving bounded linear functionals inside it. The secant theorem differentiates the actual gradient curve. Joint continuity uses the proved uniform Hessian bound as a dominated-convergence envelope.
