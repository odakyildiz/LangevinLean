The actual score increments and local defect constants

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

All quantities in this section come from the constructed stationary Langevin path. The estimates 6sβ2d6s\beta^2d6sβ2d for score increments and 3h3β2d3h^3\beta^2d3h3β2d for the integrated defect are derived, and subtracting the actual diffusion and Euler updates gives the exact synchronous error equation.

01Deriving the primitive score-increment estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. On a stationary reference segment,

E∥Ynh+s−Ynh∥2≤d(2βs2+4s),E∥∇V(Ynh+s)−∇V(Ynh)∥2≤6sβ2d\mathbb E\|Y_{nh+s}-Y_{nh}\|^2\le d(2\beta s^2+4s),\qquad \mathbb E\|\nabla V(Y_{nh+s})-\nabla V(Y_{nh})\|^2\le6s\beta^2dE∥Ynh+s​−Ynh​∥2≤d(2βs2+4s),E∥∇V(Ynh+s​)−∇V(Ynh​)∥2≤6sβ2d

for 0≤s≤h0\le s\le h0≤s≤h, hβ≤1h\beta\le1hβ≤1.

Proof. The integral equation splits the displacement into integrated drift and Brownian noise. Squared triangle and time Cauchy–Schwarz, the stationary score moment and exact Brownian variance give the displacement polynomial. Gradient Lipschitzness multiplies it by β2\beta^2β2. Since βs≤1\beta s\le1βs≤1, the polynomial is at most 6s6s6s. Continuity follows from the continuous reference segment and Lipschitz drift map.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.11.6; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The generic reference lemmas use the actual reference integral equation and noise moment. reference_score_increment_energy applies displacement_polynomial_le to the proved polynomial estimate. The increment is defined by the true Lipschitz gradient applied to the reference states.

02Actual scores and the constant-three defect §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construction and bounds. Set Fn=∇V(Ynh)F_n=\nabla V(Y_{nh})Fn​=∇V(Ynh​), In(s)=Fn−∇V(Ynh+s)I_n(s)=F_n-\nabla V(Y_{nh+s})In​(s)=Fn​−∇V(Ynh+s​) and Dn=∫0hIn(s) dsD_n=\int_0^h I_n(s)\,dsDn​=∫0h​In​(s)ds. Then

∥Fn∥22≤βd,∥In(s)∥22≤6sβ2d,∥Dn∥22≤3h3β2d.\|F_n\|_2^2\le\beta d,\quad\|I_n(s)\|_2^2\le6s\beta^2d,\quad\|D_n\|_2^2\le3h^3\beta^2d.∥Fn​∥22​≤βd,∥In​(s)∥22​≤6sβ2d,∥Dn​∥22​≤3h3β2d.

Proof. Gibbs stationarity supplies the score moment. Specializing the preceding increment estimate gives the second bound. The time curve is continuous and hence square integrable on the compact interval. Applying time Cauchy–Schwarz and integrating 6sβ2d6s\beta^2d6sβ2d gives the last constant. The endpoint increment is exactly Fn−Fn+1F_n-F_{n+1}Fn​−Fn+1​.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.28.2.

actualScore, actualScoreIncrement and actualDefect use the constructed diffusion. Their moment, continuity and endpoint theorems discharge the generic local-defect estimate directly; none of these bounds is a primitive comparison field.

03Subtracting the true updates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The reference endpoint satisfies

Y(n+1)h=Ynh−hFn+2h ξn+Dn.Y_{(n+1)h}=Y_{nh}-hF_n+\sqrt{2h}\,\xi_n+D_n.Y(n+1)h​=Ynh​−hFn​+2h​ξn​+Dn​.

Proof. Integrating the definition of InI_nIn​ gives Dn=hFn−∫0h∇V(Ynh+s) dsD_n=hF_n-\int_0^h\nabla V(Y_{nh+s})\,dsDn​=hFn​−∫0h​∇V(Ynh+s​)ds. Substitute this into the actual Langevin integral equation and use its exact Brownian endpoint. Subtracting the Euler update cancels the shared innovation and gives

en+1=en−h(∇V(Ynh)−∇V(Xn))+Dn,en=Ynh−Xn.e_{n+1}=e_n-h\bigl(\nabla V(Y_{nh})-\nabla V(X_n)\bigr)+D_n,\qquad e_n=Y_{nh}-X_n.en+1​=en​−h(∇V(Ynh​)−∇V(Xn​))+Dn​,en​=Ynh​−Xn​.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.
