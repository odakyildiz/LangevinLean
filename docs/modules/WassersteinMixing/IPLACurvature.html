The exact IPLA curvature, time and coordinate scaling

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; original statements proved by a modified argument

The supplied manuscript is audited by exact source hashes and compared with arXiv:2608.02430v1. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 are proved under their original C2C^2C2 Hessian assumptions, with the same constants, using a modified proof. The actual Langevin dynamics, reversible semigroup, corrector, Gaussian ULA invariant law and comparison estimates are constructed. The paper's generator/eigenbasis construction and Gaussian-smoothing passage are not formalized. The separate IPLA application derives new bounds by combining this result with Project 03's particle geometry and parameter concentration.

  • Francesco Pedrotti and Peter A. Whalley (2026). Wasserstein mixing time of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02430, version 1 (3 August 2026). Paper HTML · Full paper

For N>0N>0N>0 particles, let the joint potential U(θ,x)U(\theta,x)U(θ,x) be twice continuously differentiable, mmm-strongly convex and have an LLL-Lipschitz gradient, with 0<m≤L0<m\le L0<m≤L. For a positive IPLA step γ\gammaγ satisfying γL≤1\gamma L\le1γL≤1, the scaled state has dimension dN=dθ+Ndxd_N=d_\theta+Nd_xdN​=dθ​+Ndx​, potential VN(z)=∑i=1NU(θ,Nzi)V_N(z)=\sum_{i=1}^NU(\theta,\sqrt N z_i)VN​(z)=∑i=1N​U(θ,N​zi​), curvature constants Nm,NLNm,NLNm,NL, and ULA step γ/N\gamma/Nγ/N. Every lifted potential hypothesis and every coordinate-change identity is proved from these assumptions.

01Deriving the lifted numerical parameters and regularity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The scaled IPLA parameters are

αN=Nm,βN=NL,hN=γN,dN=dθ+Ndx.\alpha_N=Nm,\qquad\beta_N=NL,\qquad h_N=\frac{\gamma}{N},\qquad d_N=d_\theta+Nd_x.αN​=Nm,βN​=NL,hN​=Nγ​,dN​=dθ​+Ndx​.

They satisfy 0<αN≤βN0<\alpha_N\le\beta_N0<αN​≤βN​, hN>0h_N>0hN​>0, and hNβN≤1h_N\beta_N\le1hN​βN​≤1. The finite-sum potential VNV_NVN​ belongs to C2C^2C2.

Proof. Positivity of the particle number and of m,γm,\gammam,γ gives the strict positive parameters. Multiplying m≤Lm\le Lm≤L by NNN gives the curvature ordering. Exact cancellation yields hNβN=γL≤1h_N\beta_N=\gamma L\le1hN​βN​=γL≤1. Each map z↦(θ,Nzi)z\mapsto(\theta,\sqrt N z_i)z↦(θ,N​zi​) is continuous linear, so composition with U∈C2U\in C^2U∈C2 is twice continuously differentiable; a finite sum preserves this regularity.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3.

02Transporting the actual gradient through an orthogonal coordinate map §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For a linear isometric equivalence e:H→Fe:H\to Fe:H→F and a differentiable potential V:H→RV:H\to\mathbb RV:H→R,

∇(V∘e−1)(y)=e ∇V(e−1y).\nabla(V\circ e^{-1})(y)=e\,\nabla V(e^{-1}y).∇(V∘e−1)(y)=e∇V(e−1y).

Proof. Pair each side with an arbitrary vector w∈Fw\in Fw∈F. The derivative chain rule identifies the pairing on the left as DV(e−1y)[e−1w]DV(e^{-1}y)[e^{-1}w]DV(e−1y)[e−1w]. The gradient identity turns it into ⟨∇V(e−1y),e−1w⟩\langle\nabla V(e^{-1}y),e^{-1}w\rangle⟨∇V(e−1y),e−1w⟩. Preservation of the inner product by eee gives the pairing with the right side. Equality against every vector proves the vector identity.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

gradient_reindex uses extensionality by the real inner product, the exact HasFDerivAt.comp chain rule, and LinearIsometryEquiv.inner_map_eq_flip. This proves an identity for the actual gradient rather than introducing a transformed gradient as a separate assumption.

03Preserving the potential contract under reindexing §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. A linear isometric coordinate change preserves C2C^2C2 regularity, the same strong-convexity constant α\alphaα, and the same gradient-Lipschitz constant β\betaβ.

Proof. Smoothness follows by composition with the inverse linear isometry. For strong convexity, apply the original convex-combination inequality to e−1x,e−1ye^{-1}x,e^{-1}ye−1x,e−1y. Linearity preserves their convex combination and the isometry preserves ∥x−y∥\|x-y\|∥x−y∥, giving the same quadratic correction. For smoothness of the gradient, use the proved gradient identity and the isometric distance equalities on both sides of the original β\betaβ-Lipschitz estimate. None of the numerical constants changes.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

potentialAssumptions_reindex assembles all three fields of PotentialAssumptions. It transports the original StrongConvexOn inequality directly and rewrites the Lipschitz estimate using gradient_reindex. The result is valid for arbitrary complete real inner product spaces related by the given linear isometric equivalence.

04Discharging the lifted assumptions from the joint potential §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. The joint assumptions U∈C2U\in C^2U∈C2, mmm-strong convexity and an LLL-Lipschitz gradient imply the entire lifted contract with constants Nm,NLNm,NLNm,NL. In particular, the literal joint Hessian bounds

mI≼∇2U≼LImI\preccurlyeq\nabla^2U\preccurlyeq LImI≼∇2U≼LI

suffice, with the same constants.

Proof. The exact identity ∑i∥(δθ,N δzi)∥2=N∥δz∥2\sum_i\|(\delta\theta,\sqrt N\,\delta z_i)\|^2=N\|\delta z\|^2∑i​∥(δθ,N​δzi​)∥2=N∥δz∥2 makes the sum of the strong-convexity inequalities have constant NmNmNm. The lifted gradient chain rule, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz over the replicated pair coordinates, gives Lipschitz constant NLNLNL. The preceding finite-sum argument supplies C2C^2C2 regularity. When joint Hessian bounds are the input, the already proved Hessian-to-potential theorem first derives joint strong convexity and gradient Lipschitzness. Thus no semigroup, invariant measure, stationary process or comparison estimate is an input here.

Earlier results used here: IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.3; IPLA-SC, Section 3.1.4.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

ipla_potentialAssumptions combines ipla_potential_contDiff with IPLA.potential_strongConvex and IPLA.potential_gradient_lipschitz; these earlier IPLA lemmas prove the sharp factor of the particle number. ipla_potentialAssumptions_of_hessian applies HessianAssumptions.toPotentialAssumptions before this lift. Both return the complete potential contract needed by the constructed Wasserstein comparison.
