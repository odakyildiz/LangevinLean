Affine transport bounds for arbitrary initial distributions

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; checked main sampling proofs

The correspondence uses arXiv:2605.31413v1, identical to the manuscript audited here. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3C^3C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The source audit distinguishes this main-theorem scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (2026). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.31413, version 1 (29 May 2026). Full paper

The main theorems are stated for every initial probability distribution, including distributions at infinite Wasserstein distance from the target. For this purpose distances are taken in the extended half-line [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞], with the conventions x+∞=∞x+\infty=\inftyx+∞=∞ and c⋅∞=∞c\cdot\infty=\inftyc⋅∞=∞ for c>0c>0c>0. This section shows that an affine bound W22(μ′,π′)≤a W22(μ,π)+b\mathsf W_2^2(\mu',\pi')\le a\,\mathsf W_2^2(\mu,\pi)+bW22​(μ′,π′)≤aW22​(μ,π)+b, proved under the assumption W2(μ,π)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(μ,π)<∞, holds in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] without that assumption, provided a>0a>0a>0 and b≥0b\ge0b≥0: if the initial distance is infinite, the right-hand side is infinite. No moment assumption on the initial distribution is therefore needed in the final statements.

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let μ,π,μ′,π′\mu,\pi,\mu',\pi'μ,π,μ′,π′ be measures on a normed space EEE, and let a>0a>0a>0 and b≥0b\ge0b≥0. Assume that, whenever W2(μ,π)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(μ,π)<∞, also W2(μ′,π′)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu',\pi')<\inftyW2​(μ′,π′)<∞ and

W2(μ′,π′)2≤a W2(μ,π)2+b(an inequality between real numbers).\mathsf W_2(\mu',\pi')^2\le a\,\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)^2+b\qquad\text{(an inequality between real numbers).}W2​(μ′,π′)2≤aW2​(μ,π)2+b(an inequality between real numbers).

Then, in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞], W2(μ′,π′)2≤a W2(μ,π)2+b\mathsf W_2(\mu',\pi')^2\le a\,\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)^2+bW2​(μ′,π′)2≤aW2​(μ,π)2+b holds without any finiteness assumption.

Proof. If W2(μ,π)=∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)=\inftyW2​(μ,π)=∞, then W2(μ,π)2=∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)^2=\inftyW2​(μ,π)2=∞ and a⋅∞=∞a\cdot\infty=\inftya⋅∞=∞ because a>0a>0a>0, so the right-hand side is ∞\infty∞ and the inequality holds. If W2(μ,π)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(μ,π)<∞, then by assumption W2(μ′,π′)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu',\pi')<\inftyW2​(μ′,π′)<∞, so both distances are real numbers and the real inequality holds. The inclusion of [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞) into [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] preserves order, sums and products of nonnegative numbers; since a W2(μ,π)2≥0a\,\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)^2\ge0aW2​(μ,π)2≥0 and b≥0b\ge0b≥0, the real inequality is also an inequality in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞]. □\square□

Both sign conditions are needed. If a=0a=0a=0, then 0⋅∞=00\cdot\infty=00⋅∞=0 in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] and an infinite initial distance would not make the right-hand side infinite. If b<0b<0b<0, the right-hand side could not be interpreted in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞].

How the Lean proof follows this argument

squared_transport_extended states the conclusion in ℝ≥0∞: ULA.wasserstein2 μ' π' ^ 2 ≤ ENNReal.ofReal a * ULA.wasserstein2 μ π ^ 2 + ENNReal.ofReal b, where ENNReal.ofReal embeds a real number into [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] (negative numbers are sent to 000). The hypotheses are ha : 0 < a, hb : 0 ≤ b, houtput (finiteness of the output distance when the input distance is finite) and hbound (the real inequality under the same condition, written with ULA.wasserstein2Real).

  1. by_cases hi : ULA.wasserstein2 μ π = ⊤. In the infinite case, simp evaluates the right-hand side to ⊤=∞\top=\infty⊤=∞, using ENNReal.ofReal_pos.mpr ha to know that the coefficient is nonzero.
  2. In the finite case, h applies the monotone map ENNReal.ofReal to hbound hi; ENNReal.ofReal_add and ENNReal.ofReal_mul split the image of the right-hand side (using the nonnegativity of both summands), and simpa only with ENNReal.ofReal_toReal for the two finite distances (houtput hi and hi) identifies the images of the real distances with the extended ones.
