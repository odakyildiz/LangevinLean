Exact Gaussian averaging of the quadratic Taylor polynomial

References and publication status for this development

Supplied manuscript; checked sampling proofs and separate source mistakes

The exact supplied manuscript is the correspondence authority. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. Verified counterexamples to the false Gaussian-design proposition, its empirical fourth-moment event and an intermediate SGLD transient comparison have separate pages linked from the top of the overview. The complete source inventory distinguishes this main-theorem review scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (undated manuscript). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. Supplied Arxiv_version_May_26.tex; publication status not established.

All expectations here use the actual standard Gaussian measure in a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space. The quadratic trace identity identifies the diffusion coefficient in the Langevin generator.

For an actual standard Gaussian vector and any continuous bilinear form,

E[⟨u,Z⟩⟨v,Z⟩]=⟨u,v⟩,E[B(Z,Z)]=∑iB(ei,ei),Z∼N(0,I).\mathbb E[\langle u,Z\rangle\langle v,Z\rangle]=\langle u,v\rangle, \qquad \mathbb E[B(Z,Z)]=\sum_i B(e_i,e_i),\qquad Z\sim N(0,I).E[⟨u,Z⟩⟨v,Z⟩]=⟨u,v⟩,E[B(Z,Z)]=i∑​B(ei​,ei​),Z∼N(0,I).

Writing the quadratic Taylor polynomial as T2ϕ(x;w)=ϕ(x)+Dϕ(x)[w]+12D2ϕ(x)[w,w]\mathcal T_2\phi(x;w)=\phi(x)+D\phi(x)[w]+\tfrac12D^2\phi(x)[w,w]T2​ϕ(x;w)=ϕ(x)+Dϕ(x)[w]+21​D2ϕ(x)[w,w], its Gaussian expectation is exactly

Statement and proof

01Use the true covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the Gaussian covariance bilinear form to two linear observations. The standard Gaussian has mean zero and identity covariance, so the expectation of ⟨u,Z⟩⟨v,Z⟩ equals ⟨u,v⟩. Square integrability justifies the product integral.

02Turn a quadratic form into its trace §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand both arguments of a continuous bilinear form in the standard orthonormal basis. Interchange finite sums and integrals using Gaussian second moments. The covariance identity kills off-diagonal terms, leaving the sum of the diagonal values.

03Average the entire Euler Taylor polynomial §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand the increment hv+√(2h)Z in the linear and quadratic derivative terms. Gaussian centering removes its linear-noise and drift-noise terms; the trace is the actual Laplacian. The remaining deterministic quadratic drift is h²D²φ[v,v]/2. This is an exact equality for the Taylor polynomial, prior to any remainder estimate.
