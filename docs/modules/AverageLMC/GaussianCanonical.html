It constructs the independent Brownian extension, the actual integral solution, its stationary Gibbs law, and the exact Gaussian output law. Mathematical statement and proof

Extend the initial coupling by the explicit canonical product Brownian probability space. Its initial algorithm state X has law μ and its reference state Z has the true Gibbs law. The Lipschitz drift constructs a continuous L² Langevin integral solution; stationarity follows from the already proved Gibbs-stationarity theorem. Independent scalar Brownian paths provide independent standard Gaussian innovations at every positive time. The algorithm center A=X−h∇f(X) is a measurable function of the initial state, so it remains independent of those innovations. The endpoint A+B(h) has exactly the ordinary Gaussian LMC transition law. Local coordinate smoothing at A gives the affine potential bound needed by the Gaussian energy theorem. Integrated strong convexity controls ‖A−Z‖², and integrated deterministic descent controls E f(A)−E f(X). These potential terms cancel. Identifying the lifted gradient norm with the score moment under μ leaves precisely (1−mh) times the initial coupling cost squared, (Mav+m)h²d, and h²(hLhLhL−1) times the score second moment. Output transport finiteness follows from the constructed L² coupling before any real-valued distance is used.
