01Express the remainder using a genuine Hessian difference §

Mathematical statement and proof

Restrict the test to the line x+tx+tx+t vvv. The ordinary Taylor theorem with first-order polynomial and second derivative remainder gives a point uuu strictly between zero and one. The first derivative on this line is the Frechet derivative applied to vvv; the second derivative is the actual second Frechet derivative applied twice to vvv. Subtract the quadratic Taylor term at xxx. The remaining error is exactly one half of the difference between the Hessians at x+ux+ux+u vvv and xxx, evaluated twice on vvv.

02Use uniform Hessian continuity without a third derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

A compactly supported C2C^{2}C2 test has a continuous compactly supported Hessian, hence a uniformly continuous and bounded Hessian. Given η\etaη positive, choose δ\deltaδ so that Hessian differences at distance less than δ\deltaδ are below 2η2\eta2η. If the displacement norm is below δ\deltaδ, the segment remainder is at most η\etaη times its squared norm. For larger displacements, the uniform Hessian bound gives a quadratic error, which is bounded by a fixed cubic multiple because the displacement norm is at least δ\deltaδ. Combining the cases proves

∣R2(x,v)∣≤η∥v∥2+Cη∥v∥3.|R_2(x,v)|\le\eta\|v\|^2+C_\eta\|v\|^3.∣R2​(x,v)∣≤η∥v∥2+Cη​∥v∥3.

The same bounded Hessian proves that the actual gradient of the compact C2C^{2}C2 test is Lipschitz.

03Separate the deterministic drift from Gaussian noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand the test first at the Gaussian perturbation alone. Its linear term averages to zero and its quadratic term averages to hhh times the true Laplacian. Integrating the C2C^{2}C2 remainder uses the exact Gaussian second moment and finite Gaussian third moment. Choose η\etaη small enough that the quadratic Gaussian contribution is at most ε\varepsilonε hhh. Insert the deterministic hvhvhv shift afterward using the first-order Lipschitz-gradient remainder. This contributes only h2h^2h2 times the squared drift norm and a mixed h3/2h^{3/2}h3/2 term. For hhh at most one, their sum is bounded by

h(ε+Kεh)(1+∥v∥2).h(\varepsilon+K_\varepsilon\sqrt h)(1+\|v\|^2).h(ε+Kε​h​)(1+∥v∥2).

The bound is uniform in the center, drift and admissible step size. No third moment of the drift is required.

04Lift the uniform bound through bounded past weights §

Mathematical statement and proof

The C2C^{2}C2 Laplacian is continuous as a finite sum of continuous second derivatives. This makes the entire weak residual measurable for measurable random centers and drifts. If the past weight has absolute value at most AAA almost everywhere, multiply the pointwise residual bound by AAA and integrate. Square integrability of the random drift makes the majorant integrable. The resulting bound is Ah(ε+Kh)Ah(\varepsilon+K\sqrt h)Ah(ε+Kh​) times one plus the drift second moment. The fixed-KKK version retains a supplied uniform pointwise constant unchanged, so the same constant can be used across every Euler mesh and mesh state.
