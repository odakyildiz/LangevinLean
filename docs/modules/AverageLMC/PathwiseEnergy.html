01Smooth potentials are integrable on L2L^{2}L2 §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the quadratic upper model both to fff and to −f-f−f at zero. These two bounds control the absolute value of f(x)f(x)f(x) by the sum of ∣f(0)∣|f(0)|∣f(0)∣, the product of the initial gradient norm and ∥x∥\|x\|∥x∥, and L∥x∥2/2L\|x\|^{2}/2L∥x∥2/2. A square-integrable random vector has integrable norm and squared norm, so comparison proves integrability of fff evaluated at its L2L^{2}L2 representative. The second theorem transfers the result from the quotient-space representative to any supplied random vector with finite second moment by its almost-everywhere equality with the canonical square-integrable representative.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.9.1; IPLA-SC, Section 3.11.1.

02Integrate the strong supporting plane §

Mathematical statement and proof

At each outcome, strong convexity gives f(X)−f(Y)f(X)-f(Y)f(X)−f(Y) at least the inner product of ∇f(Y)\nabla f(Y)∇f(Y) with X−YX-YX−Y plus m∥X−Y∥2/2m\|X-Y\|^{2}/2m∥X−Y∥2/2. Every scalar term is integrable by quadratic growth, and the inner product of two L2L^{2}L2 vectors is integrable by the L2L^{2}L2 Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Integration, the definition of the L2L^{2}L2 inner product, and the exact squared-norm integral identity give the supporting-plane inequality on L2L^{2}L2 itself. The lifted vector field is the actual gradient applied pointwise, rather than a supplied random drift.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.3; ULA-SC, Section 3.2.2; IPLA-SC, Section 3.11.1; Proximal, Section 3.15.2.

03Differentiate after the common noise cancels §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a continuous vector field ggg, the primitive A+∫0tg(s) dsA+\int_0^t g(s)\,dsA+∫0t​g(s)ds has derivative g(t)g(t)g(t). The derivative of its squared Hilbert norm is twice its inner product with g(t)g(t)g(t); the fundamental theorem of calculus proves the displayed exact energy identity. In the Langevin integral equation, X+B(t)−Y(t)X+B(t)-Y(t)X+B(t)−Y(t) equals X−Y(0)+∫0t∇f(Y(s)) dsX-Y(0)+\int_0^t\nabla f(Y(s))\,dsX−Y(0)+∫0t​∇f(Y(s))ds because the same BBB drives both processes. Substitute this expression into the Hilbert identity. No derivative of a Brownian path and no stochastic isometry is assumed.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.13.2; ULA-SC, Section 3.13.5.
