Statement and proof

01Define and identify the true generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

The vector field is ∇Δf−D(∇f)∇f. The directional-derivative trace formula makes Δf continuously differentiable for C³ f. The Riesz representation of its derivative gives continuity of ∇Δf; evaluation of the continuous Hessian on the continuous gradient gives continuity of the second term. The proved vector commutation identity identifies this field exactly with Δ(∇f)−D(∇f)∇f, the Langevin generator applied to ∇f.

02Bound the two actual derivative terms pointwise §

Mathematical statement and proof

A globally L-Lipschitz gradient has Fréchet derivative operator norm at most L at every point. Its value on ∇f is therefore bounded by L‖∇f‖. Separately, the paper’s hypothesis ‖∇Δf‖²≤Mδ²p implies ‖∇Δf‖≤Mδ√p because both norms and Mδ are nonnegative. Neither bound asks for the Hessian to be Lipschitz.

03Construct finite second moments for the generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Laplacian-gradient term is a continuous function bounded uniformly by Mδ√p, hence belongs to L² on every probability space. The Hessian-gradient term is continuous and bounded by L times the actual gradient; the latter has finite second moment because it is a Lipschitz field applied to an L² state. Subtraction constructs the finite second moment of the actual generator itself. These are conclusions of the potential assumptions, not moment premises supplied to a local-error theorem.

04Derive the sharp stationary generator constant §

Mathematical statement and proof

Represent each generator term in L² and use the exact almost-everywhere pointwise identities. The first representative has norm at most Mδ√p; the second has norm at most L times the lifted score norm. The triangle inequality therefore bounds the actual generator norm by L‖∇f(Y)‖₂+Mδ√p. When Y has the true Gibbs law, pushforward integration identifies its score second moment with the Gibbs integral. The sharp coordinate-score theorem bounds that integral by Mav p. Taking its nonnegative square root yields the required stationary constant L√(Mav p)+Mδ√p.
