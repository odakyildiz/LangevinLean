Smoothing by arbitrary independent centered coordinates

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; checked main sampling proofs

The correspondence uses arXiv:2605.31413v1, identical to the manuscript audited here. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3C^3C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The source audit distinguishes this main-theorem scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (2026). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.31413, version 1 (29 May 2026). Full paper

This module proves the full expectation conclusion of the manuscript smoothing lemma. The coordinates may have any distributions with zero mean and unit second moment; Gaussianity is used only in the final specialization. The bound uses the exact coordinate Taylor suprema and their arithmetic average. All potential, score, and product integrability follows from the supplied coordinate second moments and the gradient Lipschitz assumption.

01Build square-integrable partial coordinate sums §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a finite set SSS of coordinate indices, define ZSZ_SZS​ as the sum of ξj\xi_jξj​ eje_jej​ over jjj in SSS. Each scalar coordinate is square integrable, and the map aaa to aejae_jaej​ is continuous linear, so every vector summand and every finite partial sum is square integrable. The actual gradient evaluated at an L2L^{2}L2 state is also L2L^{2}L2: apply the existing Lipschitz drift lift to the canonical L2L^{2}L2 representative, then transfer back along its almost-everywhere equality. Adding the deterministic center and scaling preserve these moments.

03Sum every coordinate and recover the exact average-smoothness coefficient §

Mathematical statement and proof

For the full index set, the coordinate sum is exactly the Euclidean vector with coordinates ξj\xi_jξj​. Choose ccc equal to the square root of 2s2s2s, whose square is 2s2s2s because sss is nonnegative. Smooth potentials have quadratic growth, so the perturbed potential and its difference from the deterministic center are integrable. The finite-set theorem yields

E[f(x+2s ξ)−f(x)]≤s∑jMj=Mavps.\mathbb E[f(x+\sqrt{2s}\,\xi)-f(x)]\le s\sum_j M_j=M_{\rm av}ps.E[f(x+2s​ξ)−f(x)]≤sj∑​Mj​=Mav​ps.

The final equality uses the literal definition Mav=p−1∑jMjM_{\mathrm{av}}=p^{-1}\sum_jM_jMav​=p−1∑j​Mj​ and the positive dimension.

04Specialize to the actual standard Gaussian measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Take the product of n=pn=pn=p real Gaussian laws with mean zero and variance one. Each coordinate evaluation preserves its marginal Gaussian measure; the product coordinates are mutually independent, square integrable, centered, and have second moment one. Apply the arbitrary-coordinate theorem on this product. Its pushforward is identified by the Euclidean coordinate map with the actual standard Gaussian law, so the change-of-measure integral is precisely the Gaussian smoothing integral used by the LMC transition.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.4.1.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

integral_stdGaussian_smoothing gives the formal counterpart of this step. The main intermediate facts are μ, hl, hξ, hmean, hvar, h in that order.
