Sharp initialization radius from actual transport

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; checked main sampling proofs

The correspondence uses arXiv:2605.31413v1, identical to the manuscript audited here. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3C^3C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The source audit distinguishes this main-theorem scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (2026). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.31413, version 1 (29 May 2026). Full paper

The centered stochastic-gradient proof needs the initial squared distance from the true minimizer. This theorem derives it from finite initial transport and the sharp Gibbs concentration radius, rather than assuming a separate initialization moment bound.

For any true minimizer x⋆x_\starx⋆​, strong convexity gives the sharp Gibbs radius, and the coupling argument bounds the initial radius by the actual transport distance:

Mathematical statement and proof

Choose any finite-cost coupling of μ\muμ and the actual Gibbs law. Both marginals have finite second moments: the Gibbs moment follows from strong convexity, and the μ\muμ moment follows from finite transport. At each pair (xxx,yyy), write x−anchor=(x−y)+(y−anchor)x-anchor=(x-y)+(y-anchor)x−anchor=(x−y)+(y−anchor); the squared norm is at most twice the squared coupling displacement plus twice the squared target radius. Integrate, use the two exact marginal pushforwards, and identify the coupling displacement integral with the square of its L2L^{2}L2 coupling cost. The true minimizer and strong convexity give the sharp target radius at most p/mp/mp/m. Thus every finite coupling bounds the initial radius by twice its squared cost plus 2p/m2p/m2p/m. Passing to the actual transport infimum proves the asserted inequality without assuming an optimal coupling or a Hessian regularity condition.
