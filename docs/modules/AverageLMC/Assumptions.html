01Euclidean coordinates and literal Taylor quotients §

Mathematical statement and proof

The state space is Rp\mathbb R^pRp, and eje_jej​ is its jjjth unit vector. The coordinate Taylor quotient is twice the difference between f(x+aej)f(x+ae_j)f(x+aej​) and its first order approximation at xxx, divided by a2a^2a2. The defining set ranges over every xxx and every nonzero real aaa. Its supremum is exactly MjM_jMj​, and the average of these ppp constants is MavM_{\rm av}Mav​. A displacement a=1a=1a=1 at x=0x=0x=0 proves that every coordinate quotient set is nonempty. The unit norm and coordinate inner-product identities connect these definitions to Euclidean calculus.

02The actual global gradient seminorm §

Mathematical statement and proof

The global gradient seminorm is the supremum of the gradient difference norm divided by the state difference norm over unequal states. For ppp positive, a pair consisting of zero and one coordinate vector witnesses nonemptiness. Any supplied gradient Lipschitz constant MMM bounds every quotient, so it bounds the supremum. The quotients are nonnegative, hence the supremum is nonnegative. Conversely, each quotient is at most this bounded supremum. Multiplying by the strictly positive state difference norm proves that the gradient is Lipschitz with its exact seminorm. Thus subsequent inequalities may use the literal constant in the paper instead of an arbitrary larger bound.

03Bound and attain the coordinate quadratic model §

Mathematical statement and proof

The shared differentiable descent lemma gives an upper quadratic model with coefficient M/2M/2M/2. At a coordinate displacement aejae_jaej​, the inner product is aaa times the jjjth gradient coordinate and the squared displacement norm is a2a^2a2. For nonzero aaa, multiply the upper model by 222 and divide by a2a^2a2 to show that every Taylor quotient is at most MMM. This proves boundedness of the quotient set and MjM_jMj​ at most MMM. Each quotient is also at most its supremum MjM_jMj​. Multiplication by a2a^2a2 and rearrangement recover the sharper coordinate upper model with coefficient Mj/2M_j/2Mj​/2. The zero displacement follows directly. No existence of a second derivative is needed.

04Compare strong convexity, average smoothness and global smoothness §

Mathematical statement and proof

The strong-convexity lower model at zero and eje_jej​ gives the same first order term as the coordinate upper model, with quadratic coefficients m/2m/2m/2 and Mj/2M_j/2Mj​/2 respectively. Subtraction yields mmm at most MjM_jMj​. Summing the upper and lower bounds over the ppp coordinates and dividing by positive ppp proves mmm at most MavM_{\rm av}Mav​ at most MMM. Apply the upper bounds to the actual nonnegative global gradient seminorm, using its proved Lipschitz property, to obtain MjM_jMj​ at most M∞M_\inftyM∞​ and MavM_{\rm av}Mav​ at most M∞M_\inftyM∞​ with exactly the paper's supremum constants.

05Actual Hessian, Laplacian and finite-sum notation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Hessian is the second Fréchet derivative evaluated on pairs of coordinate vectors, and the Laplacian is its canonical trace. Expanding the Laplacian in the canonical orthonormal basis gives exactly the matrix trace of this Hessian. The Laplacian smoothness assumption requires three continuous derivatives and the displayed bound on the squared norm of the gradient of the Laplacian, with the factor ppp from (lip:2). It does not introduce a Hessian Lipschitz requirement. Finally, the root mean square component smoothness is the square root of the average of the squared exact component-gradient seminorms, as in (eq:Mtwo).

Formal correspondence. The exact definitions are hessian, laplacian, LaplacianSmooth and rmsComponentSmoothness; the Laplacian is the Mathlib operator.
