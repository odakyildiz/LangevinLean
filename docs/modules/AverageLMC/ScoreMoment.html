01Obtain the exact coordinate score MGF by translation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coordinate quadratic model implies

e−f(x)e−a∂jf(x)≤eMja2/2e−f(x+aej).e^{-f(x)}e^{-a\partial_j f(x)}\le e^{M_j a^2/2}e^{-f(x+ae_j)}.e−f(x)e−a∂j​f(x)≤eMj​a2/2e−f(x+aej​).

Replacing a by minus t produces the Gaussian MGF bound for the actual jth score coordinate, with proxy MjM_jMj​. The same finite exponential integral bound supplies exponential integrability, so this is an actual sub-Gaussian statement rather than a formal integral comparison.

02Sum sharp second moments without second differentiability §

Mathematical statement and proof

Strong convexity implies m at most every coordinate Taylor supremum, so each MjM_jMj​ is nonnegative. Apply the established sub-Gaussian second-moment theorem to the coordinate score: its uncentered second moment is at most MjM_jMj​, and square integrability is derived at the same time. The squared Euclidean norm is the sum of the squared coordinates. Integral linearity and the finite sum therefore give

Eπ∥∇f∥2≤∑jMj=pMav.\mathbb E_\pi\|\nabla f\|^2\le\sum_j M_j=pM_{\rm av}.Eπ​∥∇f∥2≤j∑​Mj​=pMav​.

Only differentiability, a Lipschitz gradient, and positive strong convexity are used in this sharper score bound.

03Identify coordinate derivatives and control all weighted integrals §

Mathematical statement and proof

A normalized Gibbs integral is integrable exactly when its exponential-weighted Lebesgue integrand is integrable, since the partition function is finite and positive. The coordinate score equals the directional Frechet derivative in eje_jej​. Differentiating that expression identifies its derivative in eje_jej​ with the actual diagonal Hessian entry. C2 regularity makes this coordinate score C1. Projecting the global gradient Lipschitz inequality bounds the coordinate score with the same Lipschitz constant, and hence bounds its derivative. These facts justify the weighted integration by parts used next.

04Prove the score-Hessian identity by global integration by parts §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set psi equal to the jth score coordinate. Its first and second moments are finite by the first-order Gibbs score theorem. Its directional derivative is bounded by M, so that derivative is Gibbs integrable as well. The three unnormalized functions needed for global integration by parts are therefore integrable: the derivative of psi times e^{-f}, psi times the derivative of e^{-f}, and psi e^{-f}. The chain rule gives DjD_jDj​ e^{-f}=-e^{-f} psi. Integration by parts cancels its minus sign and proves

∫(∂jf)2 dπ=∫∂jjf dπ.\int(\partial_j f)^2\,d\pi=\int\partial_{jj}f\,d\pi.∫(∂j​f)2dπ=∫∂jj​fdπ.

No decay or boundary term is supplied as a hypothesis. Summing the finite coordinate identities and using trace(Hessian f)=Laplacian f proves the full score-Laplacian identity. The partition-based statement works whenever normalization is finite; strong convexity supplies normalization automatically.

05Differentiate the coordinate quadratic model to bound each Hessian diagonal §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the coordinate upper model at x with displacement a eje_jej​ and at x+a eje_jej​ with displacement minus a eje_jej​. Addition gives a times the difference of the coordinate scores at most MjM_jMj​ a squared. For a positive, divide by a squared and take the right derivative at zero. Its actual derivative is the diagonal Hessian entry, so ∂jj\partial_{jj}∂jj​ f(x) is at most MjM_jMj​. Taking the trace proves the pointwise Laplacian bound p MavM_{\rm av}Mav​. Conversely strong gradient monotonicity along the same coordinate line bounds its difference quotient below by m. Taking the same derivative gives m at most every diagonal Hessian entry. In the convex case m=0, their sum is nonnegative.
