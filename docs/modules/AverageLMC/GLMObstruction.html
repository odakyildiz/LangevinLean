01The oscillating component and its actual derivatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define g(t)=t squared minus sin(t), g'(t)=2t minus cos(t), and g''(t)=2 plus sin(t). Differentiate these formulas using the power and trigonometric derivative rules; the third derivative is cos(t). These are derivatives of the same component, rather than independently supplied derivative fields. Polynomial and sine functions are smooth on the whole real line, so this choice satisfies the component regularity in the manuscript.

02A fixed derivative bound independent of dimension §

Mathematical statement and proof

The inequality sin(t) at least minus one gives g''(t) at least one. Therefore g'' is nonnegative and its absolute value is itself. The bounds sin(t) at most one and absolute cos(t) at most one yield absolute g''(t) plus absolute g'''(t) at most four, uniformly over t. The component selector uses this oscillating function at the distinguished index and the quadratic at every other index. Each quadratic has second derivative two and third derivative zero. The module proves smoothness, convexity and the bound on actual second and third derivatives for every selected component. Thus the same fixed CgC_gCg​=4 applies to every component in every dimension.

03Write the finite-sum potential and its score explicitly §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a finite design family XiX_iXi​ and a distinguished index i0i_0i0​, the potential is the sum of the squared inner products with XiX_iXi​, minus one copy of sin of the inner product with Xi0X_{i_0}Xi0​​. Expanding the selector and summing the unique sine correction proves that this is exactly the GLM sum with the certified component family. Its smoothness on the whole state space follows from finite sums and composition of smooth polynomials, linear functionals and sine. Its constant quadratic Hessian Q is twice the sum of the rank-one operators XiX_iXi​ XiX_iXi​ transpose. The score is Qx minus cos of the inner product with a times a, where a=Xi0X_{i_0}Xi0​​. Differentiate each squared linear functional, sum the resulting continuous linear derivatives, and subtract the derivative of the single sine term. The result is the inner-product functional represented by this explicit score.

04Differentiate the score to obtain the Hessian §

Mathematical statement and proof

Differentiate Qx using the constant linear map Q. The derivative of the negative cosine term is sin of the inner product with a times the rank-one operator aa transpose. Thus the Hessian is H(x)=Q plus sin of the inner product with a times aa transpose. This formula is proved as the Frechet derivative of the explicit score. In a complete real inner-product space, the previously proved derivative of the potential identifies this score as its actual gradient. Taking that gradient's derivative then identifies H as the actual Hessian. The operator norm used for these continuous linear maps is exactly the Euclidean operator norm relevant to the manuscript's M2M_2M2​.

05Differentiate the Hessian along the design direction §

Mathematical statement and proof

Restrict the Hessian to the path x=t a. Its sine coefficient becomes sin(t times the squared norm of a). At t=0 this scalar function has derivative equal to the squared norm of a. Consequently the derivative of H(t a) at zero is the squared norm of a times the rank-one operator aa transpose. The rank-one operator has norm equal to the squared norm of a, so the Hessian path derivative has norm equal to the fourth power of the norm of a.

06Every Hessian Lipschitz constant has cubic growth §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose H is M-Lipschitz in operator norm, with M nonnegative. Along x=t a, the path is M times the norm of a Lipschitz, because its state displacement has norm absolute t times the norm of a. A derivative of a Lipschitz path has norm at most its Lipschitz constant. The preceding derivative calculation therefore gives the fourth power of the norm of a at most M times the norm of a. If a is nonzero, divide by its positive norm to obtain the cube of its norm at most M. If a is zero, the result follows from M nonnegative. With n=p and a standard Gaussian first design vector, the separate Gaussian module shows why this necessary cubic lower bound prevents a uniform order-n bound with probability one half.
