Coupling lifts and the squared transport infimum

References and publication status for this development

Supplied manuscript; checked sampling proofs and separate source mistakes

The exact supplied manuscript is the correspondence authority. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. Verified counterexamples to the false Gaussian-design proposition, its empirical fourth-moment event and an intermediate SGLD transient comparison have separate pages linked from the top of the overview. The complete source inventory distinguishes this main-theorem review scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (undated manuscript). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. Supplied Arxiv_version_May_26.tex; publication status not established.

The SGLD argument begins with any finite-cost coupling of the full-gradient output and the Gibbs target. A regular conditional distribution lifts this coupling to the original initialization and Gaussian space. No optimal coupling is assumed.

Statement and proof

01Pass real quadratic estimates through the infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a nonnegative coefficient, choose finite couplings with costs arbitrarily close to the Wasserstein infimum and pass to the limit by continuity. For a negative coefficient, any finite coupling suffices: multiplication reverses the comparison between its squared cost and the infimum. The transport specialization retains an explicit finiteness condition for the output before its real value is used.

02Disintegrate the output coupling over its first marginal §

Mathematical statement and proof

The conditional kernel of the chosen coupling is composed with the original output map. Integrating this kernel against the original input law constructs a probability measure on input and target. Its input marginal is the original law. Mapping input and target to output and target recovers the chosen coupling, by the disintegration identity; taking its second marginal therefore recovers the Gibbs target. These identities justify adding an independent stochastic-gradient index to an actual output coupling.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.17.1.
