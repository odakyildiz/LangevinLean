This section contains two general facts about quadratic transport that are used when a one-step bound is proved by constructing couplings. Let EEE be a normed space with its Borel sets (in the applications E=RpE=\mathbb R^pE=Rp). For measures μ,π\mu,\piμ,π on EEE, a coupling of μ\muμ and π\piπ is a measure γ\gammaγ on E×EE\times EE×E whose first and second marginals are μ\muμ and π\piπ. Its cost is C(γ)=(∫∥x−y∥2 γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞]C(\gamma)=\bigl(\int\|x-y\|^2\,\gamma(dx,dy)\bigr)^{1/2}\in[0,\infty]C(γ)=(∫∥x−y∥2γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞], and the Wasserstein distance is W2(μ,π)=inf⁡γC(γ)∈[0,∞]\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)=\inf_\gamma C(\gamma)\in[0,\infty]W2​(μ,π)=infγ​C(γ)∈[0,∞], the infimum over all couplings. No optimal coupling is assumed to exist.

The first result passes a bound from couplings to the distance: if W2(μ,π)<∞\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(μ,π)<∞ and r≤a C(γ)2+br\le a\,C(\gamma)^2+br≤aC(γ)2+b for every coupling γ\gammaγ of finite cost, then r≤a W2(μ,π)2+br\le a\,\mathsf W_2(\mu,\pi)^2+br≤aW2​(μ,π)2+b, for every real coefficient aaa. The second result lifts a coupling of an output law to the input space: if F:S→EF:S\to EF:S→E is measurable, PPP is a probability measure on SSS and γ\gammaγ couples the law P∘F−1P\circ F^{-1}P∘F−1 of FFF with π\piπ, then there is a probability measure RRR on S×ES\times ES×E with first marginal PPP under which the pair (F(s),y)(F(s),y)(F(s),y) has law γ\gammaγ. In the stochastic-gradient argument sss is the input of one step, F(s)F(s)F(s) the full-gradient output, and the lift allows an independent random index to be adjoined to an arbitrary coupling of the full-gradient output with the target.

02Lifting an output coupling to the input space §

Mathematical statement and proof

Setting. Let (S,S)(S,\mathcal S)(S,S) be a measurable space with a probability measure PPP, let EEE be a standard Borel space (for instance Rp\mathbb R^pRp), let F:S→EF:S\to EF:S→E be measurable, and let γ\gammaγ be a coupling of the law P∘F−1P\circ F^{-1}P∘F−1 of FFF and a measure π\piπ on EEE. Since the first marginal of γ\gammaγ is a probability measure, γ\gammaγ is a probability measure. Because EEE is standard Borel, γ\gammaγ admits a disintegration with respect to its first coordinate: a Markov kernel KKK from EEE to EEE (each K(x,⋅)K(x,\cdot)K(x,⋅) is a probability measure and x↦K(x,B)x\mapsto K(x,B)x↦K(x,B) is measurable) such that

γ(C)=∫EK(x,Cx) (P∘F−1)(dx),Cx={y:(x,y)∈C},C⊆E×E measurable.(2)\begin{aligned} \gamma(C)&=\int_E K(x,C_x)\,(P\circ F^{-1})(dx),\\ C_x&=\{y:(x,y)\in C\},\qquad C\subseteq E\times E\text{ measurable}. \qquad(2) \end{aligned}γ(C)Cx​​=∫E​K(x,Cx​)(P∘F−1)(dx),={y:(x,y)∈C},C⊆E×E measurable.(2)​

Define the measure RRR on S×ES\times ES×E by

R(G)=∫SK(F(s),Gs) P(ds),Gs={y:(s,y)∈G},R(G)=\int_S K\bigl(F(s),G_s\bigr)\,P(ds),\qquad G_s=\{y:(s,y)\in G\},R(G)=∫S​K(F(s),Gs​)P(ds),Gs​={y:(s,y)∈G},

that is, sss is drawn from PPP and then yyy from K(F(s),⋅)K(F(s),\cdot)K(F(s),⋅).

Claim. (1) RRR is a probability measure. (2) The first marginal of RRR is PPP. (3) The image of RRR under (s,y)↦(F(s),y)(s,y)\mapsto(F(s),y)(s,y)↦(F(s),y) is γ\gammaγ. (4) The second marginal of RRR is π\piπ.

(3) Let C⊆E×EC\subseteq E\times EC⊆E×E be measurable and G={(s,y):(F(s),y)∈C}G=\{(s,y):(F(s),y)\in C\}G={(s,y):(F(s),y)∈C}. Then Gs=CF(s)G_s=C_{F(s)}Gs​=CF(s)​, so

R(G)=∫SK(F(s),CF(s)) P(ds)=∫EK(x,Cx) (P∘F−1)(dx)=γ(C),R(G)=\int_SK\bigl(F(s),C_{F(s)}\bigr)\,P(ds)=\int_EK(x,C_x)\,(P\circ F^{-1})(dx)=\gamma(C),R(G)=∫S​K(F(s),CF(s)​)P(ds)=∫E​K(x,Cx​)(P∘F−1)(dx)=γ(C),

where the middle equality is the change-of-variables formula for the image measure P∘F−1P\circ F^{-1}P∘F−1 applied to the measurable function x↦K(x,Cx)x\mapsto K(x,C_x)x↦K(x,Cx​), and the last equality is (2).

(4) The second coordinate of (F(s),y)(F(s),y)(F(s),y) is yyy. Taking the image of the measure in (3) under the second projection, the second marginal of RRR equals the second marginal of γ\gammaγ, which is π\piπ. □\square□

In probabilistic terms: on the probability space (S×E,R)(S\times E,R)(S×E,R), the coordinate sss has law PPP, the coordinate yyy has law π\piπ, and (F(s),y)(F(s),y)(F(s),y) has the prescribed joint law γ\gammaγ. Every coupling of the output law with the target is therefore realized jointly with the input of the step.
