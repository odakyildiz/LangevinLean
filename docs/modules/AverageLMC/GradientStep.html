01The gradient-step lemma (eq:basic-gd) §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand the squared distance after the deterministic gradient step: the result is the original squared distance minus twice h times the inner product of the displacement and the gradient, plus h squared times the squared gradient norm. The supporting-plane inequality for m-strong convexity bounds the negative inner product by f(y)-f(theta) minus m/2 times the squared displacement norm. Multiply that inequality by the nonnegative scalar 2h and substitute it in the expansion. Collecting the squared-distance coefficients gives 1-mh, precisely the paper's displayed gradient-step lemma.

02Deterministic descent in the smoothing lemma (eq:exp-upper) §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the shared quadratic upper model for a differentiable potential with M-Lipschitz gradient at the point theta-h times its gradient. Its first order term is minus h times the squared gradient norm, and the quadratic remainder is M h squared/2 times that norm. Multiply by two and factor to obtain minus h times (2-hM) times the squared gradient norm. If hM is at most 2, this right side is nonpositive, which proves that the gradient step does not increase the potential. This verifies the deterministic conclusion of the paper's smoothing lemma; it does not assert the separate expectation inequality for random noise.
