The gradient-step and descent inequalities

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; checked main sampling proofs

The correspondence uses arXiv:2605.31413v1, identical to the manuscript audited here. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3C^3C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The source audit distinguishes this main-theorem scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (2026). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.31413, version 1 (29 May 2026). Full paper

This section proves the two deterministic inequalities used in the proof of Theorem 1. They concern one gradient step θ↦θ−h∇f(θ)\theta\mapsto\theta-h\nabla f(\theta)θ↦θ−h∇f(θ) with step h≥0h\ge0h≥0 for a differentiable potential fff on a real Hilbert space HHH (in the applications H=RpH=\mathbb R^pH=Rp). The first, the gradient-step lemma (eq:basic-gd) of the paper, uses mmm-strong convexity:

∥θ−h∇f(θ)−y∥2≤(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥∇f(θ)∥2(θ,y∈H).\|\theta-h\nabla f(\theta)-y\|^2\le(1-mh)\|\theta-y\|^2+2h\bigl(f(y)-f(\theta)\bigr)+h^2\|\nabla f(\theta)\|^2\qquad(\theta,y\in H).∥θ−h∇f(θ)−y∥2≤(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥∇f(θ)∥2(θ,y∈H).

The second, the deterministic half (eq:exp-upper) of the smoothing lemma, uses an MMM-Lipschitz gradient:

2(f(θ−h∇f(θ))−f(θ))≤−h(2−hM)∥∇f(θ)∥2.2\bigl(f(\theta-h\nabla f(\theta))-f(\theta)\bigr)\le-h(2-hM)\|\nabla f(\theta)\|^2.2(f(θ−h∇f(θ))−f(θ))≤−h(2−hM)∥∇f(θ)∥2.

In the proof of Theorem 1 the first inequality is applied with θ=ϑ\theta=\varthetaθ=ϑ and y=Yy=Yy=Y, where Y∼πY\sim\piY∼π is coupled with the current iterate ϑ\varthetaϑ; the second controls the potential after the gradient step. When the two are added, the potential values cancel and the coefficient h2(hM−1)h^2(hM-1)h2(hM−1) of ∥∇f(ϑ)∥2\|\nabla f(\vartheta)\|^2∥∇f(ϑ)∥2 remains.

Statement and proof

01The gradient-step lemma §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let HHH be a real Hilbert space, let f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R be differentiable and mmm-strongly convex for some m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, and let h≥0h\ge0h≥0. Then for all θ,y∈H\theta,y\in Hθ,y∈H,

∥θ−h∇f(θ)−y∥2≤(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥∇f(θ)∥2.\|\theta-h\nabla f(\theta)-y\|^2\le(1-mh)\|\theta-y\|^2+2h\bigl(f(y)-f(\theta)\bigr)+h^2\|\nabla f(\theta)\|^2.∥θ−h∇f(θ)−y∥2≤(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥∇f(θ)∥2.

Proof. Write g=∇f(θ)g=\nabla f(\theta)g=∇f(θ) and d=θ−yd=\theta-yd=θ−y. Since θ−hg−y=d−hg\theta-hg-y=d-hgθ−hg−y=d−hg, expanding the square of the norm with ∥u−v∥2=∥u∥2−2⟨u,v⟩+∥v∥2\|u-v\|^2=\|u\|^2-2\langle u,v\rangle+\|v\|^2∥u−v∥2=∥u∥2−2⟨u,v⟩+∥v∥2 and ∥hg∥=∣h∣ ∥g∥=h∥g∥\|hg\|=|h|\,\|g\|=h\|g\|∥hg∥=∣h∣∥g∥=h∥g∥ gives

∥θ−hg−y∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨d,g⟩+h2∥g∥2.(1)\|\theta-hg-y\|^2=\|d\|^2-2h\langle d,g\rangle+h^2\|g\|^2.\qquad(1)∥θ−hg−y∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨d,g⟩+h2∥g∥2.(1)

The supporting-plane inequality of a differentiable mmm-strongly convex function (inequality (3) of the previous section, valid in every real Hilbert space) at the base point θ\thetaθ and the point yyy reads

f(θ)+⟨g,y−θ⟩+m2∥y−θ∥2≤f(y).f(\theta)+\langle g,y-\theta\rangle+\frac m2\|y-\theta\|^2\le f(y).f(θ)+⟨g,y−θ⟩+2m​∥y−θ∥2≤f(y).

Since y−θ=−dy-\theta=-dy−θ=−d, we have ⟨g,y−θ⟩=−⟨d,g⟩\langle g,y-\theta\rangle=-\langle d,g\rangle⟨g,y−θ⟩=−⟨d,g⟩ and ∥y−θ∥=∥d∥\|y-\theta\|=\|d\|∥y−θ∥=∥d∥, so the inequality becomes

−⟨d,g⟩≤f(y)−f(θ)−m2∥d∥2.(2)-\langle d,g\rangle\le f(y)-f(\theta)-\frac m2\|d\|^2.\qquad(2)−⟨d,g⟩≤f(y)−f(θ)−2m​∥d∥2.(2)

Multiplying (2) by the nonnegative number 2h2h2h preserves the inequality: −2h⟨d,g⟩≤2h(f(y)−f(θ))−mh∥d∥2-2h\langle d,g\rangle\le2h(f(y)-f(\theta))-mh\|d\|^2−2h⟨d,g⟩≤2h(f(y)−f(θ))−mh∥d∥2. Substituting this bound into (1),

∥θ−hg−y∥2≤∥d∥2−mh∥d∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥g∥2,\|\theta-hg-y\|^2\le\|d\|^2-mh\|d\|^2+2h\bigl(f(y)-f(\theta)\bigr)+h^2\|g\|^2,∥θ−hg−y∥2≤∥d∥2−mh∥d∥2+2h(f(y)−f(θ))+h2∥g∥2,

which is the claim, since ∥d∥2−mh∥d∥2=(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2\|d\|^2-mh\|d\|^2=(1-mh)\|\theta-y\|^2∥d∥2−mh∥d∥2=(1−mh)∥θ−y∥2. □\square□

The hypothesis h≥0h\ge0h≥0 is used twice: to write ∥hg∥=h∥g∥\|hg\|=h\|g\|∥hg∥=h∥g∥ and to multiply (2) by 2h2h2h. For h=0h=0h=0 the claim reduces to ∥θ−y∥2≤∥θ−y∥2\|\theta-y\|^2\le\|\theta-y\|^2∥θ−y∥2≤∥θ−y∥2. No sign condition on mmm and no smoothness of ∇f\nabla f∇f are needed.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The ambient space is a type H with the instances [NormedAddCommGroup H] [InnerProductSpace ℝ H] [CompleteSpace H], that is, a real Hilbert space; gradient f θ is the vector representing the derivative of f at θ and h • v is scalar multiplication. The hypotheses of gradient_step are hdiff : Differentiable ℝ f, hsc : StrongConvexOn Set.univ m f and hh : 0 ≤ h.

  1. hfirst is the supporting-plane inequality, the library lemma ULA.strong_first_order hdiff hsc θ y.
  2. hnorm (∥y−θ∥=∥θ−y∥\|y-\theta\|=\|\theta-y\|∥y−θ∥=∥θ−y∥, by norm_sub_rev) and hinner (⟨g,y−θ⟩=−⟨θ−y,g⟩\langle g,y-\theta\rangle=-\langle\theta-y,g\rangle⟨g,y−θ⟩=−⟨θ−y,g⟩, by writing y−θ=−(θ−y)y-\theta=-(\theta-y)y−θ=−(θ−y) with module, then inner_neg_right and real_inner_comm) rewrite hfirst into (2).
  3. hscaled multiplies (2) by 2h≥02h\ge02h≥0 with mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left; the nonnegativity of 2h2h2h is checked by positivity.
  4. The goal is rewritten into the expansion (1): the vector identity θ−hg−y=(θ−y)−hg\theta-hg-y=(\theta-y)-hgθ−hg−y=(θ−y)−hg (by module), norm_sub_sq_real for the square of a difference, real_inner_smul_right to extract hhh from the inner product, and norm_smul, Real.norm_eq_abs, abs_of_nonneg hh for ∥hg∥=h∥g∥\|hg\|=h\|g\|∥hg∥=h∥g∥. nlinarith then combines the rewritten goal with hscaled.

02Descent of the potential along a gradient step §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let HHH be a real Hilbert space, let f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R be differentiable with MMM-Lipschitz gradient for some M≥0M\ge0M≥0, and let θ∈H\theta\in Hθ∈H and h≥0h\ge0h≥0. Then

2(f(θ−h∇f(θ))−f(θ))≤−h(2−hM)∥∇f(θ)∥2.(3)2\bigl(f(\theta-h\nabla f(\theta))-f(\theta)\bigr)\le-h(2-hM)\|\nabla f(\theta)\|^2.\qquad(3)2(f(θ−h∇f(θ))−f(θ))≤−h(2−hM)∥∇f(θ)∥2.(3)

If moreover hM≤2hM\le2hM≤2, then f(θ−h∇f(θ))≤f(θ)f(\theta-h\nabla f(\theta))\le f(\theta)f(θ−h∇f(θ))≤f(θ).

Proof. Write g=∇f(θ)g=\nabla f(\theta)g=∇f(θ). The descent inequality f(z)≤f(θ)+⟨g,z−θ⟩+M2∥z−θ∥2f(z)\le f(\theta)+\langle g,z-\theta\rangle+\frac M2\|z-\theta\|^2f(z)≤f(θ)+⟨g,z−θ⟩+2M​∥z−θ∥2, proved in the previous section from the fundamental theorem of calculus along the segment from θ\thetaθ to zzz, applies to z=θ−hgz=\theta-hgz=θ−hg. There z−θ=−hgz-\theta=-hgz−θ=−hg, so ⟨g,z−θ⟩=−h⟨g,g⟩=−h∥g∥2\langle g,z-\theta\rangle=-h\langle g,g\rangle=-h\|g\|^2⟨g,z−θ⟩=−h⟨g,g⟩=−h∥g∥2 and ∥z−θ∥2=∣−h∣2∥g∥2=h2∥g∥2\|z-\theta\|^2=|-h|^2\|g\|^2=h^2\|g\|^2∥z−θ∥2=∣−h∣2∥g∥2=h2∥g∥2. Hence

f(θ−hg)≤f(θ)−h∥g∥2+M2h2∥g∥2.f(\theta-hg)\le f(\theta)-h\|g\|^2+\frac M2h^2\|g\|^2.f(θ−hg)≤f(θ)−h∥g∥2+2M​h2∥g∥2.

Subtracting f(θ)f(\theta)f(θ) and multiplying by 222,

2(f(θ−hg)−f(θ))≤−2h∥g∥2+Mh2∥g∥2=−h(2−hM)∥g∥2,2\bigl(f(\theta-hg)-f(\theta)\bigr)\le-2h\|g\|^2+Mh^2\|g\|^2=-h(2-hM)\|g\|^2,2(f(θ−hg)−f(θ))≤−2h∥g∥2+Mh2∥g∥2=−h(2−hM)∥g∥2,

which is (3). If hM≤2hM\le2hM≤2, the three factors −h≤0-h\le0−h≤0, 2−hM≥02-hM\ge02−hM≥0 and ∥g∥2≥0\|g\|^2\ge0∥g∥2≥0 make the right-hand side of (3) a product of a nonpositive and two nonnegative numbers, hence ≤0\le0≤0. Therefore 2(f(θ−hg)−f(θ))≤02(f(\theta-hg)-f(\theta))\le02(f(θ−hg)−f(θ))≤0, which is the second assertion. □\square□

In Theorem 1 the step satisfies hM∞≤1hM_\infty\le1hM∞​≤1, so the second assertion applies: the gradient step does not increase the potential. Only (3) itself enters the one-step bound, where its right-hand side combines with the term h2∥∇f(ϑ)∥2h^2\|\nabla f(\vartheta)\|^2h2∥∇f(ϑ)∥2 of the gradient-step lemma.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The Lipschitz hypothesis is hLip : LipschitzWith M (gradient f) with M : ℝ≥0, a nonnegative real; (M : ℝ) is its coercion to R\mathbb RR.

  1. In gradient_descent, hs is the descent inequality ULA.Analysis.smooth_upper_bound applied at θ and θ - h • gradient f θ. heq computes the displacement −hg-hg−hg (by module), and the rewrites real_inner_smul_right, real_inner_self_eq_norm_sq, norm_smul, Real.norm_eq_abs, abs_neg and abs_of_nonneg hh evaluate ⟨g,−hg⟩=−h∥g∥2\langle g,-hg\rangle=-h\|g\|^2⟨g,−hg⟩=−h∥g∥2 and ∥−hg∥=h∥g∥\|-hg\|=h\|g\|∥−hg∥=h∥g∥ inside hs. nlinarith multiplies by 222 and rearranges, giving (3).
  2. gradient_descent_nonincreasing adds hstep : h * (M : ℝ) ≤ 2. hneg shows that the right-hand side of (3) is ≤0\le0≤0 by two applications of mul_nonpos_of_nonpos_of_nonneg, with neg_nonpos.mpr hh for −h≤0-h\le0−h≤0, sub_nonneg.mpr hstep for 2−hM≥02-hM\ge02−hM≥0 and sq_nonneg for ∥g∥2≥0\|g\|^2\ge0∥g∥2≥0; linarith combines hs (inequality (3)) with hneg.
