Statement and proof

01Fixed directional derivatives and Schwarz symmetry §

Mathematical statement and proof

A directional derivative is the actual Fréchet derivative applied to a fixed vector. A continuously differentiable derivative map loses one order of regularity, and evaluation at a fixed vector preserves the remaining regularity. Differentiating that evaluation identifies a second directional derivative with the actual second Fréchet derivative applied to the two directions. Schwarz symmetry for C2C^{2}C2 functions then commutes the two directional derivatives as functions of the base point. These statements are proved for arbitrary real normed spaces, before choosing Euclidean coordinates.

02Commute the needed three derivatives without analyticity §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a C3C^{3}C3 potential, first commute the two inner directional derivatives using C2C^{2}C2 symmetry of fff. Then commute the outer pair using C2C^{2}C2 symmetry of one first directional derivative. This gives DuDuDvf=DvDuDufD_{u}D_{u}D_{v}f=D_{v}D_{u}D_{u}fDu​Du​Dv​f=Dv​Du​Du​f. The argument establishes exactly the third-order permutation needed for the Laplacian and avoids any appeal to an analytic-only iterated-derivative permutation theorem.

03Expand the canonical trace and differentiate the finite sum §

Mathematical statement and proof

The canonical orthonormal-basis formula for the Laplacian expresses it as the sum of second directional derivatives along the coordinate vectors. The jjjth coordinate of the actual gradient equals the directional derivative along eje_jej​ by the gradient–Fréchet-derivative identity. A finite sum of differentiable scalar functions can be differentiated term by term. Finally, the gradient is C2C^{2}C2 because it is the continuous Riesz inverse applied to the C2C^{2}C2 derivative map of a C3C^{3}C3 potential.

04Identify the vector Laplacian coordinate by coordinate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Continuous linear projection onto coordinate jjj commutes with the vector Laplacian. Its scalar observable is DjfD_jfDj​f, whose Laplacian is the sum of DiDiDjfD_iD_iD_j fDi​Di​Dj​f. The jjjth coordinate of ∇Δf\nabla \Delta f∇Δf is DjD_jDj​ of the finite Laplacian sum, namely the sum of DjDiDifD_jD_iD_i fDj​Di​Di​f. Apply the proved third-derivative commutation term by term, then use equality of all Euclidean coordinates to obtain the full vector identity.
