Transfer Gibbs observations to the actual stationary process

References and publication status for this development

Preprint source; checked main sampling proofs

The correspondence uses arXiv:2605.31413v1, identical to the manuscript audited here. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3C^3C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The source audit distinguishes this main-theorem scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (2026). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.31413, version 1 (29 May 2026). Full paper

Stationarity is an equality of actual marginal laws. This module transfers genuine L2L^{2}L2 moments and approximation errors from the Gibbs measure to the constructed process, and converts finite coordinate Dynkin identities into vector inner products.

01Represent every square-integrable stationary observation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Rewrite the observation's Gibbs moment with the actual marginal map and pull it back to the original probability space. The resulting square-integrability estimate defines a genuine observation in the space of square-integrable random vectors modulo almost-everywhere equality. Its almost-everywhere representative is exactly the original vector function evaluated at the actual process state.

02Preserve the exact squared approximation error §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^{2}L2 inner-product formula identifies the squared norm with the integral of the squared representative norm. The proved representative identity and the stationary law map turn this into the actual Gibbs moment. Applying the same argument to differences preserves the complete squared approximation error, with no change of constants or dependence on time.

03Sum scalar weighted identities into vector duality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Each coordinate product is integrable by domination with the product of the two vector norms and the L2L^{2}L2 Cauchy inequality. Expand the Euclidean inner product as the finite coordinate sum, then interchange this finite sum and the genuine integral. The result is exactly the sum of scalar weighted expectations used by the compact-test Dynkin theorem; no separate coordinatewise bound introduces a dimension factor.
