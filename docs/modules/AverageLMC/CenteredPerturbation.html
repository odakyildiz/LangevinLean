Centered perturbations add their actual variance to transport

References and publication status for this development

Supplied manuscript; checked main sampling proofs

The exact supplied manuscript is the correspondence authority. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. The complete source inventory distinguishes this main-theorem review scope from broader auxiliary statements not reproduced in full standalone generality; literal full-paper proof coverage is not claimed.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik Karagulyan (undated manuscript). Improved Guarantees for Langevin Monte Carlo with Average Smoothness. Supplied Arxiv_version_May_26.tex; publication status not established.

A square-integrable perturbation with zero conditional mean may be added to a measurable full-gradient output. This module constructs every required coupling and proves the variance addition estimate used by SGLD.

Statement and proof

01Translate quadratic transport cost into an ordinary integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

The square of the real L2 seminorm is the integral of the squared norm. Square integrability supplies finiteness, so taking the real value cannot discard an infinite cost.

02Lift, expand and cancel the cross term §

Mathematical statement and proof

Disintegrate each finite-cost output coupling to the original input, and extend it by the independent index law. Expand the squared displacement after adding the perturbation. Fubini and the continuous linear functional given by inner product with the fixed displacement turn the cross term into the inner product with the conditional mean, which is zero. The remaining two energies are the original coupling cost and the actual perturbation second moment. Passing this bound through all finite coupling costs proves the squared Wasserstein inequality.
