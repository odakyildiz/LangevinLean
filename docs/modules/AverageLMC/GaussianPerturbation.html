Random-center smoothing and the integrated Gaussian energy bound

References and publication status for this development

Supplied manuscript; checked sampling proofs and separate source mistakes

The exact supplied manuscript is the correspondence authority. Its main average-smoothness LMC, decreasing-step, Laplacian-smooth LMC and fixed-point control-variate SGLD statements are proved for actual Gaussian sampling laws with the source constants. Extended transport endpoints cover arbitrary probability initializations. The Laplacian proof uses exactly C3 regularity and the gradient-of-Laplacian condition. Verified counterexamples to the false Gaussian-design proposition, its empirical fourth-moment event and an intermediate SGLD transient comparison have separate pages linked from the top of the overview. This module carries out the expectation and time-integration steps used in the improved one-step estimate. Independence promotes a deterministic smoothing estimate to a random center; strong convexity and orthogonality then give the exact Gaussian energy constant.

Statement and proof

01Promote smoothing to an independent random center §

Mathematical statement and proof

For independent L² vectors X and Z, their joint pushforward measure is the product of their marginal laws. The function F(x,z)=f(x+c z)−f(x) is continuous and integrable under this joint law because both arguments have finite second moment and f has quadratic growth. The pushforward identity and Fubini turn its expectation into the iterated integral. Integrate the pointwise smoothing bound over the law of X, which is a probability measure, to retain the same constant. Finally use almost-everywhere representatives to identify the pointwise sum with the sum in L².

02Integrate strong convexity with the future-noise correction §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let w be a common lower bound for the squared coupling costs between X+B(h) and the stationary comparison Y(t). Orthogonality expands the latter cost exactly as ‖X+B(t)−Y(t)‖²+2(h−t)d. Integrate the strong supporting-plane bound, using an affine upper bound a+b t for the potential expectation difference. The integrand is bounded by 2a+2mhd−mw+(2b−2md)t. Its explicit integral is 2ha+mh²d−mhw+h²b. Combine this with the exact synchronous energy identity and the endpoint coupling bound to obtain (1+mh)w≤‖X−Y(0)‖²+2ha+h²b+mh²d. Every input involving noise or stationarity is discharged for the actual Gaussian step by the canonical construction in the following modules.
