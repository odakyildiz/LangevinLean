Wasserstein and relative-entropy convergence of ULA

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.651.651.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

There are two convergence results for the unadjusted Langevin recursion. Positive strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient give an explicit quadratic-Wasserstein estimate by common-noise coupling with a stationary continuous reference. A target log-Sobolev inequality and a Lipschitz gradient give a relative-entropy recurrence from finite initial relative entropy by positive-time Gaussian interpolation.

In each proof, a local estimate is integrated or summed and then used in a scalar recurrence. The supporting results establish the probability laws, second moments or entropy integrability, density regularity, and limiting identities required at the interpolation endpoints. Strong convexity supplies distance contraction and target tail bounds in the Wasserstein proof. The log-Sobolev inequality converts Fisher dissipation into entropy contraction in the entropy proof, which does not require convexity of the potential.

01Wasserstein convergence under strong convexity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The imported modules prove Gibbs normalization and score moments, construct independent Brownian coordinates and L2L^2L2 Langevin solutions, establish Gibbs stationarity by invariant Metropolis approximation, and assemble a stationary coupling reference for every finite initial coupling. Taking the infimum of the resulting costs gives

under differentiability, m>0m>0m>0 strong convexity, LLL-Lipschitz gradient, 0<h≤2/(m+L)0<h\le2/(m+L)0<h≤2/(m+L), and finite initial W2W_2W2​. The alternative stable branch and the theorem for an square-integrable iid-Gaussian L2L^2L2 recursion are imported as well.

The strongly convex convergence theorem controls W2(μk,π)W_2(\mu_k,\pi)W2​(μk​,π) by a geometric initialization term and a step-dependent additive bound. Its proof first constructs the stationary diffusion, then couples the Gaussian chain to that diffusion. Strong convexity is used both for contraction and for the target's Gaussian tails.

02Relative-entropy convergence and its hypotheses §

Mathematical statement and proof

The imported entropy results prove the recurrence under their stated interpolation and analytic hypotheses, giving the bound Hk≤e−αhkH0+8hdL2/αH_k\le e^{-\alpha hk}H_0+8hdL^2/\alphaHk​≤e−αhkH0​+8hdL2/α. Their hypotheses are not removed by this import. The compiled library and the two source audits verify the proofs and list their logical dependencies. The module pages also display the source statements with their mathematical explanations.

The entropy proof assumes a log-Sobolev inequality and finite initial relative entropy. It derives a discrete affine recurrence from a positive-time interpolation, with endpoint limits supplying the initial value. Convexity of the potential is not required for that argument; the two convergence results therefore have distinct hypotheses and conclusions.
