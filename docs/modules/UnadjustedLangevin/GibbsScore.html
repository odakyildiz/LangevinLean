Subgaussian Gibbs score and its sharp second moment

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Differentiability, an LLL-Lipschitz gradient, and finite Gibbs normalization suffice for ∫∥∇U∥2dπ≤Ld\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\pi\le Ld∫∥∇U∥2dπ≤Ld. Strong convexity is used only by the final wrapper to derive normalization.

01gibbsWeight mul exp score le §

Mathematical statement and proof

The descent lemma gives U(x+v)≤U(x)+⟨∇U(x),v⟩+(L/2)∥v∥2U(x+v)\le U(x)+\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle+(L/2)\|v\|^2U(x+v)≤U(x)+⟨∇U(x),v⟩+(L/2)∥v∥2. Negate and exponentiate, then rearrange to e−U(x)e−⟨∇U(x),v⟩≤eL∥v∥2/2e−U(x+v)e^{-U(x)}e^{-\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle}\le e^{L\|v\|^2/2}e^{-U(x+v)}e−U(x)e−⟨∇U(x),v⟩≤eL∥v∥2/2e−U(x+v). Lean first establishes the real inequality and then embeds it in the extended nonnegative numbers.

04sq le exp add exp neg sub two §

Mathematical statement and proof

The series cosh⁡a=∑n≥0a2n/(2n)!\cosh a=\sum_{n\ge0}a^{2n}/(2n)!cosha=∑n≥0​a2n/(2n)! has nonnegative terms, so retaining its first two terms gives cosh⁡a≥1+a2/2\cosh a\ge1+a^2/2cosha≥1+a2/2. Substitute 2cosh⁡a=ea+e−a2\cosh a=e^a+e^{-a}2cosha=ea+e−a and rearrange to a2≤ea+e−a−2a^2\le e^a+e^{-a}-2a2≤ea+e−a−2. Lean uses Real.hasSum_cosh with sum_le_hasSum, explicitly proving nonnegativity of every discarded even-power term.

05integral sq le of subgaussian §

Mathematical statement and proof

Integrate t2X2≤etX+e−tX−2t^2X^2\le e^{tX}+e^{-tX}-2t2X2≤etX+e−tX−2, giving t2EX2≤2(ect2/2−1)t^2\mathbb EX^2\le2(e^{ct^2/2}-1)t2EX2≤2(ect2/2−1). 06integral norm sq gradient gibbs le §

Mathematical statement and proof

Choose an orthonormal basis (ei)i=1d(e_i)_{i=1}^d(ei​)i=1d​ and use Parseval: ∥∇U(x)∥2=∑i⟨∇U(x),ei⟩2\|\nabla U(x)\|^2=\sum_i\langle\nabla U(x),e_i\rangle^2∥∇U(x)∥2=∑i​⟨∇U(x),ei​⟩2. Each coordinate is subgaussian with proxy LLL, hence has second moment at most LLL. Finite-sum integrability permits integration term by term, yielding EπU∥∇U∥2≤Ld\mathbb E_{\pi_U}\|\nabla U\|^2\le LdEπU​​∥∇U∥2≤Ld.

07memLp gradient gibbsMeasure §

Mathematical statement and proof

The coordinate subgaussian bounds prove integrability of every squared gradient coordinate. Summing over the finite orthonormal basis proves integrability of ∥∇U∥2\|\nabla U\|^2∥∇U∥2. Lipschitz continuity provides strong measurability, and the MemLp square criterion packages those facts as ∇U∈L2(πU)\nabla U\in L^2(\pi_U)∇U∈L2(πU​).
