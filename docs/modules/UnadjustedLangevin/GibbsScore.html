Subgaussian Gibbs score and its sharp second moment

Differentiability, an LLL-Lipschitz gradient, and finite Gibbs normalization suffice for ∫∥∇U∥2dπ≤Ld\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\pi\le Ld∫∥∇U∥2dπ≤Ld. Strong convexity is used only by the final wrapper to derive normalization.

01Translate the Gibbs weight §

Mathematical statement and proof

The smooth upper model U(x+v)≤U(x)+⟨∇U(x),v⟩+L2∥v∥2U(x+v)\le U(x)+\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle+\frac L2\|v\|^2U(x+v)≤U(x)+⟨∇U(x),v⟩+2L​∥v∥2 rearranges to

e−U(x)e−⟨∇U(x),v⟩≤eL∥v∥2/2e−U(x+v).e^{-U(x)}e^{-\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle}\le e^{L\|v\|^2/2}e^{-U(x+v)}.e−U(x)e−⟨∇U(x),v⟩≤eL∥v∥2/2e−U(x+v).

Integrate, translate x+vx+vx+v, and cancel the finite positive partition function. Substitution v↦−tvv\mapsto-tvv↦−tv gives Eπet⟨∇U,v⟩≤eLt2∥v∥2/2\mathbb E_\pi e^{t\langle\nabla U,v\rangle}\le e^{Lt^2\|v\|^2/2}Eπ​et⟨∇U,v⟩≤eLt2∥v∥2/2. The finite exponential bound also proves the integrability needed for the subgaussian-MGF predicate.

02Recover a second moment from the MGF §

Mathematical statement and proof

The even power series of cosh yields a2≤ea+e−a−2a^2\le e^a+e^{-a}-2a2≤ea+e−a−2. Apply this with a=2s Xa=\sqrt{2s}\,Xa=2s​X, integrate, and use the two MGF bounds to obtain

EX2≤ecs−1s(s>0).\mathbb E X^2\le\frac{e^{cs}-1}{s}\quad(s>0).EX2≤secs−1​(s>0).

Taking s↓0s\downarrow0s↓0 gives EX2≤c\mathbb E X^2\le cEX2≤c. Subgaussian integrability supplies every moment before the integral comparisons are used.

03Sum an orthonormal basis §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each orthonormal basis vector eie_iei​, the scalar score has subgaussian parameter LLL, so E⟨∇U,ei⟩2≤L\mathbb E\langle\nabla U,e_i\rangle^2\le LE⟨∇U,ei​⟩2≤L. Parseval and the finite sum give E∥∇U∥2≤∑i=1dL=Ld\mathbb E\|\nabla U\|^2\le\sum_{i=1}^dL=LdE∥∇U∥2≤∑i=1d​L=Ld, and show ∇U∈L2(π)\nabla U\in L^2(\pi)∇U∈L2(π). The strong-convexity version inserts the finite-partition theorem from Gibbs. This translation argument does not assume a Hessian or use an unproved integration-by-parts formula for the score.
