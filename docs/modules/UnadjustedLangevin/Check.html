Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Mathematical statement and proof

After building the library, run lake env lean UnadjustedLangevin/Check.lean. Each #print axioms reports transitive logical dependencies of its named theorem; the intended output consists only of the standard Lean logical axioms, with no sorryAx and no added mathematical axiom. The audit spans the route

potential geometry  ⟶  Brownian reference and Gibbs stationarity  ⟶  W2 bounds.\text{potential geometry}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Brownian reference and Gibbs stationarity}\;\longrightarrow\;W_2\text{ bounds}.potential geometry⟶Brownian reference and Gibbs stationarity⟶W2​ bounds.

The final #check commands expose the complete endpoint types. An axiom audit confirms kernel dependencies; theorem hypotheses still govern every application, including the explicitly conditional weak-PDE interface.

This module imports the assembled strong-convexity theory and queries the kernel-visible axiom dependencies of its public endpoints. A successful run checks that the named proof terms elaborate from the imported declarations. The mathematical arguments are given on the linked theorem modules; this audit file introduces no additional mathematical hypothesis or theorem.
