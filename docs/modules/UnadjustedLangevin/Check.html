References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

The convergence statements assume the specified potential regularity, step range, and initial integrability. Deterministic contraction and local-defect estimates give the mean-square bound. The construction of independent Gaussian innovations identifies the numerical marginal laws, and taking the initial coupling infimum gives the Wasserstein bound.

Results stated with a supplied reference or a weak-equation uniqueness hypothesis retain those assumptions. An application must either provide them or use the subsequent theorems that prove the required reference properties. The convergence statements are therefore read together with their complete hypotheses.

Mathematical statement and proof

The source audit lists the logical dependencies and complete hypotheses of the convergence theorems after compilation. It covers the potential estimates, Brownian reference, Gibbs stationarity, and Wasserstein conclusions. The dependency check does not remove theorem hypotheses, including the stated uniqueness assumptions of conditional weak-equation results.

This module imports the assembled strong-convexity theory and checks the logical dependencies of its public convergence theorems. A successful run verifies the compiled proofs using the imported lemmas. The mathematical arguments are given on the linked theorem modules; this audit file introduces no additional mathematical hypothesis or theorem.

The convergence result separates deterministic geometry, probabilistic construction, and the final transport comparison. Each application must verify the hypotheses at the appropriate level: gradient regularity for contraction, target moments and stationarity for local error, and independent innovations for identifying the numerical laws. Conditional weak-equation statements retain their uniqueness hypotheses.
