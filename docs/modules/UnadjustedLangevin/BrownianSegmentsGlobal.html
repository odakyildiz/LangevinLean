Module proof · Mathematics alongside Lean

Identify chosen reference segments with one global solution

The segment construction is compatible with the unique global integral solution. This lets stationarity of a global Brownian Langevin solution prove stationarity of every chosen reference segment.

01brownianLangevinNoise increment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand the two increments from zero and subtract them. Their common [B0][B_0][B0​] term cancels, leaving 2([Ba+t]−[Ba])=Na(t)\sqrt2([B_{a+t}]-[B_a])=N_a(t)2​([Ba+t​]−[Ba​])=Na​(t). This is an algebraic cocycle identity in L2L^2L2, valid for the real-time extension as defined.

02lp brownian integral shift §

Mathematical statement and proof

Subtract the global integral equations at a+ta+ta+t and aaa. Integral additivity gives the drift integral over [a,a+t][a,a+t][a,a+t], and time translation turns it into ∫0t∇U(Ya+s)ds\int_0^t\nabla U(Y_{a+s})ds∫0t​∇U(Ya+s​)ds. The noise cocycle gives Na(t)N_a(t)Na​(t), yielding the local segment equation with initial state YaY_aYa​.

03brownianSegmentFrom eq global §

Mathematical statement and proof

The shifted global path and the selected local segment are continuous solutions of the same L2L^2L2 integral equation with the same initial value and noise input. Apply L2L^2L2 uniqueness to identify them on [0,h][0,h][0,h]. The horizon premise ensures the shifted interval lies inside the global solution interval.

04brownianKnots eq global §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct on the knot index. At zero, the global equation and zero-starting noise give the prescribed initial state. If the current knot equals YnhY_{nh}Ynh​, segment/global uniqueness makes its endpoint equal Y(n+1)hY_{(n+1)h}Y(n+1)h​, establishing the next knot identity.
