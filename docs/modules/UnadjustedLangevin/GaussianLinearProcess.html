Scalar Gaussian linear maps give jointly Gaussian processes

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.651.651.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

Let T:H→L2(P;R)T:H\to L^2(P;\mathbb R)T:H→L2(P;R) be linear and suppose every T(x)T(x)T(x) has a scalar Gaussian law. For any finite vectors xix_ixi​ and coefficients aia_iai​, linearity gives ∑iaiT(xi)=T(∑iaixi)\sum_i a_iT(x_i)=T(\sum_i a_ix_i)∑i​ai​T(xi​)=T(∑i​ai​xi​). The right side is Gaussian by assumption, proving joint Gaussianity of the finite family.

Applying this argument to the vectors indexed by finitely many process times gives a Gaussian process. The time index set may be uncountable. This conclusion specifies finite-dimensional laws and does not assert sample-path continuity or independence without the corresponding covariance conditions.

01Finite-dimensional Gaussianity of a linear family §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the finite-dimensional Gaussian criterion: every scalar linear functional of (T(fi))i(T(f_i))_i(T(fi​))i​ must be Gaussian. A finite linear combination equals T(∑iaifi)T(\sum_i a_if_i)T(∑i​ai​fi​) as an L2L^2L2 class by linearity, hence agrees almost everywhere as a random variable. The assumed Gaussianity of every T(x)T(x)T(x) proves the criterion.

For coefficients aia_iai​, linearity gives ∑iaiT(vi)=T(∑iaivi)\sum_i a_iT(v_i)=T(\sum_i a_iv_i)∑i​ai​T(vi​)=T(∑i​ai​vi​). The scalar Gaussian hypothesis applied to that one index verifies the Gaussian law of every finite linear combination.

02A Gaussian process from a Gaussian linear map §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each finite set of times, apply the preceding joint-Gaussian theorem to the corresponding finite family of vectors f(t)f(t)f(t). This verifies the finite-dimensional-law definition of joint Gaussianity of every finite collection. The index set may be uncountable; this statement alone asserts no sample-path continuity.

The finite-dimensional criterion just established applies to every finite choice of process times. Thus the family is a Gaussian process, with its joint distributions determined by its mean and covariance data.
