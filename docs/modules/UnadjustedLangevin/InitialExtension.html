Let X\mathcal XX (states) and S\mathcal SS (noise source) be measurable spaces, μ\muμ and π\piπ measures on X\mathcal XX, and QQQ a measure on S\mathcal SS. A coupling of μ\muμ and π\piπ is a measure γ\gammaγ on X×X\mathcal X\times\mathcal XX×X whose two marginals are μ\muμ and π\piπ: γ(A×X)=μ(A)\gamma(A\times\mathcal X)=\mu(A)γ(A×X)=μ(A) and γ(X×A)=π(A)\gamma(\mathcal X\times A)=\pi(A)γ(X×A)=π(A) for measurable AAA. When X\mathcal XX is a normed space, the cost of γ\gammaγ is

C(γ)=(∫∥x−y∥2 γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞],\mathcal C(\gamma)=\Bigl(\int\|x-y\|^2\,\gamma(dx,dy)\Bigr)^{1/2}\in[0,\infty],C(γ)=(∫∥x−y∥2γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞],

and the Wasserstein distance W2(μ,π)W_2(\mu,\pi)W2​(μ,π) is the infimum of C(γ)\mathcal C(\gamma)C(γ) over all couplings γ\gammaγ of μ\muμ and π\piπ.

For an arbitrary coupling γ\gammaγ we build the probability space (X×X)×S(\mathcal X\times\mathcal X)\times\mathcal S(X×X)×S with the product measure γ⊗Q\gamma\otimes Qγ⊗Q, whose points we write ((x,y),s)((x,y),s)((x,y),s): xxx will be the initial state of the algorithm, yyy the initial state of the reference process, and sss the noise. Consequently every measurable function of (x,s)(x,s)(x,s) — in particular every iterate of a noise-driven algorithm — has a law that does not depend on the chosen coupling. 01A coupling of probability measures is a probability measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let μ\muμ be a probability measure on X\mathcal XX, π\piπ a measure on X\mathcal XX, and γ\gammaγ a coupling of μ\muμ and π\piπ. Then γ\gammaγ is a probability measure.

Proof. The first marginal of γ\gammaγ is μ\muμ, so

γ(X×X)=γ(pr1−1(X))=μ(X)=1.□\gamma(\mathcal X\times\mathcal X)=\gamma\bigl(\mathrm{pr}_1^{-1}(\mathcal X)\bigr)=\mu(\mathcal X)=1.\qquad\squareγ(X×X)=γ(pr1−1​(X))=μ(X)=1.□

Only the first marginal is used; in the applications both μ\muμ and π\piπ are probability measures.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

A coupling is the structure Coupling μ π of the section on couplings, with fields joint (the measure γ\gammaγ on H × H), fst : joint.map Prod.fst = μ and snd : joint.map Prod.snd = π. In Coupling.isProbabilityMeasure the auxiliary instance states that the first marginal is a probability measure: rw [c.fst] turns it into the hypothesis on μ\muμ, closed by infer_instance. Mathlib's Measure.isProbabilityMeasure_of_map then transfers this to γ\gammaγ: if the image of a measure under an almost-everywhere measurable map (here measurable_fst.aemeasurable) is a probability measure, so is the measure.
