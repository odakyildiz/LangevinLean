Realize every initial coupling independently of the noise

Given any coupling γ\gammaγ of μ\muμ and π\piπ, extend it to γ⊗Q\gamma\otimes Qγ⊗Q. This preserves its quadratic cost and supplies independent noise without requiring an optimal coupling.

01Marginals and initial independence §

Mathematical statement and proof

A coupling whose first marginal is a probability is itself a probability, by evaluating total mass through projection. Define P=γ⊗QP=\gamma\otimes QP=γ⊗Q. Its three projections have laws γ,μ,π\gamma,\mu,\piγ,μ,π, and the noise projection has law QQQ. The product construction makes the joint initial pair independent of the noise, and in particular makes its first coordinate independent of the noise. Projecting to (X0,ω)(X_0,\omega)(X0​,ω) gives exactly μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q.

02The output law does not depend on the coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

For measurable f(x,ω)f(x,\omega)f(x,ω), composition of pushforwards gives f#Law⁡(X0,ω)=f#(μ⊗Q)f_\#\operatorname{Law}(X_0,\omega)=f_\#(\mu\otimes Q)f#​Law(X0​,ω)=f#​(μ⊗Q). Apply this to the measurable chain map. If Q=η⊗NQ=\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}Q=η⊗N, coordinate evaluation yields innovation law η\etaη, independence of all innovation coordinates and independence of the initialization from the entire innovation path.

03Preserve L² membership and cost §

Mathematical statement and proof

The projection P→γP\to\gammaP→γ is measure preserving, so ∥X0−Y0∥L2(P)\|X_0-Y_0\|_{L^2(P)}∥X0​−Y0​∥L2(P)​ equals the original coupling cost. A finite cost makes the difference square integrable. If π\piπ has a finite second moment then Y0∈L2Y_0\in L^2Y0​∈L2; adding the difference gives X0∈L2X_0\in L^2X0​∈L2. Form their L2L^2L2 classes. Their almost-everywhere representative equalities preserve the marginal laws, and the L2L^2L2 difference norm is exactly (cost⁡(γ))R(\operatorname{cost}(\gamma))_{\mathbb R}(cost(γ))R​. These identities are the bridge to infimizing the initial coupling cost.
