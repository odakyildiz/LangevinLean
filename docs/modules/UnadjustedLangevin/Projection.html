Project transport convergence into parameter RMS error

For a continuous linear contraction T:H→KT:H\to KT:H→K, reference point bbb, and equilibrium projected radius ccc, the parameter error obeys (∫∥Tx−b∥2dμ)1/2≤W2(μ,π)+c(\int\|Tx-b\|^2d\mu)^{1/2}\le W_2(\mu,\pi)+c(∫∥Tx−b∥2dμ)1/2≤W2​(μ,π)+c. This derives the estimate directly from couplings.

01Minkowski on an arbitrary coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define R(μ)=∥Tx−b∥L2(μ)R(\mu)=\|Tx-b\|_{L^2(\mu)}R(μ)=∥Tx−b∥L2(μ)​ as an extended norm, and its real-valued version by conversion. The operator bound ∥T∥≤1\|T\|\le1∥T∥≤1 gives ∥T(X−Y)∥2≤∥X−Y∥2\|T(X-Y)\|_2\le\|X-Y\|_2∥T(X−Y)∥2​≤∥X−Y∥2​. Decompose TX−b=T(X−Y)+(TY−b)TX-b=T(X-Y)+(TY-b)TX−b=T(X−Y)+(TY−b), apply Minkowski under each coupling, and use the marginal identities to obtain R(μ)≤cost⁡(γ)+R(π)R(\mu)\le\operatorname{cost}(\gamma)+R(\pi)R(μ)≤cost(γ)+R(π). Infimizing over the nonempty coupling family gives R(μ)≤W2(μ,π)+R(π)R(\mu)\le W_2(\mu,\pi)+R(\pi)R(μ)≤W2​(μ,π)+R(π).

02Finiteness and the second-moment interpretation §

Mathematical statement and proof

If the transport distance is finite and R(π)≤ofReal⁡(c)R(\pi)\le\operatorname{ofReal}(c)R(π)≤ofReal(c), the preceding upper bound proves R(μ)<∞R(\mu)<\inftyR(μ)<∞. With this finite-norm premise, the L2L^2L2 norm formula gives RR(μ)=∫∥Tx−b∥2dμR_{\mathbb R}(\mu)=\sqrt{\int\|Tx-b\|^2d\mu}RR​(μ)=∫∥Tx−b∥2dμ​. In particular, explicit L2L^2L2 membership and the second-moment bound ∫∥Tx−b∥2dπ≤c2\int\|Tx-b\|^2d\pi\le c^2∫∥Tx−b∥2dπ≤c2, c≥0c\ge0c≥0, imply R(π)≤ofReal⁡(c)R(\pi)\le\operatorname{ofReal}(c)R(π)≤ofReal(c).

03Convert the finite bound to real quantities §

Mathematical statement and proof

Selecting a finite coupling ensures that the infimum family is nonempty. The extended inequality R(μ)≤W2(μ,π)+ofReal⁡(c)R(\mu)\le W_2(\mu,\pi)+\operatorname{ofReal}(c)R(μ)≤W2​(μ,π)+ofReal(c) has a finite right side. Monotonicity of conversion to R\mathbb RR, together with c≥0c\ge0c≥0, yields RR(μ)≤W2,R(μ,π)+cR_{\mathbb R}(\mu)\le W_{2,\mathbb R}(\mu,\pi)+cRR​(μ)≤W2,R​(μ,π)+c. Thus neither the real integral nor the real-valued distance can mask an infinite quantity.
