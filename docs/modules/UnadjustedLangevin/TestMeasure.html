Compactly supported smooth tests determine a finite measure

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

On a finite-dimensional real normed space, finite measures agreeing on all compactly supported smooth tests are equal. Agreement on compactly supported C2C^2C2 tests is sufficient as well.

Mathematical statement and proof

Let ξ=μ+ν\xi=\mu+\nuξ=μ+ν, so both finite measures are absolutely continuous with respect to ξ\xiξ. Write their Radon–Nikodym densities as f=dμ/dξf=d\mu/d\xif=dμ/dξ and g=dν/dξg=d\nu/d\xig=dν/dξ. Equality of the given smooth compact test integrals becomes ∫φf dξ=∫φg dξ\int\varphi f\,d\xi=\int\varphi g\,d\xi∫φfdξ=∫φgdξ.

The smooth-test uniqueness theorem ae_eq_of_integral_contDiff_smul_eq proves f=gf=gf=g almost everywhere, using their locally integrable real values. Both derivatives are finite almost everywhere because the measures are finite, so equality of real values lifts back to equality of the extended densities. Reconstructing each measure as ξ.withDensity of its Radon–Nikodym derivative gives μ=ν\mu=\nuμ=ν.

02measure eq of integral contDiff two eq §

Mathematical statement and proof

Every smooth test is C2C^2C2, so the assumed equality on compactly supported C2C^2C2 functions applies to the smooth test class used by the preceding measure-determination theorem. Supply that restricted equality and obtain equality of the finite Borel measures. No density or absolute continuity is needed.
