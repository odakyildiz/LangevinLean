Convergence under stationary Brownian reference laws

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Construct square-mean continuous Langevin reference segments from the supplied Brownian coordinates and potential assumptions. Assume explicitly that every segment has Gibbs law at the stated times. Brownian covariance bounds their noise increments, and the Gibbs score moment bounds their drifts.

The resulting local defect is of order h3/2h^{3/2}h3/2. Combining its explicit constant with deterministic contraction gives the affine mean-square recurrence and the stated convergence bound. The initial error in this result is the supplied joint initialization's L2L^2L2 distance. Segment stationarity remains a hypothesis here; the later construction proves stationarity and identifies the Gaussian transition laws.

Mathematical statement and proof

Build the reference from Brownian segments using the displayed all-time Gibbs-law premise. Strong convexity proves finite partition and m≤Lm\le Lm≤L, while Brownian laws prove the noise radius and the Gibbs score theorem proves its drift bound. Apply the analytic L2L^2L2 theorem with the supplied joint initialization, yielding its initial class distance. This intermediate theorem retains its explicit stationarity premise.

Stationarity is an explicit hypothesis of the supplied segments. Once that property holds, Brownian covariance and the Gibbs score moment give all local-error constants automatically. A later construction proves stationarity and identifies the independent Gaussian chain, producing the unconditional sampling theorem.
