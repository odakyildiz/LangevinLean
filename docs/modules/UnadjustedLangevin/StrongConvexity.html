We begin with the deterministic geometry behind synchronous Langevin coupling. Throughout this part, ⟨⋅,⋅⟩\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle⟨⋅,⋅⟩ and ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\|∥⋅∥ are the real inner product and its norm. Strong convexity uses the normalization m/2m/2m/2: the function v↦f(v)−m2∥v∥2v\mapsto f(v)-\frac m2\|v\|^2v↦f(v)−2m​∥v∥2 is convex.

The formal normalization is StrongConvexOn Set.univ m f, meaning convexity after subtracting m∥x∥2/2m\|x\|^2/2m∥x∥2/2. fderiv ℝ f x is the continuous linear derivative; gradient f x is its Hilbert-space representative, so Df(x)[v]=⟨∇f(x),v⟩Df(x)[v]=\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangleDf(x)[v]=⟨∇f(x),v⟩. The proofs explicitly move between these two representations.

01The supporting-plane inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let HHH be a real inner-product space and let f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R be differentiable and convex on all of HHH. For any x,y∈Hx,y\in Hx,y∈H,

f(x)+Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y).f(x)+Df(x)[y-x]\le f(y).f(x)+Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y).

Restrict fff to the affine line ℓ(t)=x+t(y−x)\ell(t)=x+t(y-x)ℓ(t)=x+t(y−x). Since affine composition preserves convexity, φ=f∘ℓ\varphi=f\circ\ellφ=f∘ℓ is convex on R\mathbb RR, with φ(0)=f(x)\varphi(0)=f(x)φ(0)=f(x) and φ(1)=f(y)\varphi(1)=f(y)φ(1)=f(y).

The derivative of the line is its constant direction:

ℓ′(0)=y−x.\ell'(0)=y-x.ℓ′(0)=y−x.

By the chain rule, the derivative of the restricted function is

φ′(0)=Df(x)[y−x].\varphi'(0)=Df(x)[y-x].φ′(0)=Df(x)[y−x].

For a differentiable convex function, the derivative at the left endpoint is bounded by the secant slope to a later point. Applying this at 0<10<10<1 gives

Df(x)[y−x]≤φ(1)−φ(0)1−0.Df(x)[y-x]\le\frac{\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)}{1-0}.Df(x)[y−x]≤1−0φ(1)−φ(0)​.

Evaluate the two endpoints and rearrange:

Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y)−f(x),f(x)+Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y).Df(x)[y-x]\le f(y)-f(x),\qquad f(x)+Df(x)[y-x]\le f(y).Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y)−f(x),f(x)+Df(x)[y−x]≤f(y).

02Strong monotonicity of the gradient §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let HHH be a real Hilbert space. If f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R is differentiable and mmm-strongly convex, where m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, then for every x,y∈Hx,y\in Hx,y∈H,

m∥x−y∥2≤⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩.m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle.m∥x−y∥2≤⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩.

Remove the strong-convexity quadratic. Set

g(v)=f(v)−m2∥v∥2.g(v)=f(v)-\frac m2\|v\|^2.g(v)=f(v)−2m​∥v∥2.

The definition of strong convexity says precisely that ggg is convex.

The squared norm has derivative D∥v∥2[u]=2⟨v,u⟩D\|v\|^2[u]=2\langle v,u\rangleD∥v∥2[u]=2⟨v,u⟩. Consequently ggg is differentiable and

Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]−m⟨v,u⟩.Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]-m\langle v,u\rangle.Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]−m⟨v,u⟩.

Apply the supporting-plane inequality in both directions, and substitute the derivative just computed:

g(x)+Dg(x)[y−x]≤g(y),g(y)+Dg(y)[x−y]≤g(x).g(x)+Dg(x)[y-x]\le g(y),\qquad g(y)+Dg(y)[x-y]\le g(x).g(x)+Dg(x)[y−x]≤g(y),g(y)+Dg(y)[x−y]≤g(x).

Express the derivative of fff using its gradient. The two inequalities become

f(x)−m2∥x∥2+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩−m⟨x,y−x⟩≤f(y)−m2∥y∥2,f(y)−m2∥y∥2+⟨∇f(y),x−y⟩−m⟨y,x−y⟩≤f(x)−m2∥x∥2.\begin{aligned}f(x)-\tfrac m2\|x\|^2+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle-m\langle x,y-x\rangle&\le f(y)-\tfrac m2\|y\|^2,\\ f(y)-\tfrac m2\|y\|^2+\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle-m\langle y,x-y\rangle&\le f(x)-\tfrac m2\|x\|^2.\end{aligned}f(x)−2m​∥x∥2+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩−m⟨x,y−x⟩f(y)−2m​∥y∥2+⟨∇f(y),x−y⟩−m⟨y,x−y⟩​≤f(y)−2m​∥y∥2,≤f(x)−2m​∥x∥2.​

Add these inequalities. The function values and quadratic values cancel. Using ⟨x−y,x−y⟩=∥x−y∥2\langle x-y,x-y\rangle=\|x-y\|^2⟨x−y,x−y⟩=∥x−y∥2, what remains is

0≤⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩−m∥x−y∥2.0\le\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle-m\|x-y\|^2.0≤⟨∇f(x)−∇f(y),x−y⟩−m∥x−y∥2.

This is the required monotonicity inequality.

03The quadratic lower model §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let HHH be a real Hilbert space, f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R differentiable, and m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R. If fff is mmm-strongly convex on all of HHH, then for every x,y∈Hx,y\in Hx,y∈H,

f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+m2∥y−x∥2≤f(y).f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac m2\|y-x\|^2\le f(y).f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+2m​∥y−x∥2≤f(y).

Set g(v)=f(v)−m2∥v∥2g(v)=f(v)-\frac m2\|v\|^2g(v)=f(v)−2m​∥v∥2. Strong convexity makes ggg convex.

Differentiate this shifted potential:

Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]−m⟨v,u⟩.Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]-m\langle v,u\rangle.Dg(v)[u]=Df(v)[u]−m⟨v,u⟩.

In particular, ggg is differentiable on HHH.

Its supporting-plane inequality at xxx, together with Df(x)[u]=⟨∇f(x),u⟩Df(x)[u]=\langle\nabla f(x),u\rangleDf(x)[u]=⟨∇f(x),u⟩, yields

f(x)−m2∥x∥2+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩−m⟨x,y−x⟩≤f(y)−m2∥y∥2.f(x)-\frac m2\|x\|^2+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle-m\langle x,y-x\rangle\le f(y)-\frac m2\|y\|^2.f(x)−2m​∥x∥2+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩−m⟨x,y−x⟩≤f(y)−2m​∥y∥2.

Collect the quadratic correction by the identity

∥y∥2−∥x∥2−2⟨x,y−x⟩=∥y−x∥2.\|y\|^2-\|x\|^2-2\langle x,y-x\rangle=\|y-x\|^2.∥y∥2−∥x∥2−2⟨x,y−x⟩=∥y−x∥2.

Moving terms gives the desired lower model.
