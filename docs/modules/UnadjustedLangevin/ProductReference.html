For each finite-cost initial coupling ccc, form its product with a fixed independent noise source. The numerical input law is μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q on every such extension, so the numerical output law is independent of ccc. The initial class distance equals the cost of ccc. Supply a reference on each extension with the stated integral equation, stationary law, and common innovations.

Apply the mean-square convergence estimate on every extension. Since its output law and constants are independent of ccc, take the infimum of the initial costs to obtain W2(μ,π)W_2(\mu,\pi)W2​(μ,π). One finite-cost initial coupling first proves that the output transport distance is finite. The two stable step ranges then give the stated initial-error factors and additive bounds.

01The pointwise numerical update §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the ordinary Euler update Fh(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2hzF_h(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}zFh​(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h​z in an arbitrary Hilbert-space presentation. It is the pointwise function whose lifted composition gives the L2L^2L2 numerical chain.

The update uses one current state and one innovation vector. Its additive Gaussian coefficient is 2h\sqrt{2h}2h​, corresponding to covariance 2hI2hI2hI when the innovation is standard. Defining this ordinary map before lifting it to L2L^2L2 specifies the update whose law is computed by measurable pushforwards.

02Measurability of the update §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Lipschitz gradient is continuous. Compose it with the first coordinate projection and combine the remaining continuous scalar and vector operations. The uncurried map (x,z)↦Fh(x,z)(x,z)\mapsto F_h(x,z)(x,z)↦Fh​(x,z) is therefore measurable.

Lipschitzness of the gradient gives continuity of x↦∇U(x)x\mapsto\nabla U(x)x↦∇U(x). Both coordinate projections on the state–innovation product are continuous, and fixed scalar combinations preserve continuity. Thus the entire update is measurable for any fixed step. No coupling or reference hypothesis enters this elementary regularity fact.

03Square-integrability of the product noise §

Mathematical statement and proof

The noise projection from c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q to QQQ is measure preserving because the coupling is a probability. Pull back the supplied L2(Q)L^2(Q)L2(Q) noise function along this projection. Its square integrability is unchanged.

For a source innovation WnW_nWn​, the extended variable is Wn(s)W_n(s)Wn​(s). Its squared norm integral under c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q equals c(E×E)∫∥Wn∥2dQc(E\times E)\int\|W_n\|^2dQc(E×E)∫∥Wn​∥2dQ, hence the original finite second moment. The added initial coordinates therefore do not alter innovation integrability, just as they do not alter its distribution.

04The product-space noise class §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the preceding square-integrability proof to form the class of ((x,y),s)↦Wn(s)((x,y),s)\mapsto W_n(s)((x,y),s)↦Wn​(s) on the product extension. Both the numerical chain and reference use this same L2L^2L2 innovation.

The lifted innovation is the square-integrable variable represented by the pulled-back source function. It is fixed before selecting any reference trajectory, so both numerical and reference constructions can be required to use precisely this innovation. This choice is what later makes the algorithm's output law independent of the second initial marginal and coupling dependence.

05Its representative identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The selected noise representative equals the pulled-back source innovation Wn(s)W_n(s)Wn​(s) almost everywhere. Thus its arithmetic inside the square-integrable update agrees with the ordinary pointwise Gaussian update, and its coordinate laws are those of the original innovation.

Almost surely, evaluating the constructed innovation gives exactly the original source innovation evaluated at sss. Consequently its scalar arithmetic in the square-integrable Euler update agrees with the raw pointwise formula. These representative equalities also permit Gaussian coordinate laws and path independence to be transferred to the exact random variables used by the numerical chain.

07The representative of the product chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct through the lifted chain and use the representative identities for initial state, product noise, lifted drift, addition, and scalar multiplication. At each fixed index the result agrees almost everywhere with the ordinary recursion driven by xxx and W(s)W(s)W(s).

At each successor, replace the lifted gradient, state, and innovation by their pointwise representatives on a common full-measure set. The resulting equation is exactly the ordinary Euler recursion. Induction proves agreement for every fixed finite index. This permits its law to be computed from the raw coordinates (x,s)(x,s)(x,s), which have already been identified independently of the coupling.

08The output law of the product chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

Replace the L2L^2L2 chain by its almost-everywhere pointwise recursion. That recursion depends only on the algorithm's initial coordinate and noise sample, so the pushforward law under the independent product extension gives its coupling-independent law. The result is exactly the numerical law on the product construction.

The pointwise recursion ignores the reference coordinate yyy. Under the extension, the law of the remaining pair (x,s)(x,s)(x,s) is always μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q. Pushforward composition therefore gives the same product-input output law for every finite initial coupling. Almost-sure replacement transfers this identity to the analytic chain whose errors are estimated in L2L^2L2.

09The product probability measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

A coupling of the initial probability with the reference measure is itself a probability, and its product with the fixed probability noise source is a probability. The total mass is therefore one on every extension.

Every initial coupling has mass one by either marginal identity, and the source probability has mass one. Their product is consequently normalized. This fact is established separately on each extension and allows the same probability-space estimates to be reused uniformly over all couplings. It does not presume finite coupling cost or any invariant law of the numerical update.

10A reference on the independent product space §

Mathematical statement and proof

For every finite-cost initial coupling, the hypothesis supplies an integral reference on that coupling's product extension, with its initial state matching the second coordinate. Algorithm output laws and initial costs are consequences of the product construction; convergence is not an additional assumption.

The required data are the following: on each finite-cost extension, provide continuous joined segments with the integral equation, matching Euler noise and target-law moment bounds. The numerical law and initial cost are already consequences of the product construction. Thus the reference hypothesis specifies these analytic data, while the convergence estimate is proved from those data rather than included among them.

11Finite output transport distance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Finite initial Wasserstein distance supplies at least one finite-cost initial coupling. On its product extension, the numerical and reference variables belong to L2L^2L2, giving a finite-cost output coupling. Their established laws identify its marginals with the common output law and π\piπ, hence the output Wasserstein distance is finite.

Finite initial transport guarantees the existence of at least one finite-cost initial coupling. Its analytic numerical and reference variables are in L2L^2L2, so their difference has finite norm and their joint law has finite quadratic cost. Its first marginal is the common numerical output and its second is π\piπ. Therefore output transport is finite before the real-valued numerical inequality is stated.

12The small-step bound from a product reference §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each finite-cost initial coupling ccc, apply the analytic L2L^2L2 Dalalyan theorem on c⊗Qc\otimes Qc⊗Q. The initial class-distance theorem identifies its initial error exactly with the real coupling cost, and the common output-law theorem identifies its numerical law.

The resulting bound holds for every finite ccc, with coefficient (1−mh)n≥0(1-mh)^n\ge0(1−mh)n≥0. Taking the infimum of their costs gives W2(μ,π)W_2(\mu,\pi)W2​(μ,π), without selecting an optimal coupling. Output finiteness is proved first so the final real-valued inequality cannot conceal infinity.

For each initial coupling cost CCC, synchronous analysis gives output distance at most (1−mh)nC+(33/20)(L/m)hd(1-mh)^nC+(33/20)(L/m)\sqrt{hd}(1−mh)nC+(33/20)(L/m)hd​. The multiplicative coefficient is nonnegative and the other quantities do not depend on the chosen coupling. Taking the cost infimum therefore replaces CCC by initial W2W_2W2​. Finiteness was established separately, so this operation is an inequality between finite real distances.
