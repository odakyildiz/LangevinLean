Normalized increments are independent standard Gaussians

For a pre-Brownian coordinate and positive grid spacing hhh, Zn=(B(n+1)h−Bnh)/hZ_n=(B_{(n+1)h}-B_{nh})/\sqrt hZn​=(B(n+1)h​−Bnh​)/h​ has standard normal law. Across independent coordinate paths, the entire coordinate-time family is independent.

01Normalize and compute the scalar moment §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Brownian increment has variance ∣(n+1)h−nh∣=h|(n+1)h-nh|=h∣(n+1)h−nh∣=h. Scaling by 1/h1/\sqrt h1/h​ gives variance one and mean zero. Transfer the scalar Gaussian L2L^2L2 membership and second moment through that law to get Zn∈L2Z_n\in L^2Zn​∈L2 and EZn2=1\mathbb EZ_n^2=1EZn2​=1. The division requires h>0h>0h>0.

02Combine time and coordinate independence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Monotonicity of n↦nhn\mapsto nhn↦nh makes the grid increments independent, and coordinatewise division preserves independence. For a family of rows, independence of the row paths and independence within each row imply independence of the flattened family (i,n)↦Zi,n(i,n)\mapsto Z_{i,n}(i,n)↦Zi,n​. The auxiliary theorem first replaces almost-measurable countable paths by measurable versions, applies the product-index independence theorem, and transfers back almost everywhere. Apply it to normalized increments; independence of full coordinate paths also implies the needed grid-path independence.

03The entire innovation law §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a countable spatial index, the coordinate-time path is almost measurable. An independent family with all marginals N(0,1)\mathcal N(0,1)N(0,1) has the infinite product law, so Law⁡((Zi,n)i,n)=⨂(i,n)N(0,1)\operatorname{Law}((Z_{i,n})_{i,n})=\bigotimes_{(i,n)}\mathcal N(0,1)Law((Zi,n​)i,n​)=⨂(i,n)​N(0,1).
