01langevin integral stability §

Mathematical statement and proof

Subtract the two integral equations so that their common additive input cancels. The integral norm inequality and Lipschitz drift give u(t)≤∥x−y∥+L∫0tu(s)dsu(t)\le\|x-y\|+L\int_0^t u(s)dsu(t)≤∥x−y∥+L∫0t​u(s)ds, where u(t)=∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥u(t)=\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|u(t)=∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥ is continuous. Grönwall's inequality yields u(t)≤∥x−y∥eLtu(t)\le\|x-y\|e^{Lt}u(t)≤∥x−y∥eLt. All time integrals are justified by continuity on the compact horizon.

02langevin integral unique §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set the two initial states equal in the stability estimate. The right side becomes 0eLt=00e^{Lt}=00eLt=0, forcing ∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥=0\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|=0∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥=0 for each t∈[0,h]t\in[0,h]t∈[0,h]. Norm-zero equivalence gives equality of the vectors, packaged in Lean as EqOn on the interval.

03lp langevin integral stability §

Mathematical statement and proof

Specialize Banach-space stability to the actual lifted gradient on L2(P;K)L^2(P;K)L2(P;K). Its Lipschitz theorem supplies the required constant, so ∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥2≤eLt∥Y(0)−Z(0)∥2\|Y(t)-Z(t)\|_2\le e^{Lt}\|Y(0)-Z(0)\|_2∥Y(t)−Z(t)∥2​≤eLt∥Y(0)−Z(0)∥2​. This compares solutions with a common L2L^2L2-noise input and possibly different initial random variables.

04lp langevin integral unique §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the same-input uniqueness theorem in L2(P;K)L^2(P;K)L2(P;K). Equality here is equality of L2L^2L2 classes for each time, hence almost-everywhere equality of representatives at each fixed time. The conclusion does not by itself select one exceptional null set valid simultaneously at every real time.
