Propagation of finite error moments

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

A finite initial coupling error and finite local defects suffice to justify all real transport estimates. Common-noise contraction propagates these finite moments to every iterate.

These estimates justify the Bochner integrals used in the probabilistic local-defect bound. The dominating functions are built from the stated score and noise norms, and the measure-theoretic steps establish measurability together with finite integral, rather than relying on an unproved finite integral.

The key information is about the difference of the coupled states. A domination ∥A∥≤q∥D∥\|A\|\le q\|D\|∥A∥≤q∥D∥ transfers the finite moment of DDD to AAA, and addition of the finite-moment defect then transfers it to the next error. Thus moment propagation does not require separate bounds on every noise term in the error recursion: the shared noise has already canceled. Contractivity q<1q<1q<1 is needed for convergence, but finiteness propagates for every finite nonnegative qqq.

01Finite moments survive a contracted update §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let p∈[0,+∞]p\in[0,+\infty]p∈[0,+∞], q∈Rq\in\mathbb Rq∈R, and D,A,V,E:Ω→HD,A,V,E:\Omega\to HD,A,V,E:Ω→H. Assume AAA is almost everywhere strongly measurable, E=A−VE=A-VE=A−V almost everywhere, and ∥A∥≤q∥D∥\|A\|\le q\|D\|∥A∥≤q∥D∥ almost everywhere. If D,V∈Lp(P;H)D,V\in L^p(P;H)D,V∈Lp(P;H), then E∈Lp(P;H)E\in L^p(P;H)E∈Lp(P;H).

The domination transfers the finite LpL^pLp moment of DDD to AAA.

The difference A−VA-VA−V belongs to LpL^pLp. Almost everywhere equality then transfers this property to EEE.

The comparison of norms supplies finite ppp-moment size, while almost-everywhere strong measurability supplies the measurability part of membership. Combining both is necessary: a numerical upper bound alone would not define a valid Bochner-integrable random variable. The equality E=A−VE=A-VE=A−V then transfers membership through the algebra of LpL^pLp.

Induct on nnn. The initial case is the assumed finite initial error; for the successor, assume Xn−Yn∈L2X_n-Y_n\in L^2Xn​−Yn​∈L2.

Set A=Th(U,h,Xn,ξn)−Th(U,h,Yn,ξn)A=T_h(U,h,X_n,\xi_n)-T_h(U,h,Y_n,\xi_n)A=Th​(U,h,Xn​,ξn​)−Th​(U,h,Yn​,ξn​), the update difference before adding the reference defect.

The gradient and both update inputs are measurable, so the update difference AAA is measurable and hence almost everywhere strongly measurable.

Subtract the recursive updates on the full-measure set where the reference equation holds. This gives Xn+1−Yn+1=A−VnX_{n+1}-Y_{n+1}=A-V_nXn+1​−Yn+1​=A−Vn​.

The common-noise contraction gives ∥A∥≤q∥Xn−Yn∥\|A\|\le q\|X_n-Y_n\|∥A∥≤q∥Xn​−Yn​∥ pointwise, hence almost everywhere.

Apply the finite-moment lemma to the induction hypothesis and Vn∈L2V_n\in L^2Vn​∈L2, proving Xn+1−Yn+1∈L2X_{n+1}-Y_{n+1}\in L^2Xn+1​−Yn+1​∈L2.

At step nnn, the shared-noise update difference is dominated by q∥Xn−Yn∥q\|X_n-Y_n\|q∥Xn​−Yn​∥. The assumed defect has a finite second moment, so their difference does as well. Induction repeats this argument for every finite index. The proof establishes moments even in regimes with q≥1q\ge1q≥1; the strict upper bound on qqq is used only later for decay.
