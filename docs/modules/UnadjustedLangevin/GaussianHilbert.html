Construct an isonormal Gaussian family from product coordinates

An explicit product of standard normal coordinates realizes a linear isometry I:H→L2I:H\to L^2I:H→L2 with I(x)∼N(0,∥x∥2)I(x)\sim\mathcal N(0,\|x\|^2)I(x)∼N(0,∥x∥2) and covariance E[I(x)I(y)]=⟨x,y⟩\mathbb E[I(x)I(y)]=\langle x,y\rangleE[I(x)I(y)]=⟨x,y⟩.

01Independent orthonormal Gaussian coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

The product-probability construction gives total mass one for Q=⨂i∈IN(0,1)Q=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal I}\mathcal N(0,1)Q=⨂i∈I​N(0,1), since every scalar factor has mass one. On this space, evaluation ω↦ωi\omega\mapsto\omega_iω↦ωi​ has standard normal law and mean zero. Regard it as an L2L^2L2 class ZiZ_iZi​; the representative agrees with evaluation almost everywhere. Independence gives EZiZj=0\mathbb E Z_iZ_j=0EZi​Zj​=0 for distinct indices, while the scalar Gaussian second moment gives EZi2=1\mathbb E Z_i^2=1EZi2​=1. Thus (Zi)(Z_i)(Zi​) is an orthonormal family in L2(Q)L^2(Q)L2(Q).

02Square-summable synthesis and an arbitrary Hilbert basis §

Mathematical statement and proof

The orthogonal-family extension defines an isometry S:ℓ2(I)→L2(Q)S:\ell^2(\mathcal I)\to L^2(Q)S:ℓ2(I)→L2(Q) with S(a)=∑iaiZiS(a)=\sum_i a_iZ_iS(a)=∑i​ai​Zi​, convergence in L2L^2L2. For a Hilbert basis (bi)(b_i)(bi​) of HHH, compose its coordinate isometry with SSS: I(x)=S((⟨bi,x⟩)i)I(x)=S((\langle b_i,x\rangle)_i)I(x)=S((⟨bi​,x⟩)i​). The two sum theorems record these convergent series explicitly.

03Mean and variance commute with L² limits §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expectation is the continuous functional X↦⟨1,X⟩L2X\mapsto\langle1,X\rangle_{L^2}X↦⟨1,X⟩L2​. Every finite linear combination of the centered coordinates has expectation zero; passing to the convergent sum gives ES(a)=EI(x)=0\mathbb ES(a)=\mathbb EI(x)=0ES(a)=EI(x)=0. For a centered X∈L2X\in L^2X∈L2, the definition of variance simplifies to Var⁡(X)=∫X2dQ=∥X∥22\operatorname{Var}(X)=\int X^2dQ=\|X\|_2^2Var(X)=∫X2dQ=∥X∥22​.

04Identify the Gaussian law and covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induction identifies each finite L2L^2L2 sum with ∑iaiωi\sum_i a_i\omega_i∑i​ai​ωi​ almost everywhere. Independence and Gaussian stability under finite linear combinations make it Gaussian; its mean is zero and variance is its squared L2L^2L2 norm. The Gaussian closure theorem for L2L^2L2 limits therefore gives S(a)∼N(0,∥a∥2)S(a)\sim\mathcal N(0,\|a\|^2)S(a)∼N(0,∥a∥2), then I(x)∼N(0,∥x∥2)I(x)\sim\mathcal N(0,\|x\|^2)I(x)∼N(0,∥x∥2). Finally centered covariance is the L2L^2L2 inner product, and the isometry gives Cov⁡(I(x),I(y))=⟨x,y⟩\operatorname{Cov}(I(x),I(y))=\langle x,y\rangleCov(I(x),I(y))=⟨x,y⟩.
