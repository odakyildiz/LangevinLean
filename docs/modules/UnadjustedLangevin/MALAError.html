The result consists of a trapezoid error for the potential increment and a difference of squared gradient norms. 05A trapezoid bound for the potential increment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the smooth upper and lower first-order models for the potential increment at the two endpoints. Compare the increment with the average endpoint directional derivative, and use the Lipschitz bound on the derivative difference. The resulting trapezoid error is at most L∥y−x∥2L\|y-x\|^2L∥y−x∥2; no second derivative of UUU is assumed.

Restrict UUU to the segment x+tdx+tdx+td. Its derivative is ⟨∇U(x+td),d⟩\langle\nabla U(x+td),d\rangle⟨∇U(x+td),d⟩, whose variation is at most L∣t−s∣∥d∥2L|t-s|\|d\|^2L∣t−s∣∥d∥2. Integrating this variation bounds the difference between the true increment and the endpoint trapezoid, using only a Lipschitz first derivative.
