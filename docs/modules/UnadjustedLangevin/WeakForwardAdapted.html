The continuous integral solution remains in a complete closed subspace if the initial point and additive input belong to that subspace and the drift maps it into itself. Solve within the subspace, include that solution in the ambient space, and apply uniqueness there. Continuous linear inclusion commutes with the Bochner integral.

The square-integrable variables measurable for a specified sub-sigma-algebra form a closed subspace. Composition with the Lipschitz drift preserves it. Taking the sub-sigma-algebra generated by initialization and the noise up to TTT therefore proves measurability of the solution from those data. This is derived from the integral equation and does not assume adaptedness in advance.

02Measurability of the lifted drift §

Mathematical statement and proof

A Lipschitz map is continuous, so composing it with an almost-everywhere strongly measurable XXX preserves that measurability for the specified sub-sigma-algebra. The representative of the lifted drift agrees almost everywhere with this composition. Transport across that equality proves the result independently of the representative selected for the L2L^2L2 class.

Composition preserves measurability for the chosen smaller sigma-algebra because the drift is continuous. Almost-everywhere representative equality transfers this property to the lifted class independently of the representative chosen for it.

03Past measurability of the integral solution §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^2L2 variables measurable for a sub-sigma-algebra form a complete closed submodule. The preceding composition theorem makes that submodule invariant under the lifted drift. Apply submodule preservation using measurability of the initialization and every noise value up to TTT; the integral solution then belongs to the same submodule at each time.

The past-measurable classes form a closed subspace of L2L^2L2. Initialization and the relevant noise values belong to it, so the invariant-subspace principle applies to the integral solution up to the chosen horizon.

04The sigma-algebra generated by past observations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the past sigma-algebra as the smallest sigma-algebra making the initialization and every noise observation B(t)B(t)B(t), 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T0≤t≤T, measurable. It is the join of their generated sigma-algebras, so it contains exactly the information needed for the stated past-measurability conclusion.

The join is the smallest sigma-algebra making the initialization and all noise observations up to time TTT measurable. Thus it expresses exactly the information allowed in the adaptedness conclusion.

05Inclusion in the ambient sigma-algebra §

Mathematical statement and proof

Every generator of the past is measurable in the ambient sigma-algebra. The universal property of a supremum of sigma-algebras therefore gives FT≤F\mathcal F_T\le\mathcal FFT​≤F. This inclusion is required to use the closed L2L^2L2 subspace of variables measurable from the past.

Each generating sigma-algebra is contained in the ambient one because the original observations are measurable. Taking their join preserves that inclusion, so the corresponding closed subspace of L2L^2L2 is defined.

06Measurability of the initial state from the past §

Mathematical statement and proof

The sigma-algebra generated by the initial variable is one summand of the defining join for FT\mathcal F_TFT​. Its inclusion makes the initial variable measurable for FT\mathcal F_TFT​, and the measurable representative of an L2L^2L2 variable supplies the almost-everywhere strong-measurability formulation used by the submodule theorem.

Initialization is included as an explicit generator of the past. This retains any dependence present in the initial random state while excluding information from future innovations.

07Measurability of every previous noise value §

Mathematical statement and proof

For t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T], the sigma-algebra generated by B(t)B(t)B(t) is included in the indexed join defining the past. Measurability of the representative with respect to its generated sigma-algebra then extends along this inclusion, giving B(t)B(t)B(t)'s measurability from FT\mathcal F_TFT​.

For t≤Tt\le Tt≤T, the observation B(t)B(t)B(t) is one of the generators. Its measurability follows by the inclusion of that generated sigma-algebra in the joined past.

08Adaptedness to the natural past §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the sub-sigma-algebra solution theorem to the explicitly generated FT\mathcal F_TFT​. The three preceding lemmas verify the ambient inclusion, initialization, and noise-measurability hypotheses. Thus every solution value up to TTT is measurable from initialization and past noise, with no adaptedness assumption added to the solution.

Applying preservation with the constructed past shows that the solution uses only initialization and previous noise values. This is derived from the integral equation and uniqueness, rather than assumed as a separate solution property.
