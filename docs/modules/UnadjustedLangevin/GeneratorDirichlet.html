For compactly supported C2C^2C2 tests φ,ψ\varphi,\psiφ,ψ, the generator obeys ∫φLψ dπ=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπ\int\varphi\mathcal L\psi\,d\pi=-\int\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle d\pi∫φLψdπ=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπ. This provides the symmetric Dirichlet form at the test-function level.

The identity ∫φLψ dπU=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπU\int\varphi\mathcal L\psi\,d\pi_U=-\int\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle d\pi_U∫φLψdπU​=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπU​ is obtained by moving one derivative onto the weighted test. The derivative of the Gibbs weight supplies exactly the potential-drift term, so no term remains unaccounted for. Compact support makes the integration by parts global without a boundary contribution. Symmetry follows from symmetry of the inner product, and choosing ψ=φ\psi=\varphiψ=φ identifies the nonnegative energy whose negative is the generator quadratic form.

01Differentiating the Gibbs-weighted test §

Mathematical statement and proof

Differentiate the product e−Uφe^{-U}\varphie−Uφ: D(e−Uφ)(x)[v]=e−U(x)(Dφ(x)[v]−⟨∇U(x),v⟩φ(x))D(e^{-U}\varphi)(x)[v]=e^{-U(x)}(D\varphi(x)[v]-\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle\varphi(x))D(e−Uφ)(x)[v]=e−U(x)(Dφ(x)[v]−⟨∇U(x),v⟩φ(x)). Combine the product rule with the already proved derivative of the Gibbs weight, then collects the common exponential factor algebraically.

Factoring out e−Ue^{-U}e−U from the product rule leaves the test derivative minus its multiplication by the potential's directional derivative. This is the exact cancellation needed to pair with the generator.

02Weighted directional integration by parts §

Mathematical statement and proof

Integrate by parts the derivative of e−Uφe^{-U}\varphie−Uφ against Dψ[v]D\psi[v]Dψ[v]. The weighted product rule separates a Dφ[v]Dψ[v]D\varphi[v]D\psi[v]Dφ[v]Dψ[v] term from the drift term, giving ∫e−UφLvψ=−∫e−UDφ[v]Dψ[v]\int e^{-U}\varphi L_v\psi=-\int e^{-U}D\varphi[v]D\psi[v]∫e−UφLv​ψ=−∫e−UDφ[v]Dψ[v]. Compact support and C2C^2C2 regularity justify every derivative and integral involved.

Integrating Dv(e−UφDvψ)D_v(e^{-U}\varphi D_v\psi)Dv​(e−UφDv​ψ) gives zero. Expanding this derivative and moving the term involving DvφD_v\varphiDv​φ to the other side yields the negative directional energy.

03The directional Gibbs Dirichlet identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Convert both sides of the weighted directional identity to Gibbs integrals. The same real normalization factor multiplies both integrals, producing ∫φLvψ dπU=−∫Dφ[v]Dψ[v]dπU\int\varphi L_v\psi\,d\pi_U=-\int D\varphi[v]D\psi[v]d\pi_U∫φLv​ψdπU​=−∫Dφ[v]Dψ[v]dπU​. This is a change of measure, with no new integration-by-parts step.

Both weighted integrals carry the same inverse-partition factor. Applying the Gibbs change of density to each side therefore preserves the equality and its negative sign.

04Integrability of the gradient pairing §

Mathematical statement and proof

The gradients are continuous; the gradient of the compactly supported test φ\varphiφ vanishes outside its compact support. Thus x↦⟨∇φ(x),∇ψ(x)⟩x\mapsto\langle\nabla\varphi(x),\nabla\psi(x)\ranglex↦⟨∇φ(x),∇ψ(x)⟩ is continuous with compact support. The general compact-test Gibbs integrability theorem applies even though ψ\psiψ itself need not have compact support in this statement.

Even if the second test is not compactly supported, the first gradient vanishes outside its compact support. The product of the two gradients is thus localized to a compact set where both are bounded.

05Integrability of the test-generator product §

Mathematical statement and proof

The generator of the C2C^2C2 test ψ\psiψ is continuous under the Lipschitz-gradient assumption on UUU. Multiplication by the continuous compactly supported φ\varphiφ makes the product compactly supported, hence Gibbs integrable. This is the exact product needed on the left side of the Dirichlet identity.

The compactly supported multiplier localizes the continuous generator value. This makes the left side of the Dirichlet identity absolutely integrable before any integration-by-parts algebra is used.

06The full Dirichlet identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Sum the directional Dirichlet identities over the orthonormal basis. Finite-sum integrability allows the integral to pass through the sum, and Parseval identifies ∑iDφ[ei]Dψ[ei]=⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩\sum_iD\varphi[e_i]D\psi[e_i]=\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle∑i​Dφ[ei​]Dψ[ei​]=⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩. Therefore ∫φLψ dπU=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπU\int\varphi L\psi\,d\pi_U=-\int\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle d\pi_U∫φLψdπU​=−∫⟨∇φ,∇ψ⟩dπU​.

Summing the directional energies reconstructs the gradient inner product through orthonormal expansion. The finite sum and absolute integrability justify carrying this reconstruction through the integral.

07Symmetry of the generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the Dirichlet identity in both orders (φ,ψ)(\varphi,\psi)(φ,ψ) and (ψ,φ)(\psi,\varphi)(ψ,φ). Symmetry of the real inner product makes the resulting energy integrands equal pointwise. Hence ∫φLψ dπU=∫ψLφ dπU\int\varphi L\psi\,d\pi_U=\int\psi L\varphi\,d\pi_U∫φLψdπU​=∫ψLφdπU​.

Exchanging the two tests exchanges the two gradients in the energy pairing. Symmetry of the real inner product makes these energies identical, proving symmetry of the generator on the stated domain.

08The generator self-pairing §

Mathematical statement and proof

Specialize the bilinear identity to ψ=φ\psi=\varphiψ=φ, then rewrite ⟨∇φ,∇φ⟩=∥∇φ∥2\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi\rangle=\|\nabla\varphi\|^2⟨∇φ,∇φ⟩=∥∇φ∥2. This identifies the quadratic generator form exactly: ∫φLφ dπU=−∫∥∇φ∥2dπU\int\varphi L\varphi\,d\pi_U=-\int\|\nabla\varphi\|^2d\pi_U∫φLφdπU​=−∫∥∇φ∥2dπU​.

With the two tests equal, their gradient pairing is the squared gradient norm. This identifies the generator quadratic form exactly, rather than merely bounding its sign.

09Dissipativity of the generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

The self-pairing identity expresses the generator quadratic form as minus an integral of a nonnegative square. The integral is nonnegative, so its negative is at most zero. The proof uses the actual integrable energy established earlier, giving dissipativity on compactly supported C2C^2C2 tests.

The squared gradient norm is nonnegative pointwise and integrable. Its integral is therefore nonnegative, so the self-pairing generator integral is nonpositive. This is the announced dissipativity statement.
