Dalalyan bounds for an implemented L² Gaussian recursion

This endpoint applies the canonical-law theorem to an actual chain on a supplied probability space. Its inputs are the original potential assumptions, a square-integrable initialization, independent standard Gaussian scalar innovations and independence of the initial variable from the innovation sequence.

01Finite initial transport is automatic §

Mathematical statement and proof

The law of an L2L^2L2 variable is a probability measure with finite second moment. Strong convexity supplies the same properties for Gibbs. Their independent product is a finite-cost quadratic coupling; hence W2(Law⁡(X0),π)<∞W_2(\operatorname{Law}(X_0),\pi)<\inftyW2​(Law(X0​),π)<∞. The private probability lemma verifies mass preservation through the almost-measurable representative.

02Identify the implemented chain and apply the theorem §

Mathematical statement and proof

Scalar independence over all time-coordinate pairs and standard normal coordinate laws imply independent standard Gaussian vector innovations. With initial independence, the chain law is PhkLaw⁡(X0)P_h^k\operatorname{Law}(X_0)Phk​Law(X0​). Rewrite this law in the canonical small-step theorem to get

W2(Law⁡(Xk),π)≤(1−mh)kW2(Law⁡(X0),π)+3320Lmhd.W_2(\operatorname{Law}(X_k),\pi)\le(1-mh)^kW_2(\operatorname{Law}(X_0),\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{hd}.W2​(Law(Xk​),π)≤(1−mh)kW2​(Law(X0​),π)+2033​mL​hd​.

The large-step wrapper repeats the same law identification and uses factor Lh−1Lh-1Lh−1 and bias 3320Lh2−Lhhd\frac{33}{20}\frac{Lh}{2-Lh}\sqrt{hd}2033​2−LhLh​hd​. Neither wrapper assumes a stationary reference or separate initial moment of the law beyond the provided L2L^2L2 initialization.
