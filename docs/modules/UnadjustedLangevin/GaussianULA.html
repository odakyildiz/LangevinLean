Dalalyan bounds for an implemented L² Gaussian recursion

This endpoint applies the canonical-law theorem to an actual chain on a supplied probability space. Its inputs are the original potential assumptions, a square-integrable initialization, independent standard Gaussian scalar innovations and independence of the initial variable from the innovation sequence.

01initialLaw probability §

Mathematical statement and proof

The representative of the L2L^2L2 initialization is measurable. Its pushforward of a probability measure therefore has total mass one, giving the probability instance required by the law-level canonical ULA theorem.

02gaussianULA initial transport finite §

Mathematical statement and proof

The actual initialization is in L2L^2L2, so its law has a finite second moment. Strong convexity proves the same for Gibbs and proves its normalization. The independent product coupling of these two laws has finite quadratic cost, hence their Wasserstein distance is finite.

03dalalyan of gaussian innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assemble the jointly independent standard scalar Gaussian coordinates into iid multivariate standard innovations. Initialization independence and the L2L^2L2 representative theorem identify the implementation's law with Phk(PX0)P_h^k(P_{X_0})Phk​(PX0​​). Derive finite initial transport from its moments, then apply the unconditional canonical small-step theorem and rewrite the law back to the implementation.
