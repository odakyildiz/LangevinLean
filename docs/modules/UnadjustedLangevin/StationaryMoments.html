A reference variable YYY whose law is π\piπ inherits ∥∇U(Y)∥L2≤Ld\|\nabla U(Y)\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{Ld}∥∇U(Y)∥L2​≤Ld​. The assertion that its law is Gibbs is a displayed premise here.

If Y#P=πUY_\#P=\pi_UY#​P=πU​, change of variables gives ∥∇U(Y)∥22=∫∥∇U∥2dπU\|\nabla U(Y)\|_2^2=\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\pi_U∥∇U(Y)∥22​=∫∥∇U∥2dπU​. The Gibbs score estimate bounds the last expression by LdLdLd, independently of the time at which the stationary variable is observed. Taking the nonnegative square root supplies exactly the reference drift bound Ld\sqrt{Ld}Ld​ used in the local-error calculation. This uses the stationary law, rather than a pathwise bound on the drift.

01The reference drift norm from its Gibbs law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The representative of The pointwise-composition operator equals ∇U(Y)\nabla U(Y)∇U(Y) almost everywhere. Change variables using P∘Y−1=πUP\circ Y^{-1}=\pi_UP∘Y−1=πU​, so its squared L2(P)L^2(P)L2(P) norm is ∫∥∇U∥2dπU≤Ld\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\pi_U\le Ld∫∥∇U∥2dπU​≤Ld. Nonnegativity of the norm and of LdLdLd then gives ∥∇U(Y)∥2≤Ld\|\nabla U(Y)\|_2\le\sqrt{Ld}∥∇U(Y)∥2​≤Ld​. The law identity, rather than independence, is the input to this transfer.

The equality of laws transports the squared gradient integral from the sample space to Gibbs. The lifted drift's norm-square identity then gives ∥G[Y]∥22≤Ld\|\mathcal G[Y]\|_2^2\le Ld∥G[Y]∥22​≤Ld, and taking square roots yields the reference bound.

02The drift moment under strong convexity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use strong convexity to prove ZU<∞Z_U<\inftyZU​<∞, and apply the previous law-transfer theorem with that proof. The conclusion is the same Ld\sqrt{Ld}Ld​ bound for the lifted drift of any L2L^2L2 variable with Gibbs law. There is no separate assumed drift-moment bound hidden in this specialization.

Positive strong convexity supplies the normalization and moment properties needed to apply the preceding stationary-law calculation. The resulting bound is independent of the particular representation or coupling of the stationary random vector.
