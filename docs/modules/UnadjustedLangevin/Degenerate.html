Zero-dimensional state spaces have zero transport cost

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

On a subsingleton state space every two states coincide, so every coupling has zero quadratic difference cost. Probability laws on such a space therefore have zero Wasserstein distance.

01couplingCost eq zero of subsingleton §

Mathematical statement and proof

In a subsingleton state space every pair satisfies x=yx=yx=y, so the displacement function is identically zero. Its extended L2L^2L2 norm is zero under every pair measure, hence every coupling cost vanishes.

02wasserstein2 eq zero of subsingleton §

Mathematical statement and proof

The independent product of two probabilities is a coupling, so the coupling type is nonempty. Every coupling has zero cost by the preceding result; taking their infimum gives W2(μ,π)=0W_2(\mu,\pi)=0W2​(μ,π)=0.

03wasserstein2Real eq zero of subsingleton §

Mathematical statement and proof

Rewrite the extended Wasserstein distance as zero using the subsingleton theorem. Its real conversion is also zero. This covers dimension-zero spaces without invoking the nontrivial-space comparison m≤Lm\le Lm≤L.
