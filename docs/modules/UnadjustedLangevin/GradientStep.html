01The squared Euler-step bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let x,y,gx,gyx,y,g_x,g_yx,y,gx​,gy​ lie in a real inner-product space, let m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, L≥0L\ge0L≥0, and h≥0h\ge0h≥0. Assume

m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx−gy,x−y⟩,∥gx−gy∥≤L∥x−y∥.m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle g_x-g_y,x-y\rangle,\qquad\|g_x-g_y\|\le L\|x-y\|.m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx​−gy​,x−y⟩,∥gx​−gy​∥≤L∥x−y∥.

Then

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥x−y∥2.\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|^2\le(1-2hm+h^2L^2)\|x-y\|^2.∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥x−y∥2.

Both sides of the Lipschitz estimate are nonnegative, so squaring preserves the inequality:

∥gx−gy∥2≤L2∥x−y∥2.\|g_x-g_y\|^2\le L^2\|x-y\|^2.∥gx​−gy​∥2≤L2∥x−y∥2.

Multiply strong monotonicity by 2h≥02h\ge02h≥0, and multiply the squared Lipschitz estimate by h2≥0h^2\ge0h2≥0:

2hm∥x−y∥2≤2h⟨gx−gy,x−y⟩,h2∥gx−gy∥2≤h2L2∥x−y∥2.2hm\|x-y\|^2\le2h\langle g_x-g_y,x-y\rangle,\qquad h^2\|g_x-g_y\|^2\le h^2L^2\|x-y\|^2.2hm∥x−y∥2≤2h⟨gx​−gy​,x−y⟩,h2∥gx​−gy​∥2≤h2L2∥x−y∥2.

Write d=x−yd=x-yd=x−y, v=gx−gyv=g_x-g_yv=gx​−gy​. Expanding the squared norm and applying the preceding estimates gives

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥2=∥d−hv∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨v,d⟩+h2∥v∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥d∥2.\begin{aligned}\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|^2&=\|d-hv\|^2\\&=\|d\|^2-2h\langle v,d\rangle+h^2\|v\|^2\\&\le(1-2hm+h^2L^2)\|d\|^2.\end{aligned}∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥2​=∥d−hv∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨v,d⟩+h2∥v∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥d∥2.​

Apply the lower bound on the mixed inner product before any absolute-value estimate. The absolute-value estimate alone would give (1+hL)2(1+hL)^2(1+hL)2. The resulting expression 1−2hm+h2L21-2hm+h^2L^21−2hm+h2L2 therefore follows from the signed inner-product bound and the squared gradient-difference bound.

02Taking the square root §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let HHH be a real inner-product space, x,y,gx,gy∈Hx,y,g_x,g_y\in Hx,y,gx​,gy​∈H, m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, L≥0L\ge0L≥0, and h≥0h\ge0h≥0. Assume

m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx−gy,x−y⟩,∥gx−gy∥≤L∥x−y∥.m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle g_x-g_y,x-y\rangle,\qquad\|g_x-g_y\|\le L\|x-y\|.m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx​−gy​,x−y⟩,∥gx​−gy​∥≤L∥x−y∥.

Then

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2 ∥x−y∥.\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|\le\sqrt{1-2hm+h^2L^2}\,\|x-y\|.∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2​∥x−y∥.

No separate sign assumption on 1−2hm+h2L21-2hm+h^2L^21−2hm+h2L2 is required; the real square root is interpreted as zero on nonpositive inputs.

Apply the preceding squared estimate. With A=∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥A=\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|A=∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥, r=∥x−y∥r=\|x-y\|r=∥x−y∥, and c=1−2hm+h2L2c=1-2hm+h^2L^2c=1−2hm+h2L2, it says A2≤cr2A^2\le cr^2A2≤cr2.

Since A≥0A\ge0A≥0, take square roots and use r2≥0r^2\ge0r2≥0:

A≤cr2=c r2=c r.A\le\sqrt{cr^2}=\sqrt c\,\sqrt{r^2}=\sqrt c\,r.A≤cr2​=c​r2​=c​r.

The square-root product identity is valid here even without a sign hypothesis on ccc, because the second factor r2r^2r2 is nonnegative.

If r=0r=0r=0, the Lipschitz estimate implies gx=gyg_x=g_ygx​=gy​, and both sides vanish. If r>0r>0r>0, the squared estimate forces c≥0c\ge0c≥0, so ordinary square-root monotonicity applies. Consequently, under the hypotheses, a negative coefficient is possible only in the zero-distance case.
