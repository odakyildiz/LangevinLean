m∥d∥2≤⟨v,d⟩and∥v∥≤L∥d∥m\|d\|^2\le\langle v,d\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|v\|\le L\|d\|m∥d∥2≤⟨v,d⟩and∥v∥≤L∥d∥

for some m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R and L≥0L\ge0L≥0, then expanding ∥d−hv∥2\|d-hv\|^2∥d−hv∥2 gives

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥x−y∥2,\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|^2\le(1-2hm+h^2L^2)\|x-y\|^2,∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥x−y∥2,

01The squared Euler-step bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let HHH be a real inner-product space, x,y,gx,gy∈Hx,y,g_x,g_y\in Hx,y,gx​,gy​∈H, m,h∈Rm,h\in\mathbb Rm,h∈R and L≥0L\ge0L≥0. Assume h≥0h\ge0h≥0 and

m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx−gy,x−y⟩,∥gx−gy∥≤L∥x−y∥.m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle g_x-g_y,x-y\rangle,\qquad\|g_x-g_y\|\le L\|x-y\|.m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx​−gy​,x−y⟩,∥gx​−gy​∥≤L∥x−y∥.

Then

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2) ∥x−y∥2.\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|^2\le(1-2hm+h^2L^2)\,\|x-y\|^2.∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥2≤(1−2hm+h2L2)∥x−y∥2.

Proof. Write d=x−yd=x-yd=x−y and v=gx−gyv=g_x-g_yv=gx​−gy​.

(a) Squared Lipschitz bound. Since 0≤∥v∥≤L∥d∥0\le\|v\|\le L\|d\|0≤∥v∥≤L∥d∥ and both sides are nonnegative (here L≥0L\ge0L≥0 is used), squaring preserves the inequality:

∥v∥2≤L2∥d∥2.(1)\|v\|^2\le L^2\|d\|^2.\qquad(1)∥v∥2≤L2∥d∥2.(1)

(b) Scaled hypotheses. Multiplying the monotonicity hypothesis by 2h≥02h\ge02h≥0 and inequality (1) by h2≥0h^2\ge0h2≥0,

2hm∥d∥2≤2h⟨v,d⟩,h2∥v∥2≤h2L2∥d∥2.(2)2hm\|d\|^2\le2h\langle v,d\rangle,\qquad h^2\|v\|^2\le h^2L^2\|d\|^2.\qquad(2)2hm∥d∥2≤2h⟨v,d⟩,h2∥v∥2≤h2L2∥d∥2.(2)

(c) Expansion. By linearity, (x−hgx)−(y−hgy)=d−hv(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)=d-hv(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)=d−hv. Expanding the square of the norm, and using ∥hv∥=∣h∣ ∥v∥=h∥v∥\|hv\|=|h|\,\|v\|=h\|v\|∥hv∥=∣h∣∥v∥=h∥v∥ because h≥0h\ge0h≥0,

∥d−hv∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨v,d⟩+h2∥v∥2.\|d-hv\|^2=\|d\|^2-2h\langle v,d\rangle+h^2\|v\|^2.∥d−hv∥2=∥d∥2−2h⟨v,d⟩+h2∥v∥2.

(d) Conclusion. Bounding −2h⟨v,d⟩-2h\langle v,d\rangle−2h⟨v,d⟩ by −2hm∥d∥2-2hm\|d\|^2−2hm∥d∥2 and h2∥v∥2h^2\|v\|^2h2∥v∥2 by h2L2∥d∥2h^2L^2\|d\|^2h2L2∥d∥2, both by (2),

∥d−hv∥2≤∥d∥2−2hm∥d∥2+h2L2∥d∥2=(1−2hm+h2L2)∥d∥2.□\|d-hv\|^2\le\|d\|^2-2hm\|d\|^2+h^2L^2\|d\|^2=(1-2hm+h^2L^2)\|d\|^2.\qquad\square∥d−hv∥2≤∥d∥2−2hm∥d∥2+h2L2∥d∥2=(1−2hm+h2L2)∥d∥2.□

The cross term is bounded with the signed lower bound ⟨v,d⟩≥m∥d∥2\langle v,d\rangle\ge m\|d\|^2⟨v,d⟩≥m∥d∥2. Bounding it instead by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality would only give the factor (1+hL)2(1+hL)^2(1+hL)2, which exceeds one for every h>0h>0h>0 with L>0L>0L>0.

  1. hLipSq is (1); nlinarith squares hLip using the nonnegativity facts norm_nonneg and L.coe_nonneg.
  2. hmonoScaled and hLipScaled are the two inequalities (2), obtained with mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left from 2h≥02h\ge02h≥0 (by positivity) and h2≥0h^2\ge0h2≥0 (sq_nonneg h).
  3. The rw block is (c): the identity (x−hgx)−(y−hgy)=d−hv(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)=d-hv(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)=d−hv is proved by the module tactic (normalisation in modules over a ring); norm_sub_sq_real expands ∥d−hv∥2\|d-hv\|^2∥d−hv∥2; real_inner_smul_right, norm_smul, Real.norm_eq_abs and abs_of_nonneg hh extract the scalar hhh; real_inner_comm orders the inner product as in hmono.
  4. nlinarith combines the expansion with hmonoScaled and hLipScaled, which is (d).

02The square-root Euler-step bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Under the hypotheses of the previous step (HHH a real inner-product space, m∈Rm\in\mathbb Rm∈R, L≥0L\ge0L≥0, h≥0h\ge0h≥0, m∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx−gy,x−y⟩m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle g_x-g_y,x-y\ranglem∥x−y∥2≤⟨gx​−gy​,x−y⟩ and ∥gx−gy∥≤L∥x−y∥\|g_x-g_y\|\le L\|x-y\|∥gx​−gy​∥≤L∥x−y∥),

∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2  ∥x−y∥,\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|\le\sqrt{1-2hm+h^2L^2}\;\|x-y\|,∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2​∥x−y∥,

where s=0\sqrt s=0s​=0 for s<0s<0s<0.

Proof. Put A=∥(x−hgx)−(y−hgy)∥≥0A=\|(x-hg_x)-(y-hg_y)\|\ge0A=∥(x−hgx​)−(y−hgy​)∥≥0, r=∥x−y∥≥0r=\|x-y\|\ge0r=∥x−y∥≥0 and c=1−2hm+h2L2c=1-2hm+h^2L^2c=1−2hm+h2L2. The previous step gives A2≤cr2A^2\le cr^2A2≤cr2. We use three elementary facts about the square root with the stated convention.

  1. If A2≤sA^2\le sA2≤s for a real number sss, then A≤sA\le\sqrt sA≤s​. If s<0s<0s<0, the hypothesis 0≤A2≤s0\le A^2\le s0≤A2≤s is impossible. If s≥0s\ge0s≥0, monotonicity of the square root on [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞) gives ∣A∣=A2≤s|A|=\sqrt{A^2}\le\sqrt s∣A∣=A2​≤s​, and A≤∣A∣A\le|A|A≤∣A∣.
  2. cr2=c r2\sqrt{cr^2}=\sqrt c\,\sqrt{r^2}cr2​=c​r2​ for every real ccc. If c≥0c\ge0c≥0 this is the product rule for square roots of nonnegative numbers. If c<0c<0c<0 and r≠0r\ne0r=0, then cr2<0cr^2<0cr2<0 and both sides are 000. If r=0r=0r=0, both sides are 000.
  3. r2=r\sqrt{r^2}=rr2​=r, because r≥0r\ge0r≥0.

By the first fact with s=cr2s=cr^2s=cr2, then the second and the third, A≤cr2=c r2=c rA\le\sqrt{cr^2}=\sqrt c\,\sqrt{r^2}=\sqrt c\,rA≤cr2​=c​r2​=c​r. □\square□

The convention s=0\sqrt s=0s​=0 for s<0s<0s<0 only matters when r=0r=0r=0: if r>0r>0r>0, then 0≤A2≤cr20\le A^2\le cr^20≤A2≤cr2 forces c≥0c\ge0c≥0. If r=0r=0r=0, the Lipschitz hypothesis forces gx=gyg_x=g_ygx​=gy​, and both sides of the claim vanish.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

In gradient_step_contract the square root is Real.sqrt, which Mathlib defines to be 000 on negative arguments; this is the convention of the claim. The hypotheses are those of gradient_step_sq_bound.

  1. hsq is the squared bound A2≤cr2A^2\le cr^2A2≤cr2 of the previous step.
  2. hbound := Real.le_sqrt_of_sq_le hsq is the first fact: from A2≤cr2A^2\le cr^2A2≤cr2 it yields A≤cr2A\le\sqrt{cr^2}A≤cr2​.
  3. rwa [Real.sqrt_mul' _ (sq_nonneg _), Real.sqrt_sq (norm_nonneg _)] at hbound applies the second fact (Real.sqrt_mul' splits c r2\sqrt{c\,r^2}cr2​ for any ccc provided r2≥0r^2\ge0r2≥0) and the third fact (Real.sqrt_sq with r≥0r\ge0r≥0); the rewritten hbound is the goal.
