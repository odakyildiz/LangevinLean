The Langevin generator annihilates Gibbs averages

For compactly supported C2C^2C2 test functions, Lφ=Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\mathcal L\varphi=\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangleLφ=Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩ has zero Gibbs mean. These identities establish infinitesimal invariance; they alone do not assert stationarity of a stochastic process.

02Integration by parts in one direction §

Mathematical statement and proof

The chain rule gives Dve−U=−e−U⟨∇U,v⟩D_ve^{-U}=-e^{-U}\langle\nabla U,v\rangleDv​e−U=−e−U⟨∇U,v⟩. Set g=Dvφg=D_v\varphig=Dv​φ; its compact support eliminates the boundary term in ∫e−UDvg=−∫(Dve−U)g\int e^{-U}D_vg=-\int(D_ve^{-U})g∫e−UDv​g=−∫(Dv​e−U)g. The right side is exactly the drift contribution, hence ∫e−ULvφ=0\int e^{-U}\mathcal L_v\varphi=0∫e−ULv​φ=0. Multiply by the normalizing constant to obtain ∫Lvφ dπ=0\int\mathcal L_v\varphi\,d\pi=0∫Lv​φdπ=0.

03Regularity, support and finite sums §

Mathematical statement and proof

The second directional derivative of a C2C^2C2 test is continuous, and the gradient of UUU is continuous by its Lipschitz assumption. Derivatives of a compactly supported test have compact support, so Lvφ\mathcal L_v\varphiLv​φ is continuous, compactly supported and Gibbs integrable. Finite sums preserve these integrability properties; integrate the directional identities term by term to get zero mean for the full generator.

04Identify the invariant expression §

Mathematical statement and proof

In an orthonormal basis, the sum of second directional derivatives is the Laplacian, and ∑i⟨∇U,ei⟩⟨∇φ,ei⟩=⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\sum_i\langle\nabla U,e_i\rangle\langle\nabla\varphi,e_i\rangle=\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangle∑i​⟨∇U,ei​⟩⟨∇φ,ei​⟩=⟨∇U,∇φ⟩. Thus the defined generator equals the usual expression, and substitution proves ∫(Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩)dπ=0\int(\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangle)d\pi=0∫(Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩)dπ=0.
