For a compactly supported C2C^2C2 test φ\varphiφ, set Lφ=Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\mathcal L\varphi=\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangleLφ=Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩. In a fixed direction vvv, the product derivative Dv(e−UDvφ)D_v(e^{-U}D_v\varphi)Dv​(e−UDv​φ) equals the Gibbs weight times the directional generator. Its integral is zero because the differentiated product has compact support. Summing over an orthonormal basis and dividing by the positive finite partition gives ∫Lφ dπU=0\int\mathcal L\varphi\,d\pi_U=0∫LφdπU​=0.

Continuity and compact support justify the integrals and the finite sums. The resulting identity is infinitesimal invariance on the stated test class; it does not by itself identify the time marginals of a stochastic process.

01The generator in one direction §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a fixed direction vvv, define Lvφ(x)=D(Dφ(⋅)[v])(x)[v]−⟨∇U(x),v⟩Dφ(x)[v]L_v\varphi(x)=D(D\varphi(\cdot)[v])(x)[v]-\langle\nabla U(x),v\rangle D\varphi(x)[v]Lv​φ(x)=D(Dφ(⋅)[v])(x)[v]−⟨∇U(x),v⟩Dφ(x)[v]. The first term is a directional second derivative of the test; the second is the directional gradient drift. No Hessian of UUU is part of this definition.

For a direction vvv, the second derivative contributes D2φ[v,v]D^2\varphi[v,v]D2φ[v,v], while the drift contributes −⟨∇U,v⟩Dφ[v]-\langle\nabla U,v\rangle D\varphi[v]−⟨∇U,v⟩Dφ[v]. The weighted product derivative in this direction is the expression integrated below.

02The Langevin generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Choose the standard orthonormal basis and set Lφ=∑iLeiφL\varphi=\sum_iL_{e_i}\varphiLφ=∑i​Lei​​φ. The finite sum makes the construction meaningful in dimension zero and permits integration by parts one direction at a time. A subsequent identity proves that this is the usual basis-independent Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangleΔφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩.

Summing over an orthonormal basis yields the Euclidean diffusion operator. The basis is finite, so all sums in the definition and subsequent integrations are finite sums.

03The weighted Gibbs integral formula §

Mathematical statement and proof

Under the positive finite partition hypothesis, changing measure gives, for every integrable test,

∫f dπU=ZU−1∫e−U(x)f(x)dx.\int f\,d\pi_U=Z_U^{-1}\int e^{-U(x)}f(x)dx.∫fdπU​=ZU−1​∫e−U(x)f(x)dx.

Continuity of the potential makes the density measurable, and the same finite scalar ZU−1Z_U^{-1}ZU−1​ multiplies each weighted integral.

Changing to Gibbs inserts the same scalar normalization Z−1Z^{-1}Z−1 into every weighted integral. Positivity and finiteness of ZZZ make this the usual density-change formula for signed integrable tests.

04Integrability of a compactly supported test §

Mathematical statement and proof

A continuous compactly supported fff gives a continuous compactly supported product e−Ufe^{-U}fe−Uf, hence a Lebesgue-integrable function. The weighted-density integrability criterion and multiplication by the real normalization factor transfer this fact to πU\pi_UπU​. This explicitly supplies the integrability needed by subsequent linearity arguments.

A continuous function with compact support is bounded on its support. Multiplication by the continuous Gibbs weight preserves compact support, and hence provides a Lebesgue-integrable weighted function.

05The derivative of the Gibbs weight §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the chain rule to x↦e−U(x)x\mapsto e^{-U(x)}x↦e−U(x): D(e−U)(x)[v]=−e−U(x)DU(x)[v]D(e^{-U})(x)[v]=-e^{-U(x)}DU(x)[v]D(e−U)(x)[v]=−e−U(x)DU(x)[v]. The minus sign arises from differentiating −U-U−U, and the exponential factor remains unchanged. Identifying the directional derivative of UUU with its gradient pairing fixes the sign of the generator drift term.

06The weighted directional generator integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Differentiate e−U(x)Dφ(x)[v]e^{-U(x)}D\varphi(x)[v]e−U(x)Dφ(x)[v] in direction vvv. The product rule gives exactly e−U(x)Lvφ(x)e^{-U(x)}L_v\varphi(x)e−U(x)Lv​φ(x). Since derivatives of a compactly supported C2C^2C2 test remain compactly supported, this weighted first derivative is integrable and has zero integral by integration by parts. The proof establishes continuity and compact support before invoking that theorem.

The product derivative is Dv(e−UDvφ)=e−U(Dv2φ−DvU Dvφ)D_v(e^{-U}D_v\varphi)=e^{-U}(D_v^2\varphi-D_vU\,D_v\varphi)Dv​(e−UDv​φ)=e−U(Dv2​φ−Dv​UDv​φ). Its integral vanishes because the product has compact support, giving the weighted directional invariance identity.

07The directional Gibbs generator integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the weighted-to-Gibbs integral formula to LvφL_v\varphiLv​φ. Its weighted Lebesgue integral is zero, so multiplication by the normalization factor still gives zero. This is infinitesimal invariance for a single direction and does not yet identify the law of a time-evolving process.

Dividing the zero weighted integral by the positive partition leaves zero. This is the normalized directional invariance statement, retaining the same compactly supported test and requiring no new derivative calculation.

08Continuity of the directional generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

The C2C^2C2 hypothesis makes the first and second derivatives of φ\varphiφ continuous. The gradient of UUU is continuous by its Lipschitz hypothesis. Evaluating continuous linear maps in the fixed direction vvv, multiplying the scalar factors, and subtracting therefore gives continuity of LvφL_v\varphiLv​φ.

The second derivative of the test and the gradient of the potential are continuous. Evaluation in a fixed direction, multiplication, and subtraction therefore preserve continuity of the directional generator.

09Compact support of the directional generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Outside the compact support of φ\varphiφ, the test is locally zero, so its first and second derivatives vanish there. Both terms of LvφL_v\varphiLv​φ therefore vanish. The support inclusion puts the support of the generator inside the same compact set; no support property of UUU is required.

Outside the support of a smooth compactly supported test, the test vanishes on a neighborhood, so all its derivatives vanish there. Both generator terms therefore have support inside that same compact set.

10Integrability of the directional generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

Combine continuity of LvφL_v\varphiLv​φ with preservation of compact support. The established compact-support Gibbs integrability theorem then gives absolute integrability. This permits ordinary integral algebra, even though the ambient generator is not bounded uniformly for arbitrary tests.

Continuity alone would not ensure integrability on an unbounded space. Here compact support supplies the needed localization, and the Gibbs density is continuous and bounded on that compact set.

11Integrability of the full generator §

Mathematical statement and proof

The full generator is the finite sum of its directional pieces. Each piece is integrable under πU\pi_UπU​, so the finite-sum integrability theorem applies. Finiteness of the basis is the reason no exchange of an infinite series and integral is required.

Each directional summand has an absolutely integrable generator value. The finite orthonormal-basis sum is therefore integrable, allowing the full generator integral to be distributed term by term.

12The weighted full generator integral §

Mathematical statement and proof

Expand the finite basis sum defining LφL\varphiLφ and distribute the Gibbs weight over it. Compact support supplies integrability of every summand, allowing ∫∑ie−ULeiφ=∑i∫e−ULeiφ\int\sum_i e^{-U}L_{e_i}\varphi=\sum_i\int e^{-U}L_{e_i}\varphi∫∑i​e−ULei​​φ=∑i​∫e−ULei​​φ. Distribute the Gibbs weight through the finite basis sum and integrate. Every directional integral has already been shown to be zero, so their sum vanishes without an infinite-series interchange.

14The Laplacian-minus-drift formula §

Mathematical statement and proof

The sum of the directional second derivatives over an orthonormal basis is the Laplacian. For the drift sum, Dφ(x)[ei]=⟨∇φ(x),ei⟩D\varphi(x)[e_i]=\langle\nabla\varphi(x),e_i\rangleDφ(x)[ei​]=⟨∇φ(x),ei​⟩; Parseval turns the sum of products with ⟨∇U(x),ei⟩\langle\nabla U(x),e_i\rangle⟨∇U(x),ei​⟩ into ⟨∇U(x),∇φ(x)⟩\langle\nabla U(x),\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle⟨∇U(x),∇φ(x)⟩. The proof rewrites the basis definition into this conventional formula pointwise.

Parseval's identity gives ∑i⟨∇U,ei⟩⟨∇φ,ei⟩=⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\sum_i\langle\nabla U,e_i\rangle\langle\nabla\varphi,e_i\rangle=\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangle∑i​⟨∇U,ei​⟩⟨∇φ,ei​⟩=⟨∇U,∇φ⟩. The analogous sum of second directional derivatives is the Laplacian.

15The stationary identity in conventional notation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Replace the conventional expression Δφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla U,\nabla\varphi\rangleΔφ−⟨∇U,∇φ⟩ by the equal basis-defined generator inside the integral. The established zero-generator integral then gives zero. The rewrite makes the displayed mathematical identity refer to the same function that the preceding calculation integrated.

Substitution of the pointwise generator formula yields the conventional stationary identity. It applies on the stated compactly supported test class; stationarity of the stochastic evolution is established later by identifying its laws.
