The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.651.651.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

Let HHH be a real inner product space with its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra. The characteristic function of a finite measure ν\nuν on HHH is

φν(t)=∫Hei⟨x,t⟩ ν(dx),t∈H.\varphi_\nu(t)=\int_He^{i\langle x,t\rangle}\,\nu(dx),\qquad t\in H.φν​(t)=∫H​ei⟨x,t⟩ν(dx),t∈H.

If HHH is complete and separable (for instance H=RdH=\mathbb R^dH=Rd), two finite measures with the same characteristic function are equal. This section proves a criterion for identifying the law of a limit. Suppose Xn→XX_n\to XXn​→X in L2(P;H)L^2(P;H)L2(P;H), and suppose that for each nnn there is a coupling ρn\rho_nρn​ of the law of XnX_nXn​ with a fixed probability measure π\piπ such that the expected distance ∫∥x−y∥ ρn(dx,dy)\int\|x-y\|\,\rho_n(dx,dy)∫∥x−y∥ρn​(dx,dy) tends to 000. Then the law of XXX is π\piπ.

The proof uses two estimates for the characteristic test x↦ei⟨x,t⟩x\mapsto e^{i\langle x,t\rangle}x↦ei⟨x,t⟩, which is bounded by 111 and ∥t∥\|t\|∥t∥-Lipschitz: the difference of the characteristic functions of the two marginals of a coupling is at most ∥t∥\|t\|∥t∥ times its expected distance, and X↦φPX(t)X\mapsto\varphi_{P_X}(t)X↦φPX​​(t) is continuous on L2L^2L2. So φPXn(t)\varphi_{P_{X_n}}(t)φPXn​​​(t) converges both to φPX(t)\varphi_{P_X}(t)φPX​​(t) and to φπ(t)\varphi_\pi(t)φπ​(t). The criterion is applied in the next section to Euler approximations of the Langevin equation coupled with Metropolis-adjusted chains that preserve the Gibbs measure. Only first moments of the couplings are used, and no optimal coupling is required.

01A Lipschitz bound for characteristic tests §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let HHH be a real inner product space and t,x,y∈Ht,x,y\in Ht,x,y∈H. Then

∣ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩∣≤∥t∥ ∥x−y∥.\bigl|e^{i\langle x,t\rangle}-e^{i\langle y,t\rangle}\bigr|\le\|t\|\,\|x-y\|.​ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩​≤∥t∥∥x−y∥.

Proof. For real numbers u,vu,vu,v, the map θ↦eiθ\theta\mapsto e^{i\theta}θ↦eiθ has derivative ieiθie^{i\theta}ieiθ of modulus 111, so

∣eiu−eiv∣=∣∫vuieiθ dθ∣≤∣u−v∣:(1)\bigl|e^{iu}-e^{iv}\bigr|=\Bigl|\int_v^u ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta\Bigr|\le|u-v|:\qquad(1)​eiu−eiv​=​∫vu​ieiθdθ​≤∣u−v∣:(1)

the unit circle is parametrised by a 111-Lipschitz map. Apply (1) with u=⟨x,t⟩u=\langle x,t\rangleu=⟨x,t⟩ and v=⟨y,t⟩v=\langle y,t\ranglev=⟨y,t⟩. By linearity of the inner product in its first argument, u−v=⟨x−y,t⟩u-v=\langle x-y,t\rangleu−v=⟨x−y,t⟩, and by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality ∣⟨x−y,t⟩∣≤∥x−y∥ ∥t∥|\langle x-y,t\rangle|\le\|x-y\|\,\|t\|∣⟨x−y,t⟩∣≤∥x−y∥∥t∥. □\square□

02Characteristic differences controlled by the coupling distance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let HHH be a real inner product space with its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra and a second countable topology, let ρ\rhoρ be a probability measure on H×HH\times HH×H with marginals ρ1=(pr1)#ρ\rho_1=(\mathrm{pr}_1)_\#\rhoρ1​=(pr1​)#​ρ and ρ2=(pr2)#ρ\rho_2=(\mathrm{pr}_2)_\#\rhoρ2​=(pr2​)#​ρ, and assume ∫∥x−y∥ ρ(dx,dy)<∞\int\|x-y\|\,\rho(dx,dy)<\infty∫∥x−y∥ρ(dx,dy)<∞. Then for every t∈Ht\in Ht∈H,

∣φρ1(t)−φρ2(t)∣≤∥t∥∫∥x−y∥ ρ(dx,dy).\bigl|\varphi_{\rho_1}(t)-\varphi_{\rho_2}(t)\bigr|\le\|t\|\int\|x-y\|\,\rho(dx,dy).​φρ1​​(t)−φρ2​​(t)​≤∥t∥∫∥x−y∥ρ(dx,dy).

Proof. By the change of variables formula for image measures,

φρ1(t)=∫ei⟨x,t⟩ ρ(dx,dy),φρ2(t)=∫ei⟨y,t⟩ ρ(dx,dy).\varphi_{\rho_1}(t)=\int e^{i\langle x,t\rangle}\,\rho(dx,dy),\qquad\varphi_{\rho_2}(t)=\int e^{i\langle y,t\rangle}\,\rho(dx,dy).φρ1​​(t)=∫ei⟨x,t⟩ρ(dx,dy),φρ2​​(t)=∫ei⟨y,t⟩ρ(dx,dy).

Both integrands are continuous, hence measurable, and bounded in modulus by 111; since ρ\rhoρ is a probability measure they are integrable. By linearity of the integral, the inequality ∣∫f dρ∣≤∫∣f∣ dρ|\int f\,d\rho|\le\int|f|\,d\rho∣∫fdρ∣≤∫∣f∣dρ, the previous step and monotonicity of the integral,

∣φρ1(t)−φρ2(t)∣=∣∫(ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩) dρ∣≤∫∣ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩∣ dρ≤∫∥t∥ ∥x−y∥ dρ.\bigl|\varphi_{\rho_1}(t)-\varphi_{\rho_2}(t)\bigr|=\Bigl|\int\bigl(e^{i\langle x,t\rangle}-e^{i\langle y,t\rangle}\bigr)\,d\rho\Bigr|\le\int\bigl|e^{i\langle x,t\rangle}-e^{i\langle y,t\rangle}\bigr|\,d\rho\le\int\|t\|\,\|x-y\|\,d\rho .​φρ1​​(t)−φρ2​​(t)​=​∫(ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩)dρ​≤∫​ei⟨x,t⟩−ei⟨y,t⟩​dρ≤∫∥t∥∥x−y∥dρ.

The last integral is ∥t∥∫∥x−y∥ dρ\|t\|\int\|x-y\|\,d\rho∥t∥∫∥x−y∥dρ, which is finite by the integrability hypothesis. □\square□

04Equality of limiting laws from vanishing coupling distance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Limits below are taken along a fixed nontrivial filter on an index set III; the reader may take I=NI=\mathbb NI=N and limits as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞, which is the case used later.

Claim. Let PPP be a probability measure on Ω\OmegaΩ and HHH a real Hilbert space that is separable, with its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra. Let Xi,X∈L2(P;H)X_i,X\in L^2(P;H)Xi​,X∈L2(P;H) (i∈Ii\in Ii∈I), let π\piπ be a probability measure on HHH, and let ρi\rho_iρi​ be probability measures on H×HH\times HH×H such that for every iii

(pr1)#ρi=PXi,(pr2)#ρi=π,∫∥x−y∥ ρi(dx,dy)<∞,(\mathrm{pr}_1)_\#\rho_i=P_{X_i},\qquad(\mathrm{pr}_2)_\#\rho_i=\pi,\qquad\int\|x-y\|\,\rho_i(dx,dy)<\infty,(pr1​)#​ρi​=PXi​​,(pr2​)#​ρi​=π,∫∥x−y∥ρi​(dx,dy)<∞,

and assume Xi→XX_i\to XXi​→X in L2(P;H)L^2(P;H)L2(P;H) and ∫∥x−y∥ ρi(dx,dy)→0\int\|x-y\|\,\rho_i(dx,dy)\to0∫∥x−y∥ρi​(dx,dy)→0. Then PX=πP_X=\piPX​=π.

Proof. Fix t∈Ht\in Ht∈H.

First limit. By the previous step, X↦φPX(t)X\mapsto\varphi_{P_X}(t)X↦φPX​​(t) is continuous on L2(P;H)L^2(P;H)L2(P;H), so Xi→XX_i\to XXi​→X gives φPXi(t)→φPX(t)\varphi_{P_{X_i}}(t)\to\varphi_{P_X}(t)φPXi​​​(t)→φPX​​(t).

Second limit. By the second step applied to ρi\rho_iρi​, whose marginals are PXiP_{X_i}PXi​​ and π\piπ,

0≤∣φPXi(t)−φπ(t)∣≤∥t∥∫∥x−y∥ ρi(dx,dy)⟶∥t∥⋅0=0,0\le\bigl|\varphi_{P_{X_i}}(t)-\varphi_\pi(t)\bigr|\le\|t\|\int\|x-y\|\,\rho_i(dx,dy)\longrightarrow\|t\|\cdot0=0,0≤​φPXi​​​(t)−φπ​(t)​≤∥t∥∫∥x−y∥ρi​(dx,dy)⟶∥t∥⋅0=0,

so, by the squeeze principle, φPXi(t)→φπ(t)\varphi_{P_{X_i}}(t)\to\varphi_\pi(t)φPXi​​​(t)→φπ​(t).

Conclusion. Limits in C\mathbb CC along a nontrivial filter are unique, so φPX(t)=φπ(t)\varphi_{P_X}(t)=\varphi_\pi(t)φPX​​(t)=φπ​(t). As ttt was arbitrary, PXP_XPX​ and π\piπ have the same characteristic function. Both are finite measures (PX(H)=P(Ω)=1P_X(H)=P(\Omega)=1PX​(H)=P(Ω)=1) on a separable Hilbert space, and such measures are determined by their characteristic functions. Hence PX=πP_X=\piPX​=π. □\square□

The couplings ρi\rho_iρi​ need not be optimal, and only their first moments of ∥x−y∥\|x-y\|∥x−y∥ enter. Nontriviality of the filter excludes the degenerate situation in which every statement holds “eventually” and limits are not unique.
