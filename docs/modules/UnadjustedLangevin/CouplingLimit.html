01characteristicTest norm sub le §

Mathematical statement and proof

The unit-circle map is 111-Lipschitz, so the difference of the two characters is at most ∣⟨x−y,t⟩∣|\langle x-y,t\rangle|∣⟨x−y,t⟩∣. Cauchy–Schwarz then bounds this by ∥t∥∥x−y∥\|t\|\|x-y\|∥t∥∥x−y∥. Thus characteristic tests have an explicit Lipschitz constant at each fixed frequency.

02charFun marginals norm sub le §

Mathematical statement and proof

Pull both marginal characteristic integrals back to the coupling space and subtract. Each character is bounded by one, hence integrable under the probability coupling. The integral norm inequality and the pointwise Lipschitz estimate give ∣μ^(t)−π^(t)∣≤∥t∥E∥X−Y∥|\widehat\mu(t)-\widehat\pi(t)|\le\|t\|\mathbb E\|X-Y\|∣μ​(t)−π(t)∣≤∥t∥E∥X−Y∥, with the distance's integrability explicitly supplied.

03continuous charFun map Lp hilbert §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the continuous linear functional x↦⟨t,x⟩x\mapsto\langle t,x\ranglex↦⟨t,x⟩ to an HHH-valued L2L^2L2 variable. Its lifted map is continuous on L2L^2L2. The Hilbert-valued characteristic function at ttt is the scalar projected characteristic function at frequency one, so the scalar continuity theorem proves the claim.

04measure map Lp eq of coupling cost tendsto §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each ttt, the coupling inequality forces the approximating characteristic functions toward π^(t)\widehat\pi(t)π(t), since the expected distance tends to zero. Independently, L2L^2L2 convergence forces them toward PX^(t)\widehat{P_X}(t)PX​​(t). Uniqueness of limits along a nontrivial filter identifies these values, and Measure.ext_of_charFun yields PX=πP_X=\piPX​=π.
