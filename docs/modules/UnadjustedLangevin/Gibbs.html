Gibbs normalization and second moments

On a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space, differentiability and mmm-strong convexity with m>0m>0m>0 imply that π(dx)=Z−1e−U(x)dx\pi(dx)=Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}dxπ(dx)=Z−1e−U(x)dx is a probability measure with finite second moment. This establishes existence of the target measure used throughout the coupling proof.

02A Gaussian envelope from strong convexity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The first-order strong-convexity inequality at zero gives U(x)≥U(0)+⟨∇U(0),x⟩+m2∥x∥2U(x)\ge U(0)+\langle\nabla U(0),x\rangle+\frac m2\|x\|^2U(x)≥U(0)+⟨∇U(0),x⟩+2m​∥x∥2. Complete a square, or use ∥∇U(0)∥∥x∥≤m4∥x∥2+∥∇U(0)∥2/m\|\nabla U(0)\|\|x\|\le\frac m4\|x\|^2+\|\nabla U(0)\|^2/m∥∇U(0)∥∥x∥≤4m​∥x∥2+∥∇U(0)∥2/m, to obtain

U(x)≥U(0)−∥∇U(0)∥2/m+(m/4)∥x∥2.U(x)\ge U(0)-\|\nabla U(0)\|^2/m+(m/4)\|x\|^2.U(x)≥U(0)−∥∇U(0)∥2/m+(m/4)∥x∥2.

The standard finite-dimensional Gaussian integral proves integrability of e−a∥x∥2e^{-a\|x\|^2}e−a∥x∥2 for a>0a>0a>0. The displayed envelope therefore dominates e−Ue^{-U}e−U by a constant times such an integrable Gaussian.

03Polynomial moments and the partition guards §

Mathematical statement and proof

To integrate ∥x∥2e−a∥x∥2\|x\|^2e^{-a\|x\|^2}∥x∥2e−a∥x∥2, absorb the polynomial into half of the Gaussian decay, using u≤euu\le e^uu≤eu for u≥0u\ge0u≥0. Apply the same quadratic envelope to UUU. The weight is everywhere strictly positive and continuous, hence its integral is positive; its Gaussian envelope makes that integral finite. Consequently

0<Z<∞,Z=ofReal⁡ ⁣(∫e−U(x)dx).0<Z<\infty,\qquad Z=\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\int e^{-U(x)}dx\right).0<Z<∞,Z=ofReal(∫e−U(x)dx).

These guards justify taking reciprocals and converting the extended integral into a real integral.

04Probability and L² moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

The total mass of Z−1w dxZ^{-1}w\,dxZ−1wdx is Z−1Z=1Z^{-1}Z=1Z−1Z=1. The already proved weighted second-moment integrability transfers through the with-density formula and finite rescaling, giving ∫∥x∥2dπ<∞\int\|x\|^2d\pi<\infty∫∥x∥2dπ<∞. Thus the identity belongs to L2(π)L^2(\pi)L2(π), and so does x↦x−zx\mapsto x-zx↦x−z for every fixed zzz, because constants belong to L2L^2L2 under a probability measure.
