An abstract family of compatible finite-coupling references

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

This interface isolates the passage from a bound for each initial coupling to a theorem with the exact initial Wasserstein distance. A concrete product construction later supplies this interface without assuming an optimal plan.

Mathematical statement and proof

Restrict the coupling type to those with couplingCost⁡≠∞\operatorname{couplingCost}\ne\inftycouplingCost=∞. This subtype records the integrability needed to realize initial class distances and convert them to real values while taking the Wasserstein infimum.

02ReferenceFamily §

Mathematical statement and proof

For each finite coupling, supply initial and noise L2L^2L2 variables and an L2Reference. The fields identify initial law, bound initial class distance by that coupling's cost, and require one common output law across the family. These are explicit construction obligations, with no convergence estimate among them.

03ReferenceFamily.output finite §

Mathematical statement and proof

Choose one finite initial coupling using the finite initial Wasserstein premise. Its supplied numerical/reference L2L^2L2 variables yield a finite-cost output coupling. The output-law and stationary-reference fields identify its marginals, proving W2(νn,π)<∞W_2(\nu_n,\pi)<\inftyW2​(νn​,π)<∞.

04Pass the analytic bound through the infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the analytic L2L^2L2 estimate to every finite coupling realization in the family. Replace its initial error by the coupling cost and its numerical law by the common νn\nu_nνn​. Because (1−mh)n≥0(1-mh)^n\ge0(1−mh)n≥0, the affine finite-coupling infimum theorem gives the displayed bound with the true initial Wasserstein distance.
