01Identify the canonical output law §

Mathematical statement and proof

On each initial-extension product, Brownian source innovations have independent standard normal scalar coordinates, hence iid standard Gaussian vector laws, independently of initialization. The lifted-chain law theorem therefore identifies the common product output with PhkμP_h^k\muPhk​μ, where Phμ=Law⁡(X−h∇U(X)+2hZ)P_h\mu=\operatorname{Law}(X-h\nabla U(X)+\sqrt{2h}Z)Ph​μ=Law(X−h∇U(X)+2h​Z). Finite coupling and Gibbs-moment premises justify its L2L^2L2 realization.

02Derive stationarity for every reference segment §

Mathematical statement and proof

Construct a global integral solution on a horizon containing the desired segment. BrownianSegmentsGlobal identifies the chosen segment with a time shift of that solution. GibbsStationarity proves that each global value has Gibbs law, using the Metropolis refinement argument and product independence from the reference initial state. Thus independent Brownian coordinates build every ProductReference field, including stationarity, from the potential assumptions. The explicit canonical Brownian construction supplies one such source, and its normalized increments define canonicalEulerNoise.

03Guard finiteness and expose the final bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

A finite initial Wasserstein distance yields a finite initial coupling. The canonical-output identity rewrites the product output as PhkμP_h^k\muPhk​μ; the product-reference finiteness theorem proves W2(Phkμ,π)<∞W_2(P_h^k\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(Phk​μ,π)<∞. W2(Phkμ,π)≤(1−mh)kW2(μ,π)+3320Lmhd.W_2(P_h^k\mu,\pi)\le(1-mh)^kW_2(\mu,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac Lm\sqrt{hd}.W2​(Phk​μ,π)≤(1−mh)kW2​(μ,π)+2033​mL​hd​.

For 2/(m+L)≤h<2/L2/(m+L)\le h<2/L2/(m+L)≤h<2/L, the same assembly gives

W2(Phkμ,π)≤(Lh−1)kW2(μ,π)+3320Lh2−Lhhd.W_2(P_h^k\mu,\pi)\le(Lh-1)^kW_2(\mu,\pi)+\frac{33}{20}\frac{Lh}{2-Lh}\sqrt{hd}.W2​(Phk​μ,π)≤(Lh−1)kW2​(μ,π)+2033​2−LhLh​hd​.
