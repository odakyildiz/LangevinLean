The vector of Brownian coordinates defines an additive input Na(t)=2([Ba+t]−[Ba])N_a(t)=\sqrt2([B_{a+t}]-[B_a])Na​(t)=2​([Ba+t​]−[Ba​]) in L2L^2L2. It is continuous in mean square, vanishes at local time zero, and has norm 2dt\sqrt{2d}\sqrt t2d​t​ for nonnegative starting and elapsed times. These properties follow from the coordinate Gaussian increment moments.

Apply the deterministic existence theorem in the Banach space of square-integrable random variables with the Lipschitz gradient-composition map. It gives a continuous solution of the Langevin integral equation on a short interval and, by concatenation, on every finite horizon. The equation and noise moment are proved for the constructed solution; no sample-path differentiability is required.

01The Langevin-scaled Brownian input §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define Na(t)=2([Ba+t]−[Ba])N_a(t)=\sqrt2([B_{a+t}]-[B_a])Na​(t)=2​([Ba+t​]−[Ba​]) in L2L^2L2, using the real-time Brownian extension. The subtraction fixes the input at zero at local time zero, and the 2\sqrt22​ factor gives the overdamped Langevin diffusion normalization.

The initial absolute time aaa fixes which Brownian increment is used during this segment. For a,t≥0a,t\ge0a,t≥0, the input is 2(Ba+t−Ba)\sqrt2(B_{a+t}-B_a)2​(Ba+t​−Ba​), and every scalar coordinate is centered Gaussian with variance 2t2t2t. Subtracting the value at the starting time ensures that a local integral equation starts with zero additive input even when the driving Brownian path has already evolved before the segment begins.

02Continuity of the Brownian input in square mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

The real-time Brownian map is continuous in L2L^2L2 from its Gaussian increment moments. Composition with t↦a+tt\mapsto a+tt↦a+t, subtraction of the fixed class [Ba][B_a][Ba​], and scalar multiplication preserve continuity. Thus the additive input satisfies the deterministic Picard theorem's continuity requirement.

Mean-square continuity is the relevant regularity for an integral equation in L2L^2L2. The increment identity E∥Bt−Bs∥2=d∣t−s∣\mathbb E\|B_t-B_s\|^2=d|t-s|E∥Bt​−Bs​∥2=d∣t−s∣ makes the norm difference tend to zero. Translating time, subtracting one fixed vector and multiplying by 2\sqrt22​ preserve this property. Thus continuity is derived from the coordinate increment law.

03The Brownian input at zero §

Mathematical statement and proof

At local time zero both Brownian terms are [Ba][B_a][Ba​], so they cancel and their scalar multiple is zero. The defining formula gives zero by vector subtraction; no exceptional-set statement is needed because this is equality in L2L^2L2.

A local solution is prescribed to have initial state Y0Y_0Y0​. Evaluating its integral equation at zero gives Y(0)−Y0=Na(0)Y(0)-Y_0=N_a(0)Y(0)−Y0​=Na​(0), since the zero-length integral vanishes. The equality Na(0)=0N_a(0)=0Na​(0)=0 therefore enforces the intended initial condition exactly. This cancellation holds for the square-integrable random vectors themselves, regardless of which pointwise versions are used.

04The exact input norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a,t≥0a,t\ge0a,t≥0, apply the exact real-time Brownian increment moment formula. It gives ∥Na(t)∥2=2dt\|N_a(t)\|_2=\sqrt{2d}\sqrt t∥Na​(t)∥2​=2d​t​, with d=∣ι∣d=|\iota|d=∣ι∣. The scalar Gaussian increment laws prove this value; it is not an additional noise-bound hypothesis.

There are ddd scalar increment coordinates, each with variance ttt and zero mean. Therefore E∥Na(t)∥2=2∑j=1dt=2dt\mathbb E\|N_a(t)\|^2=2\sum_{j=1}^d t=2dtE∥Na​(t)∥2=2∑j=1d​t=2dt. Taking the nonnegative square root gives the exact norm. The identity for the squared Euclidean norm is a sum of coordinate squares and contains no mixed terms. Thus the norm formula uses the scalar increment laws alone and requires no spatial independence assumption.

05A short-interval square-mean Langevin solution §

Mathematical statement and proof

Supply the continuous, zero-starting NaN_aNa​ to the short-interval fixed-point theorem in the square-integrable Hilbert space. It constructs Yt−Y0=−∫0tG(∇U)(Ys)ds+Na(t)Y_t-Y_0=-\int_0^t\mathcal G(\nabla U)(Y_s)ds+N_a(t)Yt​−Y0​=−∫0t​G(∇U)(Ys​)ds+Na​(t) for Lh≤1/2Lh\le1/2Lh≤1/2. The same constructed input also satisfies the proved noise norm identity. Neither the integral equation nor its noise estimate is assumed.

On a short interval, the map taking a trial path to Y0−∫0t∇U(Ys)ds+Na(t)Y_0-\int_0^t\nabla U(Y_s)ds+N_a(t)Y0​−∫0t​∇U(Ys​)ds+Na​(t) has contraction constant at most LhLhLh in the supremum of L2L^2L2 norms. The stated restriction makes this map a contraction. The noise is only added, so its continuity is sufficient; it need not have an ordinary time derivative.

06A solution on every finite horizon §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the unrestricted finite-horizon L2L^2L2 existence theorem with the same constructed Brownian input. The resulting solution is continuous in L2L^2L2, starts at the prescribed random variable, and satisfies the gradient integral equation. The exact 2dt\sqrt{2d}\sqrt t2d​t​ noise formula is included as a proved property.

The global finite-horizon construction joins sufficiently short fixed-point intervals, using each endpoint as the next initial state. Lipschitzness of the score preserves square integrability and gives uniqueness by Grönwall. The resulting equation is therefore a solution of the gradient integral equation on the required horizon. The Brownian norm formula remains unchanged because it concerns the supplied additive input, not the unknown solution.
