An explicit Gaussian construction of pre-Brownian coordinates

The isonormal family on the time Hilbert space constructs a Gaussian process with covariance min⁡(s,t)\min(s,t)min(s,t). Independent product copies supply the Brownian coordinates required by the canonical ULA theorem. The construction proves pre-Brownian laws; continuous sample paths are not asserted or needed, because continuity is established in L2L^2L2.

01Time indicators encode Brownian covariance §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use Ht=L2(R,dx)H_t=L^2(\mathbb R,dx)Ht​=L2(R,dx) and ut=1(0,t]u_t=1_{(0,t]}ut​=1(0,t]​ for t≥0t\ge0t≥0. Their inner product is the length of the intersection, ⟨us,ut⟩=min⁡(s,t)\langle u_s,u_t\rangle=\min(s,t)⟨us​,ut​⟩=min(s,t). Choose a Hilbert basis of the time space and the independent standard normal product probability indexed by this basis. Its probability instance reuses the product-Gaussian mass-one theorem.

02Apply the isonormal map §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define Bt=I(ut)B_t=I(u_t)Bt​=I(ut​), first as an L2L^2L2 class and then by its measurable representative. Isonormality gives mean zero and covariance min⁡(s,t)\min(s,t)min(s,t); linear Gaussian projection gives all finite-dimensional Gaussian laws. A centered Gaussian process with this covariance has the pre-Brownian law, which includes independent increments and the required scalar normal increment laws.

03Take independent spatial copies §

Mathematical statement and proof

Every factor QQQ has total mass one, so its infinite product again has total mass one. On ⨂i∈IQ\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal I}Q⨂i∈I​Q, define Bi(t,ω)=Bt(ωi)B_i(t,\omega)=B_t(\omega_i)Bi​(t,ω)=Bt​(ωi​). Each projection is measure preserving, hence each coordinate keeps its pre-Brownian laws. The product construction makes the whole coordinate paths independent. Composing each path with evaluation at the grid nhnhnh preserves independence and yields independent coordinate grid paths for every h≥0h\ge0h≥0.
