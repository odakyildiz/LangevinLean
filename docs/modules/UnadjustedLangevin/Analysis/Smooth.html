Let HHH be a real Hilbert space, let L≥0L\ge0L≥0, and let f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R be differentiable at every point, with gradient ∇f\nabla f∇f (the vector representing the derivative, Df(x)[u]=⟨∇f(x),u⟩Df(x)[u]=\langle\nabla f(x),u\rangleDf(x)[u]=⟨∇f(x),u⟩). The gradient is LLL-Lipschitz if ∥∇f(u)−∇f(v)∥≤L∥u−v∥\|\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)\|\le L\|u-v\|∥∇f(u)−∇f(v)∥≤L∥u−v∥ for all u,v∈Hu,v\in Hu,v∈H. This section proves the quadratic upper bound

f(y)≤f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+L2∥y−x∥2(x,y∈H),f(y)\le f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac L2\|y-x\|^2\qquad(x,y\in H),f(y)≤f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+2L​∥y−x∥2(x,y∈H),

often called the descent lemma. Together with the quadratic lower bound of strong convexity it yields the interpolation inequality behind the sharp contraction of the gradient step, and it is also used for the moment bounds of the Gibbs measure. The proof restricts fff to the segment from xxx to yyy, subtracts the linear part and the quadratic correction, and shows that the resulting function of one variable is nonincreasing; no second derivative of fff is used.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

smooth_upper_bound takes L : ℝ≥0, a nonnegative real number (the coercion (L : ℝ) is the same number viewed in R\mathbb RR), hf : Differentiable ℝ f, and hLip : LipschitzWith L (gradient f), which is Mathlib's statement dist⁡(∇f(u),∇f(v))≤Ldist⁡(u,v)\operatorname{dist}(\nabla f(u),\nabla f(v))\le L\operatorname{dist}(u,v)dist(∇f(u),∇f(v))≤Ldist(u,v), i.e. the Lipschitz bound of the claim. The space carries [CompleteSpace H] so that gradient f is defined. The proof is the argument above.

  1. The local definitions d, a, b and q are ddd, aaa, bbb and qqq.
  2. hline is the derivative ddd of t↦x+tdt\mapsto x+tdt↦x+td, obtained from hasDerivAt_id by smul_const d and const_add x. hcomp is the chain rule (hf (x + t • d)).hasFDerivAt.comp_hasDerivAt t (hline t); the simpa step uses inner_gradient_left to write the derivative as ⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩\langle\nabla f(x+td),d\rangle⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩.
  3. hq is (a). The term after convert builds the derivative of qqq from the rules for subtracting a constant (sub_const), for the linear term ((hasDerivAt_id t).mul_const a) and for the quadratic term (((hasDerivAt_id t).pow 2).const_mul and mul_const b); the two side goals of convert ... using 1 identify the function with qqq (ext s, simp only) and the derivative with ⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩−a−Ltb\langle\nabla f(x+td),d\rangle-a-Ltb⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩−a−Ltb (ring).
  4. hq_nonpos is (b) for ttt in interior (Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1), the open interval (0,1)(0,1)(0,1): ht0 gives t≥0t\ge0t≥0; hnorm is (1), from hLip.dist_le_mul (x + t • d) x with distances written as norms; hinner is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality real_inner_le_norm; hprod multiplies hnorm by ∥d∥\|d\|∥d∥ (mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_right); after inner_sub_left, nlinarith combines them.
  5. hanti is (c): the Mathlib lemma antitoneOn_of_deriv_nonpos (a consequence of the mean value theorem) applied on the convex set Set.Icc 0 1, with continuity and differentiability obtained from hq, and with deriv q t identified with the formula of hq by (hq t).deriv.
  6. hfinal is q(1)≤q(0)q(1)\le q(0)q(1)≤q(0). The dsimp and simp only calls evaluate qqq at 000 and 111, hxy : x + (y - x) = y (proved by abel) replaces x+dx+dx+d by yyy, and linarith rearranges as in (d).
