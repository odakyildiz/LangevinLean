The quadratic upper model

Let f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R be differentiable with LLL-Lipschitz gradient, where L≥0L\ge0L≥0. For d=y−xd=y-xd=y−x, the derivative along x+tdx+tdx+td is ⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩\langle\nabla f(x+td),d\rangle⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩. Its difference from ⟨∇f(x),d⟩\langle\nabla f(x),d\rangle⟨∇f(x),d⟩ is at most Lt∥d∥2Lt\|d\|^2Lt∥d∥2 on [0,1][0,1][0,1]. Integrating gives the quadratic upper bound with remainder L∥d∥2/2L\|d\|^2/2L∥d∥2/2. The proof below also obtains this bound by showing that the segment function minus this quadratic is nonincreasing. Neither argument assumes a Hessian.

Mathematical statement and proof

Let HHH be a real Hilbert space and L≥0L\ge0L≥0. Suppose f:H→Rf:H\to\mathbb Rf:H→R is differentiable and

∥∇f(u)−∇f(v)∥≤L∥u−v∥(u,v∈H).\|\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)\|\le L\|u-v\|\quad(u,v\in H).∥∇f(u)−∇f(v)∥≤L∥u−v∥(u,v∈H).

Then for every x,yx,yx,y,

f(y)≤f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+L2∥y−x∥2.f(y)\le f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac L2\|y-x\|^2.f(y)≤f(x)+⟨∇f(x),y−x⟩+2L​∥y−x∥2.

Set d=y−xd=y-xd=y−x, a=⟨∇f(x),d⟩a=\langle\nabla f(x),d\ranglea=⟨∇f(x),d⟩, b=∥d∥2b=\|d\|^2b=∥d∥2, and define the segment function

q(t)=f(x+td)−f(x)−ta−L2t2b.q(t)=f(x+td)-f(x)-ta-\frac L2t^2b.q(t)=f(x+td)−f(x)−ta−2L​t2b.

It suffices to show q(1)≤q(0)=0q(1)\le q(0)=0q(1)≤q(0)=0.

The segment has derivative ddd. The chain rule therefore gives, for every real ttt,

ddtf(x+td)=⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩.\frac{d}{dt}f(x+td)=\langle\nabla f(x+td),d\rangle.dtd​f(x+td)=⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩.

Subtract the derivatives of the affine and quadratic terms:

q′(t)=⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩−a−Ltb.q'(t)=\langle\nabla f(x+td),d\rangle-a-Ltb.q′(t)=⟨∇f(x+td),d⟩−a−Ltb.

For ttt in the interior of [0,1][0,1][0,1], we have t≥0t\ge0t≥0. Lipschitz continuity gives

∥∇f(x+td)−∇f(x)∥≤L∥td∥=Lt∥d∥.\|\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\|\le L\|td\|=Lt\|d\|.∥∇f(x+td)−∇f(x)∥≤L∥td∥=Lt∥d∥.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

⟨∇f(x+td)−∇f(x),d⟩≤∥∇f(x+td)−∇f(x)∥ ∥d∥≤Lt∥d∥2.\langle\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x),d\rangle\le\|\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\|\,\|d\|\le Lt\|d\|^2.⟨∇f(x+td)−∇f(x),d⟩≤∥∇f(x+td)−∇f(x)∥∥d∥≤Lt∥d∥2.

Substituting aaa and bbb shows that q′(t)≤0q'(t)\le0q′(t)≤0.

The derivative formula also makes qqq continuous on [0,1][0,1][0,1] and differentiable in its interior. The derivative criterion for monotonicity now implies that qqq is nonincreasing on this interval.

Compare the two endpoints:

q(1)≤q(0),f(x+d)−f(x)−a−L2b≤0.q(1)\le q(0),\qquad f(x+d)-f(x)-a-\frac L2b\le0.q(1)≤q(0),f(x+d)−f(x)−a−2L​b≤0.

Finally x+d=x+(y−x)=yx+d=x+(y-x)=yx+d=x+(y−x)=y. Substitute the definitions of a,b,da,b,da,b,d and rearrange to obtain the quadratic upper bound.

Equivalently, integrating the segment derivative gives f(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),d⟩=∫01⟨∇f(x+td)−∇f(x),d⟩dtf(y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),d\rangle=\int_0^1\langle\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x),d\rangle dtf(y)−f(x)−⟨∇f(x),d⟩=∫01​⟨∇f(x+td)−∇f(x),d⟩dt. The integrand is bounded above by Lt∥d∥2Lt\|d\|^2Lt∥d∥2. Its integral is L∥d∥2/2L\|d\|^2/2L∥d∥2/2, which gives the stated constant. The monotonicity argument and the integral argument both use only the first derivative.
