A uniform cubic remainder for smooth test functions

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Second-order expansions of smooth compactly supported tests have a remainder bounded by a constant times ∥v∥3\|v\|^3∥v∥3. These analytic estimates support weak forward-equation calculations.

01secondOrderExpansion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define T2φ(x;v)=φ(x)+Dφ(x)[v]+12D2φ(x)[v,v]T_2\varphi(x;v)=\varphi(x)+D\varphi(x)[v]+\frac12D^2\varphi(x)[v,v]T2​φ(x;v)=φ(x)+Dφ(x)[v]+21​D2φ(x)[v,v]. The iterated Fréchet derivative is a continuous bilinear map, evaluated twice at the same displacement. This separates the deterministic polynomial from the Gaussian expectation calculations performed later.

Mathematical statement and proof

Restrict φ\varphiφ to the segment t↦x+tvt\mapsto x+tvt↦x+tv. Its third scalar derivative is D3φ(x+tv)[v,v,v]D^3\varphi(x+tv)[v,v,v]D3φ(x+tv)[v,v,v], bounded by C∥v∥3C\|v\|^3C∥v∥3. Taylor's theorem with the factorial remainder gives ∣φ(x+v)−T2φ(x;v)∣≤(C/6)∥v∥3|\varphi(x+v)-T_2\varphi(x;v)|\le(C/6)\|v\|^3∣φ(x+v)−T2​φ(x;v)∣≤(C/6)∥v∥3. Lean identifies the first three coefficients with the iterated Fréchet derivatives.

03compactSmooth secondOrderExpansion remainder §

Mathematical statement and proof

Smoothness makes D3φD^3\varphiD3φ continuous, and compact support of φ\varphiφ implies compact support of its derivatives. Hence its operator norm is globally bounded by some nonnegative constant. Apply the previous Taylor theorem and absorb the factor 1/61/61/6 into that constant to obtain a uniform cubic remainder bound.
