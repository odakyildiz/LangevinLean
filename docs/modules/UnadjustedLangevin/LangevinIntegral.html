Let the state space be a complete real normed space, let ggg be globally LLL-Lipschitz, and let BBB be continuous with B(0)=0B(0)=0B(0)=0. For a fixed initial state xxx, consider Y(t)−x=−∫0tg(Y(s))ds+B(t)Y(t)-x=-\int_0^tg(Y(s))ds+B(t)Y(t)−x=−∫0t​g(Y(s))ds+B(t). Setting Y=Z+BY=Z+BY=Z+B gives an integral equation with time-dependent drift f(t,z)=−g(z+B(t))f(t,z)=-g(z+B(t))f(t,z)=−g(z+B(t)), continuous in time and LLL-Lipschitz in zzz.

A contraction argument gives a continuous solution on a sufficiently short interval. Restarting with the preceding endpoint and shifted additive input, and concatenating finitely many such solutions, gives existence on every finite nonnegative horizon. Taking the state space to be L2(P;K)L^2(P;K)L2(P;K) gives continuity and the integral equation in mean square. This does not assert differentiability or sample-path regularity of the noise.

01Solving the integral equation on a short interval §

Mathematical statement and proof

Remove the additive input by writing Y(t)=Z(t)+B(t)Y(t)=Z(t)+B(t)Y(t)=Z(t)+B(t). The new unknown satisfies Z(t)=x+∫0tf(s,Z(s))dsZ(t)=x+\int_0^t f(s,Z(s))dsZ(t)=x+∫0t​f(s,Z(s))ds, with f(s,z)=−g(z+B(s))f(s,z)=-g(z+B(s))f(s,z)=−g(z+B(s)). The proof checks these bounds and invokes the contraction fixed-point theorem.

The chosen fixed point gives a continuous ZZZ with its integral identity. The integral identity holds for the resulting continuous real-time function on [0,h][0,h][0,h].

The shifted unknown has an ordinary time integral even if the additive input has no derivative. The short-time radius and drift bounds ensure that the Picard map preserves its closed ball, while the Lipschitz estimate controls contraction there.

02Splicing two integral-equation segments §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use Y(t)Y(t)Y(t) up to time aaa, and Z(t−a)Z(t-a)Z(t−a) afterward. The equality Z(0)=Y(a)Z(0)=Y(a)Z(0)=Y(a) makes the pasted path continuous at the join. Split ∫0t=∫0a+∫at\int_0^t=\int_0^a+\int_a^t∫0t​=∫0a​+∫at​, translate the second integral, and add the two supplied equations; the terms B(a)B(a)B(a) cancel, giving the same equation on [0,a+b][0,a+b][0,a+b].

At the join, the two path values coincide by the restart condition. For later times split the drift integral at the joining time and translate the second part; the two input increments then add to the original input difference.

03Existence on every finite horizon §

Mathematical statement and proof

Choose a positive segment length small enough that Lδ≤1/2L\delta\le1/2Lδ≤1/2. Construct the first Picard segment, then repeatedly solve with initial state at the previous endpoint and shifted input B(a+s)−B(a)B(a+s)-B(a)B(a+s)−B(a). The splicing lemma combines finitely many segments covering the prescribed horizon. The local contraction restriction determines the construction mesh and imposes no restriction on the final horizon.

A finite horizon can be covered by finitely many equal short intervals. At each restart the shifted input is continuous and starts at zero, so the same local theorem applies repeatedly with a new initial point.

04A short-interval solution in square mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the Banach-space Picard theorem to H=L2(P;K)H=L^2(P;K)H=L2(P;K), g(X)=G(∇U)(X)g(X)=\mathcal G(\nabla U)(X)g(X)=G(∇U)(X). The lifted drift has the same Lipschitz constant as ∇U\nabla U∇U. Completeness of L2L^2L2 and continuity of the supplied L2L^2L2-noise path are the exact fixed-point hypotheses.

The lifted gradient's global Lipschitz constant is unchanged, and the space of square-integrable vectors is complete. These facts verify the short-interval fixed-point assumptions in the random-vector Hilbert space.

05A finite-horizon solution in square mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use the finite-horizon Banach-space existence theorem with the lifted gradient drift. The conclusion is continuity in the L2L^2L2 norm and an equality of L2L^2L2 classes at every time in the horizon. The Bochner time integral therefore takes values in the same Hilbert space; no pathwise differentiability of the random noise is asserted.

The finite-horizon construction produces one continuous L2L^2L2-valued curve. Its Bochner drift integral is well defined by continuity, and the integral identity is asserted at each time in the prescribed horizon.
