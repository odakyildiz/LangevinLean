Existence of the additive Langevin integral equation

In a complete real normed space, a globally Lipschitz drift and a continuous additive path BBB, B(0)=0B(0)=0B(0)=0, determine a continuous solution of Yt−x=−∫0tg(Ys)ds+BtY_t-x=-\int_0^tg(Y_s)ds+B_tYt​−x=−∫0t​g(Ys​)ds+Bt​ on every finite nonnegative horizon. The construction is deterministic and later applies with the Banach space L2(P)L^2(P)L2(P).

02Splice consecutive solutions §

Mathematical statement and proof

If YYY solves on [0,a][0,a][0,a] and ZZZ starts at YaY_aYa​ and solves with shifted noise B(a+t)−B(a)B(a+t)-B(a)B(a+t)−B(a) on [0,b][0,b][0,b], define St=YtS_t=Y_tSt​=Yt​ for t≤at\le at≤a, and St=Zt−aS_t=Z_{t-a}St​=Zt−a​ otherwise. The endpoint condition makes SSS continuous. Split the drift integral at aaa and change variables in the second piece; the two equations add to the equation on [0,a+b][0,a+b][0,a+b]. Take δ=1/[2(L+1)]\delta=1/[2(L+1)]δ=1/[2(L+1)] and induct over intervals of length δ\deltaδ. An integer nnn with h≤nδh\le n\deltah≤nδ gives the desired finite-horizon solution by restriction.

03Lift to random variables in L² §

Mathematical statement and proof

The centered Lipschitz composition construction makes G(X)=∇U(X)G(X)=\nabla U(X)G(X)=∇U(X) a globally LLL-Lipschitz map on L2(P;K)L^2(P;K)L2(P;K). Completeness of that space allows the two deterministic existence theorems to apply verbatim. Hence continuous L2L^2L2-noise produces a continuous L2L^2L2-solution, first for Lh≤1/2Lh\le1/2Lh≤1/2, then on an arbitrary finite nonnegative horizon.
