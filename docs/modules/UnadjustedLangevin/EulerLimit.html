Euler approximations converge to the integral solution

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Let YYY solve the additive integral equation with continuous input BBB on [0,T][0,T][0,T], and use the same input increments in the Euler approximation. Subtracting exact and numerical increments cancels BBB. If the drift changes by at most ε\varepsilonε within each mesh interval, the one-step remainder is at most hεh\varepsilonhε, and the Euler map has Lipschitz factor 1+Lh1+Lh1+Lh.

The finite recurrence and (1+Lh)n≤eLnh(1+Lh)^n\le e^{Lnh}(1+Lh)n≤eLnh give endpoint error at most TεeLTT\varepsilon e^{LT}TεeLT when nh=Tnh=Tnh=T. Uniform continuity of the drift along the solution makes ε\varepsilonε tend to zero as the mesh tends to zero. The argument applies in a Banach space, including the space of square-integrable random variables; the latter conclusion is convergence in mean square.

01The Euler chain for a fixed additive input §

Mathematical statement and proof

Using increments of one fixed input makes the discrete and continuous equations directly comparable. The numerical error is caused by drift freezing, rather than by approximating the noise itself.

08Euler convergence with the same additive input §

Mathematical statement and proof

The continuous drift path g∘Yg\circ Yg∘Y is uniformly continuous on [0,T][0,T][0,T]. Given any positive error tolerance, choose a drift modulus ε\varepsilonε making TεeLTT\varepsilon e^{LT}TεeLT small, then take the mesh T/(n+1)T/(n+1)T/(n+1) below its continuity radius. The previous error bound gives endpoint convergence. The case T=0T=0T=0 is separated so no division by the horizon is needed.

Uniform continuity of the exact drift curve supplies a single mesh size that controls every cell of the horizon. Sending that mesh size to zero makes the remainder tolerance arbitrarily small, and hence the endpoint error tends to zero.

09Euler convergence in square mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the deterministic matching-input Euler convergence theorem in L2(P;H)L^2(P;H)L2(P;H), using the Lipschitz lifted gradient. The numerical endpoint converges in the L2L^2L2 norm to the constructed solution at TTT. Expectations of bounded Lipschitz tests then converge by Cauchy–Schwarz.

Convergence is in the norm of the random-vector Hilbert space. It therefore controls expectations of bounded Lipschitz tests by Cauchy–Schwarz, which is the form later used to identify the limiting marginal law.
