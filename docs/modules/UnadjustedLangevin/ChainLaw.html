The law of a recursion driven by independent noise

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.651.651.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

Let X\mathcal XX (states) and Z\mathcal ZZ (noise values) be measurable spaces, let ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN carry the product σ\sigmaσ-algebra generated by the coordinate maps w↦wkw\mapsto w_kw↦wk​, and let F:X×Z→XF:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\to\mathcal XF:X×Z→X be an update rule. The recursion driven by a noise sequence w=(wk)k∈Nw=(w_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}w=(wk​)k∈N​ is

Φ0(x,w)=x,Φn+1(x,w)=F(Φn(x,w),wn).\Phi_0(x,w)=x,\qquad\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F\bigl(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n\bigr).Φ0​(x,w)=x,Φn+1​(x,w)=F(Φn​(x,w),wn​).

For a measure μ\muμ on X\mathcal XX and a measure QQQ on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN we define the chain law Ln(μ,Q)=(Φn)#(μ⊗Q)\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)=(\Phi_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q)Ln​(μ,Q)=(Φn​)#​(μ⊗Q), the image of the product measure under Φn\Phi_nΦn​; here f#ν(A)=ν(f−1(A))f_\#\nu(A)=\nu(f^{-1}(A))f#​ν(A)=ν(f−1(A)) denotes the image (pushforward) of a measure. We prove that Φn\Phi_nΦn​ is measurable when FFF is, that L0(μ,Q)=μ\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)=\muL0​(μ,Q)=μ, and that every realisation of the recursion on a probability space (Ω,F,P)(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)(Ω,F,P), with an initial state XXX independent of the noise sequence WWW, has law Ln(PX,PW)\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W)Ln​(PX​,PW​), where PXP_XPX​ is the law of XXX. If the noise variables WnW_nWn​ are independent with common law η\etaη, then PWP_WPW​ is the infinite product η⊗N\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}η⊗N, so the law of the nnn-th iterate depends only on FFF, PXP_XPX​ and η\etaη. Two realisations on different probability spaces with the same input laws therefore have the same output laws.

The main application is the unadjusted Langevin algorithm on Rd\mathbb R^dRd, with update F(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h zF(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,zF(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h​z, for both the pointwise recursion and its realisation in L2L^2L2. Throughout, ⋅\sqrt{\cdot}⋅​ is the nonnegative square root, with the convention x=0\sqrt x=0x​=0 for x<0x<0x<0; the identities of this section hold for every real step hhh, and the convention only affects h<0h<0h<0, which never occurs in the estimates. Consequently the law of the algorithm does not depend on how the initial state is coupled with other random variables, provided it is independent of the noise; this is what allows the coupling argument to choose the initial coupling freely.

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Φ0(x,w)=x,Φn+1(x,w)=F(Φn(x,w),wn).\Phi_0(x,w)=x,\qquad\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F\bigl(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n\bigr).Φ0​(x,w)=x,Φn+1​(x,w)=F(Φn​(x,w),wn​).

By induction, Φn(x,w)\Phi_n(x,w)Φn​(x,w) depends only on xxx and on w0,…,wn−1w_0,\dots,w_{n-1}w0​,…,wn−1​: this is clear for n=0n=0n=0, and Φn+1\Phi_{n+1}Φn+1​ uses Φn\Phi_nΦn​ and the single additional coordinate wnw_nwn​. 04Measurability of the iterates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let X,Z\mathcal X,\mathcal ZX,Z be measurable spaces and let F:X×Z→XF:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\to\mathcal XF:X×Z→X be measurable for the product σ\sigmaσ-algebra on X×Z\mathcal X\times\mathcal ZX×Z. Then for every n∈Nn\in\mathbb Nn∈N the map (x,w)↦Φn(x,w)(x,w)\mapsto\Phi_n(x,w)(x,w)↦Φn​(x,w) is measurable from X×ZN\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}X×ZN to X\mathcal XX.

Proof. Induction on nnn.

Base case. Φ0(x,w)=x\Phi_0(x,w)=xΦ0​(x,w)=x is the first coordinate projection, which is measurable for the product σ\sigmaσ-algebra by definition of that σ\sigmaσ-algebra.

Inductive step. Assume Φn\Phi_nΦn​ is measurable. The map (x,w)↦wn(x,w)\mapsto w_n(x,w)↦wn​ is the composition of the second projection with the coordinate map w↦wnw\mapsto w_nw↦wn​, and both are measurable (the latter by definition of the product σ\sigmaσ-algebra on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN). A map into a product X×Z\mathcal X\times\mathcal ZX×Z is measurable if both of its components are, so (x,w)↦(Φn(x,w),wn)(x,w)\mapsto(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)(x,w)↦(Φn​(x,w),wn​) is measurable. Composing with the measurable FFF shows that Φn+1(x,w)=F(Φn(x,w),wn)\Phi_{n+1}(x,w)=F(\Phi_n(x,w),w_n)Φn+1​(x,w)=F(Φn​(x,w),wn​) is measurable. □\square□

Only joint measurability of FFF is used, not continuity. Measurability of Φn\Phi_nΦn​ is what makes the image measures below well defined.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

The conclusion is measurability of fun p : H × (ℕ → Z) ↦ noiseDrivenChain F p.1 p.2 n, where p.1 and p.2 are the two components of the pair. The proof is induction n with: the zero case is measurable_fst; the succ case is hF.comp (ih.prodMk ((measurable_pi_apply n).comp measurable_snd)), in which ih.prodMk pairs the measurable Φn\Phi_nΦn​ with the measurable map (x,w)↦wn(x,w)\mapsto w_n(x,w)↦wn​ (the coordinate evaluation measurable_pi_apply n after the projection measurable_snd), and hF.comp composes with FFF.

05The chain law as an image of a product measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Let μ\muμ be a measure on X\mathcal XX, QQQ a measure on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN and n∈Nn\in\mathbb Nn∈N. The law of the nnn-th iterate with initial law μ\muμ and noise-path law QQQ is the image of the product measure under Φn\Phi_nΦn​:

Ln(μ,Q)=(Φn)#(μ⊗Q),Ln(μ,Q)(A)=(μ⊗Q)({(x,w):Φn(x,w)∈A})\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)=(\Phi_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q),\qquad\mathcal L_n(\mu,Q)(A)=(\mu\otimes Q)\bigl(\{(x,w):\Phi_n(x,w)\in A\}\bigr)Ln​(μ,Q)=(Φn​)#​(μ⊗Q),Ln​(μ,Q)(A)=(μ⊗Q)({(x,w):Φn​(x,w)∈A})

The definition involves only FFF, μ\muμ and QQQ, not a probability space on which the algorithm is run.

06The initial law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let μ\muμ be a measure on X\mathcal XX and QQQ a probability measure on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN. Then L0(μ,Q)=μ\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)=\muL0​(μ,Q)=μ.

Proof. Since Φ0(x,w)=x\Phi_0(x,w)=xΦ0​(x,w)=x, the map Φ0\Phi_0Φ0​ is the first projection. For measurable A⊆XA\subseteq\mathcal XA⊆X the preimage is the rectangle A×ZNA\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}A×ZN, and the product measure of a rectangle is the product of the measures of its sides:

L0(μ,Q)(A)=(μ⊗Q)(A×ZN)=μ(A) Q(ZN)=μ(A)⋅1=μ(A).\mathcal L_0(\mu,Q)(A)=(\mu\otimes Q)\bigl(A\times\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}\bigr)=\mu(A)\,Q(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})=\mu(A)\cdot1=\mu(A).L0​(μ,Q)(A)=(μ⊗Q)(A×ZN)=μ(A)Q(ZN)=μ(A)⋅1=μ(A).

Two measures that agree on all measurable sets are equal. □\square□

The hypothesis Q(ZN)=1Q(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})=1Q(ZN)=1 is used in the third equality; for a general finite QQQ the result would be Q(ZN) μQ(\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N})\,\muQ(ZN)μ.

07The law of an independent realisation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Recall that random variables X:Ω→XX:\Omega\to\mathcal XX:Ω→X and W:Ω→ZNW:\Omega\to\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}W:Ω→ZN on a probability space (Ω,F,P)(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)(Ω,F,P) are independent if P(X∈A, W∈B)=P(X∈A) P(W∈B)P(X\in A,\ W\in B)=P(X\in A)\,P(W\in B)P(X∈A, W∈B)=P(X∈A)P(W∈B) for all measurable AAA, BBB; equivalently, the joint law of (X,W)(X,W)(X,W) is the product of the marginal laws, P(X,W)=PX⊗PWP_{(X,W)}=P_X\otimes P_WP(X,W)​=PX​⊗PW​ (two measures on a product space that agree on measurable rectangles agree, by the uniqueness of extension from a π\piπ-system).

Claim. Let PPP be a probability measure on Ω\OmegaΩ, FFF measurable, X:Ω→XX:\Omega\to\mathcal XX:Ω→X and W:Ω→ZNW:\Omega\to\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}W:Ω→ZN almost-everywhere measurable and independent. Then for every nnn,

PΦn(X,W)=Ln(PX,PW),P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W),PΦn​(X,W)​=Ln​(PX​,PW​),

where Φn(X,W)\Phi_n(X,W)Φn​(X,W) is the random variable ω↦Φn(X(ω),W(ω))\omega\mapsto\Phi_n(X(\omega),W(\omega))ω↦Φn​(X(ω),W(ω)).

Proof. The random variable Φn(X,W)\Phi_n(X,W)Φn​(X,W) is the composition of ω↦(X(ω),W(ω))\omega\mapsto(X(\omega),W(\omega))ω↦(X(ω),W(ω)) with Φn\Phi_nΦn​. The image of a measure under a composition is the iterated image, (Φn∘(X,W))#P=(Φn)#((X,W)#P)(\Phi_n\circ(X,W))_\#P=(\Phi_n)_\#\bigl((X,W)_\#P\bigr)(Φn​∘(X,W))#​P=(Φn​)#​((X,W)#​P); this holds for the measurable Φn\Phi_nΦn​ (previous measurability step) and the almost-everywhere measurable pair (X,W)(X,W)(X,W). By independence (X,W)#P=PX⊗PW(X,W)_\#P=P_X\otimes P_W(X,W)#​P=PX​⊗PW​. Hence

PΦn(X,W)=(Φn)#(PX⊗PW)=Ln(PX,PW).□P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=(\Phi_n)_\#\bigl(P_X\otimes P_W\bigr)=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W).\qquad\squarePΦn​(X,W)​=(Φn​)#​(PX​⊗PW​)=Ln​(PX​,PW​).□

08Independent identically distributed innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Recall that W0,W1,…W_0,W_1,\dotsW0​,W1​,… are (mutually) independent if for all finite sets J⊆NJ\subseteq\mathbb NJ⊆N and measurable Bj⊆ZB_j\subseteq\mathcal ZBj​⊆Z, P(Wj∈Bj ∀j∈J)=∏j∈JP(Wj∈Bj)P(W_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J)=\prod_{j\in J}P(W_j\in B_j)P(Wj​∈Bj​ ∀j∈J)=∏j∈J​P(Wj​∈Bj​). For a probability measure η\etaη on Z\mathcal ZZ, the infinite product η⊗N\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}η⊗N is the unique probability measure on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN with η⊗N({w:wj∈Bj ∀j∈J})=∏j∈Jη(Bj)\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}(\{w:w_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J\})=\prod_{j\in J}\eta(B_j)η⊗N({w:wj​∈Bj​ ∀j∈J})=∏j∈J​η(Bj​) for all such JJJ and BjB_jBj​.

Claim. Let PPP be a probability measure, FFF measurable, X:Ω→XX:\Omega\to\mathcal XX:Ω→X and Wk:Ω→ZW_k:\Omega\to\mathcal ZWk​:Ω→Z (k∈Nk\in\mathbb Nk∈N) almost-everywhere measurable, with XXX independent of the sequence W=(Wk)k∈NW=(W_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}W=(Wk​)k∈N​, the WkW_kWk​ mutually independent, and PWk=ηP_{W_k}=\etaPWk​​=η for every kkk. Then for every nnn,

PΦn(X,W)=Ln(PX,η⊗N).P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n\bigl(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}\bigr).PΦn​(X,W)​=Ln​(PX​,η⊗N).

Proof. Path law. The map ω↦(Wk(ω))k\omega\mapsto(W_k(\omega))_{k}ω↦(Wk​(ω))k​ into ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN is almost-everywhere measurable, because each coordinate is and the index set N\mathbb NN is countable. For finite JJJ and measurable BjB_jBj​, independence and PWj=ηP_{W_j}=\etaPWj​​=η give

PW({w:wj∈Bj ∀j∈J})=∏j∈JP(Wj∈Bj)=∏j∈Jη(Bj),P_W\bigl(\{w:w_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J\}\bigr)=\prod_{j\in J}P(W_j\in B_j)=\prod_{j\in J}\eta(B_j),PW​({w:wj​∈Bj​ ∀j∈J})=j∈J∏​P(Wj​∈Bj​)=j∈J∏​η(Bj​),

so by the uniqueness of the infinite product, PW=η⊗NP_W=\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}PW​=η⊗N.

Conclusion. The previous step, applied to XXX and the sequence WWW, gives PΦn(X,W)=Ln(PX,PW)=Ln(PX,η⊗N)P_{\Phi_n(X,W)}=\mathcal L_n(P_X,P_W)=\mathcal L_n(P_X,\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N})PΦn​(X,W)​=Ln​(PX​,PW​)=Ln​(PX​,η⊗N). □\square□
