A noise-driven recursion has a law independent of its realization

For measurable F(x,z)F(x,z)F(x,z), the recursion Xn+1=F(Xn,Zn)X_{n+1}=F(X_n,Z_n)Xn+1​=F(Xn​,Zn​) has a law determined by the initial law and the full noise law when the initialization and noise are independent.

At index kkk, the state depends only on initialization and previous innovations. Under the stated independence hypotheses the pair formed by that state and the next innovation has product law. Pushing this pair through the update map yields the transition-law recursion. Induction consequently shows that any two realizations with the same initial law and innovation law have the same state law at every index, independently of their particular sample spaces.

01The recursion driven by a noise sequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define X0=xX_0=xX0​=x, Xn+1=F(Xn,wn)X_{n+1}=F(X_n,w_n)Xn+1​=F(Xn​,wn​) for any update and noise path. No distribution assumptions are part of the recursion; they enter only when a law is assigned to its inputs.

For a fixed input (x,w)(x,w)(x,w), the finite-time state uses only the first finitely many coordinates of the innovation path. No probability measure is needed to evaluate it. This separates the deterministic algorithm from its probabilistic realization and permits the same recursion to be applied on canonical product sources and on user-supplied probability spaces.

02The noise-driven chain at zero §

Mathematical statement and proof

The zero clause of the recursion evaluates to the supplied initial state. This defining identity is the base case for all law and measurability inductions.

Before any update, the output is exactly xxx, independently of the innovation path. Consequently its law under an independent input product must be the initial marginal. The same identity also starts the induction proving that two deterministic recursions with the same update and inputs agree at every finite step.

04Measurability of the noise-driven chain §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct on nnn. The base map is the initial-state projection. At the successor, pair the measurable current state with the measurable coordinate evaluation w↦wnw\mapsto w_nw↦wn​, then compose with the measurable uncurried update. Thus each iterate is a measurable function of initialization and the full noise path.

The current state is a measurable function of the joint initialization/path input by induction. Coordinate evaluation on path space is measurable, so pairing it with that current state gives a measurable pair. Composing with the measurable update proves the successor claim. No regularity beyond measurability is needed for this law-level recursion theorem.

05The marginal law of the recursion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Push μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q through the measurable recursion output at index nnn. The product gives independent initialization and noise path. This canonical law depends only on the initial law, noise-path law, and update, not on a particular sample-space construction.

If FnF_nFn​ denotes the finite recursion map, define its canonical distribution by (Fn)#(μ⊗Q)(F_n)_\#(\mu\otimes Q)(Fn​)#​(μ⊗Q). The product specifies independence of initialization and the entire innovation path while allowing arbitrary dependence within QQQ. Thus this definition applies before specializing to iid noise and makes the exact inputs determining the output distribution explicit.

06Its initial law §

Mathematical statement and proof

At index zero the output map is first-coordinate projection. Since QQQ has mass one, the first marginal of μ⊗Q\mu\otimes Qμ⊗Q is exactly μ\muμ. This proves that the canonical law starts from the supplied initial law.

For a measurable initial-state set AAA, the probability of the zero-step output lying in AAA is (μ⊗Q)(A×ΩQ)=μ(A)Q(ΩQ)=μ(A)(\mu\otimes Q)(A\times\Omega_Q)=\mu(A)Q(\Omega_Q)=\mu(A)(μ⊗Q)(A×ΩQ​)=μ(A)Q(ΩQ​)=μ(A). The noise source's unit mass is the only normalization needed here. This direct marginal computation proves that the canonical construction preserves the specified initialization.

07Law identification under independence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Independence gives the joint input law P(X,W)=PX⊗PWP_{(X,W)}=P_X\otimes P_WP(X,W)​=PX​⊗PW​. Push that identity through the measurable recursion output and use pushforward composition. The resulting output law is exactly map w) n.

Independence means that the joint law of initialization and complete noise path factors into their two marginals. Applying the same measurable recursion to this equality yields equality of output laws. In particular the result does not require the input variables to have a concrete product-space representation; their factorized joint distribution is sufficient.

08Law identification for independent identical innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Joint independence of the innovations and their common law η\etaη identify the entire noise-path law with ⨂nη\bigotimes_n\eta⨂n​η. Substitute this identity into the independent-initialization law theorem. Countability of the time index supplies path-valued measurability.

For iid innovations with common law η\etaη, every finite cylinder event factors as a product of η\etaη-probabilities. These cylinder probabilities identify the complete path law as η⊗N\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}η⊗N. Substituting this law in the independent-input result shows that the algorithm's distribution is determined by FFF, μ\muμ and η\etaη, with no remaining source-specific information.

09Uniqueness of the resulting marginal laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the canonical iid-law formula on both probability spaces. Equality of their initial laws and the shared innovation law makes the right sides identical, so their iterate laws agree. The theorem compares distributions without coupling the two constructions.

Both realizations are measurable images of the same abstract input probability μ⊗η⊗N\mu\otimes\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}μ⊗η⊗N. Hence each measurable output set receives the same probability in both. The conclusion is equality of their distributions at a fixed finite iteration, even though their random variables live on different spaces and no joint coupling between them was specified.

10Equality with the ULA recursion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct on the index with F(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2hzF(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}zF(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h​z. At zero both recursions return the same state. If they agree at index nnn, their successor equations apply the same function to the same state and innovation, so they agree at index n+1n+1n+1. This identifies the generic noise-driven recursion with ULA pointwise.

With the Euler update F(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h zF(x,z)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,zF(x,z)=x−h∇U(x)+2h​z, both pointwise constructions have the same initial clause and the same successor clause. Applying the induction hypothesis inside this common function proves equality at the next step. The argument is purely deterministic, so it can be reused with every supplied innovation sequence before assigning Gaussian laws.

11Measurability of the ULA update §

Mathematical statement and proof

Lipschitzness makes ∇U\nabla U∇U continuous. The joint ULA update is obtained from coordinate projections and that gradient by scalar multiplication and addition, hence is measurable. This discharges the generic recursion's update-measurability premise.

The map (x,z)↦x−h∇U(x)+2h z(x,z)\mapsto x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z(x,z)↦x−h∇U(x)+2h​z is jointly continuous because the gradient is Lipschitz and hence continuous. Coordinate projections, addition and scalar multiplication preserve continuity. Thus its measurability does not require a step restriction, strong convexity, or Gaussianity; those hypotheses enter only the later moment and convergence estimates.

12The ULA law under independent identical innovations §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use measurability of the Gaussian Euler update in the generic iid law theorem, then rewrite the generic recursion using equality of the two Gaussian Euler recursions. The result identifies the actual ULA construction with its canonical product-noise law.

Apply the general iid realization formula to the measurable Euler update. The deterministic recursion equality identifies its output with the actual Euler chain. Its time-nnn law is therefore the canonical pushforward of the independent initialization and iid innovation path. This is the distributional bridge used to compare numerical chains constructed on different coupling extensions.

13The square-integrable chain has the same law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The L2L^2L2 chain representative agrees almost everywhere with the pointwise ULA recursion of the input representatives. Apply the pointwise law theorem and transfer pushforwards across that almost-everywhere equality. Quotient representative choices therefore do not change the algorithm law.

At every fixed finite index, the square-integrable recursion and its ordinary pointwise update agree almost surely. Almost-surely equal measurable random variables have the same pushforward law. The pointwise iid theorem therefore transfers to the square-integrable realization. This transfer ensures that analytic L2L^2L2 estimates apply to the actual algorithm law, without depending on choices of representative random functions.
