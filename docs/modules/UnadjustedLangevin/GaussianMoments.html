01Second moment of a centered Gaussian §

Mathematical statement and proof

For nonnegative variance vvv, the centered Gaussian law has second moment

∫Rx2 dN(0,v)(x)=v.\int_{\mathbb R}x^2\,d\mathcal N(0,v)(x)=v.∫R​x2dN(0,v)(x)=v.

The known Gaussian variance is vvv. Expand variance as ∫(x−E[x])2\int(x-\mathbb E[x])^2∫(x−E[x])2, and insert the Gaussian mean E[x]=0\mathbb E[x]=0E[x]=0.

Simplify x−0=xx-0=xx−0=x. The variance integral is now exactly the required second moment.

The variance parameter is the second argument of N(0,v)\mathcal N(0,v)N(0,v), not its standard deviation. Centering therefore makes the variance integral exactly the squared-variable integral. At v=0v=0v=0, the Gaussian is a point mass at zero and the same identity gives zero, so no positive-variance exception is needed.

02Gaussian coordinates give a square-integrable vector §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let X:Ω→EdX:\Omega\to E_dX:Ω→Ed​, and suppose every coordinate satisfies Xi∼N(0,v)X_i\sim\mathcal N(0,v)Xi​∼N(0,v) under PPP, with v≥0v\ge0v≥0. Then X∈L2(P;Ed)X\in L^2(P;E_d)X∈L2(P;Ed​).

In finite-dimensional Euclidean space, square integrability is equivalent to square integrability of each coordinate. Each coordinate belongs to L2L^2L2 because its Gaussian law has a finite second moment; transfer that membership along the equality of laws.

A finite sum of integrable nonnegative coordinate squares is integrable. Conversely each coordinate square is bounded by the squared Euclidean norm. These two inequalities prove the equivalence between vector and coordinate L2L^2L2 membership in finite dimension. Coordinate independence is not used for this membership statement.

03Sum the coordinate moments §

Mathematical statement and proof

Under the same coordinate Gaussian laws,

∫Ω∥X(ω)∥2 dP(ω)=dv.\int_\Omega\|X(\omega)\|^2\,dP(\omega)=dv.∫Ω​∥X(ω)∥2dP(ω)=dv.

For each coordinate iii, retain the proved square integrability Xi∈L2(P)X_i\in L^2(P)Xi​∈L2(P). It will justify integrating the finite sum of squared coordinates.

Change variables using the law of XiX_iXi​, then apply the scalar Gaussian moment:

∫ΩXi2 dP=∫Rx2 dN(0,v)=v.\int_\Omega X_i^2\,dP=\int_{\mathbb R}x^2\,d\mathcal N(0,v)=v.∫Ω​Xi2​dP=∫R​x2dN(0,v)=v.

Expand the squared Euclidean norm and interchange the finite sum and integral:

∫Ω∥X∥2 dP=∑i=0d−1∫ΩXi2 dP.\int_\Omega\|X\|^2\,dP=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\int_\Omega X_i^2\,dP.∫Ω​∥X∥2dP=i=0∑d−1​∫Ω​Xi2​dP.

Every summand is integrable by its L2L^2L2 membership.

Each of the ddd summands equals vvv; hence their sum is dvdvdv.

No cross terms occur in the identity for the squared Euclidean norm. Thus possible correlations between distinct coordinates do not change this second moment; their individual variances determine it. The finite sum may be exchanged with the integral because each square has already been proved integrable.

04The Gaussian vector as an L2L^2L2 class §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let [X][X][X] be the L2L^2L2 equivalence class constructed from XXX, whose coordinates have laws N(0,v)\mathcal N(0,v)N(0,v). Then

∥[X]∥L22=dv.\|[X]\|_{L^2}^2=dv.∥[X]∥L22​=dv.

Express the squared L2L^2L2 norm as the self-inner-product, then use the definition of the L2L^2L2 inner product as an integral.

The chosen representative of [X][X][X] equals XXX almost everywhere. Therefore the integrand ⟨[X](ω),[X](ω)⟩\langle[X](\omega),[X](\omega)\rangle⟨[X](ω),[X](ω)⟩ agrees almost everywhere with ∥X(ω)∥2\|X(\omega)\|^2∥X(ω)∥2, so the two integrals agree.

Substitute the integral identity and the vector second-moment calculation ∫∥X∥2 dP=dv\int\|X\|^2\,dP=dv∫∥X∥2dP=dv.

An L2L^2L2 class identifies random vectors that agree almost everywhere. The norm is independent of the chosen representative, so the second-moment calculation for the original random vector computes the class norm as well. The inner-product formula computes the norm without specifying values on exceptional null sets.

05The square-root Gaussian norm §

Mathematical statement and proof

For the same Gaussian coordinate laws,

∥[X]∥L2=dv.\|[X]\|_{L^2}=\sqrt{dv}.∥[X]∥L2​=dv​.

This includes d=0d=0d=0 and v=0v=0v=0.

Replace dvdvdv by ∥[X]∥2\|[X]\|^2∥[X]∥2. Since norms are nonnegative, ∥[X]∥2=∥[X]∥\sqrt{\|[X]\|^2}=\|[X]\|∥[X]∥2​=∥[X]∥.

The argument takes the unique nonnegative square root of dv≥0dv\ge0dv≥0. A sign choice is therefore unnecessary, and the formula remains valid at both degenerate endpoints. This is the norm of the Gaussian vector in the L2L^2L2 reference space.

06Include the Langevin diffusion factor §

Mathematical statement and proof

If every coordinate of XXX has law N(0,v)\mathcal N(0,v)N(0,v), then the Langevin-scaled vector satisfies

∥2[X]∥L2=2dv.\|\sqrt2[X]\|_{L^2}=\sqrt{2d}\sqrt v.∥2​[X]∥L2​=2d​v​.

07Assemble Brownian coordinates §

Mathematical statement and proof

For scalar processes Wi:[0,∞)×Ω→RW_i:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb RWi​:[0,∞)×Ω→R, define the Euclidean vector

Wt(ω):=(W0(t,ω),…,Wd−1(t,ω))∈Ed.W_t(\omega):=(W_0(t,\omega),\ldots,W_{d-1}(t,\omega))\in E_d.Wt​(ω):=(W0​(t,ω),…,Wd−1​(t,ω))∈Ed​.

Collect the coordinates at the fixed time ttt and outcome ω\omegaω, using the finite-dimensional ℓ2\ell^2ℓ2 norm.

Assembling the coordinates changes the codomain from a finite family of scalar variables to Rd\mathbb R^dRd. Evaluating coordinate iii of the resulting vector simply recovers the original scalar variable at that index. 11Define an increment from any starting time §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a,t≥0a,t\ge0a,t≥0, define the vector increment over the interval [a,a+t][a,a+t][a,a+t] by

ΔaWt(ω):=Wa+t(ω)−Wa(ω).\Delta_aW_t(\omega):=W_{a+t}(\omega)-W_a(\omega).Δa​Wt​(ω):=Wa+t​(ω)−Wa​(ω).

Subtract the two Euclidean vectors at the interval endpoints. Each coordinate is the corresponding scalar Brownian increment.

The elapsed time parameter is kept separate from the starting time. This permits every reference step to be described on its local interval [0,h][0,h][0,h], while its underlying Brownian input is taken from the global interval [a,a+h][a,a+h][a,a+h]. Vector subtraction is performed coordinatewise, so scalar increment laws apply immediately.

12Increment variance is elapsed time §

Mathematical statement and proof

Under the scalar pre-Brownian assumptions, for every a,t≥0a,t\ge0a,t≥0 and every coordinate iii,

Wi(a+t)−Wi(a)∼N(0,t).W_i(a+t)-W_i(a)\sim\mathcal N(0,t).Wi​(a+t)−Wi​(a)∼N(0,t).

It remains to identify that variance. As real numbers,

∣(a+t)−a∣=∣t∣=t,|(a+t)-a|=|t|=t,∣(a+t)−a∣=∣t∣=t,

The increment law depends only on elapsed time. Consequently the same scalar Gaussian moment bounds apply on every reference segment of the same length.
