Passing from a chosen initial coupling to an infimum

References and publication status for this development

Paper references

The two step-size regimes and the coefficient 1.65 follow Theorem 1(a)–(b) of Dalalyan and Karagulyan (2019). Dalalyan (2017), Theorem 1 and Section 6, gives the earlier synchronous-coupling argument.

  • Arnak S. Dalalyan and Avetik G. Karagulyan (2019). User-friendly guarantees for the Langevin Monte Carlo with inaccurate gradient. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 129(12), 5278–5311. arXiv
  • Arnak Dalalyan (2017). Further and stronger analogy between sampling and optimization: Langevin Monte Carlo and gradient descent. Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Learning Theory, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 65, 678–689.

Let the output measures be fixed and assume that every initial coupling gives an affine bound in its cost with the same constants. Taking the infimum over couplings gives the corresponding bound in W2(μ,ν)W_2(\mu,\nu)W2​(μ,ν). This uses the infimum property and does not assume an optimal coupling.

If the initial transport distance is finite, a finite-cost coupling exists. Approximate minimizing couplings then suffice even if the assumed estimate is stated only for finite-cost couplings. When the affine coefficient is zero, one such coupling already proves the constant bound. When it is positive, couplings with cost approaching the infimum give the result by taking a limit.

01A uniform affine estimate passes to the infimum §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let a≥0a\ge0a≥0 be finite, b∈[0,+∞]b\in[0,+\infty]b∈[0,+∞], and Γ(μ,ν)≠∅\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\ne\varnothingΓ(μ,ν)=∅. If the fixed output measures satisfy

W2(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+bfor every γ∈Γ(μ,ν),W_2(\mu',\nu')\le ac(\gamma)+b\quad\text{for every }\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu),W2​(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+bfor every γ∈Γ(μ,ν),

then W2(μ′,ν′)≤aW2(μ,ν)+bW_2(\mu',\nu')\le aW_2(\mu,\nu)+bW2​(μ′,ν′)≤aW2​(μ,ν)+b.

Use the nonempty coupling family and expand transport distances as infima.

Multiplication by finite a≥0a\ge0a≥0 and addition of bbb commute with the nonempty extended-real infimum, including a=0a=0a=0. Thus aW2(μ,ν)+b=inf⁡γ(ac(γ)+b)aW_2(\mu,\nu)+b=\inf_\gamma(ac(\gamma)+b)aW2​(μ,ν)+b=infγ​(ac(γ)+b).

The output distance lies below every member of this last family, hence below its infimum.

For a>0a>0a>0, choose couplings whose costs approach the infimum and apply the estimate to each one. Multiplication by aaa and addition of bbb commute with this monotone affine approximation. At a=0a=0a=0, the conclusion is simply the common constant bound bbb, so no optimizer or limiting transport cost is required.

02A finite infimum supplies a finite-cost coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

If W2(μ,ν)<+∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<+\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<+∞, then some γ∈Γ(μ,ν)\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)γ∈Γ(μ,ν) has c(γ)<+∞c(\gamma)<+\inftyc(γ)<+∞. Attainment of the infimum is not asserted.

The coupling-cost infimum is strictly below +∞+\infty+∞ by the finiteness hypothesis.

An infimum strictly below a threshold has a member strictly below that threshold. Apply this with threshold +∞+\infty+∞.

If every admissible coupling had infinite cost, the infimum of their costs would also be infinite. Thus a finite infimum provides at least one finite-cost plan. More quantitatively, an approximate minimizer with cost below the finite infimum plus one is sufficient to establish the required finiteness.

03The real uniform-coupling estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 be finite and assume W2(μ,ν)<+∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<+\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<+∞. If W2(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+bW_2(\mu',\nu')\le ac(\gamma)+bW2​(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+b for every initial coupling, then

W2(μ′,ν′)≤aW2(μ,ν)+b.W_2(\mu',\nu')\le aW_2(\mu,\nu)+b.W2​(μ′,ν′)≤aW2​(μ,ν)+b.

A finite initial distance supplies a coupling. Apply the extended uniform-coupling lemma to obtain the desired inequality in extended reals.

The product aW2(μ,ν)aW_2(\mu,\nu)aW2​(μ,ν) is finite.

Its sum with finite bbb is finite, so the extended output inequality permits monotone real conversion.

Distribute real conversion through the finite sum and product to obtain the real inequality.

The extended estimate is first applied while retaining finite transport and coupling values. Their embeddings and real values then agree, so the affine inequality may be read in ordinary real arithmetic. Every quantity used in this real inequality is finite by the preceding bounds.

04Finite-cost initial couplings suffice §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0 be finite and assume W2(μ,ν)<∞W_2(\mu,\nu)<\inftyW2​(μ,ν)<∞ and W2(μ′,ν′)<∞W_2(\mu',\nu')<\inftyW2​(μ′,ν′)<∞. Suppose every finite-cost initial coupling satisfies

W2(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+b(γ∈Γ(μ,ν), c(γ)<∞).W_2(\mu',\nu')\le a c(\gamma)+b\qquad (\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu),\ c(\gamma)<\infty).W2​(μ′,ν′)≤ac(γ)+b(γ∈Γ(μ,ν), c(γ)<∞).

For every ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, finiteness of the initial infimum supplies a coupling γε\gamma_\varepsilonγε​ with

c(γε)<W2(μ,ν)+ε<∞.c(\gamma_\varepsilon)<W_2(\mu,\nu)+\varepsilon<\infty.c(γε​)<W2​(μ,ν)+ε<∞.

Insert this coupling into the uniform estimate. For a>0a>0a>0, let ε↓0\varepsilon\downarrow0ε↓0 to obtain

W2(μ′,ν′)≤aW2(μ,ν)+b.W_2(\mu',\nu')\le aW_2(\mu,\nu)+b.W2​(μ′,ν′)≤aW2​(μ,ν)+b.

If a=0a=0a=0, any one of these finite-cost couplings already gives the conclusion W2(μ′,ν′)≤bW_2(\mu',\nu')\le bW2​(μ′,ν′)≤b. Thus no expression involving zero times infinite cost is needed. Infinite-cost couplings cannot improve a finite infimum, and no minimizing coupling is assumed to exist.
