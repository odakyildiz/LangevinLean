01The Euclidean state space §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a natural number ddd, write Ed=RdE_d=\mathbb R^dEd​=Rd with its Euclidean inner product and norm. Vectors may be represented as real-valued functions on the finite index set {0,…,d−1}\{0,\ldots,d-1\}{0,…,d−1}; when d=0d=0d=0, this index set is empty.

Introduce the abbreviation

Ed:=Rd.E_d:=\mathbb R^d.Ed​:=Rd.

This equips the coordinate functions with the Euclidean norm ∥x∥=(∑i=0d−1xi2)1/2\|x\|=(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}x_i^2)^{1/2}∥x∥=(∑i=0d−1​xi2​)1/2, so the subsequent gradient and distance estimates use the usual geometry of Rd\mathbb R^dRd.

The coordinate expression for the inner product is ⟨x,y⟩=∑i=0d−1xiyi\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}x_iy_i⟨x,y⟩=∑i=0d−1​xi​yi​. In particular, coordinatewise addition and scalar multiplication agree with the Euclidean vector operations used by the update. When d=0d=0d=0, the empty coordinate list gives one state and a zero norm; later nondegenerate arguments separate this case explicitly.

02One unadjusted Langevin step §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a potential U:Ed→RU:E_d\to\mathbb RU:Ed​→R, a real step size hhh, a state x∈Edx\in E_dx∈Ed​, and a noise vector z∈Edz\in E_dz∈Ed​, define

Thz(x)=x−h∇U(x)+2h z.T_h^z(x)=x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z.Thz​(x)=x−h∇U(x)+2h​z.

The definition itself imposes no sign condition on hhh; later contraction results assume h≥0h\ge0h≥0.

The update consists of the current state, the drift increment −h∇U(x)-h\nabla U(x)−h∇U(x), and the noise increment 2h z\sqrt{2h}\,z2h​z.

For h>0h>0h>0 and standard Gaussian zzz, the conditional covariance of the noise increment is 2hId2hI_d2hId​. The deterministic center is x−h∇U(x)x-h\nabla U(x)x−h∇U(x), so the update is the Euler discretization of drift −∇U-\nabla U−∇U and diffusion 2\sqrt22​. The definition remains a deterministic function until a law for its inputs is specified.

03Shared noise cancels §

Mathematical statement and proof

For every potential U:Ed→RU:E_d\to\mathbb RU:Ed​→R, h∈Rh\in\mathbb Rh∈R, and vectors x,y,z∈Edx,y,z\in E_dx,y,z∈Ed​,

Thz(x)−Thz(y)=(x−h∇U(x))−(y−h∇U(y)).T_h^z(x)-T_h^z(y)=(x-h\nabla U(x))-(y-h\nabla U(y)).Thz​(x)−Thz​(y)=(x−h∇U(x))−(y−h∇U(y)).

Expand the two updates. The two copies of 2h z\sqrt{2h}\,z2h​z subtract to zero:

(x−h∇U(x)+2h z)−(y−h∇U(y)+2h z)=(x−h∇U(x))−(y−h∇U(y)).(x-h\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,z)-(y-h\nabla U(y)+\sqrt{2h}\,z)=(x-h\nabla U(x))-(y-h\nabla U(y)).(x−h∇U(x)+2h​z)−(y−h∇U(y)+2h​z)=(x−h∇U(x))−(y−h∇U(y)).

The cancellation uses the same realization of zzz, rather than merely two noises with the same distribution. If the noises differed, an additional term 2h(z−z′)\sqrt{2h}(z-z')2h​(z−z′) would remain and would need its own moment estimate. With equal innovations the additional term is zero, so the deterministic Euler bound applies to the update difference.

04The initial shared-noise estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose U:Ed→RU:E_d\to\mathbb RU:Ed​→R is differentiable and mmm-strongly convex, ∇U\nabla U∇U is LLL-Lipschitz with L≥0L\ge0L≥0, and h≥0h\ge0h≥0. For every x,y,z∈Edx,y,z\in E_dx,y,z∈Ed​,

∥Thz(x)−Thz(y)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2 ∥x−y∥.\|T_h^z(x)-T_h^z(y)\|\le\sqrt{1-2hm+h^2L^2}\,\|x-y\|.∥Thz​(x)−Thz​(y)∥≤1−2hm+h2L2​∥x−y∥.

For h>0h>0h>0 this factor is strictly below one whenever hL2<2mhL^2<2mhL2<2m.

Cancel the common noise. The left-hand side becomes the distance between the two deterministic gradient steps.

Strong convexity gives

m∥x−y∥2≤⟨∇U(x)−∇U(y),x−y⟩,m\|x-y\|^2\le\langle\nabla U(x)-\nabla U(y),x-y\rangle,m∥x−y∥2≤⟨∇U(x)−∇U(y),x−y⟩,

and the Lipschitz assumption gives ∥∇U(x)−∇U(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥\|\nabla U(x)-\nabla U(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|∥∇U(x)−∇U(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥. Insert these two inequalities into the Euler-step square-root bound.

The coefficient is independent of the noise distribution because cancellation occurs before taking a norm. Hence the inequality holds pointwise and may be integrated against any coupled starting law. A strict contraction requires 1−2hm+h2L2<11-2hm+h^2L^2<11−2hm+h2L2<1; for h>0h>0h>0, this is the stated inequality between the linear and quadratic terms in the coefficient.
