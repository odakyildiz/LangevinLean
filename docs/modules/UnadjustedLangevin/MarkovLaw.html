which is the law of F(X,ξ)F(X,\xi)F(X,ξ) when X∼μX\sim\muX∼μ and ξ∼η\xi\sim\etaξ∼η are independent, and its iterates μ0=μ\mu_0=\muμ0​=μ, μn+1=Mη(μn)\mu_{n+1}=M_\eta(\mu_n)μn+1​=Mη​(μn​). The main result is that for measurable FFF and probability measures μ\muμ, η\etaη,

the law of the recursion driven by independent identically distributed noise is the nnn-fold iterate of the one-step transition. The key point is that under μ⊗η⊗N\mu\otimes\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}μ⊗η⊗N the current state Φn(x,w)\Phi_n(x,w)Φn​(x,w) is independent of the next noise value wnw_nwn​; this is proved, not assumed, by writing Φn(x,w)\Phi_n(x,w)Φn​(x,w) as a measurable function of xxx and the finite prefix (w0,…,wn−1)(w_0,\dots,w_{n-1})(w0​,…,wn−1​), which is independent of wnw_nwn​. 01The state as a function of a finite noise prefix §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For n∈Nn\in\mathbb Nn∈N write Zn\mathcal Z^nZn for the set of finite sequences v=(v0,…,vn−1)v=(v_0,\dots,v_{n-1})v=(v0​,…,vn−1​). Define Ψn:X×Zn→X\Psi_n:\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z^n\to\mathcal XΨn​:X×Zn→X by recursion on nnn:

Ψ0(x,∅)=x,Ψn+1(x,v)=F(Ψn(x,(v0,…,vn−1)), vn)(v∈Zn+1).\Psi_0(x,\varnothing)=x,\qquad\Psi_{n+1}(x,v)=F\bigl(\Psi_n(x,(v_0,\dots,v_{n-1})),\,v_n\bigr)\quad(v\in\mathcal Z^{n+1}).Ψ0​(x,∅)=x,Ψn+1​(x,v)=F(Ψn​(x,(v0​,…,vn−1​)),vn​)(v∈Zn+1).

This is the recursion of the previous section evaluated on a finite noise sequence: in the successor clause the first nnn coordinates of vvv produce the current state, and the last coordinate vnv_nvn​ is the noise of the next step. Its purpose is to exhibit the nnn-th state as a function of finitely many noise coordinates.

04Independence of the prefix and the fresh innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let η\etaη be a probability measure on Z\mathcal ZZ and Q=η⊗NQ=\eta^{\otimes\mathbb N}Q=η⊗N. Then under QQQ the prefix πn(w)=(w0,…,wn−1)\pi_n(w)=(w_0,\dots,w_{n-1})πn​(w)=(w0​,…,wn−1​) and the coordinate wnw_nwn​ are independent random variables on ZN\mathcal Z^{\mathbb N}ZN.

Proof. Step 1. Under QQQ the coordinate maps w↦wkw\mapsto w_kw↦wk​, k∈Nk\in\mathbb Nk∈N, are mutually independent. Indeed, for a finite set JJJ and measurable BjB_jBj​, the defining property of the infinite product gives Q(wj∈Bj ∀j∈J)=∏j∈Jη(Bj)Q(w_j\in B_j\ \forall j\in J)=\prod_{j\in J}\eta(B_j)Q(wj​∈Bj​ ∀j∈J)=∏j∈J​η(Bj​), and η(Bj)=Q(wj∈Bj)\eta(B_j)=Q(w_j\in B_j)η(Bj​)=Q(wj​∈Bj​) by the same property with a single index.

Step 2 (grouping). If a family of random variables is mutually independent and S,TS,TS,T are disjoint finite index sets, then the vectors (wk)k∈S(w_k)_{k\in S}(wk​)k∈S​ and (wk)k∈T(w_k)_{k\in T}(wk​)k∈T​ are independent. (The events determined by the SSS-variables of the form ⋂k∈S{wk∈Bk}\bigcap_{k\in S}\{w_k\in B_k\}⋂k∈S​{wk​∈Bk​} form a π\piπ-system generating their σ\sigmaσ-algebra, similarly for TTT; mutual independence gives the product rule on these π\piπ-systems, and the π\piπ–λ\lambdaλ theorem extends it to the generated σ\sigmaσ-algebras.) Apply this with S={0,…,n−1}S=\{0,\dots,n-1\}S={0,…,n−1} and T={n}T=\{n\}T={n}, which are disjoint.

Step 3. Measurable functions of independent random variables are independent. The prefix πn(w)\pi_n(w)πn​(w) is the vector (wk)k∈S(w_k)_{k\in S}(wk​)k∈S​ re-indexed by {0,…,n−1}\{0,\dots,n-1\}{0,…,n−1}, and wnw_nwn​ is the value at the single index of TTT; both re-indexings are measurable. □\square□

The statement concerns the whole prefix jointly, which is stronger than independence of wnw_nwn​ from each wkw_kwk​, k<nk<nk<n, separately; the joint form is required because the current state depends on all earlier noise values together.
