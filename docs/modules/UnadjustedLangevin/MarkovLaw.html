For a measurable update FFF and innovation law η\etaη, define the transition Mη(μ)=F#(μ⊗η)M_\eta(\mu)=F_\#(\mu\otimes\eta)Mη​(μ)=F#​(μ⊗η). On the independent innovation product space, the current state is a measurable function of initialization and the preceding finite prefix. Independence of the next coordinate from that prefix and initialization gives joint law μn⊗η\mu_n\otimes\etaμn​⊗η for the current state and next innovation.

Pushing this product law through FFF proves the successor marginal identity μn+1=Mη(μn)\mu_{n+1}=M_\eta(\mu_n)μn+1​=Mη​(μn​). Equality at zero and induction identify the noise-driven laws with the iterated transition. Probability normalization is preserved at every step. Equality of input laws then gives this same conclusion on every probability-space realization satisfying the stated independence assumptions.

01The state as a function of a finite noise prefix §

Mathematical statement and proof

Evaluate the same recursion using only a finite prefix (w0,…,wn−1)(w_0,\ldots,w_{n-1})(w0​,…,wn−1​). At the successor, recurse on the first nnn coordinates and use the final coordinate as the next innovation. This makes finite dependence explicit for the fresh-noise independence proof.

A time-nnn state can be evaluated from an input in E×E{0,…,n−1}E\times E^{\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}E×E{0,…,n−1}. Restricting the prefix for the recursive call and reserving its final coordinate for the next update makes this finite dependence explicit. It provides the measurable factorization through past coordinates needed to prove independence from the next innovation.

02Agreement with the full recursion §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct on prefix length. Restricting an infinite path to its first nnn entries gives the same previous state, and the final finite coordinate is wnw_nwn​. The successor updates thus agree, identifying finite-prefix and infinite-path constructions pointwise.

At length zero both maps return the initial state. At length n+1n+1n+1, their previous states agree by induction after restricting to the first nnn coordinates, and both next innovations are wnw_nwn​. Applying the common update proves equality. Thus replacing the infinite path by its finite prefix does not alter the algorithm output.

03Measurability of the finite-prefix state §

Mathematical statement and proof

The zero map is the initial projection. At the successor, restriction to the earlier finite coordinates and evaluation of the last coordinate are measurable; combine them with the measurable update and the induction hypothesis. This proves the state depends measurably on a finite prefix.

Restriction of a finite coordinate family and evaluation of its last coordinate are measurable product maps. The induction hypothesis gives measurability of the previous state from the shorter prefix. Pair these two measurable outputs and compose with the update. This proves finite-prefix measurability without any probabilistic assumption and allows independence to be passed through the finite recursion.

04Independence of the prefix and the fresh innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The index sets {0,…,n−1}\{0,\ldots,n-1\}{0,…,n−1} and {n}\{n\}{n} are disjoint. Independence of the coordinate family on the iid product source therefore gives independence of the whole prefix vector and the next coordinate. This is stronger than pairwise independence of individual past entries.

On the iid path space, disjoint groups of coordinate evaluations generate independent sigma-algebras. The first group contains all indices less than nnn, and the second contains only nnn. Therefore the entire past prefix vector is independent of the fresh innovation. This joint statement is what permits an arbitrary measurable function of the prefix, including the current state, to inherit independence.

05The transition acting on a law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define Mη(μ)=F∗(μ⊗η)M_\eta(\mu)=F_*(\mu\otimes\eta)Mη​(μ)=F∗​(μ⊗η). Its product measure means the innovation is fresh and independent of the current state. The subsequent theorem derives that independence for the recursion on the independent product space, rather than assuming it at each step.

The transition sends μ\muμ to the law of F(X,Z)F(X,Z)F(X,Z) when X∼μX\sim\muX∼μ, Z∼ηZ\sim\etaZ∼η, and X⊥ZX\perp ZX⊥Z. Writing it as a product pushforward records these requirements exactly. Knowing only the two marginals would not determine the output if state and innovation were dependent, so the independence verification for the recursion establishes the required product input law.

06The iterated Markov laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Set μ0=μ\mu_0=\muμ0​=μ, μn+1=Mη(μn)\mu_{n+1}=M_\eta(\mu_n)μn+1​=Mη​(μn​). These are iterated independent-noise transition laws. The recursion alone does not yet assert that a previously defined sample-path algorithm realizes them.

The law recursion begins with μ\muμ and repeatedly applies the same independent-noise transition. It defines a candidate sequence of algorithm distributions without constructing sample paths. The next results show that the measurable iid path recursion realizes precisely this sequence. This separates a transition-level definition from the theorem identifying it with a noise-driven realization.

07The joint law of a state and fresh innovation §

Mathematical statement and proof

The current state is a measurable function of initialization and the first nnn noises. Product-source independence and prefix/fresh independence make it independent of wnw_nwn​. Its marginal is the output law of a noise-driven chain, and the innovation marginal is η\etaη; their joint law is therefore the product of these two marginals.

The current state factors through initialization and its finite innovation prefix. Initialization is independent of the entire path, and prefix/fresh independence separates the next coordinate from that past. Regrouping these facts shows independence of the current state and next innovation. Their joint law is consequently the product of the already defined current output law and η\etaη.

08The successor marginal transition §

Mathematical statement and proof

The pointwise successor is F(Xn,wn)F(X_n,w_n)F(Xn​,wn​). Push the proved product joint law of (Xn,wn)(X_n,w_n)(Xn​,wn​) through FFF; this yields μn+1=Mη(μn)\mu_{n+1}=M_\eta(\mu_n)μn+1​=Mη​(μn​) for the recursion laws on the product space. No later-time state/noise independence is assumed.

The successor state is the measurable image of (Xn,wn)(X_n,w_n)(Xn​,wn​) under FFF. The preceding product-law identity therefore gives its distribution as F#(μn⊗η)F_\#(\mu_n\otimes\eta)F#​(μn​⊗η). This proves the Markov successor relation from the supplied iid source. It does not assume conditional independence as a new property of the recursively constructed state.

09Agreement of path-space and Markov laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

The product-path law and the iterated Markov law have the same initial value and the same successor equation. Induction on nnn identifies them at every index. This proves equality of the path-based and transition-based definitions.

Equality at time zero follows from the initial marginal computation. If the two law sequences agree at time nnn, their common successor transition gives agreement at n+1n+1n+1. This induction identifies the path-space and transition-space formulations for every finite iteration. All later kernel estimates can therefore be transferred to the original finite recursion.

10Probability preservation by one transition §

Mathematical statement and proof

The independent product of two probability measures has mass one. A measurable pushforward preserves that total mass, so a transition acting on probability measures is again a probability measure.

The product of current-state and innovation probabilities has total mass one. The preimage of the entire output space under a measurable update is the entire product input space. Hence the pushforward transition also has mass one. This conclusion concerns normalization alone and does not assume that the transition preserves second moments or has an invariant distribution.

11Probability preservation at every iterate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induct from the initial probability and apply probability preservation by a measurable Gaussian transition at each successor. This proves normalization of every transition iterate and provides the probability hypotheses used in later law comparisons.

Assume the initial law is a probability. The one-step normalization theorem shows that each successor law is a probability whenever its predecessor is. Induction proves the property at every finite index. Keeping this fact available is necessary for later expectation inequalities, where the norm of a constant and the comparison between first and second moments use unit total mass.

12The noise-driven chain has the iterated transition law §

Mathematical statement and proof

First identify the independent iid realization with the product-path output law. Then apply the equality of the independent-noise marginal recursion and iterated transition laws. Every realization satisfying the original initialization/noise assumptions consequently has the same proved Markov iterate law.

An arbitrary admissible realization first has the same law as the independent path recursion, by equality of its joint input distribution. The product-path recursion then has the same law as the iterated transition. Combining these two identities yields the claimed Markov-law representation for the supplied process, including all initialization and innovation assumptions used to derive it.
