01Couplings §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Let μ,ν\mu,\nuμ,ν be measures on HHH. A coupling of μ\muμ and ν\nuν is a measure γ\gammaγ on H×HH\times HH×H such that

(pr1)#γ=μand(pr2)#γ=ν,i.e.γ(A×H)=μ(A), γ(H×B)=ν(B)(\mathrm{pr}_1)_\#\gamma=\mu\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{pr}_2)_\#\gamma=\nu,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\gamma(A\times H)=\mu(A),\ \gamma(H\times B)=\nu(B)(pr1​)#​γ=μand(pr2​)#​γ=ν,i.e.γ(A×H)=μ(A), γ(H×B)=ν(B)

for all Borel sets A,B⊆HA,B\subseteq HA,B⊆H.

The dependence between the two coordinates is arbitrary. For probability measures the product μ⊗ν\mu\otimes\nuμ⊗ν is a coupling, so Γ(μ,ν)\Gamma(\mu,\nu)Γ(μ,ν) is nonempty.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

structure Coupling (μ ν : Measure H) has three fields: joint : Measure (H × H) is γ\gammaγ; fst : joint.map Prod.fst = μ and snd : joint.map Prod.snd = ν are the two marginal conditions, where γ.map f is the image measure f#γf_\#\gammaf#​γ and Prod.fst, Prod.snd are the projections. An element of the structure is a coupling together with the proofs of its marginal conditions.

02The quadratic cost of a coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For a measure γ\gammaγ on H×HH\times HH×H,

c(γ)=∥(x,y)↦x−y∥2,γ=(∫H×H∥x−y∥2 γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞].c(\gamma)=\|(x,y)\mapsto x-y\|_{2,\gamma}=\Bigl(\int_{H\times H}\|x-y\|^2\,\gamma(dx,dy)\Bigr)^{1/2}\in[0,\infty].c(γ)=∥(x,y)↦x−y∥2,γ​=(∫H×H​∥x−y∥2γ(dx,dy))1/2∈[0,∞].

The displacement (x,y)↦x−y(x,y)\mapsto x-y(x,y)↦x−y is continuous, hence measurable, so the integral formula applies. The value ∞\infty∞ is allowed; it occurs exactly when the second moment of the displacement is infinite.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

couplingCost γ is eLpNorm (fun p : H × H ↦ p.1 - p.2) 2 γ. Mathlib's eLpNorm f p μ is the extended LpL^pLp-seminorm with values in ℝ≥0∞, the extended nonnegative reals [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞]: for p=2p=2p=2 and an almost everywhere strongly measurable fff it equals (∫∥f∥2 dμ)1/2(\int\|f\|^2\,d\mu)^{1/2}(∫∥f∥2dμ)1/2, and it is ∞\infty∞ for other functions. Keeping the value in ℝ≥0∞ prevents an infinite cost from being silently replaced by a real number.
