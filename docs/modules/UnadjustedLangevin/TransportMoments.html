Finite quadratic transport transfers second moments

Finite W2W_2W2​ distance to a finite-second-moment law supplies the other second moment automatically. This removes a redundant integrability premise when passing an initial law to the L² formulation.

01Choose one finite coupling §

Mathematical statement and proof

If W2(μ,π)<∞W_2(\mu,\pi)<\inftyW2​(μ,π)<∞, the infimum definition supplies one coupling γ\gammaγ with finite L2L^2L2 difference cost. The second marginal hypothesis gives Y∈L2(γ)Y\in L^2(\gamma)Y∈L2(γ), and X=(X−Y)+YX=(X-Y)+YX=(X−Y)+Y then gives X∈L2(γ)X\in L^2(\gamma)X∈L2(γ). Pushing the first-coordinate identity through its marginal proves id⁡∈L2(μ)\operatorname{id}\in L^2(\mu)id∈L2(μ). Conversely, if both probability laws have finite second moments, their independent product has square-integrable difference and is a finite-cost coupling, hence W2<∞W_2<\inftyW2​<∞.

02Transfer a law moment back to its realization §

Mathematical statement and proof

For an almost-measurable XXX with law μ\muμ, the pushforward L2L^2L2 equivalence says ∫∥X∥2dP=∫∥x∥2dμ\int\|X\|^2dP=\int\|x\|^2d\mu∫∥X∥2dP=∫∥x∥2dμ, including the integrability assertion.
