Prove Gibbs stationarity by refining invariant Metropolis chains

A Brownian Langevin integral solution started from Gibbs stays Gibbs. The proof uses convergent Euler approximations and exactly invariant MALA chains, with no weak-PDE uniqueness assumption.

01Identify L² Euler endpoints with the actual ULA laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

Induction identifies the representative of the lifted chain with the pointwise noise-driven recursion. The Markov-law theorem then gives Law⁡(Xn)=PhnLaw⁡(X0)\operatorname{Law}(X_n)=P_h^n\operatorname{Law}(X_0)Law(Xn​)=Phn​Law(X0​) when the innovations are iid standard Gaussian vectors and independent of the initial state. If the additive noise satisfies B((k+1)h)−B(kh)=2hZkB((k+1)h)-B(kh)=\sqrt{2h}Z_kB((k+1)h)−B(kh)=2h​Zk​, induction also identifies the additive Euler chain with this lifted chain.

02Use normalized Brownian increments §

Mathematical statement and proof

Brownian additive increments telescope across the grid and equal 2h\sqrt{2h}2h​ times the normalized Euler innovations. Therefore additive Euler endpoints driven by Brownian noise are the lifted ULA endpoints. The scalar coordinate normal laws and the stated joint coordinate-time independence imply iid standard Gaussian vector laws, using GaussianInnovationVectors. Independence from the initial state is retained explicitly.

03Pass the invariant Gibbs marginal to the continuous limit §

Mathematical statement and proof

Fix T>0T>0T>0 and choose hn=T/(n+1)h_n=T/(n+1)hn​=T/(n+1). The Euler theorem gives Xn+1hn→YTX_{n+1}^{h_n}\to Y_TXn+1hn​​→YT​ in L2L^2L2. Couple each Euler endpoint, started at Gibbs, to the MALA chain at the same mesh. Exact MALA invariance makes the second marginal Gibbs; the rejected-displacement bound gives

E∥Xn+1hn−Mn+1hn∥≤(n+1)Chnhn=CThn⟶0.\mathbb E\|X_{n+1}^{h_n}-M_{n+1}^{h_n}\|\le(n+1)Ch_n\sqrt{h_n}=CT\sqrt{h_n}\longrightarrow0.E∥Xn+1hn​​−Mn+1hn​​∥≤(n+1)Chn​hn​​=CThn​​⟶0.

For all sufficiently large nnn, the mesh satisfies both hn≤1h_n\le1hn​≤1 and the stable-step restriction. Characteristic-function comparison between these couplings and continuity under the L2L^2L2 limit identifies Law⁡(YT)=π\operatorname{Law}(Y_T)=\piLaw(YT​)=π. At T=0T=0T=0, the integral equation directly gives Y0=X0Y_0=X_0Y0​=X0​. Strong convexity supplies Gibbs moments and the finite rejection constant; the theorem also displays Brownian increment independence for every positive mesh.
