Jointly measurable density and relative score

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

The Gaussian kernel and its explicit spatial derivative are jointly measurable in positive time, starting point, and observation. Parameter integration gives jointly measurable mixture densities and derivative fields. This is the measurability needed for the subsequent space-time integrals.

At positive time the density is everywhere positive. Dividing its gradient by the density and adding ∇U\nabla U∇U yields one jointly measurable relative score field, equal pointwise to ∇log⁡rt\nabla\log r_t∇logrt​. A separately assigned value at time zero or negative times changes none of the integrals over positive time.

01Integrate a jointly measurable Gaussian kernel §

Mathematical statement and proof

The density is a nonnegative parameter integral pt(y)=∫Kt(x,y)dμ(x)p_t(y)=\int K_t(x,y)d\mu(x)pt​(y)=∫Kt​(x,y)dμ(x). The normalizer, affine center, norm, and exponential are measurable in (t,y,x)(t,y,x)(t,y,x). The parameterized nonnegative integral is therefore measurable. Passing from the extended integral to its real value preserves measurability. Multiplication by ZeU(y)Z e^{U(y)}ZeU(y) gives the joint measurability of rt(y)r_t(y)rt​(y).

The explicit positive-time formulas give one jointly measurable family on the domain (0,∞)×E(0,\infty)\times E(0,∞)×E. If a value at zero is needed to define an ambient spacetime field, it may be assigned separately on that measurable time slice. No choice at zero affects any positive-time density identity or spacetime integral.

The Gaussian kernel is a composition of measurable algebraic operations with the continuous exponential. Parameter integration preserves joint measurability in the remaining time and observation variables.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.37.13.

02Measurability of the spatial derivative §

Mathematical statement and proof

The derivative kernel is a continuous linear functional proportional to the inner product with the Gaussian residual:

DyKt(x,y)[v]=−Kt(x,y)2t⟨y−x+t∇U(x),v⟩.D_yK_t(x,y)[v]=-\frac{K_t(x,y)}{2t}\langle y-x+t\nabla U(x),v\rangle.Dy​Kt​(x,y)[v]=−2tKt​(x,y)​⟨y−x+t∇U(x),v⟩.

The inner-product-to-dual map is continuous, so this is jointly strongly measurable as a continuous-linear-map-valued function. Its Bochner integral in xxx, denoted At(y)A_t(y)At​(y), remains strongly measurable. At positive times the earlier differentiation theorem identifies At=DptA_t=D p_tAt​=Dpt​.

The explicit spatial derivative is the kernel times a linear displacement functional. Its joint measurability and finite-dimensional coordinate integration yield a measurable mixture derivative field.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.37.2.

03One relative-score field for all spacetime integrals §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define

S(t,y)=∇U(y)+pt(y)−1R−1(At(y)),\mathcal S(t,y)=\nabla U(y)+p_t(y)^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}(A_t(y)),S(t,y)=∇U(y)+pt​(y)−1R−1(At​(y)),

where R−1\mathcal R^{-1}R−1 is the inverse Riesz identification of the continuous dual with the Hilbert space. Continuous scalar multiplication, inversion as a measurable real function, and the previous measurability results make S\mathcal SS jointly strongly measurable.

For t>0t>0t>0, pt>0p_t>0pt​>0, so the log chain rule and rt=ZeUptr_t=Z e^U p_trt​=ZeUpt​ give

S(t,y)=∇U(y)+∇log⁡pt(y)=∇log⁡rt(y).\mathcal S(t,y)=\nabla U(y)+\nabla\log p_t(y)=\nabla\log r_t(y).S(t,y)=∇U(y)+∇logpt​(y)=∇logrt​(y).

Thus the measurable field is the relative score wherever it is used. Its auxiliary extension at nonpositive time introduces no analytic premise into the proof.

Dividing the mixture gradient by its strictly positive density defines one jointly measurable Lebesgue score. Adding the continuous potential gradient gives the relative score field used uniformly throughout positive-time spacetime integrals.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
