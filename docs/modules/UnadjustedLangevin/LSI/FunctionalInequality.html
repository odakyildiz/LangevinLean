Relative entropy, Fisher information and the log-Sobolev inequality

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let EEE be a real inner product space with a σ\sigmaσ-algebra (Borel where the log-Sobolev inequality is concerned), and let ν\nuν be a measure on EEE. This section fixes the three objects in which the entropy analysis of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm is written: the measure rνr\nurν with a prescribed density rrr with respect to ν\nuν, the relative Fisher information Jν(r)J_\nu(r)Jν​(r) of a prescribed density, and the log-Sobolev inequality with constant α\alphaα. The relative entropy is the Kullback–Leibler divergence: for probability measures ρ,ν\rho,\nuρ,ν,

Hν(ρ)={∫Elog⁡dρdν dρ,if ρ≪ν and log⁡dρdν∈L1(ρ),+∞,otherwise,H_\nu(\rho)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_E\log\frac{d\rho}{d\nu}\,d\rho, & \text{if }\rho\ll\nu\text{ and }\log\frac{d\rho}{d\nu}\in L^1(\rho),\\ +\infty, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}Hν​(ρ)=⎩⎨⎧​∫E​logdνdρ​dρ,+∞,​if ρ≪ν and logdνdρ​∈L1(ρ),otherwise,​

where dρ/dνd\rho/d\nudρ/dν is the Radon–Nikodym derivative. It takes values in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] (nonnegativity is Gibbs' inequality). With these definitions, ν\nuν satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality with constant α\alphaα if ν\nuν is a probability measure and

2α Hν(rν)≤Jν(r)=∫E∥∇log⁡r∥2 r dν2\alpha\,H_\nu(r\nu)\le J_\nu(r)=\int_E\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,r\,d\nu2αHν​(rν)≤Jν​(r)=∫E​∥∇logr∥2rdν

for every strictly positive continuously differentiable rrr such that rνr\nurν is a probability measure; this is the normalization of Vempala and Wibisono (2019), where it reads Hν(ρ)≤12αJν(ρ)H_\nu(\rho)\le\frac1{2\alpha}J_\nu(\rho)Hν​(ρ)≤2α1​Jν​(ρ). Both sides may be infinite. The last result of the section converts the inequality into an inequality between real numbers when both quantities are known to be finite; every later use of the log-Sobolev inequality goes through it.

01The measure defined by a density §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For a measure ν\nuν on EEE and a function r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R, let rνr\nurν be the measure

(rν)(A)=∫Ar+ dν,r+=max⁡{r,0},(r\nu)(A)=\int_A r^+\,d\nu,\qquad r^+=\max\{r,0\},(rν)(A)=∫A​r+dν,r+=max{r,0},

for measurable A⊆EA\subseteq EA⊆E. If r≥0r\ge0r≥0 this is the usual measure with density rrr with respect to ν\nuν, and ∫f d(rν)=∫fr dν\int f\,d(r\nu)=\int fr\,d\nu∫fd(rν)=∫frdν for measurable f≥0f\ge0f≥0. If moreover rrr is measurable and ∫r dν=1\int r\,d\nu=1∫rdν=1, then rνr\nurν is a probability measure.

The definition is made for every real function, and normalization is not part of it: whether rνr\nurν is a probability measure is a separate condition, imposed where it is needed (for instance in the test class of the log-Sobolev inequality). In all applications rrr is strictly positive and continuous; positivity is needed later to differentiate log⁡r\log rlogr, and continuity makes rrr measurable.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

tiltedMeasure ν r is defined as ν.withDensity (fun x ↦ ENNReal.ofReal (r x)). Mathlib's withDensity takes a density with values in ℝ≥0∞ =[0,∞]=[0,\infty]=[0,∞] and returns the measure A↦∫Af dνA\mapsto\int_A f\,d\nuA↦∫A​fdν, where the integral of a [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞]-valued function is the Lebesgue integral ∫⁻. ENNReal.ofReal converts a real number aaa into the extended nonnegative number max⁡{a,0}\max\{a,0\}max{a,0}; composing with it gives the density r+r^+r+ of the definition. No measurability or normalization hypothesis is attached to r.

02Relative Fisher information §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For a measure ν\nuν and a function r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R, the relative Fisher information of rrr is

Jν(r)=∫E∥∇log⁡r(x)∥2 (rν)(dx)∈[0,∞].J_\nu(r)=\int_E\|\nabla\log r(x)\|^2\,(r\nu)(dx)\in[0,\infty].Jν​(r)=∫E​∥∇logr(x)∥2(rν)(dx)∈[0,∞].

It is the integral of a nonnegative function with respect to the measure rνr\nurν of the previous step, so it is always defined and may equal +∞+\infty+∞. When rrr is strictly positive and differentiable, ∇log⁡r=∇r/r\nabla\log r=\nabla r/r∇logr=∇r/r and Jν(r)=∫∥∇r∥2/r dνJ_\nu(r)=\int\|\nabla r\|^2/r\,d\nuJν​(r)=∫∥∇r∥2/rdν.

The functional depends on the function rrr, not only on the measure rνr\nurν: it is the given function that is differentiated, rather than an unspecified version of the Radon–Nikodym derivative d(rν)/dνd(r\nu)/d\nud(rν)/dν, which is determined only ν\nuν-almost everywhere and need not be differentiable. If ν(dx)=Z−1e−U(x) dx\nu(dx)=Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}\,dxν(dx)=Z−1e−U(x)dx and ρ=rν\rho=r\nuρ=rν has a positive differentiable Lebesgue density ppp, then r=ZpeUr=Zpe^{U}r=ZpeU, log⁡r=log⁡p+U+log⁡Z\log r=\log p+U+\log Zlogr=logp+U+logZ, and

∇log⁡r=∇log⁡p+∇U,Jν(r)=∫∥∇log⁡p+∇U∥2 dρ.\nabla\log r=\nabla\log p+\nabla U,\qquad J_\nu(r)=\int\|\nabla\log p+\nabla U\|^2\,d\rho.∇logr=∇logp+∇U,Jν​(r)=∫∥∇logp+∇U∥2dρ.

In particular Jν(r)=0J_\nu(r)=0Jν​(r)=0 for r≡1r\equiv1r≡1, that is for ρ=ν\rho=\nuρ=ν.

03The log-Sobolev inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. Let EEE carry its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra, let ν\nuν be a measure on EEE and α∈R\alpha\in\mathbb Rα∈R. We say that ν\nuν satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality with constant α\alphaα if

  1. ν\nuν is a probability measure, and
  2. for every function r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R such that r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0 for all xxx, rrr is continuously differentiable, and rνr\nurν is a probability measure,
max⁡{2α,0}⋅Hν(rν)≤Jν(r)in [0,∞],\max\{2\alpha,0\}\cdot H_\nu(r\nu)\le J_\nu(r)\qquad\text{in }[0,\infty],max{2α,0}⋅Hν​(rν)≤Jν​(r)in [0,∞],

with the convention 0⋅∞=00\cdot\infty=00⋅∞=0.

For α>0\alpha>0α>0, the case used throughout, the factor is 2α2\alpha2α, and the inequality reads Hν(ρ)≤12αJν(r)H_\nu(\rho)\le\frac1{2\alpha}J_\nu(r)Hν​(ρ)≤2α1​Jν​(r) for ρ=rν\rho=r\nuρ=rν, which is the form in Vempala and Wibisono (2019). If Hν(rν)=∞H_\nu(r\nu)=\inftyHν​(rν)=∞, it forces Jν(r)=∞J_\nu(r)=\inftyJν​(r)=∞. For α≤0\alpha\le0α≤0 the left side is 000 and the condition reduces to normalization of ν\nuν.

The test class consists of strictly positive C1C^1C1 probability densities. The definition makes no claim about measures outside this class, so to apply it to a law ρ\rhoρ one must first exhibit a strictly positive C1C^1C1 function rrr with ρ=rν\rho=r\nuρ=rν. For the unadjusted Langevin algorithm this is done for the interpolating laws at positive times, whose Gaussian smoothing produces such densities. Strict positivity makes log⁡r\log rlogr finite and differentiable everywhere, and continuous differentiability makes ∇log⁡r=∇r/r\nabla\log r=\nabla r/r∇logr=∇r/r continuous.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

IsLogSobolev ν α is the conjunction of IsProbabilityMeasure ν (condition 1) and the universally quantified condition 2. The three assumptions on a test function are ∀ x, 0 < r x, ContDiff ℝ 1 r (continuously differentiable, the order of differentiability being 111) and IsProbabilityMeasure (tiltedMeasure ν r). The inequality ENNReal.ofReal (2 * α) * InformationTheory.klDiv (tiltedMeasure ν r) ν ≤ relativeFisher ν r is stated in ℝ≥0∞: ENNReal.ofReal (2 * α) is max⁡{2α,0}\max\{2\alpha,0\}max{2α,0}, multiplication in ℝ≥0∞ follows the convention 0⋅∞=00\cdot\infty=00⋅∞=0, and InformationTheory.klDiv is Mathlib's Kullback–Leibler divergence. For probability measures klDiv equals the formula in the summary: Mathlib defines it as ∞\infty∞ unless ρ≪ν\rho\ll\nuρ≪ν and the log-likelihood ratio is ρ\rhoρ-integrable, and otherwise as the nonnegative part of ∫log⁡dρdν dρ+ν(E)−ρ(E)\int\log\frac{d\rho}{d\nu}\,d\rho+\nu(E)-\rho(E)∫logdνdρ​dρ+ν(E)−ρ(E), whose correction term vanishes for probability measures. The Borel assumption [BorelSpace E] is an argument of the definition.

04The log-Sobolev inequality for finite quantities §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE carry its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra and let ν\nuν satisfy the log-Sobolev inequality with constant α≥0\alpha\ge0α≥0. Let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be strictly positive and continuously differentiable, with rνr\nurν a probability measure, and assume

Hν(rν)<∞andJν(r)<∞.H_\nu(r\nu)<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad J_\nu(r)<\infty.Hν​(rν)<∞andJν​(r)<∞.

Then the real numbers Hν(rν)H_\nu(r\nu)Hν​(rν) and Jν(r)J_\nu(r)Jν​(r) satisfy

2α Hν(rν)≤Jν(r).2\alpha\,H_\nu(r\nu)\le J_\nu(r).2αHν​(rν)≤Jν​(r).

Proof. By the definition applied to the admissible density rrr,

a:=max⁡{2α,0}⋅Hν(rν)≤b:=Jν(r)in [0,∞].(1)a:=\max\{2\alpha,0\}\cdot H_\nu(r\nu)\le b:=J_\nu(r)\qquad\text{in }[0,\infty].\qquad(1)a:=max{2α,0}⋅Hν​(rν)≤b:=Jν​(r)in [0,∞].(1)

The number max⁡{2α,0}\max\{2\alpha,0\}max{2α,0} is finite and Hν(rν)H_\nu(r\nu)Hν​(rν) is finite by assumption, so their product aaa is finite; bbb is finite by assumption. On the finite elements of [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] the order is the order of R\mathbb RR, so (1) is an inequality between real numbers. The real value of a product of finite elements is the product of the real values, and since α≥0\alpha\ge0α≥0 we have max⁡{2α,0}=2α\max\{2\alpha,0\}=2\alphamax{2α,0}=2α. Hence a=2αHν(rν)a=2\alpha H_\nu(r\nu)a=2αHν​(rν), and (1) is the claim. □\square□

Both finiteness hypotheses are needed. Without them one of the two extended values could be +∞+\infty+∞; since an infinite value has no real counterpart, the comparison (1) would then not determine any inequality between real numbers. In the later sections both quantities are first proved finite, and only then is this real inequality combined with differential inequalities.
