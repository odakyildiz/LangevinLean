Relative entropy, Fisher information and the log-Sobolev inequality

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let ν\nuν be a probability measure and let rrr be a positive normalized density relative to ν\nuν. Write ρr=rν\rho_r=r\nuρr​=rν. Relative entropy and relative Fisher information are denoted by Hν(ρr)=KL⁡(ρr∣ν)H_\nu(\rho_r)=\operatorname{KL}(\rho_r\mid\nu)Hν​(ρr​)=KL(ρr​∣ν) and Iν(r)=∫∥∇log⁡r∥2dρrI_\nu(r)=\int\|\nabla\log r\|^2d\rho_rIν​(r)=∫∥∇logr∥2dρr​. Both may initially equal infinity. The log-Sobolev convention used here is 2αHν(ρr)≤Iν(r)2\alpha H_\nu(\rho_r)\le I_\nu(r)2αHν​(ρr​)≤Iν​(r), tested on strictly positive C1C^1C1 densities of probability measures. Finite-value versions of the inequality are used only after both quantities have been proved finite.

01The measure defined by a density §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a measurable real function rrr, let r+(x)=max⁡{r(x),0}r_+(x)=\max\{r(x),0\}r+​(x)=max{r(x),0} and define ρr(A)=∫Ar+ dν\rho_r(A)=\int_A r_+\,d\nuρr​(A)=∫A​r+​dν. If r≥0r\ge0r≥0, this is the usual measure rνr\nurν. If also ∫r dν=1\int r\,d\nu=1∫rdν=1, it is a probability measure. Thus the measure can be defined without imposing normalization; normalization is an additional hypothesis when probability identities are used. Strict positivity is required later to differentiate log⁡r\log rlogr.

02Relative Fisher information §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a specified positive differentiable density rrr, define

Iν(r)=∫∥∇log⁡r(x)∥2 dρr(x).I_\nu(r)=\int\|\nabla\log r(x)\|^2\,d\rho_r(x).Iν​(r)=∫∥∇logr(x)∥2dρr​(x).

This is a nonnegative integral, with value in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞]. It does not require choosing a differentiable version of an unspecified almost-everywhere density: the function rrr itself is given. If ν\nuν has Gibbs density J=Z−1e−UJ=Z^{-1}e^{-U}J=Z−1e−U and ρr\rho_rρr​ has Lebesgue density ppp, then r=p/Jr=p/Jr=p/J and ∇log⁡r=∇log⁡p+∇U\nabla\log r=\nabla\log p+\nabla U∇logr=∇logp+∇U. Consequently the Fisher information measures the squared difference between the logarithmic derivatives of the two densities.

03The log-Sobolev convention §

Mathematical statement and proof

The statement that ν\nuν satisfies LSI with constant α\alphaα means that ν\nuν is a probability measure and

2αKL⁡(rν∣ν)≤Iν(r)2\alpha\operatorname{KL}(r\nu\mid\nu)\le I_\nu(r)2αKL(rν∣ν)≤Iν​(r)

for every strictly positive C1C^1C1 function rrr such that rνr\nurν is a probability measure. The inequality is initially understood for nonnegative extended quantities. The test class is part of the hypothesis: no claim that an arbitrary probability measure has such a density is included. In the ULA proof, the Gaussian convolution is first shown to produce a density in this test class; LSI is then applied to that density.

04LSI when entropy and Fisher information are finite §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose α≥0\alpha\ge0α≥0, r>0r>0r>0, r∈C1r\in C^1r∈C1, and rνr\nurν is a probability measure. Assume both KL⁡(rν∣ν)\operatorname{KL}(r\nu\mid\nu)KL(rν∣ν) and Iν(r)I_\nu(r)Iν​(r) are finite. The extended inequality supplied by LSI is then an inequality between finite nonnegative numbers. Its ordinary real form is

2αHν(rν)≤Iν(r).2\alpha H_\nu(r\nu)\le I_\nu(r).2αHν​(rν)≤Iν​(r).

The coefficient is unchanged because 2α≥02\alpha\ge02α≥0. Establishing the two finiteness conditions first permits subsequent subtraction and integration of ordinary real functions.
