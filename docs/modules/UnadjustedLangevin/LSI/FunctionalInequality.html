01Relative density, relative score, and LSI normalization §

Mathematical statement and proof

Use a density relative to the target, rather than to Lebesgue measure: ρ=rν\rho=r\nuρ=rν. When ν(dx)=Z−1e−U(x)dx\nu(dx)=Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}dxν(dx)=Z−1e−U(x)dx and ρ(dx)=p(x)dx\rho(dx)=p(x)dxρ(dx)=p(x)dx, this means r=ZpeUr=Zpe^Ur=ZpeU, so the relative score is s=∇log⁡r=∇log⁡p+∇Us=\nabla\log r=\nabla\log p+\nabla Us=∇logr=∇logp+∇U. The basic quantities are

Hν(ρ)=∫rlog⁡r dν,Jν(ρ)=∫∥∇log⁡r∥2 dρ. H_\nu(\rho)=\int r\log r\,d\nu, \qquad J_\nu(\rho)=\int\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\rho. Hν​(ρ)=∫rlogrdν,Jν​(ρ)=∫∥∇logr∥2dρ.

The normalization is 2αHν(ρ)≤Jν(ρ)2\alpha H_\nu(\rho)\leq J_\nu(\rho)2αHν​(ρ)≤Jν​(ρ). A factor of two in the LSI convention would change every stated rate, so this normalization is part of the comparison with the paper. Lean's tiltedMeasure is the actual measure with relative density rrr, and relativeFisher is its extended nonnegative score integral. IsLogSobolev asserts that the target is a probability measure and tests the inequality on strictly positive, continuously differentiable relative densities that define probability measures. An extension from that test class to arbitrary densities is not asserted here.

To compare conventions, for a positive smooth function ggg with ∫g2dν=1\int g^2d\nu=1∫g2dν=1, put r=g2r=g^2r=g2. Then ∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2dν=4∫∥∇g∥2dν\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2d\nu=4\int\|\nabla g\|^2d\nu∫r∥∇logr∥2dν=4∫∥∇g∥2dν. This explains the familiar LSI convention Ent⁡ν(g2)≤(2/α)∫∥∇g∥2dν\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(g^2)\leq(2/\alpha)\int\|\nabla g\|^2d\nuEntν​(g2)≤(2/α)∫∥∇g∥2dν. This equivalence describes the normalization; the declared property itself is the explicit relative-density formulation shown in Lean.

02Why finite KL and Fisher information are separate obligations §

Mathematical statement and proof

The entropy is Mathlib's InformationTheory.klDiv, not a function chosen solely to satisfy a recurrence. This divergence and the Fisher information take values in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞]. Lean's real-valued comparisons use ENNReal.toReal; that operation sends infinity to zero, so a real inequality alone would not establish a meaningful finite-entropy estimate.

IsLogSobolev.entropy_le_fisher therefore retains proofs that both extended quantities are finite before giving 2αH≤J2\alpha H\leq J2αH≤J over the reals. The endpoint also retains finite KL along every identified interpolation path. ula_kl_finite_of_interpolation uses its zero-time identification to prove finite KL for the corresponding ULA law, including initialization. These are the guards that make densityEntropy and ulaEntropy faithful real representations of entropy in their applications.

Proof of the real inequality: apply the LSI property to the supplied density rrr, giving ofReal⁡(2α)KL⁡≤J\operatorname{ofReal}(2\alpha)\operatorname{KL}\le JofReal(2α)KL≤J in extended nonnegative reals. The two finiteness guards make conversion order preserving and multiplicative; α≥0\alpha\ge0α≥0 rewrites the scalar conversion as 2α2\alpha2α, yielding 2αKL⁡R≤JR2\alpha\operatorname{KL}_{\mathbb R}\le J_{\mathbb R}2αKLR​≤JR​.
