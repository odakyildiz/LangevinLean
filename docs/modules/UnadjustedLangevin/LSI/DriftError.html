Young inequalities for the drift error

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let EEE be a real inner product space. In the entropy derivative along the ULA interpolation, the relative score s=∇log⁡rs=\nabla\log rs=∇logr is paired with a drift error eee, and the pairing ∫r⟨e,s⟩ dν\int r\langle e,s\rangle\,d\nu∫r⟨e,s⟩dν must be absorbed into the dissipation −∫r∥s∥2 dν-\int r\|s\|^2\,d\nu−∫r∥s∥2dν. This section proves Young's inequality in the form

⟨e,s⟩≤∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤14∥s∥2+∥e∥2,\langle e,s\rangle\le|\langle e,s\rangle|\le\frac14\|s\|^2+\|e\|^2,⟨e,s⟩≤∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤41​∥s∥2+∥e∥2,

derives from it that the weighted pairing r⟨e,s⟩r\langle e,s\rangler⟨e,s⟩ is integrable whenever it is measurable and both energies ∫r∥s∥2 dν\int r\|s\|^2\,d\nu∫r∥s∥2dν and ∫r∥e∥2 dν\int r\|e\|^2\,d\nu∫r∥e∥2dν are finite, integrates it, and combines it with the entropy identity for the forced weak equation to obtain

∫r˙ (log⁡r+1) dν≤−34∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν+∫r∥e∥2 dν.\int\dot r\,(\log r+1)\,d\nu\le-\frac34\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu+\int r\|e\|^2\,d\nu.∫r˙(logr+1)dν≤−43​∫r∥∇logr∥2dν+∫r∥e∥2dν.

This is the step ddtHν(ρt)≤−Jν(ρt)+14Jν(ρt)+E∥∇U(xt)−∇U(x0)∥2\frac{d}{dt}H_\nu(\rho_t)\le-J_\nu(\rho_t)+\frac14J_\nu(\rho_t)+\mathbb E\|\nabla U(x_t)-\nabla U(x_0)\|^2dtd​Hν​(ρt​)≤−Jν​(ρt​)+41​Jν​(ρt​)+E∥∇U(xt​)−∇U(x0​)∥2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019), Lemma 3. The coefficient 14\frac1441​ is chosen so that three quarters of the Fisher information remain. Neither a bound on the error energy nor any differential inequality is assumed.

01A Young inequality for the inner product §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For all e,s∈Ee,s\in Ee,s∈E,

⟨e,s⟩≤14∥s∥2+∥e∥2.\langle e,s\rangle\le\frac14\|s\|^2+\|e\|^2.⟨e,s⟩≤41​∥s∥2+∥e∥2.

Proof. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, ⟨e,s⟩≤∥e∥ ∥s∥\langle e,s\rangle\le\|e\|\,\|s\|⟨e,s⟩≤∥e∥∥s∥. Expanding the nonnegative square,

0≤(∥e∥−∥s∥2)2=∥e∥2−∥e∥ ∥s∥+14∥s∥2,0\le\Bigl(\|e\|-\frac{\|s\|}2\Bigr)^2=\|e\|^2-\|e\|\,\|s\|+\frac14\|s\|^2,0≤(∥e∥−2∥s∥​)2=∥e∥2−∥e∥∥s∥+41​∥s∥2,

so ∥e∥ ∥s∥≤∥e∥2+14∥s∥2\|e\|\,\|s\|\le\|e\|^2+\frac14\|s\|^2∥e∥∥s∥≤∥e∥2+41​∥s∥2. Combining the two inequalities gives the claim. □\square□

This is Young's inequality ab≤ε2a2+12εb2ab\le\frac{\varepsilon}{2}a^2+\frac1{2\varepsilon}b^2ab≤2ε​a2+2ε1​b2 with a=∥e∥a=\|e\|a=∥e∥, b=∥s∥b=\|s\|b=∥s∥ and ε=2\varepsilon=2ε=2. In the entropy derivative it is applied with sss the relative score, so that adding 14∥s∥2\frac14\|s\|^241​∥s∥2 to the dissipation −∥s∥2-\|s\|^2−∥s∥2 leaves −34∥s∥2-\frac34\|s\|^2−43​∥s∥2.

02The same inequality for the absolute value §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For all e,s∈Ee,s\in Ee,s∈E,

∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤14∥s∥2+∥e∥2.|\langle e,s\rangle|\le\frac14\|s\|^2+\|e\|^2.∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤41​∥s∥2+∥e∥2.

Proof. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in absolute form gives ∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤∥e∥ ∥s∥|\langle e,s\rangle|\le\|e\|\,\|s\|∣⟨e,s⟩∣≤∥e∥∥s∥, and ∥e∥ ∥s∥≤∥e∥2+14∥s∥2\|e\|\,\|s\|\le\|e\|^2+\frac14\|s\|^2∥e∥∥s∥≤∥e∥2+41​∥s∥2 as in the previous step. □\square□

The absolute version is needed to dominate the integrand r⟨e,s⟩r\langle e,s\rangler⟨e,s⟩, whose sign is unknown, by an integrable function.

03Integrability of the weighted pairing §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let (X,X)(X,\mathcal X)(X,X) be a measurable space with a measure ν\nuν, let r:X→Rr:X\to\mathbb Rr:X→R with r(x)≥0r(x)\ge0r(x)≥0 for all xxx, and let e,s:X→Ee,s:X\to Ee,s:X→E. Assume that x↦r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩x\mapsto r(x)\langle e(x),s(x)\ranglex↦r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩ is ν\nuν-almost-everywhere measurable and that r∥s∥2r\|s\|^2r∥s∥2 and r∥e∥2r\|e\|^2r∥e∥2 are ν\nuν-integrable. Then r⟨e,s⟩r\langle e,s\rangler⟨e,s⟩ is ν\nuν-integrable.

Proof. For every xxx, since r(x)≥0r(x)\ge0r(x)≥0, the previous step gives

∣r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩∣=r(x) ∣⟨e(x),s(x)⟩∣≤r(x)(14∥s(x)∥2+∥e(x)∥2)=14r(x)∥s(x)∥2+r(x)∥e(x)∥2.\begin{aligned}\bigl|r(x)\langle e(x),s(x)\rangle\bigr|&=r(x)\,\bigl|\langle e(x),s(x)\rangle\bigr|\\&\le r(x)\Bigl(\frac14\|s(x)\|^2+\|e(x)\|^2\Bigr)\\&=\frac14r(x)\|s(x)\|^2+r(x)\|e(x)\|^2.\end{aligned}​r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩​​=r(x)​⟨e(x),s(x)⟩​≤r(x)(41​∥s(x)∥2+∥e(x)∥2)=41​r(x)∥s(x)∥2+r(x)∥e(x)∥2.​

The right side is integrable, as a linear combination of two integrable functions. A measurable function whose absolute value is bounded by an integrable function is integrable. □\square□

Integrability of the pairing is therefore not an additional assumption once the Fisher information and the error energy are finite; only its measurability must be checked.

04The integrated Young inequality §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν, r≥0r\ge0r≥0, eee and sss be as in the previous step, and assume that r⟨e,s⟩r\langle e,s\rangler⟨e,s⟩, r∥s∥2r\|s\|^2r∥s∥2 and r∥e∥2r\|e\|^2r∥e∥2 are ν\nuν-integrable. Then

∫r⟨e,s⟩ dν≤14∫r∥s∥2 dν+∫r∥e∥2 dν.\int r\langle e,s\rangle\,d\nu\le\frac14\int r\|s\|^2\,d\nu+\int r\|e\|^2\,d\nu.∫r⟨e,s⟩dν≤41​∫r∥s∥2dν+∫r∥e∥2dν.

Proof. Multiplying the first step by r(x)≥0r(x)\ge0r(x)≥0 gives, for every xxx,

r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩≤14r(x)∥s(x)∥2+r(x)∥e(x)∥2.r(x)\langle e(x),s(x)\rangle\le\frac14r(x)\|s(x)\|^2+r(x)\|e(x)\|^2.r(x)⟨e(x),s(x)⟩≤41​r(x)∥s(x)∥2+r(x)∥e(x)∥2.

Both sides are integrable, so monotonicity of the integral and linearity of the integral on the right give the claim. □\square□

Nonnegativity of rrr is needed for the multiplication to preserve the inequality; normalization of rrr is not needed.
