Differentiate the entropy integrand under domination

The scalar identity (rlog⁡r)′=r′(log⁡r+1)(r\log r)\prime=r\prime(\log r+1)(rlogr)′=r′(logr+1), for r>0r>0r>0, lifts to an integral derivative under the explicitly supplied common local integrable bound. Domination is a premise, not a consequence of Gaussian convolution in this module.

Mathematical statement and proof

At a positive density value, ordinary calculus gives ∂t(rtlog⁡rt)=r˙t(log⁡rt+1)\partial_t(r_t\log r_t)=\dot r_t(\log r_t+1)∂t​(rt​logrt​)=r˙t​(logrt​+1). Passing this derivative through an integral requires more than the pointwise chain rule. The formal theorem assumes local measurability, integrability at the time under consideration, and an integrable function B(x)B(x)B(x) dominating ∣r˙u(x)(log⁡ru(x)+1)∣|\dot r_u(x)(\log r_u(x)+1)|∣r˙u​(x)(logru​(x)+1)∣ on a common time neighborhood. Pointwise differentiability and positivity hold there for almost every spatial point.

ddt∫rtlog⁡rt dν=∫r˙t(log⁡rt+1) dν. \frac{d}{dt}\int r_t\log r_t\,d\nu =\int\dot r_t(\log r_t+1)\,d\nu. dtd​∫rt​logrt​dν=∫r˙t​(logrt​+1)dν.

hasDerivAt_integral_mul_log_of_dominated proves both integrability of the differentiated integrand and this derivative. LocalEntropyRegularity packages the sufficient local data; it does not contain the entropy derivative as an assumed field. The common neighborhood and integrable bound are substantive analytic requirements for an application, particularly at the initial interpolation time.
