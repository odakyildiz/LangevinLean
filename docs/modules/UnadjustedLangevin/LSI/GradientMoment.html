Transfer the Gibbs score moment through a transport coupling

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound follows Theorem 2, Section 3.1, and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019). The Lean development supplies the regularity and endpoint arguments for the actual Gaussian ULA laws.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

For any finite-cost coupling of X∼μX\sim\muX∼μ and Y∼νY\sim\nuY∼ν, an LLL-Lipschitz gradient gives Eμ∥∇U∥2≤2L2E∥X−Y∥2+2Eν∥∇U∥2\mathbb E_\mu\|\nabla U\|^2\le2L^2\mathbb E\|X-Y\|^2+2\mathbb E_\nu\|\nabla U\|^2Eμ​∥∇U∥2≤2L2E∥X−Y∥2+2Eν​∥∇U∥2. This module proves that estimate together with gradient square integrability. For a normalized Gibbs target the second term is bounded by 2nL2nL2nL. An explicit entropy-cost coupling then supplies the exact gradient-moment bound used by the ULA proof. The final Gaussian ULA theorem instead derives its moment directly from entropy in EntropyMoment.lean; no transport implication is needed there.

01Derive both integrability and the coupling moment estimate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let ccc be a joint measure with marginals μ\muμ and ν\nuν. Assume ∫∥x−y∥2dc<∞\int\|x-y\|^2dc<\infty∫∥x−y∥2dc<∞, that the gradient is LLL-Lipschitz, and that ∫∥∇U∥2dν<∞\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu<\infty∫∥∇U∥2dν<∞. The theorem proves square integrability under μ\muμ and

∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤2L2∫∥x−y∥2 dc(x,y)+2∫∥∇U∥2dν.\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2\,dc(x,y)+2\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu.∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤2L2∫∥x−y∥2dc(x,y)+2∫∥∇U∥2dν.

At a pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y), write ∇U(x)=(∇U(x)−∇U(y))+∇U(y)\nabla U(x)=(\nabla U(x)-\nabla U(y))+\nabla U(y)∇U(x)=(∇U(x)−∇U(y))+∇U(y). The triangle inequality and Lipschitzness give ∥∇U(x)∥≤L∥x−y∥+∥∇U(y)∥\|\nabla U(x)\|\le L\|x-y\|+\|\nabla U(y)\|∥∇U(x)∥≤L∥x−y∥+∥∇U(y)∥. Squaring and applying (a+b)2≤2a2+2b2(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2(a+b)2≤2a2+2b2 proves the pointwise bound hpoint. Lean obtains the last elementary inequality from the nonnegative square (a−b)2(a-b)^2(a−b)2.

Before integrating the inequality, the proof establishes integrability: hsecond pulls the target squared-gradient integrand to the second marginal, while hmajor combines it with the integrable coupling cost. This dominating function and continuity of the gradient prove hfirst, integrability of the first-coordinate squared gradient. The map-integrability equivalence and the first-marginal identity then prove hμ. Thus a missing moment is derived, rather than represented by a possibly undefined real integral.

Finally integral_mono integrates hpoint. The identities hfirst_marginal and hsecond_marginal, proved by the pushforward integral formula, replace the joint-coordinate integrals by their marginal integrals and give exactly the displayed estimate.

02Insert the derived target score bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

For the normalized Gibbs probability ν=Z−1e−Udx\nu=Z^{-1}e^{-U}dxν=Z−1e−Udx, differentiability of UUU and an LLL-Lipschitz gradient imply the target estimate ∫∥∇U∥2dν≤nL\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\nu\le nL∫∥∇U∥2dν≤nL. Consequently every finite-cost coupling gives

∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤2L2∫∥x−y∥2dc+2nL.\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le2L^2\int\|x-y\|^2dc+2nL.∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤2L2∫∥x−y∥2dc+2nL.

The proof first derives Z<∞Z<\inftyZ<∞ from probability normalization, using gibbsPartition_ne_top_of_probability. The shared theorem ULA.memLp_gradient_gibbsMeasure then supplies target square integrability; its L2L^2L2 conclusion is converted into the integrability input for the preceding coupling theorem. The sharp target moment itself comes from ULA.integral_norm_sq_gradient_gibbs_le.

Those shared Gibbs results use translation invariance and a sub-Gaussian score argument under first-order smoothness, so strong convexity is unnecessary here. Substituting their target bound into the coupling estimate proves the stated result and retains the newly derived square integrability under μ\muμ.

03Obtain the exact entropy-dependent coefficient from transport control §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the preceding finite-cost coupling additionally satisfies

∫∥x−y∥2dc(x,y)≤2αDKL(μ∥ν),α>0,DKL(μ∥ν)<∞.\int\|x-y\|^2dc(x,y)\le\frac2\alpha D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu),\qquad \alpha>0,\quad D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu)<\infty.∫∥x−y∥2dc(x,y)≤α2​DKL​(μ∥ν),α>0,DKL​(μ∥ν)<∞.

Substitution into the coupling bound gives

∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤4L2αDKL(μ∥ν)+2nL.\int\|\nabla U\|^2d\mu\le\frac{4L^2}{\alpha}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu)+2nL.∫∥∇U∥2dμ≤α4L2​DKL​(μ∥ν)+2nL.

The Lean hypothesis htransport is exactly the displayed cost estimate for the supplied coupling. It is multiplied by the nonnegative factor 2L22L^22L2; heq verifies the coefficient identity 2L2(2/α)=4L2/α2L^2(2/\alpha)=4L^2/\alpha2L2(2/α)=4L2/α. The result also returns gradient square integrability from the previous theorem. Separate hypotheses record positive α\alphaα and finite extended KL before its real value is used.

This theorem completes the coupling-to-moment calculation and connects it to the already proved target score bound. It does not assert that LSI constructs this coupling or prove the LSI-to-Talagrand implication. The existing ULA endpoint still displays its gradient-moment input in this reusable coupling-based interface. The complete endpoint uses the direct score-exponential estimate in EntropyMoment.lean.
