Conditional expectation of the gradient discrepancy

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let YYY be an observed random variable and let DDD be a square-integrable random vector. Under the stated measurability assumptions, the conditional expectation given σ(Y)\sigma(Y)σ(Y) has a measurable representation e(Y)e(Y)e(Y). Conditional Jensen gives ∫∥e∥2d(Y#P)≤E∥D∥2\int\|e\|^2d(Y_\#P)\le\mathbb E\|D\|^2∫∥e∥2d(Y#​P)≤E∥D∥2. Applied to Dt=∇U(Yt)−∇U(X)D_t=\nabla U(Y_t)-\nabla U(X)Dt​=∇U(Yt​)−∇U(X), this produces the state-dependent discrepancy in the relative-density weak equation and preserves the Gaussian energy bound.

01A measurable representation of a conditional mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume D∈L2(P)D\in L^2(P)D∈L2(P) and YYY is measurable. The conditional expectation E[D∣σ(Y)]\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)]E[D∣σ(Y)] is strongly measurable with respect to σ(Y)\sigma(Y)σ(Y). Doob–Dynkin factorization, under the stated assumptions on the spaces, gives a strongly measurable field eee such that e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)]e(Y)=\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)]e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)] almost surely. Conditional Jensen and integration give

∫∥e(y)∥2d(Y#P)(y)=E∥E[D∣σ(Y)]∥2≤E∥D∥2.\int\|e(y)\|^2d(Y_\#P)(y)=\mathbb E\|\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)]\|^2\le\mathbb E\|D\|^2.∫∥e(y)∥2d(Y#​P)(y)=E∥E[D∣σ(Y)]∥2≤E∥D∥2.

The conditional expectation is square-integrable, so the integral on the left is finite. The field is determined only up to a null set under the law of YYY, which does not affect these integrals.

02The conditional mean on measurable sets §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose DDD is integrable and e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)]e(Y)=\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)]e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)] almost surely. For every measurable observation set AAA, the pushforward integral formula and the defining property of conditional expectation give

∫Ae(y)d(Y#P)(y)=∫Y−1(A)e(Y) dP=∫Y−1(A)D dP.\int_A e(y)d(Y_\#P)(y)=\int_{Y^{-1}(A)}e(Y)\,dP=\int_{Y^{-1}(A)}D\,dP.∫A​e(y)d(Y#​P)(y)=∫Y−1(A)​e(Y)dP=∫Y−1(A)​DdP.

The inverse image Y−1(A)Y^{-1}(A)Y−1(A) belongs to σ(Y)\sigma(Y)σ(Y), so the last equality is a valid conditional-expectation identity. All terms are vector-valued integrals under the stated integrability assumptions.

03Pairing the conditional mean with an observed test §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let φ\varphiφ be strongly measurable, and assume ⟨D,φ(Y)⟩\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle⟨D,φ(Y)⟩ is integrable. The function φ(Y)\varphi(Y)φ(Y) is measurable with respect to σ(Y)\sigma(Y)σ(Y). The conditional expectation rule for a continuous bilinear pairing therefore gives

E[⟨D,φ(Y)⟩∣σ(Y)]=⟨E[D∣σ(Y)],φ(Y)⟩.\mathbb E[\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle\mid\sigma(Y)]=\langle\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)],\varphi(Y)\rangle.E[⟨D,φ(Y)⟩∣σ(Y)]=⟨E[D∣σ(Y)],φ(Y)⟩.

Integrate this equality, replace the conditional mean by e(Y)e(Y)e(Y), and change variables by the law of YYY. The result is

∫⟨e(y),φ(y)⟩d(Y#P)(y)=E⟨D,φ(Y)⟩.\int\langle e(y),\varphi(y)\rangle d(Y_\#P)(y)=\mathbb E\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle.∫⟨e(y),φ(y)⟩d(Y#​P)(y)=E⟨D,φ(Y)⟩.

The integrability hypothesis is required for this test; it is not inferred for arbitrary unbounded functions.

04The gradient discrepancy before conditioning §

Mathematical statement and proof

On the product space of the initial state and independent Gaussian variable, put Yt(x,z)=x−t∇U(x)+2tzY_t(x,z)=x-t\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2t}zYt​(x,z)=x−t∇U(x)+2t​z and define

Dt(x,z)=∇U ⁣(x−t∇U(x)+2tz)−∇U(x).D_t(x,z)=\nabla U\!\left(x-t\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2t}z\right)-\nabla U(x).Dt​(x,z)=∇U(x−t∇U(x)+2t​z)−∇U(x).

Thus Dt=∇U(Yt)−∇U(X)D_t=\nabla U(Y_t)-\nabla U(X)Dt​=∇U(Yt​)−∇U(X). The order of subtraction fixes its sign in the weak equation: the conditional field is et(y)=∇U(y)−E[∇U(X)∣Yt=y]e_t(y)=\nabla U(y)-\mathbb E[\nabla U(X)\mid Y_t=y]et​(y)=∇U(y)−E[∇U(X)∣Yt​=y]. The definition alone asserts no integrability or energy bound.

05The sigma-algebra of the interpolated state §

Mathematical statement and proof

The observation at elapsed time ttt is YtY_tYt​, and its sigma-algebra is σ(Yt)={Yt−1(A):A measurable}\sigma(Y_t)=\{Y_t^{-1}(A):A\text{ measurable}\}σ(Yt​)={Yt−1​(A):A measurable}, interpreted as the generated sigma-algebra. A random variable measurable for this sigma-algebra depends only on the value of YtY_tYt​. Conditioning the gradient discrepancy on σ(Yt)\sigma(Y_t)σ(Yt​) therefore yields a field on the state space. If the gradient is Lipschitz, the observation map is measurable and this sigma-algebra is contained in the ambient one.

07The discrepancy energy relative to the Gibbs measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose a measurable nonnegative density identifies the interpolation law as ρt=rtν\rho_t=r_t\nuρt​=rt​ν. The conditional-mean construction gives a square-integrable field under ρt\rho_tρt​. Changing measure proves integrability of the weighted square under ν\nuν, and

∫rt(y)∥et(y)∥2dν(y)=∫∥et(y)∥2dρt(y)≤t2L2G0+2tnL2.\int r_t(y)\|e_t(y)\|^2d\nu(y)=\int\|e_t(y)\|^2d\rho_t(y)\le t^2L^2G_0+2tnL^2.∫rt​(y)∥et​(y)∥2dν(y)=∫∥et​(y)∥2dρt​(y)≤t2L2G0​+2tnL2.

The conditional-mean identity is unchanged. This is the form of the energy estimate that appears in entropy production.
