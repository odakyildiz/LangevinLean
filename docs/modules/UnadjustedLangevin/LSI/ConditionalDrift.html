References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound follows Theorem 2, Section 3.1, and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019). The Lean development supplies the regularity and endpoint arguments for the actual Gaussian ULA laws.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

The actual frozen Gaussian interpolation is Yt=X−t∇U(X)+2tZY_t=X-t\nabla U(X)+\sqrt{2t}ZYt​=X−t∇U(X)+2t​Z, where (X,Z)(X,Z)(X,Z) has the independent law P=μ⊗N(0,I)P=\mu\otimes\mathcal N(0,I)P=μ⊗N(0,I). Write Dt=∇U(Yt)−∇U(X)D_t=\nabla U(Y_t)-\nabla U(X)Dt​=∇U(Yt​)−∇U(X). This module constructs a measurable field ete_tet​ satisfying et(Yt)=E[Dt∣Yt]e_t(Y_t)=\mathbb E[D_t\mid Y_t]et​(Yt​)=E[Dt​∣Yt​], proves its flux identities, and derives its squared-energy bound by conditional Jensen. Thus the state-dependent error is a consequence of the joint Gaussian construction. The entropy weak equation is a separate analytic obligation.

02Identify the vector flux on every measurable set §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose a strongly measurable field eee satisfies the conditional-mean identity e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)]e(Y)=\mathbb E[D\mid\sigma(Y)]e(Y)=E[D∣σ(Y)], with DDD integrable. For every measurable A⊆SA\subseteq SA⊆S,

∫Ae(y) d(Y#P)(y)=∫Y−1(A)D(ω) dP(ω).\int_A e(y)\,d(Y_\#P)(y)=\int_{Y^{-1}(A)}D(\omega)\,dP(\omega).∫A​e(y)d(Y#​P)(y)=∫Y−1(A)​D(ω)dP(ω).

The first step, setIntegral_map, pulls the left integral back to ∫Y−1(A)e(Y)dP\int_{Y^{-1}(A)}e(Y)dP∫Y−1(A)​e(Y)dP. The almost-sure conditional-mean identity changes its integrand to the conditional expectation. Because Y−1(A)Y^{-1}(A)Y−1(A) belongs to the pulled-back sigma algebra, setIntegral_condExp replaces that expectation by DDD in the set integral. The probability assumption guarantees the finite-measure hypotheses for conditional expectation. This proves a vector-valued identification of the flux, extending beyond the energy estimate.

03Transfer integrable inner-product tests through the conditional mean §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let φ:S→F\varphi:S\to Fφ:S→F be strongly measurable and suppose ⟨D,φ(Y)⟩\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle⟨D,φ(Y)⟩ is integrable. Then

∫S⟨e(y),φ(y)⟩ d(Y#P)(y)=∫Ω⟨D,φ(Y)⟩ dP.\int_S\langle e(y),\varphi(y)\rangle\,d(Y_\#P)(y)=\int_\Omega\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle\,dP.∫S​⟨e(y),φ(y)⟩d(Y#​P)(y)=∫Ω​⟨D,φ(Y)⟩dP.

This is the flux identity needed when the test depends on the current interpolated state. The proof gives YYY its pulled-back sigma algebra and observes that φ∘Y\varphi\circ Yφ∘Y is strongly measurable for that algebra; the Lean witnesses are hYpull and hφ.comp_measurable hYpull. Conditional expectation can therefore pull this factor out of the continuous bilinear inner product:

E[⟨D,φ(Y)⟩∣Y]=⟨E[D∣Y],φ(Y)⟩.\mathbb E[\langle D,\varphi(Y)\rangle\mid Y]=\langle\mathbb E[D\mid Y],\varphi(Y)\rangle.E[⟨D,φ(Y)⟩∣Y]=⟨E[D∣Y],φ(Y)⟩.

The pushforward integral is pulled back, hcond substitutes the conditional expectation, and integrating hpull removes the conditioning. The statement allows noncompact tests whenever its displayed product is integrable; it does not prove a Fokker–Planck equation or the integrability of an arbitrary entropy test.
