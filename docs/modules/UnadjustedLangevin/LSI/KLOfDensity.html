Identify density entropy and Fisher integrals

For a strictly positive relative density, with the stated differentiability and integrability, the actual likelihood ratio equals log⁡r\log rlogr. This identifies the formal KL divergence with ∫rlog⁡r dν\int r\log r\,d\nu∫rlogrdν, proves its finiteness and identifies the real Fisher value with its score-energy integral.

Mathematical statement and proof

For a measurable nonnegative rrr, change of measure gives ∫g d(rν)=∫rg dν\int g\,d(r\nu)=\int rg\,d\nu∫gd(rν)=∫rgdν. The Radon–Nikodym derivative of the tilted measure with respect to ν\nuν is the specified density, almost everywhere. Consequently its log-likelihood ratio is log⁡r\log rlogr, almost everywhere for the tilted measure.

When rνr\nurν and ν\nuν are probability measures and rlog⁡rr\log rrlogr is integrable, the actual KL divergence is finite and its real value is precisely

Hν(rν)=∫rlog⁡r dν. H_\nu(r\nu)=\int r\log r\,d\nu. Hν​(rν)=∫rlogrdν.

These measure-theoretic identifications justify using the familiar density formula in the entropy calculus. The analogous Fisher formula is Jν(rν)=∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2dνJ_\nu(r\nu)=\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2d\nuJν​(rν)=∫r∥∇logr∥2dν, with squared-score integrability retained. Positivity and smoothness will make the score well defined pointwise; the measure-change identities themselves need less regularity.

The integrability equivalence integrable_log_tiltedMeasure_iff is useful here: integrability of log⁡r\log rlogr under rνr\nurν is exactly integrability of rlog⁡rr\log rrlogr under ν\nuν. Thus the finite-KL proof supplies the absolute-continuity and likelihood-integrability requirements of Mathlib's divergence, rather than assuming that a formally written integral is automatically finite. Similarly, relativeFisher_ne_top derives finite extended Fisher information from integrability of the squared score.
