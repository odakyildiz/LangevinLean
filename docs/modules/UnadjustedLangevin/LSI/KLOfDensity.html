Integral formulas for relative entropy and Fisher information

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let (E,E)(E,\mathcal E)(E,E) be a measurable space, ν\nuν a measure on it, and r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R a measurable function with r≥0r\ge0r≥0; rνr\nurν is the measure A↦∫Ar dνA\mapsto\int_Ar\,d\nuA↦∫A​rdν. The relative entropy Hν(rν)H_\nu(r\nu)Hν​(rν) is defined through the Radon–Nikodym derivative d(rν)/dνd(r\nu)/d\nud(rν)/dν and its logarithm, the log-likelihood ratio, while the computations in the entropy analysis are carried out with the function rrr itself. This section connects the two descriptions. It proves the change-of-measure formula ∫g d(rν)=∫rg dν\int g\,d(r\nu)=\int rg\,d\nu∫gd(rν)=∫rgdν; the equivalence log⁡r∈L1(rν)  ⟺  rlog⁡r∈L1(ν)\log r\in L^1(r\nu)\iff r\log r\in L^1(\nu)logr∈L1(rν)⟺rlogr∈L1(ν); the identity of the log-likelihood ratio with log⁡r\log rlogr almost everywhere; and, as consequences, that rlog⁡r∈L1(ν)r\log r\in L^1(\nu)rlogr∈L1(ν) implies Hν(rν)<∞H_\nu(r\nu)<\inftyHν​(rν)<∞ and, for probability measures,

Hν(rν)=∫Erlog⁡r dν.H_\nu(r\nu)=\int_Er\log r\,d\nu.Hν​(rν)=∫E​rlogrdν.

When EEE is a real inner product space, it also proves that square integrability of ∥∇log⁡r∥\|\nabla\log r\|∥∇logr∥ under rνr\nurν gives finite Fisher information with Jν(r)=∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dνJ_\nu(r)=\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nuJν​(r)=∫r∥∇logr∥2dν. Only measurability and nonnegativity of rrr are needed for the measure-change facts; positivity and smoothness enter only through the Fisher information.

In this section integrals of real or vector-valued functions are Lebesgue (Bochner) integrals, with the convention that the integral of a function that is not integrable is 000; every statement below is either an identity valid under this convention or carries an integrability hypothesis. For a finite measure ρ≪ν\rho\ll\nuρ≪ν with log-likelihood ratio ℓ=log⁡dρdν∈L1(ρ)\ell=\log\frac{d\rho}{d\nu}\in L^1(\rho)ℓ=logdνdρ​∈L1(ρ), the relative entropy is Hν(ρ)=∫ℓ dρ+ν(E)−ρ(E)H_\nu(\rho)=\int\ell\,d\rho+\nu(E)-\rho(E)Hν​(ρ)=∫ℓdρ+ν(E)−ρ(E), a nonnegative real number; otherwise Hν(ρ)=∞H_\nu(\rho)=\inftyHν​(ρ)=∞. For probability measures the correction ν(E)−ρ(E)\nu(E)-\rho(E)ν(E)−ρ(E) vanishes.

01Integration against a measure with density §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν be a measure on EEE, let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be measurable with r(x)≥0r(x)\ge0r(x)≥0 for all xxx, and let g:E→Rg:E\to\mathbb Rg:E→R be any function. Then

∫Eg d(rν)=∫Er g dν,\int_Eg\,d(r\nu)=\int_Er\,g\,d\nu,∫E​gd(rν)=∫E​rgdν,

with the convention that the integral of a non-integrable function is 000.

Proof. Nonnegative measurable functions. For a measurable set AAA, (rν)(A)=∫Ar dν=∫r1A dν(r\nu)(A)=\int_Ar\,d\nu=\int r\mathbf 1_A\,d\nu(rν)(A)=∫A​rdν=∫r1A​dν, so the formula ∫f d(rν)=∫rf dν\int f\,d(r\nu)=\int rf\,d\nu∫fd(rν)=∫rfdν holds for indicator functions, by linearity for nonnegative simple functions, and by the monotone convergence theorem (applied on both sides to an increasing sequence of simple functions converging to fff) for every measurable f:E→[0,∞]f:E\to[0,\infty]f:E→[0,∞].

Integrability. Applying this to f=∣g∣f=|g|f=∣g∣ for measurable ggg gives ∫∣g∣ d(rν)=∫r∣g∣ dν=∫∣rg∣ dν\int|g|\,d(r\nu)=\int r|g|\,d\nu=\int|rg|\,d\nu∫∣g∣d(rν)=∫r∣g∣dν=∫∣rg∣dν, so g∈L1(rν)g\in L^1(r\nu)g∈L1(rν) if and only if rg∈L1(ν)rg\in L^1(\nu)rg∈L1(ν). Measurability up to null sets transfers as well: if ggg agrees rνr\nurν-almost everywhere with a measurable g~\tilde gg~​, then rg=rg~rg=r\tilde grg=rg~​ ν\nuν-almost everywhere, because the two can differ only on a ν\nuν-null subset of {r>0}\{r>0\}{r>0} or on {r=0}\{r=0\}{r=0}, where both vanish; conversely, if rg=hrg=hrg=h ν\nuν-almost everywhere with hhh measurable, then g=1{r>0}h/rg=\mathbf 1_{\{r>0\}}h/rg=1{r>0}​h/r holds rνr\nurν-almost everywhere, since (rν)({r=0})=0(r\nu)(\{r=0\})=0(rν)({r=0})=0.

Thus ggg is integrable with respect to rνr\nurν if and only if rgrgrg is integrable with respect to ν\nuν.

The identity. If g∈L1(rν)g\in L^1(r\nu)g∈L1(rν), write g=g+−g−g=g^+-g^-g=g+−g−; both parts are integrable, the nonnegative case applies to each, and subtracting gives ∫g d(rν)=∫rg+ dν−∫rg− dν=∫rg dν\int g\,d(r\nu)=\int rg^+\,d\nu-\int rg^-\,d\nu=\int rg\,d\nu∫gd(rν)=∫rg+dν−∫rg−dν=∫rgdν. If g∉L1(rν)g\notin L^1(r\nu)g∈/L1(rν), then rg∉L1(ν)rg\notin L^1(\nu)rg∈/L1(ν) and both sides are 000 by the convention. □\square□

The density is finite at every point, so the product rgrgrg is an ordinary product of real numbers. This formula is used for g=log⁡rg=\log rg=logr and g=∥∇log⁡r∥2g=\|\nabla\log r\|^2g=∥∇logr∥2 below.

02Two equivalent integrability conditions for the logarithm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν be a measure on EEE and r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R measurable with r≥0r\ge0r≥0. Then

log⁡r∈L1(rν)⟺rlog⁡r∈L1(ν).\log r\in L^1(r\nu)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\log r\in L^1(\nu).logr∈L1(rν)⟺rlogr∈L1(ν).

Here log⁡0\log0log0 is given the value 000; this value plays no role, since (rν)({r=0})=0(r\nu)(\{r=0\})=0(rν)({r=0})=0 and rlog⁡r=0r\log r=0rlogr=0 on {r=0}\{r=0\}{r=0}.

Proof. This is the integrability part of the previous step with g=log⁡rg=\log rg=logr, which is measurable: ∫∣log⁡r∣ d(rν)=∫r∣log⁡r∣ dν\int|\log r|\,d(r\nu)=\int r|\log r|\,d\nu∫∣logr∣d(rν)=∫r∣logr∣dν, so one side is finite exactly when the other is. □\square□

The equivalence concerns the absolute integrals, so it controls the positive and the negative parts of the logarithm simultaneously. It converts the integrability of the log-likelihood ratio required by the definition of relative entropy into a condition on the entropy integrand rlog⁡rr\log rrlogr with respect to the reference measure.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

integrable_log_tiltedMeasure_iff applies Mathlib's integrable_withDensity_iff_integrable_smul' (integrability of ggg with respect to fνf\nufν is equivalent to integrability of f⋅gf\cdot gf⋅g with respect to ν\nuν, for a measurable density fff finite almost everywhere) to g=log⁡rg=\log rg=logr and the density ENNReal.ofReal (r x); simpa only [ENNReal.toReal_ofReal (hr _), smul_eq_mul] simplifies the weight to rrr. Mathlib's Real.log satisfies log⁡0=0\log0=0log0=0, as in the claim.

03The log-likelihood ratio of a measure with density §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν be a σ\sigmaσ-finite measure on EEE and r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R measurable with r≥0r\ge0r≥0. Then the log-likelihood ratio of rνr\nurν with respect to ν\nuν satisfies

log⁡d(rν)dν(x)=log⁡r(x)for (rν)-almost every x.\log\frac{d(r\nu)}{d\nu}(x)=\log r(x)\qquad\text{for }(r\nu)\text{-almost every }x.logdνd(rν)​(x)=logr(x)for (rν)-almost every x.

Proof. If ν(A)=0\nu(A)=0ν(A)=0 then (rν)(A)=∫Ar dν=0(r\nu)(A)=\int_Ar\,d\nu=0(rν)(A)=∫A​rdν=0, so rν≪νr\nu\ll\nurν≪ν. Since ν\nuν is σ\sigmaσ-finite, the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the measure rνr\nurν with respect to ν\nuν exists and is ν\nuν-almost everywhere unique; as rrr is a measurable density of rνr\nurν, we get d(rν)dν=r\frac{d(r\nu)}{d\nu}=rdνd(rν)​=r ν\nuν-almost everywhere. The exceptional set is ν\nuν-null, hence (rν)(r\nu)(rν)-null by absolute continuity, so the equality also holds (rν)(r\nu)(rν)-almost everywhere. Taking logarithms of the two equal nonnegative real numbers gives the claim. □\square□

The log-likelihood ratio is defined only up to null sets, through an arbitrary version of the Radon–Nikodym derivative; this claim allows it to be replaced by the explicit function log⁡r\log rlogr inside integrals with respect to rνr\nurν.

04Finite entropy from an integrable entropy integrand §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν be σ\sigmaσ-finite, let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be measurable with r≥0r\ge0r≥0, and assume rlog⁡r∈L1(ν)r\log r\in L^1(\nu)rlogr∈L1(ν). Then Hν(rν)<∞H_\nu(r\nu)<\inftyHν​(rν)<∞.

Proof. The divergence Hν(rν)H_\nu(r\nu)Hν​(rν) is finite as soon as rν≪νr\nu\ll\nurν≪ν and the log-likelihood ratio ℓ=log⁡d(rν)dν\ell=\log\frac{d(r\nu)}{d\nu}ℓ=logdνd(rν)​ is integrable with respect to rνr\nurν. Absolute continuity was shown in the previous step. By the second step, rlog⁡r∈L1(ν)r\log r\in L^1(\nu)rlogr∈L1(ν) gives log⁡r∈L1(rν)\log r\in L^1(r\nu)logr∈L1(rν). By the previous step ℓ=log⁡r\ell=\log rℓ=logr (rν)(r\nu)(rν)-almost everywhere, and a function that agrees almost everywhere with an integrable function is integrable. Hence ℓ∈L1(rν)\ell\in L^1(r\nu)ℓ∈L1(rν) and Hν(rν)<∞H_\nu(r\nu)<\inftyHν​(rν)<∞. □\square□

No normalization of rνr\nurν or ν\nuν is needed for finiteness. This criterion is how finite entropy is established for the explicit densities of the interpolation: one proves integrability of rlog⁡rr\log rrlogr, a condition on a concrete function, rather than integrability of an abstract log-likelihood ratio.

05The entropy as an integral of the density §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν be a probability measure, let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be measurable with r≥0r\ge0r≥0, assume that rνr\nurν is a probability measure and that rlog⁡r∈L1(ν)r\log r\in L^1(\nu)rlogr∈L1(ν). Then Hν(rν)H_\nu(r\nu)Hν​(rν) is finite and

Hν(rν)=∫Erlog⁡r dν.H_\nu(r\nu)=\int_Er\log r\,d\nu.Hν​(rν)=∫E​rlogrdν.

Proof. Finiteness is the previous step. Let ℓ\ellℓ be the log-likelihood ratio; as in the previous step, rν≪νr\nu\ll\nurν≪ν and ℓ∈L1(rν)\ell\in L^1(r\nu)ℓ∈L1(rν), so by definition

Hν(rν)=∫ℓ d(rν)+ν(E)−(rν)(E)=∫ℓ d(rν)+1−1=∫ℓ d(rν).H_\nu(r\nu)=\int\ell\,d(r\nu)+\nu(E)-(r\nu)(E)=\int\ell\,d(r\nu)+1-1=\int\ell\,d(r\nu).Hν​(rν)=∫ℓd(rν)+ν(E)−(rν)(E)=∫ℓd(rν)+1−1=∫ℓd(rν).

Since ℓ=log⁡r\ell=\log rℓ=logr (rν)(r\nu)(rν)-almost everywhere (third step), ∫ℓ d(rν)=∫log⁡r d(rν)\int\ell\,d(r\nu)=\int\log r\,d(r\nu)∫ℓd(rν)=∫logrd(rν), and by the change-of-measure formula of the first step with g=log⁡rg=\log rg=logr, ∫log⁡r d(rν)=∫rlog⁡r dν\int\log r\,d(r\nu)=\int r\log r\,d\nu∫logrd(rν)=∫rlogrdν. □\square□

Both probability assumptions are used to cancel the correction term ν(E)−(rν)(E)\nu(E)-(r\nu)(E)ν(E)−(rν)(E) in the definition of the divergence for finite measures. The resulting signed integral is what is differentiated in time in the entropy calculation; its use requires the integrability hypothesis, which also guarantees that the extended divergence is finite.

06Finite Fisher information from integrability §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE be a real inner product space with a σ\sigmaσ-algebra, ν\nuν a measure on EEE and r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R a function such that ∥∇log⁡r∥2\|\nabla\log r\|^2∥∇logr∥2 is integrable with respect to rνr\nurν. Then Jν(r)<∞J_\nu(r)<\inftyJν​(r)<∞.

Proof. By definition Jν(r)J_\nu(r)Jν​(r) is the integral, in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞], of the nonnegative function ∥∇log⁡r∥2\|\nabla\log r\|^2∥∇logr∥2 with respect to rνr\nurν. A real function f≥0f\ge0f≥0 is integrable exactly when it is almost-everywhere measurable and its integral in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] is finite. The hypothesis therefore gives Jν(r)<∞J_\nu(r)<\inftyJν​(r)<∞. □\square□

This is the finiteness condition under which the log-Sobolev inequality becomes an inequality between real numbers.

07The Fisher information as an integral against the reference measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE be a real inner product space with a σ\sigmaσ-algebra, ν\nuν a measure on EEE, r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R measurable with r≥0r\ge0r≥0, and assume that ∥∇log⁡r∥2\|\nabla\log r\|^2∥∇logr∥2 is integrable with respect to rνr\nurν. Then the finite number Jν(r)J_\nu(r)Jν​(r) satisfies

Jν(r)=∫E∥∇log⁡r∥2 d(rν)=∫Er ∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν.J_\nu(r)=\int_E\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d(r\nu)=\int_Er\,\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu.Jν​(r)=∫E​∥∇logr∥2d(rν)=∫E​r∥∇logr∥2dν.

Proof. For an integrable nonnegative function, the integral in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞] is finite and equals the Lebesgue integral as a real number; this gives the first equality, with Jν(r)J_\nu(r)Jν​(r) finite by the previous step. The second is the change-of-measure formula of the first step with g=∥∇log⁡r∥2g=\|\nabla\log r\|^2g=∥∇logr∥2. □\square□

For a strictly positive differentiable rrr the integrand is r∥∇log⁡r∥2=∥∇r∥2/rr\|\nabla\log r\|^2=\|\nabla r\|^2/rr∥∇logr∥2=∥∇r∥2/r. This representation, as an integral with respect to the reference measure ν\nuν, is the form in which the Fisher information appears after integration by parts in the entropy calculation.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

relativeFisher_toReal_eq_integral proves (relativeFisher ν r).toReal = ∫ x, r x * ‖gradient (fun y ↦ Real.log (r y)) x‖ ^ 2 ∂ν by a two-line calc. The first line is Mathlib's integral_eq_lintegral_of_nonneg_ae read backwards: for an almost-everywhere nonnegative, almost-everywhere strongly measurable fff, ∫f dμ\int f\,d\mu∫fdμ equals the real value of ∫⁻ a, ENNReal.ofReal (f a) ∂μ; nonnegativity is sq_nonneg and measurability is hint.aestronglyMeasurable. The second line is integral_tiltedMeasure.
