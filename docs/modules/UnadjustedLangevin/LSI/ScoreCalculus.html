Relative scores and the entropy test

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let EEE be a complete real inner product space and r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R a strictly positive differentiable function, a density with respect to a reference measure ν\nuν. Its relative score is ∇log⁡r\nabla\log r∇logr. This section proves the pointwise identities

∇log⁡r=∇rr,∇(log⁡r+1)=∇log⁡r,⟨∇r,∇(log⁡r+1)⟩=r ∥∇log⁡r∥2,\nabla\log r=\frac{\nabla r}{r},\qquad\nabla(\log r+1)=\nabla\log r,\qquad\bigl\langle\nabla r,\nabla(\log r+1)\bigr\rangle=r\,\|\nabla\log r\|^2,∇logr=r∇r​,∇(logr+1)=∇logr,⟨∇r,∇(logr+1)⟩=r∥∇logr∥2,

and uses them to evaluate a weak equation at the entropy test function ψ=log⁡r+1\psi=\log r+1ψ=logr+1. If r˙\dot rr˙ satisfies

∫r˙ ψ dν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ dν+∫r⟨e,∇ψ⟩ dν\int\dot r\,\psi\,d\nu=-\int\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu+\int r\langle e,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu∫r˙ψdν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩dν+∫r⟨e,∇ψ⟩dν

for every admissible C1C^1C1 test ψ\psiψ, then

∫r˙ (log⁡r+1) dν=−∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν+∫r⟨e,∇log⁡r⟩ dν,\int\dot r\,(\log r+1)\,d\nu=-\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu+\int r\langle e,\nabla\log r\rangle\,d\nu,∫r˙(logr+1)dν=−∫r∥∇logr∥2dν+∫r⟨e,∇logr⟩dν,

and without the drift error eee the second term is absent. Since ddt∫rtlog⁡rt dν=∫r˙t(log⁡rt+1) dν\frac{d}{dt}\int r_t\log r_t\,d\nu=\int\dot r_t(\log r_t+1)\,d\nudtd​∫rt​logrt​dν=∫r˙t​(logrt​+1)dν, this expresses the entropy derivative through the relative Fisher information Jν(r)=∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dνJ_\nu(r)=\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nuJν​(r)=∫r∥∇logr∥2dν and the drift-score pairing; it is the integration-by-parts step in the proof of Vempala and Wibisono (2019), Lemma 3. The test log⁡r+1\log r+1logr+1 is in general unbounded and not compactly supported, so the weak equation is assumed for all C1C^1C1 tests whose three pairings are integrable, and these integrabilities are verified for the entropy test.

01The gradient of the logarithm of a positive function §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be differentiable at xxx with r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0. Then

∇log⁡r(x)=r(x)−1 ∇r(x).\nabla\log r(x)=r(x)^{-1}\,\nabla r(x).∇logr(x)=r(x)−1∇r(x).

Proof. The logarithm is differentiable at r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0 with derivative 1/r(x)1/r(x)1/r(x). By the chain rule, the Fréchet derivative of log⁡r\log rlogr at xxx is the linear functional D(log⁡r)(x)=r(x)−1Dr(x)D(\log r)(x)=r(x)^{-1}Dr(x)D(logr)(x)=r(x)−1Dr(x). The gradient is the vector representing the derivative through the inner product, ⟨∇f(x),v⟩=Df(x)v\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle=Df(x)v⟨∇f(x),v⟩=Df(x)v for all vvv, and this representation (the Riesz isomorphism between EEE and its dual) is linear. Hence ∇log⁡r(x)=r(x)−1∇r(x)\nabla\log r(x)=r(x)^{-1}\nabla r(x)∇logr(x)=r(x)−1∇r(x). □\square□

Positivity is used twice: to differentiate the logarithm at r(x)r(x)r(x), and to make r(x)−1r(x)^{-1}r(x)−1 defined.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

In gradient_log_of_pos, hdiff : DifferentiableAt ℝ r x and hpos : 0 < r x are the hypotheses. Mathlib defines gradient f x as the image of the Fréchet derivative fderiv ℝ f x under the inverse of the Riesz isomorphism InnerProductSpace.toDual, which needs completeness of EEE (the variable [CompleteSpace E]). The proof simp only [gradient, fderiv.log hdiff hpos.ne', map_smul] unfolds the gradient, replaces the derivative of log⁡r\log rlogr by (r(x))−1⋅Dr(x)(r(x))^{-1}\cdot Dr(x)(r(x))−1⋅Dr(x) using the chain rule fderiv.log (which needs r(x)≠0r(x)\neq0r(x)=0), and pulls the scalar through the linear isomorphism by map_smul.

02The gradient of the entropy test §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be differentiable at xxx with r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0. Then

∇(log⁡r+1)(x)=∇log⁡r(x).\nabla(\log r+1)(x)=\nabla\log r(x).∇(logr+1)(x)=∇logr(x).

Proof. A function and its sum with a constant have the same increments, (f+1)(x+v)−(f+1)(x)=f(x+v)−f(x)(f+1)(x+v)-(f+1)(x)=f(x+v)-f(x)(f+1)(x+v)−(f+1)(x)=f(x+v)−f(x); hence one is differentiable at xxx exactly when the other is, with the same derivative, and the gradients coincide. Apply this to f=log⁡rf=\log rf=logr. □\square□

The two hypotheses are not needed for this identity; they are stated because it is used only for positive differentiable densities. The constant 111 appears in the time derivative ddt(rtlog⁡rt)=r˙t(log⁡rt+1)\frac{d}{dt}(r_t\log r_t)=\dot r_t(\log r_t+1)dtd​(rt​logrt​)=r˙t​(logrt​+1), but it does not change spatial gradients, so only the relative score enters the pairing in the weak equation.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

gradient_log_add_one_of_pos is proved by simp only [gradient, fderiv_add_const]; Mathlib's fderiv_add_const states that the derivative of f+cf+cf+c equals that of fff for every function fff (with Mathlib's convention that the derivative is 000 at points of non-differentiability). The hypotheses are named _hdiff and _hpos; the leading underscore marks them as unused, as observed after the proof.

03The Dirichlet integrand of the entropy test §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let r:E→Rr:E\to\mathbb Rr:E→R be differentiable at xxx with r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0. Then

⟨∇r(x),∇(log⁡r+1)(x)⟩=r(x) ∥∇log⁡r(x)∥2.\bigl\langle\nabla r(x),\nabla(\log r+1)(x)\bigr\rangle=r(x)\,\|\nabla\log r(x)\|^2.⟨∇r(x),∇(logr+1)(x)⟩=r(x)∥∇logr(x)∥2.

Proof. By the first step, ∇log⁡r(x)=r(x)−1∇r(x)\nabla\log r(x)=r(x)^{-1}\nabla r(x)∇logr(x)=r(x)−1∇r(x); multiplying by r(x)r(x)r(x) and using r(x)⋅r(x)−1=1r(x)\cdot r(x)^{-1}=1r(x)⋅r(x)−1=1 gives ∇r(x)=r(x) ∇log⁡r(x)\nabla r(x)=r(x)\,\nabla\log r(x)∇r(x)=r(x)∇logr(x). By the second step, ∇(log⁡r+1)(x)=∇log⁡r(x)\nabla(\log r+1)(x)=\nabla\log r(x)∇(logr+1)(x)=∇logr(x). Therefore, by linearity of the inner product in its first argument,

⟨∇r(x),∇(log⁡r+1)(x)⟩=⟨r(x)∇log⁡r(x),∇log⁡r(x)⟩=r(x) ∥∇log⁡r(x)∥2.□\bigl\langle\nabla r(x),\nabla(\log r+1)(x)\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle r(x)\nabla\log r(x),\nabla\log r(x)\bigr\rangle=r(x)\,\|\nabla\log r(x)\|^2.\qquad\square⟨∇r(x),∇(logr+1)(x)⟩=⟨r(x)∇logr(x),∇logr(x)⟩=r(x)∥∇logr(x)∥2.□

The left side is the integrand of the Dirichlet pairing ∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ dν\int\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩dν for the entropy test ψ=log⁡r+1\psi=\log r+1ψ=logr+1; the right side is the integrand of the relative Fisher information written as ∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu∫r∥∇logr∥2dν. The identity is pointwise; integrating it requires integrability, which is assumed in the next steps.

In inner_gradient_entropy_test, hscore is the first step; hreverse is ∇r=r⋅∇log⁡r\nabla r=r\cdot\nabla\log r∇r=r⋅∇logr, obtained by rewriting with hscore, combining the scalars (smul_smul), cancelling r(x)⋅r(x)−1r(x)\cdot r(x)^{-1}r(x)⋅r(x)−1 (mul_inv_cancel₀, needing r(x)≠0r(x)\neq0r(x)=0) and one_smul. 04Entropy production for the forced weak identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE carry a σ\sigmaσ-algebra and let ν\nuν be a measure on EEE. Let r,r˙:E→Rr,\dot r:E\to\mathbb Rr,r˙:E→R and e:E→Ee:E\to Ee:E→E, with r(x)>0r(x)>0r(x)>0 for every xxx and rrr continuously differentiable. Assume that

  1. r˙ (log⁡r+1)\dot r\,(\log r+1)r˙(logr+1), r∥∇log⁡r∥2r\|\nabla\log r\|^2r∥∇logr∥2 and r⟨e,∇log⁡r⟩r\langle e,\nabla\log r\rangler⟨e,∇logr⟩ are ν\nuν-integrable;
  2. (forced weak identity) for every continuously differentiable ψ:E→R\psi:E\to\mathbb Rψ:E→R such that r˙ψ\dot r\psir˙ψ, ⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ and r⟨e,∇ψ⟩r\langle e,\nabla\psi\rangler⟨e,∇ψ⟩ are ν\nuν-integrable, ∫r˙ ψ dν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ dν+∫r⟨e,∇ψ⟩ dν.\int\dot r\,\psi\,d\nu=-\int\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu+\int r\langle e,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu.∫r˙ψdν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩dν+∫r⟨e,∇ψ⟩dν.

Then

∫r˙ (log⁡r+1) dν=−∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν+∫r⟨e,∇log⁡r⟩ dν.\int\dot r\,(\log r+1)\,d\nu=-\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu+\int r\langle e,\nabla\log r\rangle\,d\nu.∫r˙(logr+1)dν=−∫r∥∇logr∥2dν+∫r⟨e,∇logr⟩dν.

Proof. Let ψ=log⁡r+1\psi=\log r+1ψ=logr+1. Since rrr is C1C^1C1 and never zero, log⁡r\log rlogr is C1C^1C1 by the chain rule, and adding a constant preserves this; so ψ\psiψ is C1C^1C1. We check the three integrability conditions of hypothesis 2. First, r˙ψ=r˙(log⁡r+1)\dot r\psi=\dot r(\log r+1)r˙ψ=r˙(logr+1) is integrable by hypothesis 1. Second, by the third step, ⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩=r∥∇log⁡r∥2\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle=r\|\nabla\log r\|^2⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩=r∥∇logr∥2 at every point, which is integrable by hypothesis 1. Third, by the second step, ∇ψ=∇log⁡r\nabla\psi=\nabla\log r∇ψ=∇logr, so r⟨e,∇ψ⟩=r⟨e,∇log⁡r⟩r\langle e,\nabla\psi\rangle=r\langle e,\nabla\log r\rangler⟨e,∇ψ⟩=r⟨e,∇logr⟩, integrable by hypothesis 1. Hypothesis 2 therefore applies to ψ\psiψ, and substituting the two pointwise identities into its three integrals gives the claim. □\square□

For a path t↦rtt\mapsto r_tt↦rt​ with r˙=∂trt\dot r=\partial_tr_tr˙=∂t​rt​, the left side is the derivative of the entropy ∫rtlog⁡rt dν\int r_t\log r_t\,d\nu∫rt​logrt​dν, the first term on the right is −Jν(rt)-J_\nu(r_t)−Jν​(rt​), and the second is the contribution of the drift error. All three are ordinary integrals of integrable functions; the integrability of the drift-score pairing is a separate requirement, which the next section derives from finite energies.

05Entropy production for the unforced weak identity §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let ν\nuν, rrr, r˙\dot rr˙ be as in the previous step (r>0r>0r>0, r∈C1r\in C^1r∈C1), and assume that r˙(log⁡r+1)\dot r(\log r+1)r˙(logr+1) and r∥∇log⁡r∥2r\|\nabla\log r\|^2r∥∇logr∥2 are ν\nuν-integrable and that

∫r˙ ψ dν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ dν\int\dot r\,\psi\,d\nu=-\int\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu∫r˙ψdν=−∫⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩dν

for every continuously differentiable ψ\psiψ such that r˙ψ\dot r\psir˙ψ and ⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩ are ν\nuν-integrable. Then

∫r˙ (log⁡r+1) dν=−∫r∥∇log⁡r∥2 dν.\int\dot r\,(\log r+1)\,d\nu=-\int r\|\nabla\log r\|^2\,d\nu.∫r˙(logr+1)dν=−∫r∥∇logr∥2dν.

Proof. As in the previous step, ψ=log⁡r+1\psi=\log r+1ψ=logr+1 is C1C^1C1, r˙ψ\dot r\psir˙ψ is integrable by hypothesis, and ⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩=r∥∇log⁡r∥2\langle\nabla r,\nabla\psi\rangle=r\|\nabla\log r\|^2⟨∇r,∇ψ⟩=r∥∇logr∥2 is integrable. The weak identity for ψ\psiψ and this pointwise identity give the claim. □\square□

This is the de Bruijn-type identity ddtHν(rtν)=−Jν(rt)\frac{d}{dt}H_\nu(r_t\nu)=-J_\nu(r_t)dtd​Hν​(rt​ν)=−Jν​(rt​) for a path solving the relative Fokker–Planck equation weakly, once the entropy derivative is identified with the left side. As in the forced case, the weak identity must be available for the unbounded test log⁡r+1\log r+1logr+1; a weak equation known only for compactly supported tests does not justify this substitution.

How the Lean proof follows this argument
