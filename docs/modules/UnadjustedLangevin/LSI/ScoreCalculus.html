Relative scores turn the weak equation into entropy production

The chain rule ∇log⁡r=∇r/r\nabla\log r=\nabla r/r∇logr=∇r/r converts the entropy test log⁡r+1\log r+1logr+1 into the relative score. The weak identity supplied for this test then gives Fisher dissipation, with an extra score/error pairing when drift forcing is present.

01The entropy test turns the Dirichlet term into Fisher information §

Mathematical statement and proof

Positivity gives the pointwise identities

∇log⁡r=∇rr,∇(log⁡r+1)=∇log⁡r,⟨∇r,∇(log⁡r+1)⟩=r∥∇log⁡r∥2. \nabla\log r=\frac{\nabla r}{r},\qquad \nabla(\log r+1)=\nabla\log r,\qquad \langle\nabla r,\nabla(\log r+1)\rangle =r\|\nabla\log r\|^2. ∇logr=r∇r​,∇(logr+1)=∇logr,⟨∇r,∇(logr+1)⟩=r∥∇logr∥2.

The first identity is the gradient chain rule. The second explains why the normalization term +1+1+1, introduced by differentiating rlog⁡rr\log rrlogr, contributes no spatial derivative. The third is the algebraic source of Fisher dissipation. Each is proved directly in Lean; no stochastic equation or integration by parts is needed for these pointwise statements.

For an exact Langevin density with an admissible unforced weak equation, this calculation would give H′=−JH'=-JH′=−J. The conditional continuous-time theorem combines that identity with LSI to obtain H(t)≤e−2αtH(0)H(t)\leq e^{-2\alpha t}H(0)H(t)≤e−2αtH(0). The ULA interpolation has an additional frozen-drift error, so its argument continues with a forced equation.

The unforced weak-identity theorem makes this reference calculation precise: take the admissible test log⁡r+1\log r+1logr+1 with the error field absent, and the production integral is exactly minus the weighted squared-score integral. The generic scalar theorem entropy_decay_of_dissipation then proves the rate 2α2\alpha2α, while relative_entropy_decay_of_dissipation identifies its quantities with actual finite KL and Fisher information. Neither theorem constructs a Langevin density path satisfying the supplied dissipation identity. This separation is also useful for ULA: the same dissipation calculation survives, and only the explicitly displayed error contribution changes.

02Test the weak equation with a noncompact function §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose the relative density satisfies, for each admissible continuously differentiable test ψ\psiψ,

∫r˙tψ dν=−∫⟨∇rt,∇ψ⟩ dν+∫rt⟨et,∇ψ⟩ dν. \int\dot r_t\psi\,d\nu =-\int\langle\nabla r_t,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu +\int r_t\langle e_t,\nabla\psi\rangle\,d\nu. ∫r˙t​ψdν=−∫⟨∇rt​,∇ψ⟩dν+∫rt​⟨et​,∇ψ⟩dν.

For frozen drift, the intended error is et(y)=∇U(y)−E[∇U(X)∣Yt=y]e_t(y)=\nabla U(y)-\mathbb E[\nabla U(X)\mid Y_t=y]et​(y)=∇U(y)−E[∇U(X)∣Yt​=y]. This formula explains the sign convention; constructing that conditional field and proving this weak equation remain separate obligations. The abstract weak-identity theorem itself accepts a supplied field ete_tet​.

Choose ψ=log⁡rt+1\psi=\log r_t+1ψ=logrt​+1. The score identities then give

H′(t)=−J(t)+∫rt⟨et,st⟩ dν. H'(t)=-J(t)+\int r_t\langle e_t,s_t\rangle\,d\nu. H′(t)=−J(t)+∫rt​⟨et​,st​⟩dν.

The hypothesis permits the test whenever its three integrands are integrable. Since log⁡rt+1\log r_t+1logrt​+1 generally has noncompact support, a generator identity proved only for compactly supported tests does not automatically establish this hypothesis. The passage to this entropy test needs its own analytic justification.
