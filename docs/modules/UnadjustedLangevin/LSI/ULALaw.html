The Gaussian ULA transition and its interpolation

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Let EEE be a real inner product space of finite dimension nnn, let U:E→RU:E\to\mathbb RU:E→R be a potential, and let γn\gamma_nγn​ be the standard Gaussian measure on EEE, the law of ∑i=1nξiei\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_ie_i∑i=1n​ξi​ei​ for an orthonormal basis (ei)(e_i)(ei​) and independent real standard normal variables ξ1,…,ξn\xi_1,\dots,\xi_nξ1​,…,ξn​. For t≥0t\ge0t≥0 define the update map and the transition on measures

Tt(x,z)=x−t∇U(x)+2t z,Ptρ=(Tt)#(ρ⊗γn),T_t(x,z)=x-t\nabla U(x)+\sqrt{2t}\,z,\qquad P_t\rho=(T_t)_\#(\rho\otimes\gamma_n),Tt​(x,z)=x−t∇U(x)+2t​z,Pt​ρ=(Tt​)#​(ρ⊗γn​),

so that, for a probability measure ρ\rhoρ, PtρP_t\rhoPt​ρ is the law of X−t∇U(X)+2t ZX-t\nabla U(X)+\sqrt{2t}\,ZX−t∇U(X)+2t​Z with X∼ρX\sim\rhoX∼ρ and Z∼γnZ\sim\gamma_nZ∼γn​ independent. With step hhh and initial law μ\muμ, the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) has laws ρk=Phkμ\rho_k=P_h^k\muρk​=Phk​μ, and the interpolating laws of its kkk-th step are ρk,t=Ptρk\rho_{k,t}=P_t\rho_kρk,t​=Pt​ρk​. In Vempala and Wibisono (2019) these are the laws of xk,t=xk−t∇U(xk)+2 (Wkh+t−Wkh)x_{k,t}=x_k-t\nabla U(x_k)+\sqrt2\,(W_{kh+t}-W_{kh})xk,t​=xk​−t∇U(xk​)+2​(Wkh+t​−Wkh​), 0≤t≤h0\le t\le h0≤t≤h, along the time-ttt Euler step from xk∼ρkx_k\sim\rho_kxk​∼ρk​.

The section proves the identities that connect the interpolation with the algorithm, namely P0ρ=ρP_0\rho=\rhoP0​ρ=ρ, ρ0=μ\rho_0=\muρ0​=μ, ρk+1=Phρk\rho_{k+1}=P_h\rho_kρk+1​=Ph​ρk​ and hence ρk,0=ρk\rho_{k,0}=\rho_kρk,0​=ρk​, ρk,h=ρk+1\rho_{k,h}=\rho_{k+1}ρk,h​=ρk+1​; and it proves that, when μ\muμ is a probability measure and ∇U\nabla U∇U is Lipschitz, all these laws are probability measures. It also defines the sequence of entropies Hν(ρk)H_\nu(\rho_k)Hν​(ρk​) as real numbers. No convexity or log-Sobolev assumption enters, and nothing is asserted here about densities, differentiability in ttt or entropy production; those are the subject of later sections, which use the identities proved here to turn an estimate along the interpolation into an estimate between consecutive iterates.

03The interpolating law at time zero §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. For every function U:E→RU:E\to\mathbb RU:E→R and every measure ρ\rhoρ on EEE, P0ρ=ρP_0\rho=\rhoP0​ρ=ρ.

Proof. At t=0t=0t=0, T0(x,z)=x−0⋅∇U(x)+0 z=xT_0(x,z)=x-0\cdot\nabla U(x)+\sqrt0\,z=xT0​(x,z)=x−0⋅∇U(x)+0​z=x, so T0T_0T0​ is the projection π1(x,z)=x\pi_1(x,z)=xπ1​(x,z)=x, which is measurable. For a Borel set A⊆EA\subseteq EA⊆E,

(P0ρ)(A)=(ρ⊗γn)(π1−1(A))=(ρ⊗γn)(A×E)=ρ(A) γn(E)=ρ(A),(P_0\rho)(A)=(\rho\otimes\gamma_n)\bigl(\pi_1^{-1}(A)\bigr)=(\rho\otimes\gamma_n)(A\times E)=\rho(A)\,\gamma_n(E)=\rho(A),(P0​ρ)(A)=(ρ⊗γn​)(π1−1​(A))=(ρ⊗γn​)(A×E)=ρ(A)γn​(E)=ρ(A),

where the third equality is the defining property of the product measure on rectangles (valid because γn\gamma_nγn​ is a finite measure) and the last one uses γn(E)=1\gamma_n(E)=1γn​(E)=1. □\square□

No regularity of UUU is needed, because the gradient is multiplied by 000. The identity is an equality of measures and involves no limit t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0; continuity of the interpolation at t=0t=0t=0 is a different matter, treated elsewhere.

Claim. For every k∈Nk\in\mathbb Nk∈N, ρk+1=Phρk\rho_{k+1}=P_h\rho_kρk+1​=Ph​ρk​.

Proof. For any map fff and any kkk, fk+1(x)=f(fk(x))f^{k+1}(x)=f(f^k(x))fk+1(x)=f(fk(x)); this follows by induction on kkk from fk+1=fk∘ff^{k+1}=f^k\circ ffk+1=fk∘f (both sides equal f(x)f(x)f(x) for k=0k=0k=0, and fk+2(x)=fk+1(f(x))=f(fk(f(x)))=f(fk+1(x))f^{k+2}(x)=f^{k+1}(f(x))=f(f^k(f(x)))=f(f^{k+1}(x))fk+2(x)=fk+1(f(x))=f(fk(f(x)))=f(fk+1(x)) by the induction hypothesis applied at f(x)f(x)f(x)). 07The interpolating law is a probability measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Let EEE carry its Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra, let μ\muμ be a probability measure on EEE, and let ∇U\nabla U∇U be LLL-Lipschitz for some L≥0L\ge0L≥0. Then PtμP_t\muPt​μ is a probability measure for every t∈Rt\in\mathbb Rt∈R.

Proof. A Lipschitz map is continuous, and a continuous map between metric spaces is Borel measurable; hence ∇U\nabla U∇U is measurable. The coordinate maps (x,z)↦x(x,z)\mapsto x(x,z)↦x and (x,z)↦z(x,z)\mapsto z(x,z)↦z are measurable on E×EE\times EE×E, and so are the compositions (x,z)↦t∇U(x)(x,z)\mapsto t\nabla U(x)(x,z)↦t∇U(x) and (x,z)↦2t z(x,z)\mapsto\sqrt{2t}\,z(x,z)↦2t​z. Sums and differences of measurable maps into the finite-dimensional space EEE are measurable (addition is continuous and EEE is separable). Hence TtT_tTt​ is measurable. Therefore

(Ptμ)(E)=(μ⊗γn)(Tt−1(E))=(μ⊗γn)(E×E)=μ(E) γn(E)=1.□(P_t\mu)(E)=(\mu\otimes\gamma_n)\bigl(T_t^{-1}(E)\bigr)=(\mu\otimes\gamma_n)(E\times E)=\mu(E)\,\gamma_n(E)=1. \qquad\square(Pt​μ)(E)=(μ⊗γn​)(Tt−1​(E))=(μ⊗γn​)(E×E)=μ(E)γn​(E)=1.□

The Lipschitz hypothesis is used only through the measurability of ∇U\nabla U∇U. Its role is to make the image measure well defined; without measurability of the update map the image of μ⊗γn\mu\otimes\gamma_nμ⊗γn​ is not defined.

08Every law of the algorithm is a probability measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Claim. Under the hypotheses of the previous step (Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra, μ\muμ a probability measure, ∇U\nabla U∇U Lipschitz), every ρk=Phkμ\rho_k=P_h^k\muρk​=Phk​μ is a probability measure, for every h∈Rh\in\mathbb Rh∈R.

Proof. Induction on kkk. For k=0k=0k=0, ρ0=μ\rho_0=\muρ0​=μ is a probability measure by hypothesis. 09The entropy of the laws of the algorithm §

Mathematical statement and proof

Definition. For a measure ν\nuν (the target), the entropy of the kkk-th ULA law is the real number

Hk={Hν(ρk),if Hν(ρk)<∞,0,otherwise,H_k=\begin{cases}H_\nu(\rho_k), & \text{if }H_\nu(\rho_k)<\infty,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}Hk​={Hν​(ρk​),0,​if Hν​(ρk​)<∞,otherwise,​
