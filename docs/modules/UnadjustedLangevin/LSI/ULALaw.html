The Gaussian ULA transition and its interpolation

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Statement and proof

01Entropy of the ULA iterates §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define Hk=KL⁡(ρk∣ν)H_k=\operatorname{KL}(\rho_k\mid\nu)Hk​=KL(ρk​∣ν), where ρk\rho_kρk​ is obtained by applying the Gaussian transition kkk times. The divergence is allowed to equal infinity at the definition stage. To use the sequence in a real recurrence, one must prove Hk<∞H_k<\inftyHk​<∞ at every index. In the final convergence theorem this is proved by induction from finite initial entropy, using the positive-time Gaussian density and LSI.

02Entropy at the initial law §

Mathematical statement and proof

The zeroth iterate of the transition is the identity, so ρ0=μ\rho_0=\muρ0​=μ. Substitution in the definition of entropy gives H0=KL⁡(μ∣ν)H_0=\operatorname{KL}(\mu\mid\nu)H0​=KL(μ∣ν). This equality holds independently of the step size. When the initial divergence is finite, it provides the numerical initial value in the entropy recurrence.

03Definition of the interpolating law §

Mathematical statement and proof

Let X∼μX\sim\muX∼μ and Z∼γdZ\sim\gamma_dZ∼γd​ be independent. Define

Ptμ=Law⁡(X−t∇U(X)+2tZ)=(Tt)∗(μ⊗γd).P_t\mu=\operatorname{Law}(X-t\nabla U(X)+\sqrt{2t}Z)=(T_t)_*(\mu\otimes\gamma_d).Pt​μ=Law(X−t∇U(X)+2t​Z)=(Tt​)∗​(μ⊗γd​).

Conditional on X=xX=xX=x, the new variable has Gaussian law with mean x−t∇U(x)x-t\nabla U(x)x−t∇U(x) and covariance 2tId2tI_d2tId​. The gradient is evaluated at the initial state throughout this interpolation. This conditional law determines the mixture density and the drift discrepancy used in the entropy calculation.

04Iteration of the transition §

Mathematical statement and proof

For a fixed hhh, define ρk=Phkμ\rho_k=P_h^k\muρk​=Phk​μ. Each transition is applied to the law obtained at the preceding index and integrates a new independent Gaussian variable. A stochastic recursion with innovations independent of its initial state and of its past has these marginal laws: conditioning on the current state gives PhP_hPh​, and induction identifies its distribution at every index. The definition by measures therefore does not depend on a particular probability space realizing the recursion.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

05The final law of one interpolation step §

Mathematical statement and proof

The interpolation starting from ρk\rho_kρk​ is ρk,t=Ptρk\rho_{k,t}=P_t\rho_kρk,t​=Pt​ρk​. At elapsed time hhh, the defining recursion gives

ρk,h=Phρk=ρk+1.\rho_{k,h}=P_h\rho_k=\rho_{k+1}.ρk,h​=Ph​ρk​=ρk+1​.

Consequently an entropy estimate for the interpolation at hhh is an estimate for the next ULA iterate. This equality, rather than an approximation by a continuous diffusion, is used to derive the discrete recurrence.

06Each iterate is a probability measure §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose μ\muμ is a probability measure and ∇U\nabla U∇U is Lipschitz. The gradient is continuous, so the Gaussian update map is measurable. The transition preserves total mass because it is a pushforward of the product of two probability measures. Starting with ρ0=μ\rho_0=\muρ0​=μ, induction using ρk+1=Phρk\rho_{k+1}=P_h\rho_kρk+1​=Ph​ρk​ proves that every ρk\rho_kρk​ has mass one. The probability assumptions in the subsequent entropy identities thus hold at every index.

07The interpolating law at zero §

Mathematical statement and proof

At t=0t=0t=0, the map satisfies T0(x,z)=xT_0(x,z)=xT0​(x,z)=x: both time-dependent terms vanish. Its pushforward is the first marginal of μ⊗γd\mu\otimes\gamma_dμ⊗γd​. Since the Gaussian measure has mass one, that marginal is μ\muμ. Therefore P0μ=μP_0\mu=\muP0​μ=μ. The proof is an equality of measures and uses no limiting argument or regularity of UUU.

08The zeroth iterate §

Mathematical statement and proof

By definition, the zeroth iterate of any transition map is the identity map on measures. It follows that ρ0=μ\rho_0=\muρ0​=μ. No Gaussian transition is performed at this index. In particular, the initialization in the stochastic recursion and in the entropy estimate refers to the same measure.

09The recursion for successive laws §

Mathematical statement and proof

The elementary identity for iterates of a map gives Phk+1μ=Ph(Phkμ)P_h^{k+1}\mu=P_h(P_h^k\mu)Phk+1​μ=Ph​(Phk​μ). In the notation for ULA laws this is

ρk+1=Ph(ρk).\rho_{k+1}=P_h(\rho_k).ρk+1​=Ph​(ρk​).

The order of composition agrees with one additional update of the current state using a fresh Gaussian variable. This identity is used when the one-step entropy estimate is iterated.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.

10The interpolation preserves total mass §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume ∇U\nabla U∇U is Lipschitz and μ\muμ is a probability measure. For fixed ttt, addition, scalar multiplication and continuity of the gradient make TtT_tTt​ measurable. The product μ⊗γd\mu\otimes\gamma_dμ⊗γd​ has total mass one. A measurable pushforward has the same total mass as its source; hence PtμP_t\muPt​μ is a probability measure. This argument also verifies the normalization of each density used in LSI.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.36.5; ULA-SC, Section 3.36.7.
