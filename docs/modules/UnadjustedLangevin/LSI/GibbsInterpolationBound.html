A Gaussian density comparison at the initial endpoint

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

Assume the differentiable potential has LLL-Lipschitz gradient. For 0<t<1/(4L)0<t<1/(4L)0<t<1/(4L), put δ=1−4tL>0\delta=1-4tL>0δ=1−4tL>0 and s=t/δs=t/\deltas=t/δ. The smoothness inequalities compare the transition exponent weighted by Gibbs with a Gaussian exponent in the starting variable, centered at y−s∇U(y)y-s\nabla U(y)y−s∇U(y).

Completing the square leaves the term s∥∇U(y)∥2/4s\|\nabla U(y)\|^2/4s∥∇U(y)∥2/4. Integration of the remaining Gaussian gives the normalizer ratio (s/t)d/2=δ−d/2(s/t)^{d/2}=\delta^{-d/2}(s/t)d/2=δ−d/2. Therefore the density of νPt\nu P_tνPt​ relative to ν\nuν is at most δ−d/2exp⁡(s∥∇U(y)∥2/4)\delta^{-d/2}\exp(s\|\nabla U(y)\|^2/4)δ−d/2exp(s∥∇U(y)∥2/4). This comparison supplies the reference-density correction in the initial entropy estimate, using only the stated differentiability and Lipschitz gradient assumptions.

01The Gaussian normalizer ratio §

Mathematical statement and proof

For t>0t>0t>0, integration of the radial Gaussian gives Nt=(4πt)−d/2N_t=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}Nt​=(4πt)−d/2. Therefore for positive s,ts,ts,t,

Nt/Ns=(s/t)d/2.N_t/N_s=(s/t)^{d/2}.Nt​/Ns​=(s/t)d/2.

The proof obtains the individual integrals from the finite-dimensional Gaussian integral, uses positivity to invert them, and applies the multiplication and division identities for real powers.

The normalizer scales as t−d/2t^{-d/2}t−d/2, so replacing ttt by s=t/δs=t/\deltas=t/δ contributes (s/t)d/2=δ−d/2(s/t)^{d/2}=\delta^{-d/2}(s/t)d/2=δ−d/2 when the reversed Gaussian is integrated. The direction of this ratio matters for the entropy correction's sign.

Write gx=∇U(x)g_x=\nabla U(x)gx​=∇U(x), gy=∇U(y)g_y=\nabla U(y)gy​=∇U(y), and choose positive s,ts,ts,t with s(1−4tL)=ts(1-4tL)=ts(1−4tL)=t. Only the first-order smooth upper model and Lipschitz gradient comparison are used. Their quadratic terms are collected into the reversed Gaussian square, leaving the explicit positive correction in the observation-gradient norm.

03Integrate the comparison against the Gibbs start §

Mathematical statement and proof

The relative density of the updated Gibbs law is

rν,t(y)=∫Ntexp⁡ ⁣(U(y)−U(x)−∥y−x+t∇U(x)∥24t)dx.r_{\nu,t}(y)=\int N_t\exp\!\left(U(y)-U(x)-\frac{\|y-x+t\nabla U(x)\|^2}{4t}\right)dx.rν,t​(y)=∫Nt​exp(U(y)−U(x)−4t∥y−x+t∇U(x)∥2​)dx.

Insert the pointwise comparison. The remaining xxx-dependent expression is a normalized Gaussian with time sss centered at y−s∇U(y)y-s\nabla U(y)y−s∇U(y), so its integral is one. The factors independent of xxx give

rν,t(y)≤(s/t)d/2exp⁡ ⁣(s4∥∇U(y)∥2).r_{\nu,t}(y)\le(s/t)^{d/2}\exp\!\left(\frac s4\|\nabla U(y)\|^2\right).rν,t​(y)≤(s/t)d/2exp(4s​∥∇U(y)∥2).

Normalization of the original Gibbs probability cancels its partition function. Taking s=t/(1−4tL)s=t/(1-4tL)s=t/(1−4tL) later supplies an explicit reference-density bound whose logarithmic cost tends to zero.

After pointwise comparison, integrate the reversed Gaussian in the starting variable. Its total mass is one after its own normalizer is inserted, yielding the density-ratio upper bound needed at the initial entropy endpoint.
