An integrated chain rule from spacetime integrability

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound follows Theorem 2, Section 3.1, and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019). The Lean development supplies the regularity and endpoint arguments for the actual Gaussian ULA laws.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

To differentiate entropy, bounded approximations are first integrated in time. A pointwise time derivative together with joint spacetime L1L^1L1 suffices for Fubini and the fundamental theorem of calculus; no common spatial majorant for every time derivative is assumed. The resulting spatial integral is absolutely continuous and has the integrated derivative almost everywhere. A dominated-limit theorem then passes truncated integral identities to the actual entropy identity.

For a spacetime-integrable derivative field F(t,x)F(t,x)F(t,x), Fubini gives an absolutely continuous time primitive A(t)=A(a)+∫at∫F(s,x)dν(x)dsA(t)=A(a)+\int_a^t\int F(s,x)d\nu(x)dsA(t)=A(a)+∫at​∫F(s,x)dν(x)ds. The fundamental theorem of calculus identifies its derivative almost everywhere with the inner integral. If bounded approximations have convergent endpoint values and a common integrable flux majorant, dominated convergence transfers their integrated identities to the limit. This obtains entropy absolute continuity from an integrated identity rather than assuming pointwise differentiability at every time.

Mathematical statement and proof

Apply the integrated fundamental theorem on every subinterval [a,t]⊆[a,b][a,t]\subseteq[a,b][a,t]⊆[a,b]. Restricting the spacetime measure preserves integrability, giving both integrability of f(t,⋅)f(t,\cdot)f(t,⋅) and F(t)=F(a)+∫atq(u)duF(t)=F(a)+\int_a^t q(u)duF(t)=F(a)+∫at​q(u)du, where F(t)=∫f(t,x)dν(x)F(t)=\int f(t,x)d\nu(x)F(t)=∫f(t,x)dν(x) and q(t)=∫f˙(t,x)dν(x)q(t)=\int\dot f(t,x)d\nu(x)q(t)=∫f˙​(t,x)dν(x).

Equality on the interval transfers absolute continuity to FFF. Thus regularity of the spatial integral is a conclusion of spacetime integrability and pointwise differentiation.

An integral of an L1L^1L1 time function is absolutely continuous. The spacetime norm bound makes the spatially integrated derivative such an L1L^1L1 function, supplying this regularity without an assumed entropy derivative.

03The almost-everywhere derivative of the integrated quantity §

Mathematical statement and proof

The subinterval argument gives F(t)=F(a)+∫atq(u)duF(t)=F(a)+\int_a^t q(u)duF(t)=F(a)+∫at​q(u)du for every t∈[a,b]t\in[a,b]t∈[a,b], with q∈L1(a,b)q\in L^1(a,b)q∈L1(a,b). Lebesgue differentiation supplies derivative q(t)q(t)q(t) for the indefinite integral at almost every interior time. For each such ttt, an open neighborhood stays inside (a,b)(a,b)(a,b), where the displayed identity holds. The local equality transfers the derivative to FFF, yielding F′(t)=∫f˙(t,x)dν(x)F'(t)=\int\dot f(t,x)d\nu(x)F′(t)=∫f˙​(t,x)dν(x) almost everywhere. No pointwise derivative assertion at every time, or at the endpoints, is inferred.

The fundamental theorem for absolutely continuous primitives gives the derivative almost everywhere. The exceptional time set has measure zero, which is sufficient for all later integrating-factor inequalities.

04Passing an integrated identity through dominated limits §

Mathematical statement and proof

Suppose truncated identities have the form Bn−An=∫qndνB_n-A_n=\int q_n d\nuBn​−An​=∫qn​dν. Assume the two endpoint sequences converge to their intended values, qn→qq_n\to qqn​→q almost everywhere, and ∣qn∣≤g|q_n|\le g∣qn​∣≤g with g∈L1(ν)g\in L^1(\nu)g∈L1(ν). Dominated convergence gives convergence of the right-hand integrals, while the endpoint limits give convergence of the left-hand differences. Their equality for every nnn and uniqueness of limits prove B−A=∫qdνB-A=\int qd\nuB−A=∫qdν.

In the entropy application, the endpoints are truncated entropy integrals and qnq_nqn​ are bounded-test fluxes on a fixed time interval. Spatial energy estimates supply the pointwise flux limit and a time-integrable dominating bound. This lemma passes the integrated identities to the limit without assuming in advance that the full entropy has already been differentiated.

Pass the approximating integrated identities to the limit in both endpoints and flux. Endpoint domination and spacetime flux domination are checked separately because they use different reference measures and majorants.

05Regularity supplied by the limiting integral identity §

The identity holds in an interior neighborhood of each such time, so the derivative transfers to HHH. This gives precisely the final entropy comparison inputs: local absolute continuity and the actual flux as derivative almost everywhere.

The limiting identity expresses the function as a constant plus an integral of an L1L^1L1 flux. It therefore supplies both local absolute continuity and its almost-everywhere derivative, closing the regularity needed for entropy comparison.
