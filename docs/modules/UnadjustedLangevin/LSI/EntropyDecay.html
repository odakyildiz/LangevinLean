01A scalar affine comparison from right derivatives §

Mathematical statement and proof

For continuous HHH with right derivative bounded by −aH+b-aH+b−aH+b, a>0a>0a>0, compare it to the exact scalar ODE solution. The one-sided Grönwall theorem accepts the right derivative through its right-slope liminf inequality. Simplifying its explicit solution gives H(t)≤e−atH(0)+(b/a)(1−e−at)H(t)\le e^{-at}H(0)+(b/a)(1-e^{-at})H(t)≤e−atH(0)+(b/a)(1−e−at). Neither HHH nor bbb needs to be nonnegative.

Subtract the exact affine ODE solution from HHH. Its upper right derivative is bounded by −a-a−a times the difference, and the difference starts at zero. The one-sided comparison gives a nonpositive difference throughout the interval.

02Scalar LSI and dissipation give rate 2α §

Mathematical statement and proof

The supplied dissipation identity is H′=−IH'=-IH′=−I, and the scalar LSI premise is 2αH≤I2\alpha H\le I2αH≤I. Therefore H′≤−2αHH'\le-2\alpha HH′≤−2αH. Apply the affine comparison theorem with a=2αa=2\alphaa=2α and b=0b=0b=0, obtaining H(t)≤e−2αtH(0)H(t)\le e^{-2\alpha t}H(0)H(t)≤e−2αtH(0). This scalar theorem does not itself establish the dissipation identity for a law-valued evolution.

The coefficient two is inherited from the stated normalization of LSI. The unforced dissipation identity spends the entire Fisher term, so its scalar decay rate is 2α2\alpha2α; the later frozen-drift estimate spends part of that term on error absorption.
