Data processing and a finite-entropy change of reference

A Gaussian ULA interpolation applies the same transition to the input law and the Gibbs law. Data processing controls their mutual entropy. A one-sided upper bound on the updated Gibbs density then changes the reference back to the original target, proving finiteness as well as an upper bound. Propagated gradient moments make the logarithmic density correction integrable, without any initial position-moment assumption.

01klDiv prod same right §

Mathematical statement and proof

Adjoin the same independent probability innovation η\etaη to two finite measures. The log likelihood of μ⊗η\mu\otimes\etaμ⊗η relative to ν⊗η\nu\otimes\etaν⊗η depends only on the first coordinate, so KL is unchanged. The formal proof uses Mathlib's conditional-product KL identity with the constant Markov kernel x↦ηx\mapsto\etax↦η; its conditional products are exactly the ordinary product measures. This equality supplies the independent-noise step in data processing.

02llr eq add of absolutelyContinuous §

Mathematical statement and proof

For μ≪ρ≪ν\mu\ll\rho\ll\nuμ≪ρ≪ν, the Radon-Nikodym chain rule gives dμ/dν=(dμ/dρ)(dρ/dν)d\mu/d\nu=(d\mu/d\rho)(d\rho/d\nu)dμ/dν=(dμ/dρ)(dρ/dν) almost everywhere under the integrated law μ\muμ. Each factor is positive and finite there: positivity is transported along absolute continuity, and finiteness uses the probability measures' finite Radon-Nikodym derivatives.

Convert the product of extended densities to real numbers, then apply the logarithm product rule. This proves log⁡(dμ/dν)=log⁡(dμ/dρ)+log⁡(dρ/dν)\log(d\mu/d\nu)=\log(d\mu/d\rho)+\log(d\rho/d\nu)log(dμ/dν)=log(dμ/dρ)+log(dρ/dν), as an almost-everywhere identity under μ\muμ. The positivity and finiteness checks prevent using logarithm algebra at a zero or infinite density.

03integrable exp neg llr §

Mathematical statement and proof

05klDiv finite and le of reference density bound §

Mathematical statement and proof

Assume finite H(μ∣ρ)H(\mu\mid\rho)H(μ∣ρ), ρ≪ν\rho\ll\nuρ≪ν, and dρ/dν≤ebd\rho/d\nu\le e^bdρ/dν≤eb, with b∈L1(μ)b\in L^1(\mu)b∈L1(μ). Finite KL gives μ≪ρ\mu\ll\rhoμ≪ρ, hence μ≪ν\mu\ll\nuμ≪ν. The density bound transfers to μ\muμ-almost every point, where positivity of the intermediate density permits logarithms. Thus log⁡(dρ/dν)≤b\log(d\rho/d\nu)\le blog(dρ/dν)≤b.

The log-likelihood chain rule gives log⁡(dμ/dν)≤log⁡(dμ/dρ)+b\log(d\mu/d\nu)\le\log(d\mu/d\rho)+blog(dμ/dν)≤log(dμ/dρ)+b. Its right side is integrable. The preceding one-sided integrability theorem therefore proves the new log likelihood integrable and hence H(μ∣ν)<∞H(\mu\mid\nu)<\inftyH(μ∣ν)<∞. Only after proving finiteness do we integrate the inequality, yielding H(μ∣ν)≤H(μ∣ρ)+EμbH(\mu\mid\nu)\le H(\mu\mid\rho)+\mathbb E_\mu bH(μ∣ν)≤H(μ∣ρ)+Eμ​b.
