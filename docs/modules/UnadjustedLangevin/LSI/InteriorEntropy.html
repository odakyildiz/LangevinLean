Entropy comparison using positive-time derivatives

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

A continuous function on [0,T][0,T][0,T] can satisfy an affine comparison using derivative bounds only on (0,T)(0,T)(0,T). This applies to Gaussian entropy calculations where derivatives are first proved at positive times. The second result identifies the relative-entropy derivative with the differentiated density integral at an interior time, under the stated local domination assumptions. Control near zero is required separately when passing to the initial law.

02The entropy derivative at an interior time §

Mathematical statement and proof

Fix 0<t<T0<t<T0<t<T. Suppose the relative densities are positive, C1C^1C1, normalized and locally dominated for entropy differentiation at every time in (0,T)(0,T)(0,T). Dominated differentiation gives the derivative of ∫rulog⁡ru dν\int r_u\log r_u\,d\nu∫ru​logru​dν at ttt. A sufficiently small neighborhood of ttt lies in (0,T)(0,T)(0,T); on this neighborhood the finite-density entropy formula identifies that integral with relative entropy. Equality of the functions near ttt therefore yields

ddtKL⁡(rtν∣ν)=∫r˙t(x)(log⁡rt(x)+1) dν(x).\frac d{dt}\operatorname{KL}(r_t\nu\mid\nu)=\int\dot r_t(x)(\log r_t(x)+1)\,d\nu(x).dtd​KL(rt​ν∣ν)=∫r˙t​(x)(logrt​(x)+1)dν(x).

The assumptions concern interior times only. Continuity or a one-sided bound at zero must still be proved before applying an estimate that involves the initial entropy.
