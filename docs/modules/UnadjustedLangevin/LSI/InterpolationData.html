Assumptions for a regular ULA interpolation

References and publication status for this development

Paper reference

The entropy bound is Theorem 2 of Vempala and Wibisono (2019); its proof is in Section 3.1 and Appendices B.2.1–B.2.2. The proof here establishes Gaussian density regularity, entropy differentiation at positive times, and the limit as elapsed time decreases to zero.

  • Santosh S. Vempala and Andre Wibisono (2019). Rapid Convergence of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm: Isoperimetry Suffices. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32, 8094–8106. Full paper with appendices

The assumptions specify relative densities rk,tr_{k,t}rk,t​ representing the Gaussian ULA interpolation, their time derivatives, and drift discrepancies. They require positivity, spatial regularity, local integrable domination for entropy differentiation, a weak equation valid for the entropy test, finite score and error energies, and the stated moment estimates. Under these assumptions and LSI, the probability, finiteness, one-step and iterated entropy conclusions below follow.

01The regular interpolation assumptions §

Mathematical statement and proof

For every index kkk and elapsed time t∈[0,h]t\in[0,h]t∈[0,h], suppose rk,tν=ρk,tr_{k,t}\nu=\rho_{k,t}rk,t​ν=ρk,t​. Require positive C1C^1C1 densities, local time derivatives and domination of their entropy derivatives, an admissible forced weak equation, integrable score and discrepancy energies, and the numerical bounds on these energies and the starting gradient moment. The equality of measures holds throughout the interpolation, so the density at every time represents the same law used in the algorithm. An entropy derivative, a recurrence, or a convergence bound is not among these assumptions.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

Supplying this structure means supplying each field, with the types shown in the exact statement.

02Normalization of the represented density §

Mathematical statement and proof

The Gaussian transition preserves probability, and each ULA iterate is a probability measure. Therefore the interpolation starting from that iterate has total mass one. The equality rk,tν=ρk,tr_{k,t}\nu=\rho_{k,t}rk,t​ν=ρk,t​ gives ∫rk,t dν=1\int r_{k,t}\,d\nu=1∫rk,t​dν=1. This establishes the normalization required to apply LSI to the relative density.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

03Finite entropy of each iterate §

Mathematical statement and proof

Local entropy regularity gives integrability of rk,tlog⁡rk,tr_{k,t}\log r_{k,t}rk,t​logrk,t​, hence finite KL for the represented probability measure. At elapsed time zero the equality of measures becomes rk,0ν=ρkr_{k,0}\nu=\rho_krk,0​ν=ρk​. Thus KL⁡(ρk∣ν)<∞\operatorname{KL}(\rho_k\mid\nu)<\inftyKL(ρk​∣ν)<∞ for every kkk. The target is assumed to be a probability measure; in applications this property is included in LSI.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

04The one-step consequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Define the proposed entropy derivative by integrating r˙k,t(log⁡rk,t+1)\dot r_{k,t}(\log r_{k,t}+1)r˙k,t​(logrk,t​+1). The local differentiation assumptions prove that this is the right derivative of relative entropy. The weak equation and finite energies give the Fisher-information derivative bound. LSI and the stated moment estimates then satisfy the hypotheses of the scalar one-step comparison. Identifying the laws at zero and hhh yields the recurrence for successive ULA entropies.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.

How the Lean proof follows this argument

RegularInterpolationData.one_step: the intermediate facts include H, hcalculus, hp, he, in source order; the source steps apply or rewrite ula_kl_one_step_of_interpolation. The calc block checks the successive equalities or inequalities in the argument. The remaining scalar rearrangements are checked by linarith.

05The iterated consequence §

Mathematical statement and proof

Under LSI, gradient Lipschitzness and the positive step restriction, the regular interpolation assumptions imply the one-step bound at every index. Every entropy is finite by the preceding argument. Apply the finite geometric-sum estimate to obtain the stated bound for KL⁡(ρk∣ν)\operatorname{KL}(\rho_k\mid\nu)KL(ρk​∣ν). All density, weak-equation and moment hypotheses remain in this conditional conclusion.

Earlier results used here: ULA-SC, Section 3.26.3.
